## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section describes the hydrothermal synthesis approach used to prepare the Fe₂O₃, Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95, and Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 catalysts with emphasis on reproducibility and clarity.

• Overview of Catalyst Synthesis Techniques  
A hydrothermal method is employed with two different thermal regimes (95 °C and 140 °C) to control the morphology and chemical bonding within the core–shell system. The use of a blue‑cap bottle heated in a blast air oven guarantees temperature precision, while pH control using HCl during precursor mixing ensures well‑dispersed spindle morphologies.

### Step-by-step Experimental Procedures

1. **Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation**  
   - Reagents: Dissolve 2.703 g FeCl₃·6H₂O and 0.0351 g NaH₂PO₄·H₂O in 500 mL deionized water.  
   - pH Adjustment: Add HCl to maintain optimal pH for controlled precipitate formation.  
   - Equipment: Blue‑cap bottle is used to contain the solution.

2. **Precipitation–Deposition**  
   - Immediately following mixing, a MnO₄⁻ solution is added dropwise along with a phosphate surfactant.  
   - Observations: Precipitate formation is noted as the process evolves, ensuring the MnO₂ is uniformly deposited on the Fe₂O₃ core.

3. **Drying**  
   - Conditions: The precipitate is vacuum‑dried at 70 °C overnight.  
   - Impact: This step solidifies the spindle morphology and prepares the sample for thermal treatment.

4. **Calcination**  
   - Although specific calcination parameters (e.g., 450 °C for 5 h) may vary, this step is critical in consolidating the phase structure.  
   - Note: For Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140, the addition of a potassium precursor such as K₂CO₃ at 140 °C promotes the intercalation of K⁺, enabling the formation of K₀.₄₆Mn₂O₄.

5. **Post‑treatment / Activation**  
   - In situ reduction is performed at 300 °C under a 50% H₂/50% CO atmosphere for 10 h to convert Fe₂O₃ into active iron carbide sites essential for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

### Comparative Analysis  
• Advantages:  
 – Controlled pH and precise thermal conditions yield reproducible catalyst morphology and surface area (Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 exhibits thinner, crystalline MnO₂ nanosheets and enhanced porosity).  
• Disadvantages:  
 – The strict control of reagent concentrations, pH, and reactor temperature imposes challenges in scaling the process.

This structured synthesis ensures that every step—from precursor mixing to activation—is clearly defined and reproducible for high-performance Fischer–Tropsch catalysts.

##### 3.1.3.1 Fe₂O₃ Catalyst  
- **Precursor solution**: Mixed thoroughly to ensure a homogeneous solution suitable for subsequent processing.

##### 3.1.3.3 Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 Catalyst  
- **Fe₂O₃ dispersion**: Sonicated for 10 minutes to achieve even distribution.  
- **KMnO₄-Fe₂O₃ mixture**: Stirred and subsequently reacted at 140 °C for 2 hours in an oil bath under an inert atmosphere, facilitating effective intercalation of K⁺ into the MnO₂ shell.

This structured procedure, detailing specific reagent quantities and controlled mixing parameters, supports full replicability of the synthesis steps across all catalyst formulations.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Fe₂O₃  
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**: 10.0 g FeCl₃·6H₂O dissolved in 50 mL deionized water; additional additives such as NaH₂PO₄·H₂O may be used for controlled morphology.  
- **Conditions**: Stirring at room temperature for 1 hour; the precursor solution is then transferred into a sealed vessel and heated in a blast oven at 105 °C for 48 hours.  
- **Process**:  
  - Dissolve FeCl₃·6H₂O completely in water ensuring a homogeneous solution.  
  - Combine with appropriate phosphate components to facilitate spindle-like morphology.  
  - Maintain the sealed reaction mixture at 105 °C for 48 hours.  
- **Key Steps**: Ensure complete dissolution and homogeneous reaction mixture prior to heat treatment.

##### Step 2: Purification and Drying  
- **Process**: Recover Fe₂O₃ powders by centrifugation, followed by sequential washing with deionized water and ethanol.  
- **Drying**: Vacuum-dry at 70 °C overnight.

##### Step 3: Calcination and Activation  
- **Process**: Calcine the dried powders under a controlled atmosphere, then activate at 300 °C under a 50% H₂/50% CO flow for 10 hours to convert Fe₂O₃ into active iron carbide phases.

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [2]: Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95  
##### Step 1: Catalyst Assembly (Core–Shell Formation)  
- **Reagents**: Utilize previously prepared Fe₂O₃ spindles (≈90 mg); prepare an aqueous solution containing 0.2 g KMnO₄, sonicated in 25 mL deionized water with a phosphate surfactant, and acidified with 0.4 mL HCl.  
- **Conditions**: Sonicate both Fe₂O₃ dispersion and KMnO₄ solution separately for 10 minutes.  
- **Process**:  
  - Mix the KMnO₄ solution with the Fe₂O₃ dispersion under stirring.  
  - Conduct the hydrothermal reaction at 95 °C for 10 hours to form a MnO₂ shell via controlled oxidation.
  
##### Step 2: Purification, Drying & Calcination  
- **Process**: Centrifuge the reaction mixture, rinse with water and ethanol; dry at 70 °C overnight followed by calcination under designated conditions.  

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [3]: Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140  
##### Step 1: Catalyst Assembly with Promoter Incorporation  
- **Reagents**: Start with the Fe₂O₃ precursor and an aqueous KMnO₄ solution prepared as above; include a potassium precursor (e.g., K₂CO₃) to achieve a promoter content of 2.3 wt% K⁺.  
- **Conditions**: Conduct the hydrothermal synthesis at 140 °C to favor chemical intercalation resulting in K₀.₄₆Mn₂O₄ formation.  
- **Process**:  
  - Follow the same sonication and mixing protocol as Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95.  
  - React the mixture at 140 °C for 10 hours ensuring enhanced K⁺ bonding in the MnO₂ shell.
  
##### Step 2: Purification, Drying & Final Activation  
- **Process**: Repeat centrifugation, washing, and vacuum-drying at 70 °C overnight.  
- **Activation**: Calcine and activate under identical conditions as for Fe₂O₃ to yield active iron carbide phases.

---

These step-by-step workflows ensure that each catalyst’s synthesis—from raw precursor preparation to final activation—is reproducible, with critical parameters (pH, reagent addition, stirring durations, hydrothermal temperature) precisely controlled.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation of Fischer–Tropsch catalysts is a critical pre-reaction treatment designed to convert the Fe₂O₃-based precursors into active iron carbide phases. This controlled reduction process is achieved under specific experimental conditions that balance both reductive and carburizing environments.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature  
- **Definition:** The activation temperature ensures effective reduction of Fe₂O₃, initiating the transformation into reactive iron carbide species essential for subsequent catalytic activity.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** In this study, the catalysts are activated at 300 °C under controlled heating.  
- **Observations:** This moderate temperature is sufficient to promote massive transformation without causing detrimental sintering or loss of the porous core–shell structure.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure  
- **Definition:** Activation pressure influences the interaction between the reducing gas and the catalyst; however, its role here is secondary to gas composition and temperature control.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** No specific activation pressure is provided in the protocol, implying that the reduction is performed under standard atmospheric conditions for catalyst synthesis.  
- **Observations:** The focused conditions on temperature and gas composition minimize any adverse effects that pressure variations might have induced.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas  
- **Definition:** The gas composition during activation is crucial for both eliminating oxygen from the oxide and supplying carbon for carbide formation.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** A syngas mixture with an exact ratio of 50% H₂ and 50% CO by volume is used.  
- **Observations:** The equimolar H₂/CO atmosphere creates the necessary dual environment—H₂ acts as the reducing agent while CO contributes to carburization—thereby ensuring an effective and balanced activation process.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Space-Time Velocity  
- **Definition:** Gas flow rate per unit catalyst weight determines the contact efficiency between the gas and catalyst surfaces.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** During activation, a controlled gas flow rate of 3000 mL/(g_cat·h) is maintained.  
- **Observations:** This high flow rate facilitates uniform gas distribution, contributing to an even reduction process.

#### 5.1.5 Activation Time  
- **Definition:** The duration of activation is critical to ensure complete conversion of Fe₂O₃ into active iron carbides without over-reduction or sintering.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalysts undergo activation for 10 hours.  
- **Observations:** The prolonged activation period guarantees thorough reduction, as confirmed by subsequent XRD analysis, which shows significant formation of iron carbides essential for high CO conversion and effective C–C coupling in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

Overall, these controlled conditions yield catalysts with preserved core–shell morphology and enhanced reactivity, contributing to superior Fischer–Tropsch performance.

### 5.2 Reaction

The Fischer–Tropsch reaction was performed under meticulously controlled conditions to ensure reproducibility and reliable performance data. The reaction conditions were chosen to optimize the reduction, carburization, and subsequent formation of active iron carbide phases that are critical to achieving high CO conversion and selective hydrocarbon formation.

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature
• Definition: The reaction temperature is a critical parameter that drives the reduction of iron oxides to active iron carbides while promoting CO activation and C–C coupling.  
• Experimental Conditions: The reaction was conducted at 280 °C.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase composition of the catalysts were determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD), providing insight into the retention of the hematite core and the formation of new phases upon promoter incorporation and activation.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns confirm that all samples—Fe₂O₃, Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95, and Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140—retain the hematite phase (JCPDS No. 33-0664, hexagonal, R-3C). The MnO₂ shell is amorphous, as no distinct MnO₂ peaks are observed. In Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140, a new peak at 12.5° (and two additional weak peaks) is attributed to K₀.₄₆Mn₂O₄ (JCPDS No. 42-1317, monoclinic, C2/m), indicating successful K⁺ intercalation at higher synthesis temperature. After activation (300 °C, 50% H₂/50% CO), XRD and TEM confirm the formation of iron carbides (e.g., Fe₅C₂).
- **Observations:** The emergence of K₀.₄₆Mn₂O₄ and iron carbides in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 demonstrates the structural evolution critical for enhanced Fischer–Tropsch activity.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition and Chemical State
- **Description:** Elemental composition and surface chemical states were analyzed using energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), inductively coupled plasma (ICP) analysis, and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS).
- **Findings:** ICP quantifies Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 as containing 54.2 wt% Fe, 12.5 wt% Mn, and 2.3 wt% K, while Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95 contains 56.6 wt% Fe, 11.8 wt% Mn, and only 0.1 wt% K. EDS mapping shows K localized in the shell of Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140. XPS reveals Fe 2p₃⁄₂ at 710.8 eV for Fe₂O₃, shifting by 0.3 eV in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95 and by 0.5 eV in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140, indicating increased electron density on Fe. The Mn 2p spectrum shows dominant Mn⁴⁺ (Mn 2p₃⁄₂ at 642.6 eV) with a Mn⁴⁺/Mn²⁺ ratio of 3.4. K 2p and K 2s signals are detected only in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140.
- **Observations:** The higher K content and electronic shifts in Fe and Mn confirm effective co-incorporation of Mn and K, which modulates the catalyst’s surface properties and enhances basicity.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen physisorption (BET) was used to assess specific surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution, which are critical for reactant accessibility and active site exposure.
- **Findings:** Fe₂O₃ exhibits a surface area of 25 m²/g, Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95 has 63 m²/g, and Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 reaches 91 m²/g. Pore volume and average pore size also increase with the addition and thinning of MnO₂ nanosheets at higher synthesis temperature. Both promoted samples display abundant mesopores, as indicated by hysteresis in the adsorption–desorption isotherms.
- **Observations:** The enhanced surface area and porosity in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 facilitate greater gas adsorption and exposure of internal Fe species, directly correlating with improved catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were employed to visualize catalyst morphology, nanosheet arrangement, and particle size.
- **Findings:** All samples maintain a uniform spindle-like Fe₂O₃ core. In Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95 and Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140, MnO₂ nanosheets are perpendicularly anchored onto the core, forming a shell. The nanosheets are thinner and more widely spaced in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140, resulting in a highly porous structure. Particle size is consistent across samples, as determined by direct measurement from SEM/TEM images.
- **Observations:** The formation of thinner, well-aligned MnO₂ nanosheets at higher synthesis temperature increases pore channels and maximizes active site exposure.

#### 6.1.5 Reducibility and Adsorption Properties
- **Description:** H₂ temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) and CO temperature-programmed desorption (CO-TPD) were used to evaluate reduction behavior and CO chemisorption capacity.
- **Findings:** Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 exhibits the lowest reduction temperatures, indicating facilitated reduction by Mn and K promoters. CO-TPD shows increasing CO chemisorption capacity: Fe₂O₃ (0.22 mmol CO/g Fe), Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95 (0.32 mmol CO/g Fe), and Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 (0.47 mmol CO/g Fe).
- **Observations:** Enhanced reducibility and higher CO adsorption in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 reflect increased active site density and superior activation properties, supporting its improved catalytic performance.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. It is calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Under reaction conditions of 280 °C, 2.0 MPa, and a 50% H₂/50% CO feed (GHSV: 3000 mL g⁻¹ h⁻¹), the CO conversion after 30 h was:
  - Fe₂O₃: 58.1%
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95: 78.2%
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140: 82.3%
- **Observations and Trends:** The incorporation of MnO₂ and especially K⁺ (as K₀.₄₆Mn₂O₄ in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140) significantly enhances CO conversion. Higher hydrothermal synthesis temperature (140 °C) leads to increased porosity and surface basicity, facilitating more efficient CO activation and conversion.

#### 6.2.2 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity represents the percentage of converted CO that yields methane, calculated as:
\[
\text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95: 8.99%
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140: 6.81%
- **Observations and Trends:** The lower CH₄ selectivity in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 indicates suppressed methane formation and improved C–C coupling, attributed to the enhanced basicity and optimized pore structure from K⁺ incorporation.

#### 6.2.3 C₅⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₅⁺ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with five or more carbon atoms, reflecting chain growth efficiency:
\[
\text{C}_5^+ \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{C}_5^+,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95: 63.54%
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140: 73.07%
- **Observations and Trends:** Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 demonstrates a marked increase in C₅⁺ selectivity, signifying enhanced chain growth and a favorable product distribution for liquid fuels. This improvement is directly linked to the synergistic effects of Mn and K promoters and the resulting catalyst morphology.

#### 6.2.4 Turnover Frequency (TOF)
- **Description:** TOF indicates the number of CO molecules converted per active iron site per second, serving as a measure of intrinsic catalytic activity.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe₂O₃: 6.58 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-95: 7.02 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
  - Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140: 9.43 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
- **Observations and Trends:** The highest TOF for Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 reflects the increased number and accessibility of active sites, resulting from the optimized core–shell structure and effective K⁺ intercalation.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over extended reaction times, with minimal decline indicating resistance to deactivation.
- **Experimental Data:** All catalysts maintained stable CO conversion over 30 h. Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140 exhibited sustained high conversion (82.3%) with negligible deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** The robust spindle morphology and stable iron carbide phases, especially in Fe₂O₃@MnO₂-140, underpin the observed long-term stability, attributed to the structural and compositional advantages conferred by the synthesis strategy.
