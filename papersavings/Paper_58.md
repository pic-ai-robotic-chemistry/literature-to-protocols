## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section describes the synthesis of Fe/CNT-based Fischer–Tropsch catalysts utilizing an incipient wetness impregnation method assisted by ultrasonic treatment and stirring. This approach ensures effective uptake of an aqueous solution containing Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O and promoter nitrates (Bi(NO₃)₃·5H₂O or Pb(NO₃)₂) into carbon nanotubes (CNTs). The nitric acid treatment of CNTs prior to impregnation serves to open the tube channels, remove contaminants, and render the surface hydrophilic, thereby enhancing precursor uptake via capillary forces.

### Step-by-step Experimental Procedures

#### 1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation
• Reagents: An aqueous solution prepared with Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O to achieve a target of 10 wt% iron loading; for promoted catalysts, Bi(NO₃)₃·5H₂O or Pb(NO₃)₂ is added to maintain a Fe/promoter molar ratio of 100/2.  
• Equipment: Typical laboratory glassware and an ultrasonic bath are employed.  
• Observations: Uniform mixing facilitated by ultrasonic treatment, ensuring enhanced penetration into the CNT channels.

#### 2. Incipient Wetness Impregnation (Precipitation–Deposition)
• Process: The prepared solution is introduced to both open (Fe/CNT-in) and closed (Fe/CNT-out) CNTs. For the confined catalysts, capillary forces during ultrasonic treatment draw the metal nitrates into the open CNT channels.  
• Note: The deposition is continuously monitored to avoid over-impregnation while ensuring homogeneous precursor distribution.

#### 3. Drying
• Conditions: The impregnated catalysts are dried in an oven at 80 °C for 12 hours.  
• Observations: This step removes the solvent gradually to prevent agglomeration, preserving the dispersion of metal precursors.

#### 4. Calcination
• Conditions: The dried catalysts undergo calcination at 400 °C for 4 hours under a nitrogen flow of 50 ml/min.  
• Outcome: Calcination promotes the conversion of metal nitrates to oxides, establishing the active phases required for subsequent reduction and carbidization steps.

This synthesis sequence, which integrates ultrasonic treatment and careful temperature control, ensures reproducibility and optimal active site dispersion, as evidenced by the enhanced catalytic performance reported in the article.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

This section details the procedures for reagent preparation for each Fe/CNT‐based Fischer–Tropsch catalyst. All catalysts are prepared by incipient wetness impregnation using an aqueous solution of iron and, when applicable, promoter nitrates. The procedures below are organized into three parts: Weighing, Liquid Transfer, and Mixing.

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

##### 3.1.1.1 Fe/CNT-in  
- **Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O**: Weighed in an amount calculated to yield a 10 wt% Fe loading on the CNT support.

##### 3.1.1.2 Fe/CNT-out  
- **Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O**: Weighed to achieve a 10 wt% Fe loading on the support with closed CNT channels.

##### 3.1.1.3 FeBi/CNT-in  
- **Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O**: Weighed for a 10 wt% Fe loading on open CNT channels.  
- **Bi(NO₃)₃·5H₂O**: Weighed to introduce the promoter in a Fe/promoter molar ratio of 100/2.

##### 3.1.1.4 FeBi/CNT-out  
- **Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O**: Weighed for a 10 wt% Fe loading on closed CNT channels.  
- **Bi(NO₃)₃·5H₂O**: Weighed to maintain the 100/2 molar ratio relative to iron.

##### 3.1.1.5 FePb/CNT-in  
- **Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O**: Weighed to provide 10 wt% Fe loading with the iron species confined inside open CNTs.  
- **Pb(NO₃)₂**: Weighed in an amount yielding a 100/2 Fe/Pb molar ratio.

##### 3.1.1.6 FePb/CNT-out  
- **Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O**: Weighed to achieve the 10 wt% Fe target on the support with closed CNT channels.  
- **Pb(NO₃)₂**: Weighed to comply with the 100/2 molar ratio requirement.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

For all catalyst variants, the aqueous nitrate solutions are prepared by dissolving the weighted reagents in solvent.
  
##### 3.1.2.X (Applicable to all catalysts)  
- **Aqueous nitrate solution**: Transferred onto the CNT support using incipient wetness impregnation. For confined catalysts (e.g., “-in”), the capillary forces draw the solution into the open CNT channels, while for non-confined catalysts (e.g., “-out”), the solution contacts the external surfaces. The transfer is performed under conditions that ensure reproducibility without the specification of additional equipment.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.X (Applicable to all catalysts)  
- **Nitrate-CNT mixture**: Mixed using a combination of stirring and ultrasonic treatment to ensure a homogeneous dispersion of the reagents. In confined systems, ultrasonic assistance is critical to promote the capillary uptake of the aqueous solution into the CNT interiors, while maintaining ambient temperature conditions to secure reproducibility.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Fe/CNT-based Fischer–Tropsch Catalysts
This procedure details the synthesis of Fe/CNT catalysts, including variants with promoter additions (Bi or Pb) and different CNT accessibility (Fe/CNT-in vs. Fe/CNT-out).

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: 
  - For the active phase, dissolve an amount of Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O to yield a fixed 10 wt% iron loading.
  - For promoted catalysts, add Bi(NO₃)₃·5H₂O or Pb(NO₃)₂ maintaining a Fe/promoter molar ratio of 100/2.
  - Use deionized water as the solvent.
- **Conditions**: 
  - Prepare an aqueous solution by dissolving the specified nitrates completely under vigorous stirring.
  - Ensure uniformity by applying ultrasonic treatment during the solution preparation.
- **Process**:
  - Dissolve the nitrates in a measured volume of water.
  - Stir until a homogeneous solution is achieved.
  - Use ultrasonic agitation briefly to ensure full dispersion of all reagents.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly
- **Reagents & Preparation**:
  - Prior to impregnation, treat 3.0 g of CNTs with concentrated HNO₃ (68%) at 140 °C under reflux for 14 hours to open channels and render them hydrophilic (for Fe/CNT-in catalysts). For Fe/CNT-out catalysts, employ CNTs with closed tubes.
- **Process**:
  - Impregnate the pre-treated CNTs with the aqueous metal solution using incipient wetness impregnation, where capillary forces draw the solution into the open CNT channels.
  - Enhance infiltration using combined stirring and ultrasonic treatment.
  
##### Step 3: Purification
- **Process**:
  - Wash the impregnated CNTs with deionized water to remove residual nitrates.
  - Optionally centrifuge or filter the suspension to aid in removal of unwanted byproducts.

##### Step 4: Drying & Calcination
- **Drying**: 
  - Dry the samples in an oven set to 80 °C for 12 hours to remove moisture.
- **Calcination**:
  - Calcine under a nitrogen flow (50 mL/min) at 400 °C for 4 hours. The ramp rate is controlled to avoid sintering and ensure the formation of active iron phases (including iron carbides).

##### Step 5: Activation
- **Process**:
  - Heat the calcined catalyst to 350 °C at a rate of 5 °C/min and maintain for 10 hours under a CO flow (50 mL/min) at atmospheric pressure. This step promotes the formation of the desired active sites for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

**Key Steps & Cautions**:  
• Monitor stirring and ultrasonic treatment durations to guarantee homogeneous impregnation.  
• Confirm that CNT pre-treatment effectively opens channels.  
• Maintain strict temperature and gas flow settings during drying, calcination, and activation to ensure reproducibility across catalyst variants.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 X‑ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Operational Description**: XRD was used to determine the crystalline phases of the catalysts, identifying CNT reflections and iron oxide/carbidic phases.
- **Instruments and Models**: PANalytical Empyrean X‑ray diffractometer.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Cu Kα radiation at 40 kV and 30 mA.
- **Test Parameters**: Step size of 0.02° with a 1 s step time; specific 2θ positions (e.g., 26.3°, 35.6°) served to distinguish CNT, hematite, and magnetite peaks.
- **Key Notes**: Attention was given to sample alignment to ensure accurate phase identification.

### 4.2 X‑ray Absorption Fine Structure (XAFS)
- **Operational Description**: XAFS assessed local structural and electronic properties around Fe atoms.
- **Instruments and Models**: Measurements carried out at Beamline XDS (LNLS) using Si (1 1 1) and Si (3 1 1) monochromators.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Electron beam energies in the 5–30 keV range.
- **Test Parameters**: Calibration with standard references ensured accurate edge-energy determination.
- **Key Notes**: Provided complementary data to XRD for iron speciation.

### 4.3 H₂ Temperature‑Programmed Reduction (H₂‑TPR)
- **Operational Description**: Evaluated the reducibility of iron oxide species through distinct reduction steps.
- **Instruments and Models**: AutoChem II 2920 apparatus.
- **Characterization Conditions**: 0.05 g sample, using a 5 vol% H₂/Ar flow at 30 mL/min.
- **Test Parameters**: Temperature ramp from room temperature to 900 °C at 10 °C/min.
- **Key Notes**: Confined catalysts showed reduction peaks shifted ~30 °C lower.

### 4.4 CO Temperature‑Programmed Reduction (CO‑TPR)
- **Operational Description**: Monitored the reduction behavior under CO atmosphere.
- **Instruments and Models**: Same reactor setup as H₂‑TPR.
- **Characterization Conditions**: 5 vol% CO in Ar at 30 mL/min.
- **Test Parameters**: Temperature ramp from room temperature to 900 °C at 10 °C/min.
- **Key Notes**: Provided additional insight into the carbidization steps.

### 4.5 Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)
- **Operational Description**: Determined particle size and morphology.
- **Instruments and Models**: Jeol 200 double corrected Cold FEG ARM operated at 200 kV.
- **Test Parameters**: Analysis of over 250 particles across multiple areas.
- **Key Notes**: High-resolution imaging enabled accurate size distribution assessment.

### 4.6 STEM‑HAADF & Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (EDS)
- **Operational Description**: Mapped elemental distributions (Fe and promoters) after appropriate cleaning and sample mounting on silicon wafers.
- **Key Notes**: Provided core–shell distribution insights via atomic number contrast.

### 4.7 Inductively Coupled Plasma‑OES (ICP‑OES)
- **Operational Description**: Quantitative elemental analysis using standard calibration.
- **Instruments and Models**: Agilent 720‑ES ICP‑OES with axial viewing and CCD detection.

### 4.8 X‑ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)
- **Operational Description**: Monitored surface chemical states and carbidization (Fe–C peak at 707.3 eV).
- **Instruments and Models**: LNLS SXS beamline with InSb (1 1 1) monochromator.
- **Test Parameters**: Fixed photon energy of 1840 eV, pass energy 23.5 eV, 0.1 eV energy steps.
- **Key Notes**: High resolution allowed differentiation of oxidation states.

### 4.9 BET Surface Area Analysis
- **Operational Description**: Determined specific surface area via nitrogen adsorption.
- **Instruments and Models**: Micromeritics Tristar Model 3020.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Sample degassed at 250 °C under vacuum for 2 h; isotherms measured at −196 °C.
- **Test Parameters**: Analysis conducted in the P/P₀ range of 0.05–0.3.

### 4.10 In‑situ X‑ray Absorption Near Edge Structure (XANES)
- **Operational Description**: Tracked real‐time changes in oxidation state under reactive conditions.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Temperature ramp from 30 to 350 °C in CO or syngas atmosphere.
- **Key Notes**: Enabled correlation of activation conditions with catalyst evolution.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature is critical to driving the reduction of iron oxides and promoting carburization into active iron carbide phases.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst is heated in a fixed-bed reactor at a controlled ramp rate of 5 °C/min until reaching 350 °C, where it is maintained.
- **Observations:** Operating at 350 °C under a CO atmosphere facilitates the transformation from Fe₂O₃/Fe₃O₄ to the catalytically active FexCy phase. Notably, when iron is confined within carbon nanotubes (CNT), the effective transformation occurs at a lower apparent temperature compared to non-confined catalysts.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** Activation pressure affects the kinetics of reduction and carbidization.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The activation process is conducted at atmospheric pressure (1 atm).
- **Observations:** Atmospheric pressure ensures a mild environment that minimizes the risk of over-reduction or sintering while allowing controlled formation of the active phase.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
- **Definition:** The choice of activation gas determines the extent and nature of the catalyst’s reduction and carburization.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Carbon monoxide (CO) is used as the sole activation gas with a flow rate of 50 mL/min.
- **Observations:** The CO flow not only drives the reduction of iron oxides but also promotes carburization, leading to the formation of robust iron carbide phases essential for Fischer–Tropsch activity.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Space-Time Velocity
- **Definition:** Space-time velocity controls catalyst exposure to the reactive gas flow.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Although a direct space-time velocity in mL/(g_cat·h) is not provided, the conditions include a CO flow of 50 mL/min through a reactor containing 0.1 g of catalyst.
- **Observations:** These parameters ensure a balanced gas–solid interaction, avoiding over-reduction, and support uniform activation across the catalyst bed.

#### 5.1.5 Activation Time
- **Definition:** The duration of activation is essential for complete catalyst transformation while minimizing sintering.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The activation step is maintained for a total of 10 hours.
- **Observations:** This extended dwell time under CO flow has been linked to enhanced carbidization and improved metal dispersion, notably in nanoconfined catalysts, leading to superior stability during extended Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature
- **Definition:** The reaction temperature is a critical parameter for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis, as it governs syngas activation, the reduction of iron oxides, and subsequent carbidization into the active iron carbide phase, which directly influences catalytic performance.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The reaction temperature was maintained at 350 °C during both activation and the subsequent catalytic tests.
- **Observations:** Under these conditions, while the Fe/CNT-out catalyst experienced gradual deactivation, the confined Fe/CNT-in catalyst remained stable for over 100 hours, confirming the importance of temperature in achieving optimal catalyst performance.

#### 5.2.2 Reaction Pressure
- **Definition:** Reaction pressure affects the concentration of syngas components in the reactor and the product distribution, especially favoring longer-chain hydrocarbon (C₅⁺) formation at higher pressures.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The tests were conducted in a fixed-bed reactor at pressures ranging from 1 bar to 20 bar. Pressure regulation was achieved using a back-pressure valve, ensuring precise control throughout the reaction.
- **Observations:** Increased pressure was correlated with higher CO conversion and enhanced formation of C₅⁺ hydrocarbons, aligning with the expectations for iron-based catalysts under Fischer–Tropsch conditions.

#### 5.2.3 Reaction H₂/CO Ratio
- **Definition:** The H₂/CO ratio in the feed gas plays a significant role in controlling the adsorption and subsequent conversion of syngas, thereby influencing the selectivity towards methane or light olefins.
- **Experimental Conditions:** A stoichiometric H₂/CO ratio of 1:1 was employed in all experiments.
- **Observations:** Maintaining this ratio was essential to balance the formation of light olefins while moderating methane selectivity, with additional promoter effects noted in altering the hydrocarbon chain lengths.

#### 5.2.4 Reaction Space-Time Velocity
- **Definition:** The gas hourly space velocity (GHSV) quantifies the volumetric flow rate of syngas relative to the catalyst mass, thus controlling the contact time between reactants and the catalyst.
- **Experimental Conditions:** GHSV values were reported in the range of 3.4 to 84 L·g⁻¹·h⁻¹.
- **Observations:** It was observed that higher space velocities, corresponding to reduced contact times, led to lower CO conversion rates, underscoring the necessity to optimize GHSV for balancing conversion efficiency and catalyst stability.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal phases and structural evolution of the catalysts were investigated using X-ray diffraction (XRD). This technique distinguishes between the carbon nanotube (CNT) support and various iron-containing phases by their characteristic diffraction peaks.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns revealed distinct reflections for CNTs at 2θ = 26.3° and 43.8°, corresponding to the (002) and (101) planes. Hematite (Fe₂O₃) was identified by a peak at 35.6° (JCPDS 13-0534), while magnetite (Fe₃O₄) was observed at 35.8°, 43.5°, and 53.9° (JCPDS 75-0449). After CO activation, a broad peak at ~44° indicated the formation of iron carbides (v-Fe₅C₂, ε-Fe₂.₂C), though overlapping and broadening precluded precise phase quantification.
- **Observations:** The emergence of iron carbide phases is more pronounced in confined and promoted catalysts, correlating with enhanced Fischer–Tropsch activity.

#### 6.1.2 Reducibility and Carbidization Behavior
- **Description:** Temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR, CO-TPR) was employed to assess the reducibility and carbidization kinetics of the catalysts, which are critical for generating active iron carbide phases.
- **Findings:** Three main reduction steps were observed: Fe₂O₃ → Fe₃O₄ (250–420 °C), Fe₃O₄ → FeO, and FeO → Fe⁰ (600–700 °C). Confined catalysts (Fe/CNT-in) exhibited all reduction and carbidization transitions at lower temperatures compared to non-confined (Fe/CNT-out) samples. The addition of Bi or Pb promoters further decreased reduction temperatures by 30–44 °C.
- **Observations:** Nanoconfinement and promotion synergistically facilitate iron reduction and carbidization, leading to more efficient formation of the active phase.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and scanning transmission electron microscopy with high-angle annular dark field (STEM-HAADF) imaging, along with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), were used to analyze particle size, dispersion, and promoter distribution.
- **Findings:** Iron oxide nanoparticles confined within CNTs (Fe/CNT-in) displayed a narrow size distribution with an average diameter of ~5 nm, while those on the exterior (Fe/CNT-out) averaged ~9 nm. STEM-HAADF and EDS mapping confirmed that Bi and Pb promoters form a shell around the iron-rich core after activation, resulting in a core–shell structure with an average size of ~8 nm.
- **Observations:** The confined environment restricts particle growth, ensuring high dispersion and intimate Fe–promoter contact, which is preserved after catalytic testing.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms (BET method) were used to determine surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution.
- **Findings:** Iron impregnation reduced the pore volume of closed CNTs from 0.54 cm³/g to 0.52 cm³/g, and for open CNTs (Fe/CNT-in), from 0.83 cm³/g to 0.50 cm³/g. The decrease in surface area and pore volume reflects the successful incorporation of iron species within the CNTs.
- **Observations:** The preserved mesoporosity and high dispersion are conducive to efficient reactant diffusion and catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.5 Elemental Composition and Surface Chemistry
- **Description:** Inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and in-situ X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) were used to quantify elemental composition and monitor surface chemical states.
- **Findings:** ICP-OES confirmed iron loading at ~10 wt.% and promoter content (Bi or Pb) at ~0.8 wt.%. XPS spectra showed a Fe–C peak at 707.3 eV after CO activation, indicative of iron carbide formation, with higher intensity in confined and promoted catalysts. In-situ XANES tracked the reduction of Fe₂O₃ to Fe₃O₄ and subsequent carbidization to FexCy, with more complete and lower-temperature transitions in confined, promoted samples.
- **Observations:** The efficient formation and stabilization of iron carbide phases, along with the core–shell promoter arrangement, underpin the superior catalytic activity and selectivity observed in these catalysts.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. It is calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the inlet and outlet CO flow rates, respectively, determined using N₂ as an internal standard.
- **Experimental Data:** Under 350 °C, 10 bar, and GHSV = 10.2–30.6 L·h⁻¹·g⁻¹, Fe/CNT-out exhibits a CO conversion of 14.4%, while Fe/CNT-in achieves 24.8%. Bi and Pb promotion further enhance conversion: FeBi/CNT-out and FePb/CNT-out reach 28.9% and 34.3%, respectively; FeBi/CNT-in and FePb/CNT-in attain 60–70%. At 20 bar, FePb/CNT-in achieves 76.2% conversion.
- **Observations and Trends:** Nanoconfinement and promoter addition synergistically increase CO conversion, with the highest values observed for confined, promoted catalysts.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, calculated as:
\[
\text{CO₂ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO₂,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** At 1 bar, CO₂ selectivity is 20.2% for Fe/CNT-out, increasing to 25.8% (FeBi/CNT-out), 30.5% (FePb/CNT-out), 29.2% (Fe/CNT-in), 37.3% (FeBi/CNT-in), 39.9% (FePb/CNT-in), and 42.4% (FePbK/CNT-in).
- **Observations and Trends:** Both nanoconfinement and promotion elevate CO₂ selectivity, with the highest values for confined, promoted catalysts.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
\[
\text{CH₄ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH₄,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** At 1 bar, CH₄ selectivity is 37.3% (Fe/CNT-out), 31.5% (FeBi/CNT-out), 30.9% (FePb/CNT-out), 31.9% (Fe/CNT-in), 27.0% (FeBi/CNT-in), 26.1% (FePb/CNT-in), and 19.2% (FePbK/CNT-in).
- **Observations and Trends:** Both confinement and promotion reduce CH₄ selectivity, with the lowest values for FePbK/CNT-in.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity denotes the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with three or more carbons:
\[
\text{C₃⁺ Selectivity} = 100 - \frac{F_{\text{CO₂,out}} + F_{\text{CH₄,out}} + 2F_{\text{C₂H₂,out}} + 2F_{\text{C₂H₄,out}} + 2F_{\text{C₂H₆,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The article reports that C₂–C₄ olefin selectivity is maximized (up to 58%) at an α value of 0.46 for conventional FT, with confined and promoted catalysts shifting the distribution toward higher light olefin and C₃⁺ fractions.
- **Observations and Trends:** Nanoconfinement and promotion favor light olefin and C₃⁺ hydrocarbon formation, deviating from the typical Anderson–Schulz–Flory distribution.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor represents the probability of chain growth versus termination:
\[
\alpha = \frac{\text{Moles of C added to chain}}{\text{Moles of C added to chain} + \text{Moles of chain termination}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** For conventional FT, α = 0.46. Confined and promoted catalysts exhibit lower α values, correlating with increased light olefin selectivity and reduced C₅⁺ formation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Lower α values in confined, promoted catalysts enhance light olefin selectivity.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** The deactivation rate quantifies the decline in CO conversion over time:
\[
\text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Confined and promoted catalysts display negligible deactivation over 100 h, maintaining stable CO conversion. Non-confined catalysts show gradual deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Nanoconfinement and promotion effectively suppress deactivation, attributed to stable nanoparticle size and resistance to sintering.
