## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the cosequential incipient wetness impregnation technique used for synthesizing the RLMS Series catalysts (Rh–La/M/SiO₂, where M = W, V, Ce, or Zr). The method emphasizes precise precursor addition, controlled drying, and calcination steps to ensure reproducibility and uniform dispersion of Rh, La, and the third metal.

#### • Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
1. Begin by dispersing the SiO₂ support in 50 ml of deionized water contained in a beaker.  
2. Add the required third metal precursor (ensuring 1.8 wt% loading) directly to the dispersion.  
   - Equipment: Beaker, magnetic stirrer.  
   - Observation: Uniform distribution is indicated by a thorough mixing without visible clumps.

#### • Precipitation–Deposition  
1. Immediately subject the mixture to initial drying at 70°C with continuous stirring until the water completely evaporates.  
   - This step serves as an in situ deposition of the metal precursor on the support.  
   - Observation: Complete water removal is noted by a gradual change to a dry, powdery texture.

#### • Drying and Impregnation  
1. Transfer the pre-treated support for oven drying at 120°C for 12 hours to eradicate any residual moisture.  
2. Impregnate the dried M/SiO₂ with an aqueous solution containing Rh (1.5 wt%) and La (2.5 wt%), maintaining an atomic Rh/La ratio of 0.8.
   - Equipment: Oven with programmable timer.
   - Observation: Homogeneous wet impregnation is confirmed by even coloration and paste consistency.

#### • Secondary Drying, Grinding, and Calcination  
1. Subject the impregnated support to a second drying at 120°C for 12 hours.  
2. Grind the oven-dried material into a homogeneous powder.  
3. Calcine the sample in a tubular oven at 450°C for 4 hours under continuous air flow to develop the active phase, transforming the initial Rh³⁺ (as Rh₂O₃) into its catalytically active forms.
   - Equipment: Tubular oven with controlled airflow.
   - Observation: Formation of active sites is confirmed by changes in physical appearance and can later be characterized using XPS and BET techniques.

### Comparative Analysis  
This incipient wetness impregnation method offers advantages in synthesis complexity, scalability, and cost-effectiveness over co‑precipitation techniques. The precise control over drying temperatures and durations enhances uniform precursor distribution, thereby ensuring consistent catalyst performance.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

This section details the preparation of RLMS series catalysts – Rh–La/M/SiO₂ with M = W, V, Ce, and Zr – emphasizing catalyst‑specific groups for the weighing of solid reagents, liquid transfer, and mixing to ensure full reproducibility.

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

##### 3.1.1.1 RLWS  
- **Rhodium nitrate (Rh(H₂O)(OH)₃–₍y₎(NO₃)₍y₎, y = 2–3, ~36%)**: Weighed corresponding to a Rh loading of 1.5 wt% relative to SiO₂ using an analytical balance.  
- **Lanthanum nitrate (La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O)**: Weighed to achieve a La loading of 2.5 wt%.  
- **Ammonium paratungstate hydrate (H₄[10]O₄₂W₁₂·xH₂O)**: Weighed for an intended 1.8 wt% tungsten content.

##### 3.1.1.2 RLVS  
- **Rhodium nitrate (Rh(H₂O)(OH)₃–₍y₎(NO₃)₍y₎, y = 2–3, ~36%)**: Weighed corresponding to 1.5 wt% Rh.  
- **Lanthanum nitrate (La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O)**: Weighed for a 2.5 wt% La target.  
- **Ammonium metavanadate (H₄NO₃V)**: Weighed to provide 1.8 wt% vanadium.

##### 3.1.1.3 RLCS  
- **Rhodium nitrate (Rh(H₂O)(OH)₃–₍y₎(NO₃)₍y₎, y = 2–3, ~36%)**: Weighed for a 1.5 wt% Rh loading.  
- **Lanthanum nitrate (La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O)**: Weighed achieving a 2.5 wt% La loading.  
- **Cerium nitrate hexahydrate (Ce(NO₃)₃·6H₂O)**: Weighed corresponding to 1.8 wt% Ce.

##### 3.1.1.4 RLZS  
- **Rhodium nitrate (Rh(H₂O)(OH)₃–₍y₎(NO₃)₍y₎, y = 2–3, ~36%)**: Weighed to yield 1.5 wt% Rh.  
- **Lanthanum nitrate (La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O)**: Weighed for a 2.5 wt% loading.  
- **Zirconyl nitrate hydrate (ZrO(NO₃)₂·xH₂O)**: Weighed corresponding to 1.8 wt% Zr.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

##### 3.1.2.1 RLWS  
- **Deionized water**: Poured 50 mL into a beaker containing the SiO₂ dispersion with the pre‐weighed ammonium paratungstate hydrate.

##### 3.1.2.2 RLVS  
- **Deionized water**: Poured 50 mL into a beaker containing the SiO₂ dispersion with ammonium metavanadate.

##### 3.1.2.3 RLCS  
- **Deionized water**: Poured 50 mL into a beaker containing the SiO₂ dispersion with cerium nitrate hexahydrate.

##### 3.1.2.4 RLZS  
- **Deionized water**: Poured 50 mL into a beaker containing the SiO₂ dispersion with zirconyl nitrate hydrate.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.1 RLWS  
- **Third metal precursor/SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed continuously under constant stirring at 70°C until complete water evaporation was achieved.  
- **Rh–La impregnation solution**: Mixed uniformly with the pre-treated support to promote homogeneous distribution, followed by an oven drying at 120°C for 12 hours.

##### 3.1.3.2 RLVS  
- **Third metal precursor/SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed continuously at 70°C until full evaporation of water.  
- **Rh–La impregnation solution**: Mixed thoroughly with the pre-dried support, followed by drying at 120°C for 12 hours.

##### 3.1.3.3 RLCS  
- **Third metal precursor/SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed at 70°C with constant stirring until the water evaporated completely.  
- **Rh–La impregnation solution**: Mixed uniformly with the support and subsequently dried at 120°C for 12 hours.

##### 3.1.3.4 RLZS  
- **Third metal precursor/SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed continuously at 70°C until complete evaporation of water was observed.  
- **Rh–La impregnation solution**: Mixed evenly with the support, with a subsequent drying step at 120°C for 12 hours.

These detailed steps ensure reproducibility by specifying precise chemical identities, reagent amounts, transfer volumes, and mixing conditions critical for synthesizing the multimetallic catalysts for oxygenate synthesis from syngas.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: RLMS Series – Rh–La/M/SiO₂

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: The raw chemicals include Rhodium nitrate (Rh(H₂O)(OH)₃–y(NO₃)y, ~36%), Lanthanum nitrate hexahydrate (La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O), and the third metal precursors – ammonium paratungstate hydrate (for W), ammonium metavanadate (for V), Cerium nitrate hexahydrate (for Ce), or Zirconyl nitrate hydrate (for Zr). The loadings are adjusted to 1.5 wt% Rh, 2.5 wt% La, and 1.8 wt% M relative to SiO₂.  
- **Conditions**: Quantities are based on the support weight; the atomic Rh/La ratio of 0.8 is maintained by careful reagent measurement.

- **Process**:
  - Add the required third metal precursor to a beaker containing SiO₂ dispersed in 50 mL deionized water.
  - Stir continuously while heating at 70°C until complete evaporation of water is achieved.
  
- **Key Steps**: Verify complete drying at 70°C prior to further treatment.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly
- **Reagents**: Following the initial drying, prepare a mixed aqueous solution of Rh and La precursors according to the preset loadings.
- **Conditions**: Impregnation is performed at ambient conditions with constant stirring.
- **Process**:
  - Impregnate the oven-dried M/SiO₂ with the mixed solution.
  - Ensure homogeneous mixing to achieve even distribution of Rh and La.
  
- **Key Steps**: Uniform dispersion is critical for subsequent activity.

##### Step 3: Purification
- **Process**:
  - No additional washing or filtration is stipulated; the focus is on direct drying and calcination steps.

##### Step 4: Drying & Calcination
- **Drying**: Oven-dry the impregnated support at 120°C for 12 hours to remove excess moisture.
- **Calcination**:
  - Grind the dried material to obtain a homogeneous powder.
  - Calcine in a tubular oven at 450°C for 4 hours under continuous air flow at 150 mL/min.
  
- **Key Steps**: Ensure even temperature distribution and stable air flow during calcination for optimal catalyst formation.

##### Step 5: Activation
- **Process**: Activate the catalyst via in-situ reduction under H₂ at atmospheric pressure, typically at 400°C for 1 hour with a hydrogen flow rate of 50 mL/min.
  
- **Key Steps**: Although complete reduction of Rh³⁺ is not achieved, the partial reduction yielding an active mixture of Rh⁺ and Rh⁰ is maintained for optimal oxygenate selectivity.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 BET Surface Area Analysis
- **Operational Description**: This method evaluates the catalyst’s surface area and porosity, thereby confirming its mesoporous structure. Measurements are taken based on N₂ adsorption isotherms.
- **Instruments and Models**: Micromeritics ASAP 2010.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Samples are pretreated by oven drying at 120 °C for 12 hours and degassed at 300 °C under vacuum. Measurements show type‑IV isotherms typical of mesoporous materials.
- **Test Parameters**: Surface areas are measured in the range of 7.0–10.5 m²/g, with pore diameters between 10.1 and 18.9 nm and pore volumes ranging from 0.023 to 0.033 cm³/g.
- **Key Notes**: Rigorous sample pretreatment (including drying and outgassing) is critical for reliable BET data. The low surface area values support the presence of a mesoporous structure on the catalyst support.

### 4.2 H₂-Temperature Programmed Reduction (H₂-TPR)
- **Operational Description**: H₂-TPR is employed to investigate the catalyst’s reducibility by tracking the reduction of rhodium oxide and the formation of LaRhO₃ through its temperature response.
- **Instruments and Models**: Micromeritics AutoChem II 2910 automated catalyst characterization system.
- **Characterization Conditions**: The experiment begins with sample pretreatment at 50 °C in flowing helium to remove adsorbed oxygen. It is then ramped at 5 °C/min up to a maximum temperature of 800 °C, under a reactive gas mixture.
- **Test Parameters**: A mixture of 10 vol% H₂ in helium is used at a flow rate of 10 mL/min (STP). A sharp reduction peak appears at 106–130 °C (indicating reduction of rhodium oxide), followed by a broader peak between 200–450 °C (attributed to LaRhO₃ formation).
- **Key Notes**: The ramping rate ensures high resolution of closely spaced reduction events, enhancing the differentiation between various reduction processes. Consistent gas flow and precise temperature control are key for reproducibility.

### 4.3 X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)
- **Operational Description**: XPS is utilized to determine the oxidation states of Rh in the catalysts, distinguishing between fresh and spent samples.
- **Instruments and Models**: Pyris VG Thermo Scientific spectrometer equipped with Al Kα radiation (1486.6 eV).
- **Characterization Conditions**: Measurements are conducted under high vacuum conditions, focusing on the Rh 3d sublevel.
- **Test Parameters**: Fresh catalysts display a Rh 3d₅/₂ peak at 309.7–309.9 eV (indicative of Rh₂O₃), while spent catalysts exhibit split peaks at 307.6–307.9 eV (Rh⁰) and 308.7–309.0 eV (Rh⁺).
- **Key Notes**: The clear separation of Rh oxidation states by peak splitting offers insights into reduction behavior during catalysis. Proper sample handling and sufficient outgassing are mandatory to avoid surface contamination and ensure accurate analysis.



## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The catalyst activation process is a distinct pre‐reaction treatment step designed to convert the Rh precursor into the active phase required for efficient syngas conversion in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. This process is clearly delineated from other thermal treatments used in characterization.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature plays a critical role in reducing the Rh-based precursor (initially present as Rh₂O₃) to a mixture of Rh⁺ and Rh⁰, which are essential for subsequent oxygenate production.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst is activated at 400 °C using a tubular furnace.
- **Observations:** Operating at 400 °C ensures an effective reduction without triggering unwanted sintering or over-reduction, thus maintaining a favorable particle size distribution as implied by subsequent H₂-TPR profiles.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** The activation pressure influences the reduction kinetics by controlling the gas–solid interactions during the activation process.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst activation is conducted under atmospheric pressure.
- **Observations:** Using atmospheric pressure facilitates a controlled hydrogen reduction and avoids complications that might arise from elevated pressures, ensuring uniform activation across the catalyst bed.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
- **Definition:** The choice and composition of the activation gas directly affect the reduction pathway of Rh₂O₃ to its active forms by serving as the reducing agent.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Pure hydrogen is used as the activation gas, delivered at a flow rate of 50 mL/min.
- **Observations:** The use of a hydrogen-rich atmosphere is critical in achieving a partial reduction that retains a mixture of Rh⁺ and Rh⁰, a balance that is linked to enhanced oxygenate selectivity during Fischer–Tropsch reactions.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time
- **Definition:** The duration of activation is crucial to allow complete, yet controlled, reduction of the catalyst precursor while mitigating risks of sintering.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The activation step is maintained for 1 hour.
- **Observations:** A 1-hour reduction time is sufficient for achieving the desired catalyst state, ensuring reproducibility and stability during long-term syngas conversion tests.

This activation procedure, distinct from characterization protocols (e.g., TPR treatments), is seamlessly integrated into the catalyst synthesis workflow, providing a replicable pre‐reaction treatment for ensuring optimal catalytic performance in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis experiments.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature  
The reaction temperature is crucial for defining the kinetic regime of Fischer–Tropsch synthesis and directly influences both syngas conversion and product selectivity. In the study, the reaction temperatures investigated were 260 °C, 280 °C, 300 °C, and 320 °C. At 260 °C, the catalyst, particularly the Rh–La/V/SiO₂ (RLVS) variant, demonstrated excellent oxygenate selectivity (up to 76.5%) while suppressing hydrocarbon formation and maintaining high stability over extended operation. A slight increase to 280 °C resulted in a modest decline in oxygenate selectivity with a concurrent enhancement in hydrocarbon production. These temperature variations highlight the trade-off between CO conversion efficiency and oxygenate selectivity, where lower temperatures favor oxygenate production.

#### 5.2.2 Reaction Pressure  
The Fischer–Tropsch tests were performed at a reaction pressure of 350 psig. This elevated pressure is essential for ensuring an optimal syngas composition, enhancing reactant solubility, and promoting effective interactions between the catalyst and the reactants. The constant pressure maintained the system’s equilibrium, thus contributing to consistent CO conversion rates and aiding in the durability of the catalyst during prolonged reactions.

#### 5.2.3 Reaction H₂/CO Ratio  
The H₂/CO molar ratio, defined as the ratio of hydrogen to carbon monoxide in the feed gas, was set at 2:1. This ratio is pivotal in directing the reaction pathway toward oxygenate production rather than hydrocarbon formation. A 2:1 ratio balances the availability of hydrogen for the CO insertion step without excessively favoring methane or other undesired hydrocarbon byproducts.

#### 5.2.4 Reaction Space-Time Velocity  
A space-time velocity of 6,000 sccm/g_cat was applied to optimize the exposure time of the reactants over the catalyst surface. This parameter was critical in achieving the desired CO conversion rates while preserving high oxygenate selectivity. Although higher space velocities can reduce residence time and CO conversion, the selected value effectively maintained catalyst stability and reproducibility over long-term testing.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Surface Area, Porosity, and Pore Structure
- **Description:** The textural properties of the RLMS catalysts (Rh–La/M/SiO₂, where M = W, V, Ce, Zr) were evaluated using N₂ adsorption–desorption isotherms (BET method) on a Micromeritics ASAP 2010 instrument. These parameters are critical for understanding reactant accessibility and diffusion within the catalyst.
- **Findings:** The RLMS catalysts exhibit low BET surface areas: RLWS – 7.0 m²/g, RLVS – 10.3 m²/g, RLCS – 10.5 m²/g, and RLZS – 9.3 m²/g. The average pore diameters are RLWS – 18.9 nm, RLVS – 12.4 nm, RLCS – 11.0 nm, and RLZS – 10.1 nm, with corresponding pore volumes of RLWS – 0.032 cm³/g, RLVS – 0.033 cm³/g, RLCS – 0.029 cm³/g, and RLZS – 0.023 cm³/g. All samples display type‑IV isotherms with a small hysteresis loop, characteristic of mesoporous materials.
- **Observations:** Despite the relatively low surface areas, the mesoporous structure (pore diameters 10.1–18.9 nm) ensures interconnected porosity, which is beneficial for reactant diffusion and catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.2 Reducibility and Metal–Promoter Interactions (H₂-TPR)
- **Description:** Hydrogen temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) was performed on a Micromeritics AutoChem II 2910 to assess the reducibility of the catalysts and the interaction between Rh and promoters. Reducibility influences the formation of active sites and catalytic behavior.
- **Findings:** All RLMS catalysts show a sharp reduction peak at 106–130°C, attributed to the reduction of Rh₂O₃ to metallic Rh. A secondary broad reduction peak between 200–450°C is assigned to the reduction of LaRhO₃. The shift of the primary reduction peak to higher temperatures (compared to un-promoted Rh/SiO₂) indicates strong interactions between Rh and the co-promoters.
- **Observations:** The narrow temperature range of the sharp peak suggests a uniform Rh particle size distribution. The presence of LaRhO₃ and the retarded reduction of Rh highlight the synergistic effect of La and the secondary metal, which stabilizes Rh in partially reduced states.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Chemical States (XPS)
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to determine the oxidation states of Rh in both fresh and spent catalysts, providing insight into the electronic environment and active species under reaction conditions.
- **Findings:** In fresh catalysts, the Rh 3d₅/₂ peak appears at 309.7–309.9 eV, consistent with Rh₂O₃ (Rh³⁺). After reaction, the Rh 3d₅/₂ peak splits into two components: 307.6–307.9 eV (Rh⁰) and 308.7–309.0 eV (Rh⁺), with Rh⁺ being the dominant surface species. The persistence of Rh⁺ is attributed to strong interactions with La and the secondary metal dopant.
- **Observations:** The partial reduction of Rh³⁺ to a mixture of Rh⁺ and Rh⁰, with Rh⁺ prevailing, is significant for oxygenate synthesis. The stabilization of Rh⁺ by La and the secondary promoter is linked to enhanced selectivity and suppression of undesired hydrocarbon formation.

### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction. It is calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions (T = 260°C, P = 350 psig, H₂/CO = 2, space velocity = 6,000 mL g_cat⁻¹ h⁻¹), the RLVS catalyst achieves a CO conversion of approximately 9.7%. At 280°C, CO conversion increases for all catalysts, with RLWS and RLZS reaching up to 98% at higher temperatures (>300°C).
- **Observations and Trends:** CO conversion rises with increasing temperature and decreases with higher space velocity. The RLVS catalyst maintains stable CO conversion (~9.7%) over 100 hours, demonstrating excellent activity and stability under prolonged operation.

#### 6.2.2 Oxygenate Selectivity
- **Description:** Oxygenate selectivity is reported as atomic carbon selectivity, representing the fraction of converted carbon forming oxygenated products (MeOH, EtOH, C₂+ oxygenates, excluding CO₂). It is defined by:
\[
\text{Carbon Efficiency} = \frac{n_i C_i}{\sum n_i C_i}
\]
where \( n_i \) is the number of carbon atoms in product \( i \), and \( C_i \) is its mole amount.
- **Experimental Data:** The RLVS catalyst exhibits the highest oxygenate selectivity at 260°C (76.5%). At 280°C, RLWS, RLCS, and RLZS surpass RLVS, with selectivities of 61.4%, 46.9%, and 51.8%, respectively, while RLVS drops to 34.8%.
- **Observations and Trends:** Lower temperatures favor oxygenate formation, while higher temperatures shift selectivity toward hydrocarbons. The RLVS catalyst maintains oxygenate selectivity (~76.2%) over 100 hours, confirming its stability.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ and Hydrocarbon Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the proportion of converted carbon forming methane, a key indicator of hydrocarbon byproduct formation. Hydrocarbon selectivity includes all hydrocarbon products.
\[
\text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\% C)} = \frac{n_{\text{C,CH}_4} C_{\text{CH}_4}}{\sum n_i C_i} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** For RLVS at 260°C, CH₄ selectivity is 20.7%. Hydrocarbon selectivity increases with temperature, particularly for RLVS, which shows a marked rise as oxygenate selectivity declines.
- **Observations and Trends:** The addition of La and a secondary metal (W, V, Ce, Zr) effectively suppresses methane and hydrocarbon formation at lower temperatures, with RLVS showing the lowest hydrocarbon selectivity at 260°C.

#### 6.2.4 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the extent of CO oxidation to CO₂, calculated as:
\[
\text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\% C)} = \frac{n_{\text{C,CO}_2} C_{\text{CO}_2}}{\sum n_i C_i} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** CO₂ selectivity is negligible (0.0%) for RLVS at 260°C and remains low for all catalysts under optimized conditions.
- **Observations and Trends:** Low CO₂ selectivity indicates efficient suppression of the water-gas shift reaction, favoring oxygenate production.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion and oxygenate selectivity over extended operation.
- **Experimental Data:** The RLVS catalyst maintains both CO conversion (~9.7%) and oxygenate selectivity (~76.2%) over a 100-hour time-on-stream test at 260°C.
- **Observations and Trends:** The negligible change in performance metrics over time demonstrates outstanding catalyst stability and resistance to deactivation.

**Summary:**  
The RLMS series catalysts, particularly RLVS, exhibit high CO conversion and exceptional oxygenate selectivity at lower temperatures, with suppressed hydrocarbon and CO₂ formation. The combination of La and a secondary metal promoter is critical for stabilizing Rh⁺ active sites, enhancing both activity and selectivity. Catalyst performance remains stable over prolonged operation, underscoring the robustness of the multimetallic design for syngas-to-oxygenate conversion.
