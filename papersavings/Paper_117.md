## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The synthesis of the SiO₂‐supported Cu–Fe catalysts was achieved using two impregnation techniques: co‑impregnation and sequential impregnation. Both methods ensure reproducibility through defined drying, calcination, and post‑treatment conditions. A comparative analysis of these techniques indicates that while the co‑impregnation method promotes homogeneous metal distribution through simultaneous mixing, the sequential approach allows fine‑tuning of surface metal dispersions by controlling the impregnation order.

#### • Co‑impregnation Method  
1. Precursor Mixing:  
 • SiO₂ is added to an aqueous solution containing copper nitrate [Cu(NO₃)₂] and iron nitrate [Fe(NO₃)₃], each maintaining a 10% molar fraction relative to SiO₂.  
 • The mixture is prepared at ambient conditions, ensuring even dispersion of both metal ions on the SiO₂ support.  

2. Drying:  
 • The impregnated mixture is dried at 110 °C overnight.  

3. Calcination:  
 • The dried sample is calcined in static air at 350 °C for 4 h to convert the metal nitrates to their respective oxides.  

4. Post‑treatment:  
 • A reduction step is carried out at 300 °C for 4 h using a H₂/N₂ mixture (H₂:N₂ = 1:9) to form the active metallic sites.  

#### • Sequential Impregnation Methods  
In sequential procedures, the order of metal addition is adjusted to tailor the catalyst surface composition:

1. First Impregnation:  
 • One metal precursor (either Cu(NO₃)₂ or Fe(NO₃)₃) is introduced to the pretreated silica support.  
 • The sample is dried at 110 °C overnight and calcined at 350 °C for 4 h.

2. Second Impregnation:  
 • The second metal precursor is then impregnated onto the partially modified support.  
 • The sequence is varied: Cu first then Fe for the Fe/Cu/SiO₂ catalyst, and Fe first then Cu for the Cu/Fe/SiO₂ catalyst.  

3. Post‑treatment:  
 • Following the second drying and calcination (using the same conditions), the catalyst is reduced at 300 °C for 4 h under a H₂/N₂ atmosphere.

In both methods, basic equipment—such as a solution container for mixing, an ambient evaporation setup, an oven for drying, and a furnace for calcination—ensures reproducibility and scalability, while the controlled conditions maintain precise metal loading and dispersion for optimal catalytic performance.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Cu–Fe/SiO₂ (Co-impregnation)
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: Aqueous solutions of copper nitrate (Cu(NO₃)₂) and iron nitrate (Fe(NO₃)₃) are used, each targeting a 10% molar fraction relative to SiO₂, with pretreated commercial silica gel as the support.
- **Conditions**: Both nitrates are dissolved in deionized water under ambient conditions with gentle stirring to ensure homogeneity.
- **Process**:
  - Dissolve the calculated amounts of Cu(NO₃)₂ and Fe(NO₃)₃ in deionized water.
  - Introduce the pretreated SiO₂ into the mixed solution.
  - Stir the dispersion steadily to secure uniform adsorption.
- **Key Steps**: Ensuring proper mixing at this stage is critical for optimal metal dispersion.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly, Drying & Calcination
- **Process**:
  - Evaporate the solvent slowly at ambient atmosphere.
  - Dry the impregnated support at 110 °C overnight.
  - Calcine in static air at 350 °C for 4 hours with a controlled heating rate (e.g., 3 °C/min).

##### Step 3: Activation
- **Process**:
  - Reduce the calcined catalyst at 300 °C for 4 hours under a H₂/N₂ gas mixture (1:9 ratio, flow rate ≈50 mL/min).

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [2]: Fe/Cu/SiO₂ (Sequential Impregnation – Fe First)
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: Begin with an aqueous Fe(NO₃)₃ solution for impregnation onto SiO₂, followed by Cu(NO₃)₂.
- **Process**:
  - Impregnate the SiO₂ with Fe(NO₃)₃.
  - Dry at 110 °C overnight and calcine at 350 °C (4 hours).
  - Subsequently, re-impregnate with Cu(NO₃)₂ following similar mixing procedures.
  
##### Repeat Drying, Calcination, and Activation as described above.

#### Catalyst Preparation [3]: Cu/Fe/SiO₂ (Sequential Impregnation – Cu First)

- **Reagents**: Start with Cu(NO₃)₂ solution followed by Fe(NO₃)₃.
- **Process**:
  - Impregnate the SiO₂ support with Cu(NO₃)₂.
  - Dry and calcine similarly.
  - Follow with Fe(NO₃)₃ impregnation to adjust the metal ratios.

##### Process Details:
- Ensure each impregnation is performed with rigorous stirring and controlled pH if necessary, though pH adjustment is not explicitly detailed.
- Maintain consistent drying and calcination conditions to guarantee reproducibility.

**Key Notes**:  
- The order of impregnation significantly affects metal dispersion, as confirmed by surface composition analyses (XPS and H₂-TPR).

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal phases present in the catalysts were identified by X-ray diffraction (XRD), which is essential for determining the active species and their transformations during catalyst preparation and activation.
- **Findings:** XRD analysis of the calcined catalysts revealed distinct peaks corresponding to crystalline CuO and α-Fe₂O₃, with characteristic 2θ values at 35.6°, 38.8°, 48.7°, and 33.4°. Upon reduction, these peaks disappeared, and new peaks corresponding to metallic Cu and Fe₃O₄ emerged, indicating complete reduction of CuO to Cu and α-Fe₂O₃ to Fe₃O₄. The average crystallite sizes of these phases were calculated using the Scherrer equation, with the order of CuO crystallite size being Cu-Fe/SiO₂ < Fe/Cu/SiO₂ < Cu/Fe/SiO₂, and Fe₂O₃ size decreasing in the order Fe/Cu/SiO₂ < Cu-Fe/SiO₂ < Cu/Fe/SiO₂.
- **Observations:** The transformation from oxide to metallic and mixed oxide phases upon reduction is critical for catalytic activity. The impregnation sequence influences crystallite size, which can affect dispersion and reactivity.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Textural properties, including surface area, pore volume, and average pore diameter, were determined by N₂ adsorption–desorption isotherms (BET analysis), providing insight into catalyst accessibility and potential for reactant diffusion.
- **Findings:** The BET surface areas (SBET) were: Cu-Fe/SiO₂: 208.8 m²/g, Fe/Cu/SiO₂: 178.7 m²/g, Cu/Fe/SiO₂: 212.5 m²/g, all lower than bare SiO₂ (261.4 m²/g). Pore volumes (Vp) and average pore diameters (Dp) were:  
  - Cu-Fe/SiO₂: Vp = 0.56 cm³/g, Dp = 10.7 nm  
  - Fe/Cu/SiO₂: Vp = 0.50 cm³/g, Dp = 11.2 nm  
  - Cu/Fe/SiO₂: Vp = 0.57 cm³/g, Dp = 10.9 nm  
  - SiO₂: Vp = 0.89 cm³/g, Dp = 13.7 nm
- **Observations:** The decrease in surface area and pore volume relative to bare SiO₂ is attributed to the deposition of metal oxides within the support pores. Among the catalysts, Fe/Cu/SiO₂ exhibits the lowest surface area and pore volume, indicating a denser oxide deposition.

#### 6.1.3 Reducibility and Metal Dispersion (H₂-TPR)
- **Description:** H₂ temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) was used to assess the reducibility and dispersion of metal oxides, which are key for catalytic activation.
- **Findings:** Three reduction peaks (α, β, γ) were observed. The α and β peaks correspond to highly dispersed and bulk CuO reduction, respectively, while the γ peak is associated with the stepwise reduction of Fe₂O₃. The intensity of the α peak decreased in the order Cu-Fe/SiO₂ > Fe/Cu/SiO₂ > Cu/Fe/SiO₂, indicating decreasing CuO dispersion. The γ peak’s maximum temperature increased in the order Fe/Cu/SiO₂ < Cu-Fe/SiO₂ < Cu/Fe/SiO₂, reflecting increasing Fe₂O₃ crystallite size and reduced reducibility.
- **Observations:** The impregnation sequence significantly affects both metal dispersion and reducibility, with co-impregnation favoring higher CuO dispersion.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Composition and Chemical States (XPS)
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) provided quantitative analysis of surface elemental composition and chemical states, crucial for understanding surface reactivity.
- **Findings:** Surface Cu content followed the order Fe/Cu/SiO₂ (Cu: 0.69 mol%) > Cu/Fe/SiO₂ (Cu: 0.54 mol%) > Cu–Fe/SiO₂ (Cu: 0.52 mol%). The Cu/Fe molar ratio was highest for Cu/Fe/SiO₂ (0.18), followed by Cu–Fe/SiO₂ (0.13), and lowest for Fe/Cu/SiO₂ (0.12). After reduction, XPS confirmed the presence of metallic Cu and Fe in Fe₃O₄, with the disappearance of Cu²⁺ satellite peaks indicating complete reduction.
- **Observations:** The sequence of impregnation alters both the absolute and relative surface concentrations of Cu and Fe, which correlates with catalytic performance trends.

#### 6.1.5 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Direct imaging techniques (SEM/TEM) were not reported; particle size information was derived from XRD using the Scherrer equation.
- **Findings:** Average crystallite sizes for CuO and Fe₂O₃ were determined, with trends indicating that sequential impregnation affects the size and distribution of active phases.
- **Observations:** Control over crystallite size via synthesis method is important for optimizing catalyst activity and selectivity.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed into carbon-containing products. It is calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{\sum n_i M_i}{M_{\text{CO}}} \times 100
  \]
  where \( n_i \) is the number of carbon atoms in product \( i \), \( M_i \) is the percentage of product \( i \) detected, and \( M_{\text{CO}} \) is the percentage of CO in the syngas feed.
- **Experimental Data:** Under identical reaction conditions (250 °C, 3.0 MPa, H₂/CO = 2, SV = 6000 mL/(g_cat·h)):
  - Cu–Fe/SiO₂: 13.9%
  - Fe/Cu/SiO₂: 17.6%
  - Cu/Fe/SiO₂: 15.4%
- **Observations and Trends:** The Fe/Cu/SiO₂ catalyst, with the highest surface Cu content, exhibits the greatest CO conversion. This trend highlights the positive correlation between surface Cu availability and CO activation efficiency.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, indicating the extent of the water-gas shift reaction. It is defined as:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{n_{\text{CO}_2} C_{\text{CO}_2}}{\sum n_i C_i} \times 100
  \]
  where \( n_{\text{CO}_2} \) is the carbon number in CO₂ (1), and \( C_{\text{CO}_2} \) is its molar concentration.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Cu–Fe/SiO₂: 16.6%
  - Fe/Cu/SiO₂: 17.9%
  - Cu/Fe/SiO₂: 12.5%
- **Observations and Trends:** The Cu/Fe/SiO₂ catalyst demonstrates the lowest CO₂ selectivity, indicating suppressed water-gas shift activity and enhanced selectivity toward desired alcohol products.

#### 6.2.3 Hydrocarbon (CHₓ) Selectivity
- **Description:** Hydrocarbon selectivity (CHₓ) represents the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons, including methane and higher alkanes. Calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CH}_x \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{n_{\text{CH}_x} C_{\text{CH}_x}}{\sum n_i C_i} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Cu–Fe/SiO₂: 59.9%
  - Fe/Cu/SiO₂: 56.0%
  - Cu/Fe/SiO₂: 51.4%
- **Observations and Trends:** The Cu/Fe/SiO₂ catalyst achieves the lowest hydrocarbon selectivity, favoring alcohol formation over undesired hydrocarbon byproducts.

#### 6.2.4 Alcohol (ROH) Selectivity and Space-Time Yield
- **Description:** ROH selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO forming alcohols (methanol and higher alcohols), while space-time yield (STY_ROH) quantifies alcohol productivity per catalyst mass per hour.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Cu–Fe/SiO₂: 23.5% ROH selectivity, STY_ROH: 90.0 g·kg_cat⁻¹·h⁻¹
  - Fe/Cu/SiO₂: 26.1% ROH selectivity, STY_ROH: 126.6 g·kg_cat⁻¹·h⁻¹
  - Cu/Fe/SiO₂: 36.1% ROH selectivity, STY_ROH: 153.3 g·kg_cat⁻¹·h⁻¹
- **Observations and Trends:** Sequential impregnation with Fe first (Cu/Fe/SiO₂) yields the highest alcohol selectivity and productivity, attributed to an optimal surface Cu/Fe ratio and improved metal dispersion.

#### 6.2.5 Alcohol Distribution (Methanol vs. C₂⁺ Alcohols)
- **Description:** The distribution between methanol and higher (C₂⁺) alcohols provides insight into product selectivity.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Cu–Fe/SiO₂: 67.8% methanol, 32.2% C₂⁺ alcohols
  - Fe/Cu/SiO₂: 56.3% methanol, 43.7% C₂⁺ alcohols
  - Cu/Fe/SiO₂: 62.5% methanol, 37.5% C₂⁺ alcohols
- **Observations and Trends:** Fe/Cu/SiO₂ favors higher C₂⁺ alcohol formation, while Cu/Fe/SiO₂ balances methanol and C₂⁺ alcohol selectivity, supporting its superior overall alcohol productivity.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is typically assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time. No explicit deactivation rates or long-term stability data are reported in this study.
- **Observations:** The focus is on initial performance; however, the improved selectivity and productivity of Cu/Fe/SiO₂ suggest enhanced operational stability due to optimized metal dispersion and surface composition.

**Summary:**  
The impregnation sequence critically influences the catalytic performance of SiO₂-supported Cu–Fe catalysts for higher alcohol synthesis. Sequential impregnation, particularly with Fe introduced before Cu (Cu/Fe/SiO₂), results in the highest alcohol selectivity, productivity, and lowest byproduct formation, underscoring the importance of surface composition and metal dispersion in catalyst design.
