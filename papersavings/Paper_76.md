## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The Mn‐Promoted Co/SiO₂ catalyst is synthesized via an incipient wetness impregnation technique, which has been optimized to ensure reproducibility and scalability while maintaining catalyst integrity for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

• **Overview of Catalyst Synthesis Techniques**  
  The chosen method involves the controlled deposition of metal precursors onto a silica support. Aqueous solutions are prepared with 0.67 g Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O (Sigma–Aldrich) and a variable amount (0–0.28 g) of Mn(COOCH₃)₂·4H₂O (Aldrich) to achieve a fixed Co loading of 12 wt% on PQ Corporation SiO₂ (pore diameter 27 nm). This method is straightforward, requires minimal specialized equipment, and is easily scaled up compared to more complex techniques like co‑precipitation.

#### Step-by-step Experimental Procedures

1. **Precursor Mixing / Incipient Wetness Impregnation**  
   - Dissolve 0.67 g Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O and the appropriate amount of Mn(COOCH₃)₂·4H₂O in 2.4 ml of water.  
   - The aqueous solution is applied to the silica support under constant stirring for 90 minutes, ensuring homogeneous distribution of the metal precursors.  
   - No additional precipitation–deposition step is employed in this process.

2. **Drying**  
   - After impregnation, the samples are dried at ambient conditions for 18 hours.  
   - This gradual drying process is crucial to prevent agglomeration and to preserve the uniform dispersion of active sites.

3. **Reduction (Post‑treatment)**  
   - The dried catalyst undergoes reduction in a flowing H₂ atmosphere (100 cm³/min) within a programmable furnace.  
   - The temperature is ramped from 298 K to 673 K at a rate of 4 K/min, and the catalyst is held at 673 K for 120 minutes.  
   - Precise control of the heating rate is essential to ensure uniform reduction and to prevent sintering.

4. **Cooling and Passivation**  
   - The reduced catalyst is cooled at 10 K/min to room temperature.  
   - Post‐cooling, it is passivated using a gas mixture composed of 0.5% O₂ in He (100 cm³/min) for 90 minutes. This step prevents oxidation prior to storage and further characterization.

This synthesis strategy, integrating carefully monitored reagent ratios and temperature profiles, guarantees a replicable procedure suitable for detailed catalytic evaluation in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 X-ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Operational Description**: Catalyst samples are first fully oxidized in O₂ at 673 K for 30 minutes to ensure the appropriate oxidation state before analysis. XRD is then utilized to identify Co₃O₄ phases. The diffraction peaks are processed—baseline corrected, with Ka stripping and instrumental broadening adjustments—and the Scherrer equation is applied to estimate the average Co particle size.
- **Instruments and Models**: No specific instrument manufacturer or model details are provided.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Oxidation performed at 673 K in an O₂ atmosphere; subsequent diffraction analysis is conducted under conditions ensuring accurate phase identification.
- **Test Parameters**: While specific scan ranges and step sizes are not detailed, the procedure emphasizes precise measurement of peak width at half maximum for particle size determination.
- **Key Notes**: Sample alignment and instrument calibration (baseline correction and peak processing) are critical to minimize errors. The method is limited to detecting crystalline Co₃O₄, potentially overlooking other phases.

### 4.2 H₂-Chemisorption Measurements
- **Operational Description**: Following synthesis, the catalyst is reduced in flowing H₂ at a rate of 100 cm³/min by ramping the temperature from 298 K to 673 K at 4 K/min, holding at 673 K for 120 minutes, and then cooling under H₂ at 10 K/min. After cooling, the sample is purged with He to remove physisorbed hydrogen before conducting H₂-chemisorption tests to assess the dispersion of active cobalt sites.
- **Instruments and Models**: Specific instrument details are not provided.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Reduction in H₂ atmosphere under controlled temperature ramping and He purge ensures that the catalyst surface is in a state conducive to accurate chemisorption measurements.
- **Test Parameters**: Gas flow rates (100 cm³/min in H₂) and controlled temperature ramps are key to assessing active sites.
- **Key Notes**: Precise reduction and purging are vital; variability in these steps may influence the interpretation of active cobalt dispersion.

### 4.3 O₂-Pulse Titration
- **Operational Description**: Post-reduction, the catalyst is flushed with He for 30 minutes to eliminate any residual physisorbed H₂. Pulses of 18 μmol O₂ (using Praxair 99.999% pure O₂) are introduced via a sample loop (0.421 cm³) connected to a Swagelok 40 series 4-way switching valve. The effluent is monitored with online mass spectrometry to quantify the oxygen uptake, thereby determining the percentage of fully reduced cobalt sites.
- **Instruments and Models**: The setup includes a Swagelok 40 series 4-way valve and an online MKS Minilab mass spectrometer.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Catalyst reduction conditions (as in H₂-chemisorption) are maintained prior to titration; He flushing ensures accuracy.
- **Test Parameters**: Each O₂ pulse is calibrated to 18 μmol.
- **Key Notes**: Accurate pulse delivery and monitoring are essential to correlate oxygen uptake with cobalt reducibility, demonstrating the impact of Mn promotion on catalyst behavior.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Composition
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase composition of the catalysts were determined using X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis. XRD is essential for identifying crystalline phases and estimating particle sizes, which directly influence catalytic behavior in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns of fully oxidized samples (oxidized in O₂ at 673 K for 30 minutes) revealed only Co₃O₄ features, with no evidence of CoO or metallic Co. The absence of crystalline MnOx phases suggests that Mn is either highly dispersed or amorphous on the silica support.
- **Observations:** The exclusive presence of Co₃O₄ indicates successful oxidation and uniformity in the catalyst’s crystalline phase prior to reduction. The lack of detectable MnOx phases implies effective dispersion of Mn, which may influence catalytic interfaces.

#### 6.1.2 Particle Size Estimation
- **Description:** Average Co particle sizes were estimated from XRD peak broadening using the Scherrer equation, which provides insight into the dispersion and potential activity of the catalyst.
- **Findings:** The unpromoted Co/SiO₂ catalyst (Mn/Co = 0) exhibited an average Co particle size of 6.3 nm. Increasing Mn/Co ratios led to a monotonic decrease in particle size: 5.5 nm (0.05), 4.7 nm (0.125), 4.4 nm (0.25), and 3.9 nm (0.5).
- **Observations:** Mn promotion effectively reduces Co particle size, which is generally associated with increased metal dispersion and potentially higher catalytic activity at low Mn loadings.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Dispersion and Active Site Availability
- **Description:** H₂-chemisorption measurements were used to assess the dispersion of Co and the number of accessible surface sites, both critical for catalytic performance.
- **Findings:** The unpromoted catalyst showed an H₂ uptake of 0.111 mmol g⁻¹, corresponding to a dispersion of 15% and a particle size of 7.5 nm. Upon Mn addition, H₂ uptake decreased sharply to 0.04 mmol g⁻¹ at Mn/Co = 0.05, remained nearly constant up to Mn/Co = 0.25, and dropped further to 0.005 mmol g⁻¹ at Mn/Co = 0.5.
- **Observations:** The initial addition of Mn significantly reduces the number of exposed Co sites, with further Mn increasing this effect. This suggests that Mn may block or cover Co surfaces, impacting the number of active sites available for reaction.

#### 6.1.4 Reducibility of Cobalt Sites
- **Description:** O₂-pulse titration experiments quantified the fraction of Co sites fully reduced from Co₃O₄ to metallic Co, a key parameter for Fischer–Tropsch activity.
- **Findings:** The percentage of fully reduced Co sites decreased with increasing Mn: 83% (Mn/Co = 0), 71% (0.05), 68% (0.125), 74% (0.25), and 66% (0.5).
- **Observations:** Higher Mn loadings generally lower the reducibility of Co, with the most significant decrease occurring at the lowest Mn/Co ratio. This trend reflects the influence of Mn on the reduction behavior and accessibility of Co active sites.

#### 6.1.5 Support Porosity
- **Description:** The SiO₂ support used for all catalysts had a specified pore diameter, which can affect metal dispersion and reactant diffusion.
- **Findings:** The silica support exhibited a pore diameter of 27 nm (as provided by the supplier).
- **Observations:** The large pore size is conducive to effective metal dispersion and may facilitate reactant access to active sites, although no direct changes in porosity due to Mn promotion were reported.

---
Collectively, these characterization results demonstrate that Mn promotion leads to smaller, more dispersed Co particles but also reduces the number and reducibility of accessible Co sites. These structural and surface modifications underpin the observed trends in catalytic performance for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO consumed during the reaction, calculated as:
  \[
  X_{\text{CO}} = \frac{(C_{\text{CO}}^0/C_{\text{Ar}}^0) - (C_{\text{CO}}/C_{\text{Ar}})}{(C_{\text{CO}}^0/C_{\text{Ar}}^0)}
  \]
  where \(C_{\text{CO}}^0/C_{\text{Ar}}^0\) and \(C_{\text{CO}}/C_{\text{Ar}}\) are the CO/Ar ratios at the reactor bypass and during reaction, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** At 1 atm and 493 K, CO conversions ranged from 2% to 44%. The initial CO consumption rate increased sharply with Mn/Co ratio, peaking at Mn/Co = 0.05 (about 50% higher than the unpromoted catalyst). Beyond this ratio, the rate declined. At 10 atm, both unpromoted and Mn-promoted catalysts exhibited higher CO consumption rates overall, but Mn promotion led to a lower rate due to increased CO inhibition.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn promotion at low levels (Mn/Co = 0.05) enhances CO conversion, while higher Mn/Co ratios or elevated pressure diminish this effect due to CO inhibition.

#### 6.2.2 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the percentage of converted CO forming methane, determined by GC analysis:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4\,\text{Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity was highest for unpromoted Co/SiO₂ and decreased significantly with Mn addition (Mn/Co = 0.05), remaining low and stable at higher Mn/Co ratios up to 0.5.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn promotion effectively suppresses methane formation, favoring the production of longer-chain hydrocarbons.

#### 6.2.3 C₅⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₅⁺ selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO yielding hydrocarbons with five or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_5^+\,\text{Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{C}_5^+,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** At 15% CO conversion and 1 atm, C₅⁺ selectivity increased from 32% (unpromoted) to 48% (Mn/Co = 0.125). Further increases in Mn/Co ratio did not significantly alter C₅⁺ selectivity.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn addition shifts the product distribution toward higher molecular weight hydrocarbons, with the most pronounced effect at low Mn/Co ratios.

#### 6.2.4 Chain Growth Factor (α Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor, derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory model, reflects the probability of chain propagation:
  \[
  \alpha = \text{Probability of chain growth (0 < α < 1)}
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The α factor increased with Mn promotion (e.g., Mn/Co = 0.125), correlating with higher C₅⁺ selectivity and lower methane selectivity.
- **Observations and Trends:** Enhanced α values with Mn addition indicate improved chain growth, resulting in a product slate richer in long-chain hydrocarbons.

#### 6.2.5 Olefin-to-Paraffin (O/P) Ratio in C₂–C₄ Fraction
- **Description:** The O/P ratio is the molar ratio of olefins to paraffins in the C₂–C₄ product range, determined by GC analysis.
- **Experimental Data:** The O/P ratio increased sharply with initial Mn addition (Mn/Co = 0.05) and remained constant at higher Mn/Co ratios. The ratio was also higher at 10 atm than at 1 atm.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn promotion and increased pressure both favor olefin formation among light hydrocarbons.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability was assessed by monitoring CO conversion and product selectivities over extended reaction periods (4 h at 1 atm, 20 h at 10 atm).
- **Experimental Data:** After aging, both CO conversion and selectivities remained constant, indicating stable catalyst performance under the tested conditions.
- **Observations and Trends:** The Mn-promoted Co/SiO₂ catalysts exhibit robust stability, with no significant deactivation observed during the evaluation period.

---
**Summary:** Mn promotion of Co/SiO₂ catalysts in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis enhances CO conversion and selectivity toward longer-chain hydrocarbons at low Mn/Co ratios, suppresses methane formation, increases the chain growth probability (α), and improves the olefin content in light hydrocarbons, all while maintaining excellent catalyst stability under both atmospheric and elevated pressures.
