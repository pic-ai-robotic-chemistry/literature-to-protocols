## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

Below, the synthesis of the Co/Al₂O₃‑x%La catalyst is described based on an impregnation method that integrates controlled precursor mixing with a subsequent precipitation–deposition step. This section outlines the procedure in a stepwise manner, ensuring reproducibility and clarity.

#### Impregnation Synthesis of Co/Al₂O₃‑x%La

1. • Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   – Reagents: Cobalt nitrate (10% Co) and lanthanum nitrate (with dopant ratios x = 0, 1, 5, 10) are dissolved in water or ethanol.  
   – Conditions: The reaction is maintained at room temperature under stirring, with pH controlled by incremental addition of NH₄OH (or nitric acid if an acid environment is desired).  
   – Equipment: Utilization of a calibrated pH meter and magnetic stirrer ensures precise control of reaction conditions.  
   – Observations: Uniform solution formation with initial color consistency prior to precipitate development.

2. • Precipitation–Deposition  
   – Process: Following precursor mixing, the support precursor is added dropwise to facilitate the deposition process, ensuring the cobalt and lanthanum species are evenly distributed over the alumina support.  
   – Timing: Stirring continues for 1 hour to allow complete deposition.

3. • Drying  
   – Conditions: The wet catalyst is dried at 100°C for 12–24 hours in a controlled environment, typically using a rotary evaporator or vacuum oven.  
   – Impact: This step removes residual solvents and promotes adhesion of the active precursors onto the support.

4. • Calcination  
   – Parameters: The dried catalyst is calcined at 500°C for 4 hours, ensuring decomposition of precursor compounds and uniform dispersion of the active phases.  
   – Equipment: A high‐temperature furnace is used to maintain consistent conditions.

5. • Post‑treatment  
   – Additional Step: Reduction is carried out under a hydrogen atmosphere at around 400°C for 3 hours, which activates the catalyst by converting metal oxides to their metallic state.  

### Comparative Analysis

The impregnation method offers excellent control over the distribution of cobalt and lanthanum on the alumina, enhanced loading capacity, and ease in adjusting dopant concentrations. However, the process requires meticulous control of pH and drying conditions, and limitations in scalability may arise compared to methods like hydrothermal synthesis. Reported numerical values and parameters (temperatures in °C, durations in hours, pH values, and reagent concentrations) are recorded precisely, enabling reproducibility of the catalyst synthesis by other researchers.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystalline phases of the Co/Al₂O₃-x%La catalysts were determined using X-ray diffraction (XRD), which identifies phase composition and structural evolution as a function of lanthanum doping.
- **Findings:** XRD analysis revealed that at x = 0, the catalysts exhibited prominent peaks corresponding to cobalt oxide (Co₃O₄) and γ-Al₂O₃. With increasing La content (x = 1, 5, 10), additional peaks for La₂O₃ appeared, while the intensity of Co₃O₄ peaks diminished. At the highest La doping (x = 10), metallic cobalt (Co⁰) peaks emerged, indicating partial reduction or stabilization of metallic cobalt by La.
- **Observations:** The incorporation of La alters the phase composition, suppressing cobalt oxide crystallinity and promoting La-containing phases. Optimal La doping facilitates the formation of metallic cobalt, which is beneficial for Fischer–Tropsch activity.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Textural properties, including specific surface area, pore volume, and average pore size, were measured by nitrogen adsorption-desorption isotherms using the BET method.
- **Findings:** The specific surface area decreased with increasing La content: the undoped catalyst (x = 0) had the highest surface area, while x = 10 showed the lowest. Average pore size slightly increased with higher La doping, and total pore volume decreased, indicating partial pore blockage or densification due to La incorporation. For the optimal La level (x = 5), surface area and pore volume were moderately enhanced compared to higher doping.
- **Observations:** Enhanced porosity at optimal La content improves reactant diffusion and accessibility of active sites, supporting higher catalytic efficiency.

#### 6.1.3 Reduction Behavior (H₂-TPR/CO-TPR)
- **Description:** Temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR/CO-TPR) was used to assess the reducibility of cobalt species and their interaction with the support.
- **Findings:** Reduction profiles shifted to lower temperatures with increasing La up to x = 5, indicating enhanced reducibility. Excessive La (x = 10) led to broader or higher temperature peaks, reflecting stronger metal-support interactions or less accessible cobalt species.
- **Observations:** Optimal La doping facilitates cobalt reduction, which is critical for generating active metallic sites.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) provided insights into particle morphology and size distribution.
- **Findings:** SEM images showed that low La doping resulted in smaller, more uniformly distributed particles, while higher La content led to larger, more aggregated particles. TEM confirmed these trends, with optimal La (x = 5) yielding the most uniform and finely dispersed cobalt particles.
- **Observations:** Lanthanum acts as a structural promoter, enhancing cobalt dispersion and inhibiting agglomeration at moderate doping levels.

#### 6.1.5 Elemental Composition and Surface Chemistry
- **Description:** Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) were employed to determine elemental distribution and surface oxidation states.
- **Findings:** EDS mapping confirmed homogeneous distribution of Co and La, with increased surface La content at higher doping. XPS spectra showed binding energy shifts for Co and La species, indicating changes in oxidation states and electronic environments with La addition. Surface pretreatment ensured accurate measurement of Co oxidation states.
- **Observations:** The presence of La modifies the surface chemistry, promoting better cobalt dispersion and altering electronic properties, which correlates with improved catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.6 Surface Functionalities (DRIFTS/FTIR)
- **Description:** Diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform spectroscopy (DRIFTS/FTIR) was used to identify surface functional groups and adsorbed intermediates.
- **Findings:** The spectra revealed adsorbed carbonates, bicarbonates, and formates on the catalyst surface, with their relative intensities influenced by La content.
- **Observations:** La doping modifies surface adsorption behavior, potentially affecting reaction pathways and selectivity in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of carbon monoxide transformed by the catalyst, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
  where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the molar flow rates of CO at the reactor inlet and outlet, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Under Fischer–Tropsch conditions (2.0 MPa, 240 °C, H₂/CO = 2), CO conversion increased with lanthanum doping up to x = 5, reaching a maximum at this composition. For x = 0 (undoped), conversion was lower; x = 1 showed moderate improvement, while x = 10 resulted in decreased conversion compared to x = 5.
- **Observations and Trends:** The optimal La doping (x = 5) significantly enhanced CO conversion, attributed to improved catalyst dispersion and reducibility, as supported by characterization data. Excessive La (x = 10) led to diminished performance, likely due to over-coverage or phase segregation.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, indicating the extent of the water-gas shift reaction:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CO₂ selectivity decreased as La content increased, with the lowest values observed at x = 5. The undoped catalyst exhibited higher CO₂ selectivity, while further doping beyond x = 5 did not yield additional suppression.
- **Observations and Trends:** Enhanced La doping effectively suppressed the water-gas shift side reaction, favoring hydrocarbon formation over CO₂ production.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity was highest for the undoped catalyst and decreased with increasing La content. The x = 5 catalyst demonstrated the lowest CH₄ selectivity, indicating effective suppression of methanation.
- **Observations and Trends:** The trend confirms that optimal La promotion minimizes undesired methane formation, improving hydrocarbon chain growth.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with three or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_3^+ \text{ Selectivity} = 100 - \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}} + F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_6,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The x = 5 catalyst achieved the highest C₃⁺ selectivity, with lower values for both lower and higher La doping levels.
- **Observations and Trends:** This indicates that optimal La content promotes the formation of longer-chain hydrocarbons, aligning with increased α factor values.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor quantifies the probability of chain propagation versus termination in hydrocarbon synthesis, with values closer to 1 favoring longer chains.
- **Experimental Data:** α values ranged from approximately 0.75 (x = 0) to 0.85 (x = 5), with the highest value at x = 5. Further increase in La content did not enhance α.
- **Observations and Trends:** Higher α values at optimal La doping correlate with increased selectivity toward heavy hydrocarbons, confirming the promoter’s role in chain growth.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst deactivation rate measures the decline in CO conversion over time:
  \[
  \text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}} \quad (\text{h}^{-1})
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The x = 5 catalyst exhibited the lowest deactivation rate, maintaining stable CO conversion over extended time-on-stream tests. Catalysts with x = 0 or x = 10 showed more rapid deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Optimal La doping not only enhances initial activity but also improves catalyst longevity, as validated by repeated stability tests.

**Summary:** Systematic variation of La doping in Co/Al₂O₃ catalysts demonstrates that x = 5 provides the best balance of high CO conversion, low CO₂ and CH₄ selectivity, high C₃⁺ selectivity, elevated α factor, and superior stability, as confirmed by comprehensive experimental data and supporting characterization.
