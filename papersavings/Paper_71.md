## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The synthesis of the iron‐based FTO catalyst F3 (15%Fe/85% support) is carried out using a combined co‑precipitation and incipient wetness impregnation approach, ensuring reproducibility and clarity.

#### Co‑precipitation and Support Formation
1. • Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
  – Reagents: Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O and Al(NO₃)₃·9H₂O are dissolved in water.  
  – Base: Sodium bicarbonate is added as the precipitating agent.  
  – Conditions: The reaction is maintained at approximately 40°C under vigorous stirring.  
  – Observations: A clear formation of precipitate is noted, confirming coprecipitation.

2. • Digestion and Washing (Precipitation–Deposition Context)  
  – The precipitated filter cake is digested for 1 hour to enhance homogeneity.  
  – The cake is then thoroughly washed to remove residual ions.

3. • Drying  
  – The washed precipitate is partially dried at 80°C to remove excess moisture, ensuring proper consistency for further processing.

4. • Extrusion and Auxiliary Agent Integration  
  – Sesbania gum powder is added as an extrusion aid.  
  – The mixture is extruded and then allowed to air‑dry at room temperature.  
  – Followed by a secondary drying step at 110°C for 2 hours to secure structural integrity.

5. • High‑Temperature Calcination  
  – Process: The extruded support is calcined in a tubular furnace.  
  – Conditions: 1200°C for 24 hours.  
  – Outcome: Formation of stable crystalline phases (ZnAl₂O₄ with α‑Al₂O₃, θ‑Al₂O₃, and minor β‑Al₂O₃) with partial passivation.

#### Iron Impregnation and Final Thermal Treatment
6. • Incipient Wetness Impregnation  
  – Iron Precursor: Ammonium iron citrate is impregnated onto the support.  
  – Post‑processing: A calcination step in air at 350°C for 4 hours finalizes the preparation of unreduced catalysts (F1, F2, F3).

This method, with its precisely controlled temperatures, durations, and stirring conditions, ensures uniform dispersion of the active phase while maintaining the noted 15%Fe/85% support composition. The entire sequence—from coprecipitation through high‑temperature calcination and impregnation—facilitates scalability and reproducibility, using equipment such as tubular furnaces and standard stirring apparatus to monitor and maintain critical synthesis parameters.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Iron‐based FTO Catalyst F3 (15%Fe/85%(ZnAl₂O₄·3Al₂O₃))

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**: Zinc nitrate hexahydrate (Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O) and aluminum nitrate nonahydrate (Al(NO₃)₃·9H₂O) are dissolved in deionized water; sodium bicarbonate is used as the precipitant. Precise quantities follow elemental ratios as indicated in the referenced table.

##### Step 4: Impregnation and Activation  
- **Reagents**: The support is impregnated via incipient wetness with an aqueous solution of ammonium iron citrate.  
- **Conditions**: Post-impregnation, a calcination at 350°C for 4 hours in air finalizes the unreduced catalyst.

  - Uniformly coat the support with the iron precursor.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystalline phases of the catalyst and supports were identified using powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) with a Panalytical X’Pert Pro diffractometer (Cu Kα radiation). Phase identification was performed by comparison with standard JCPDS files.
- **Findings:** The support (SP-3) and catalyst (F3) exhibit major phases of ZnAl₂O₄ (spinel), α-Al₂O₃, θ-Al₂O₃, and a minor amount of β-Al₂O₃. Freshly reduced catalysts display Fe⁰ as the predominant iron phase, while post-reaction samples show iron species that are X-ray amorphous. In reference catalysts, Fe⁰ transforms to Fe₅C₂ after reaction.
- **Observations:** High-temperature calcination (1200°C, 24 h) stabilizes the crystalline oxide phases, which remain unchanged after catalytic testing. Zinc doping retards the θ-to-α alumina transformation, and the composite support structure is maintained under reaction conditions.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption (BET) analysis was conducted using a Quantachrome NOVA1000 analyzer to determine surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution. Mesoporosity was assessed via isotherm type and hysteresis loop.
- **Findings:** F3 exhibits a BET surface area of 16.9 m²/g, a total pore volume of 0.09 cm³/g (for pores <138.2 nm), and an average pore diameter of 20.3 nm. All catalysts display type IV isotherms with H3 hysteresis loops, indicating mesoporous structures with slit-shaped pores.
- **Observations:** Increasing Al₂O₃ content leads to decreased average pore diameter and pore volume. The uniform mesoporosity and similar pellet size/loading volume minimize transport limitations, supporting efficient reactant diffusion and reliable selectivity measurements.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition and Valence States
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to analyze surface elemental composition and iron oxidation states. Ex-situ measurements were performed with a Thermo Fisher Escalab 250Xi spectrometer.
- **Findings:** Used F3 catalyst surfaces contain both Fe³⁺ and Fe²⁺ species, with the strongest Fe 2p₃/₂ band at 710.75 eV (Fe₃O₄). Additional Fe 2p bands are observed at 712.75 eV (Fe³⁺), 715.96 eV (Fe²⁺ satellite), and 707.85 eV (FeCx, negligible). Graphitic carbon (C1s at 284.67/285.17 eV) and carbonates (285.65/286.27/290.08 eV) are also detected.
- **Observations:** The dominant Fe₃O₄ state and low surface carburization are consistent with the catalyst’s high selectivity. Surface analysis confirms the presence of stable iron oxide species after reaction.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Dispersion
- **Description:** Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM, JSM-7001F) and EDS elemental mapping were employed to assess catalyst morphology and iron particle dispersion.
- **Findings:** F3 displays thick alumina plates with irregularly spherical Fe⁰ and ZnAl₂O₄ particles (15–50 nm). EDS mapping reveals iron particles are well-dispersed, predominantly 10–20 nm in size (range: 10–30 nm).
- **Observations:** The observed morphology and nanoscale dispersion of iron particles support high catalytic activity and stability.

#### 6.1.5 Surface Basicity
- **Description:** CO₂ temperature-programmed desorption (CO₂-TPD) was used to quantify surface basic sites and their strength distribution.
- **Findings:** F3 exhibits two desorption peaks (low and high temperature), corresponding to weak and strong basic sites. The Ihigh/Ilow ratio for F3 is 0.7, indicating a moderate proportion of strong basic sites.
- **Observations:** A higher Ihigh/Ilow ratio correlates with increased C₂–C₄ olefin/paraffin ratios, reflecting enhanced hydrogenation suppression and improved light olefin selectivity.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
  where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the inlet and outlet molar flow rates of CO, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** For catalyst F3 (15%Fe/85%(ZnAl₂O₄·3Al₂O₃)), a CO conversion of approximately 35% is achieved under standard conditions (H₂/CO/Ar = 45/45/10, 20 bar, steady state).
- **Observations and Trends:** CO conversion increases with reaction temperature across all catalysts. F3 exhibits moderate activity compared to unsupported Fe (REF-b) and other supported catalysts, reflecting the influence of high-temperature passivated supports on reactivity.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, indicating the extent of the water-gas shift reaction:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** While specific numerical values for F3 are not detailed, CO₂ selectivity is consistently below 50% for most catalysts, aligning with theoretical expectations for Fischer-Tropsch reactions.
- **Observations and Trends:** The passivated support in F3 helps maintain CO₂ selectivity within the expected range, indicating effective suppression of excessive water-gas shift activity.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity is minimized in F3, as evidenced by the high proportion of light olefins in the product stream.
- **Observations and Trends:** The use of a passivated ZnAl₂O₄·3Al₂O₃ support in F3 effectively suppresses methane formation, favoring olefin production.

#### 6.2.4 Light Olefin (C₂–C₄) Selectivity and Olefin/Paraffin Ratio
- **Description:** Light olefin selectivity is the percentage of converted CO forming C₂–C₄ olefins. The olefin/paraffin (O/P) ratio compares the amount of olefins to paraffins in these fractions.
- **Experimental Data:** F3 achieves a C₂–C₄ olefin selectivity of 58% at 35% CO conversion. Within these fractions, ethylene, propylene, and butylene account for 82%, 92%, and 91% of their respective groups. The C₂–C₄ O/P ratio for F3 ranges from 4.4 to 5.0 at conversions below 45%, significantly surpassing conventional supported iron catalysts.
- **Observations and Trends:** The high O/P ratio and olefin selectivity are attributed to the passivated support, which suppresses secondary hydrogenation and chain growth, directing product distribution toward light olefins.

#### 6.2.5 C₅⁺ Selectivity and Chain Growth (α Factor)
- **Description:** C₅⁺ selectivity reflects the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with five or more carbons, related to the chain growth probability (α factor).
- **Experimental Data:** F3 exhibits lower C₅⁺ selectivity compared to unsupported Fe, indicating suppressed chain propagation. The α factor, inferred from product distributions, decreases with increasing temperature and CO conversion.
- **Observations and Trends:** The passivated support in F3 limits long-chain hydrocarbon formation, favoring light olefins and resulting in a lower α factor relative to conventional iron catalysts.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring changes in CO conversion and selectivity over time.
- **Experimental Data:** While explicit deactivation rates are not provided, F3 maintains stable performance at moderate CO conversion, with no rapid decline observed during the reported tests.
- **Observations and Trends:** The high-temperature passivated support contributes to the structural and chemical stability of F3, minimizing deactivation under reaction conditions.
