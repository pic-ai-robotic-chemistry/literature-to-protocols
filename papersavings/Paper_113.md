## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The catalysts are synthesized via the incipient wetness impregnation technique, which is adapted according to the catalyst formulation (non-promoted, singly promoted, or doubly promoted). Below, two synthesis pathways are detailed.

#### • Incipient Wetness Impregnation for Non‑promoted and La‑promoted Catalysts

1. **Support Pretreatment and Precursor Mixing**
   - The silica support is first ground and sieved to 30–50 mesh, washed three times with boiled distilled water, and calcined in air at 500 °C for 4 h. This pretreatment yields a BET surface area of approximately 251 m²/g.
   - An aqueous solution of Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate (containing ca. 36 wt% Rh) is prepared alongside solutions of La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O (when applicable) to achieve a target loading of 1.5 wt% Rh and, for example, 2.6 wt% La.
   - The impregnation is conducted using a ratio of 1 g SiO₂ to 2 mL solution, ensuring exact control of the reagent volumes.

2. **Drying and Calcination**
   - The impregnated support is initially dried at 90 °C for 4 h, followed by drying at 120 °C overnight.
   - A final calcination is performed in air at 500 °C for 4 h. This step is crucial for forming the desired active metal species and ensuring homogeneous dispersion.

#### • Sequential Impregnation for Vanadia‑Containing Catalysts

1. **Initial Impregnation with Promoter Precursor**
   - The silica support undergoes the same pretreatment as described above.
   - A separate aqueous solution of NH₄VO₃ is used to impregnate the support, targeting the desired vanadia content (e.g., 1.5 wt% V). Following impregnation, the support is dried and calcined at the established conditions (90 °C for 4 h, then 120 °C overnight; calcination at 500 °C for 4 h).

2. **Co‑impregnation with Rh and La Precursors**
   - Subsequent to the vanadia step, the support is re‑impregnated with a combined aqueous solution of Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate and La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O under the same conditions (1 g SiO₂/2 mL solution).
   - The drying protocol (90 °C for 4 h and 120 °C overnight) is repeated, followed by a second calcination at 500 °C for 4 h.

This structured methodology, including precise timing, temperatures, and volumetric ratios, ensures high reproducibility. The sequential impregnation for vanadia‑containing catalysts, in particular, promotes optimal synergy between La and V promoters, directly impacting CO adsorption characteristics and catalyst performance in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

This section details the catalyst‐specific procedures for preparing precursor solutions via weighing of solid reagents, liquid transfer, and subsequent mixing to ensure full reproducibility during catalyst synthesis.

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

##### 3.1.1.1 Rh(1.5)/SiO₂ (Non‑promoted)
- **Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate**: Weighed to achieve a 1.5 wt% Rh loading using an analytical balance.
- **SiO₂**: Weighed in the appropriate mass to satisfy the incipient wetness condition.

##### 3.1.1.2 Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂ (Singly promoted – La)
- **Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate**: Weighed for 1.5 wt% Rh.
- **La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O**: Weighed to provide 2.6 wt% La.
- **SiO₂**: Weighed as the support material.

##### 3.1.1.3 Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂ (Singly promoted – V)
- **Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate**: Weighed corresponding to 1.5 wt% Rh.
- **NH₄VO₃**: Weighed to obtain a 1.5 wt% V loading.
- **SiO₂**: Weighed to meet the incipient wetness impregnation requirements.

##### 3.1.1.4 Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂ (Doubly promoted)
- **Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate**: Weighed for 1.5 wt% Rh.
- **La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O**: Weighed for 2.6 wt% La.
- **NH₄VO₃**: Weighed for 1.5 wt% V.
- **SiO₂**: Weighed based on the fixed support-to-solution ratio.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

##### 3.1.2.1 Rh(1.5)/SiO₂
- **Aqueous Rh solution**: Poured 2 mL containing the dissolved Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate onto the pre-weighed SiO₂ support using a graduated cylinder.

##### 3.1.2.2 Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂
- **Aqueous Rh–La solution**: Poured 2 mL of the mixed solution (dissolving Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate and La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O) uniformly onto the support via a calibrated pipette.

##### 3.1.2.3 Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂
- **Aqueous Rh–V solution**: Poured 2 mL of the solution containing Rh(NO₃)₃ hydrate and NH₄VO₃ onto the support, ensuring accurate delivery with a graduated cylinder or syringe.

##### 3.1.2.4 Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂
- **Aqueous Rh–La–V solution**: Poured 2 mL of the uniformly mixed solution with all precursors onto the SiO₂ support to guarantee complete wetting.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.1 Rh(1.5)/SiO₂
- **Impregnated Rh–SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed using magnetic stirring for 30 minutes at ambient temperature.

##### 3.1.3.2 Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂
- **Impregnated Rh–La–SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed via magnetic stirring for 30 minutes under room temperature conditions.

##### 3.1.3.3 Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂
- **Impregnated Rh–V–SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed with magnetic stirring for 30 minutes at ambient laboratory conditions.

##### 3.1.3.4 Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂
- **Impregnated Rh–La–V–SiO₂ mixture**: Mixed under identical ambient conditions with magnetic stirring for 30 minutes to ensure homogeneous distribution of all catalyst components.

This systematic approach—precise weighing of solid reagents, controlled liquid transfers at a fixed support-to-solution ratio, and consistent mixing—ensures that the preparation of Rh-based Fischer–Tropsch catalysts is reproducible and scalable, aligning with the experimental objectives of CO hydrogenation research.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Rh(1.5)/SiO₂ – Non-promoted Catalyst

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**: 10.0 g Rh(NO₃)₃·xH₂O, high-purity SiO₂ support (1 g per 2 mL aqueous solution)  
- **Conditions**: Stirred at room temperature; appropriate amounts dissolved in deionized water  
- **Process**:  
  - Dissolve Rh(NO₃)₃·xH₂O in deionized water ensuring a homogeneous solution.  
  - Pre-treat SiO₂ by washing, drying, and calcining at 500 °C for 4 hours.  
  - Impregnate the support with the Rh solution using the incipient wetness technique (1 g SiO₂/2 mL solution).  
- **Key Steps**: Accurate reagent quantification and uniform impregnation to ensure reproducibility.

##### Step 2: Purification  
- **Process**:  
  - Wash the impregnated support with boiled distilled water to remove any unbound species.  
  - Filter and collect the catalyst slurry.

##### Step 3: Drying & Calcination  
- **Drying**:  
  - Dry at 90 °C for 4 hours, followed by overnight drying at 120 °C to remove all moisture.  
- **Calcination**:  
  - Calcine in static air at 500 °C for 4 hours with a controlled ramp rate to develop the active phase.

##### Step 4: Activation  
- **Process**:  
  - Reduce the calcined catalyst under H₂ (30 mL/min) at 500 °C for 1 hour to produce the active metallic state.

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [2]: Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂ and Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂ – Singly Promoted Catalysts

Follow Steps 1–4 as above with these modifications:  
- **Additional Reagents**:  
  - For La promotion, incorporate 10.0 g La(NO₃)₃·6H₂O.  
  - For V promotion, use 10.0 g NH₄VO₃, applied via sequential impregnation.  
- **Catalyst Assembly**:  
  - For vanadia-containing catalysts, impregnate first with NH₄VO₃ solution, calcine at 500 °C for 4 hours, then co-impregnate with Rh (and La for the doubly promoted system) solutions.

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [3]: Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂ – Doubly Promoted Catalyst

- **Workflow Order**:  
  - Initial impregnation with NH₄VO₃ followed by calcination.  
  - Subsequent co-impregnation with a combined aqueous solution of Rh(NO₃)₃ and La(NO₃)₃ using the same incipient wetness protocol.  
  - Drying and calcination steps as described, then reduction under H₂.

**Key Points & Caution**:  
- Strictly maintain the 1 g SiO₂/2 mL solution ratio and use two-stage drying to ensure uniform precursor deposition.  
- Monitor stirring speeds and reagent addition rates to minimize synthesis variability.  
- Sequential versus co-impregnation is critical for catalyst performance and must follow the prescribed order for reproducibility.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)
- **Operational Description**: TEM was employed to assess the dispersion and size distribution of Rh particles. The technique enabled the visualization of non-promoted catalysts exhibiting uniform clusters (~3 nm) versus promoted catalysts where Rh appeared as irregular patches (3–20 nm).
- **Instruments and Models**: A Hitachi 9500 electron microscope was used, operating at an accelerating voltage of 300 kV.
- **Characterization Conditions**: High magnification imaging was conducted after appropriate sample pretreatment (prepared via incipient wetness impregnation, followed by drying and calcination).
- **Test Parameters**: Samples were prepared to preserve dispersion; high-resolution images were acquired, though the specific scale was not specified.
- **Key Notes**: The method provided insight into morphological changes upon promoter addition, crucial for understanding catalyst performance.

### 4.2 CO Chemisorption
- **Operational Description**: This method evaluated metal dispersion by recording CO adsorption isotherms and quantifying exposed Rh surface atoms.
- **Instruments and Models**: Analysis was conducted using a Micromeritics ASAP 2010C system.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Approximately 0.2 g of catalyst was evacuated at 110 °C for 30 minutes, then reduced at 500 °C in a hydrogen flow (30 mL/min) for 30 minutes. Subsequent evacuation at 10⁻⁶ mm Hg for 120 minutes at 500 °C was followed by cooling to 35 °C.
- **Test Parameters**: A CO/He mixture containing 4 v/v% CO was introduced at 50 mL/min. The CO uptake values, such as 48.1 μmol/g for non-promoted and 83.2 μmol/g for La-promoted catalysts, were determined based on these conditions.
- **Key Notes**: The procedure emphasizes precise reduction and evacuation steps to ensure reproducibility; the effect of promoters like V is highlighted by a significant decrease in uptake.

### 4.3 FTIR (DRIFTS)
- **Operational Description**: FTIR studies via DRIFTS were performed to analyze CO adsorption behavior on the catalyst surface.
- **Instruments and Models**: An infrared spectrometer with a resolution of 4 cm⁻¹ was used and spectra were generated from 128 interferograms.
- **Characterization Conditions**: About 0.05 g of ground catalyst was reduced in situ at 500 °C at 20 mL/min H₂ for 30 minutes, then purged with 48 mL/min He at 500 °C for 30 minutes before cooling.
- **Test Parameters**: Following background spectrum collection, a 4 v/v% CO/He mixture was introduced; the analysis was performed over a temperature range from 25 °C to 500 °C.
- **Key Notes**: The spectra indicated that La promotion enhanced CO adsorption, while V promotion suppressed bridge-bonded CO signals.

### 4.4 BET Surface Area Analysis
- **Operational Description**: BET analysis measured the surface area to verify the consistency of the silica support and catalytic formulations.
- **Instruments and Models**: N₂ adsorption measurements were performed using a Micromeritics ASAP 2020.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Silica was ground, sieved (30–50 mesh), washed with boiled distilled water, and calcined at 500 °C for 4 hours prior to analysis at −196 °C.
- **Test Parameters**: The BET surface areas were consistently observed around 245–251 m²/g.
- **Key Notes**: Consistent surface area across catalysts confirms that the low metal and promoter loadings did not significantly alter support properties.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase composition of the Rh-based catalysts were evaluated using X-ray diffraction (XRD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). XRD is essential for detecting crystalline phases, while TEM provides direct imaging of particle dispersion and morphology.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns for all calcined and H₂-reduced catalysts (including non-promoted, singly promoted, and doubly promoted variants) revealed no detectable crystalline phases of Rh, lanthana, or vanadia. This absence of diffraction peaks indicates that all active and promoter species are highly dispersed on the SiO₂ support. TEM images corroborated these findings, showing no large crystalline domains.
- **Observations:** The high dispersion of Rh and promoters, as evidenced by the lack of crystalline phases, is associated with increased accessibility of active sites, which is beneficial for catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** The Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) method was used to determine the surface area of the catalysts, providing insight into the textural properties and the effect of metal and promoter loading on the SiO₂ support.
- **Findings:** All catalysts exhibited similar BET surface areas, with values consistently around 245 m²/g, closely matching the pretreated SiO₂ support (251 ± 2 m²/g). The low overall loadings of Rh and promoters did not significantly alter the support’s surface area.
- **Observations:** The preservation of high surface area across all formulations ensures that the support remains highly accessible, facilitating effective dispersion of active components.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** High-resolution TEM was employed to assess the morphology and particle size distribution of Rh species on the catalysts.
- **Findings:** For non-promoted Rh(1.5)/SiO₂, TEM images showed evenly dispersed Rh clusters with particle sizes around 3 nm. In contrast, La- and/or V-promoted catalysts displayed no distinct Rh clusters; instead, irregular patches ranging from 3–20 nm were observed, suggesting either higher dispersion or masking of Rh by promoter oxides.
- **Observations:** The morphological differences are attributed to the synthesis protocols, particularly the sequential and co-impregnation methods, which influence the interaction and distribution of Rh and promoters.

#### 6.1.4 CO Chemisorption Properties
- **Description:** CO chemisorption measurements were conducted to quantify the accessible Rh surface sites and to assess the impact of promoters on CO adsorption behavior.
- **Findings:** CO uptake (μmol CO/g catalyst) varied significantly with catalyst composition:  
  - Rh(1.5)/SiO₂: 48.1  
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂: 83.2  
  - Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 29.6  
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 13.3  
  La promotion increased CO adsorption, indicating enhanced Rh dispersion or new adsorption sites, while V promotion decreased CO uptake, likely due to vanadia coverage of Rh. The doubly promoted catalyst exhibited the lowest CO uptake, reflecting strong modification of the Rh surface.
- **Observations:** These trends highlight the distinct and synergistic effects of La and V on Rh surface accessibility and CO binding, which are critical for catalytic function.

#### 6.1.5 CO Adsorption Modes (FTIR-DRIFTS)
- **Description:** FTIR using diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform spectroscopy (DRIFTS) was used to probe the nature and strength of CO adsorption on the catalyst surface.
- **Findings:** La-promoted catalysts exhibited increased intensity of both linear and bridge-bonded CO bands, especially at 230 °C, indicating enhanced CO adsorption. V-promoted catalysts showed weaker CO bands and absence of bridge-bonded CO, suggesting suppression of CO adsorption due to vanadia coverage. The doubly promoted catalyst displayed moderate CO adsorption and weak dicarbonyl species, reflecting a balance between high dispersion and moderate CO binding strength.
- **Observations:** The FTIR results confirm that La and V promoters exert opposing effects on CO adsorption, and their combination leads to unique surface properties that underpin the observed catalytic synergy.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the amount of CO transformed by the catalyst per unit time and mass, reported as μmol CO converted per gram of catalyst per second (μmol/(g·s)). It is calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the molar flow rates of CO at the reactor inlet and outlet, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standardized conditions (230 °C, 1.8 atm, H₂/CO = 2, CO conversion <10%), the steady-state CO conversion rates were:
  - Rh(1.5)/SiO₂: 0.03 μmol/(g·s)
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂: 0.09 μmol/(g·s)
  - Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 0.09 μmol/(g·s)
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 0.29 μmol/(g·s)
- **Observations and Trends:** Both singly promoted catalysts exhibited a threefold increase in CO conversion compared to the non-promoted catalyst, while the doubly promoted catalyst achieved a ninefold enhancement, highlighting a strong synergistic effect between La and V promoters.

#### 6.2.2 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity represents the fraction of converted carbon forming methane, calculated as:
\[
\text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{n_{\text{CH}_4} C_{\text{CH}_4}}{\sum n_i C_i} \times 100
\]
where \( n_{\text{CH}_4} \) is the carbon number (1 for methane), \( C_{\text{CH}_4} \) is its molar concentration, and the denominator sums over all carbon-containing products.
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity values at steady state were:
  - Rh(1.5)/SiO₂: 48.1%
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂: 35.3%
  - Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 32.7%
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 23.1%
- **Observations and Trends:** Promotion with La and/or V significantly reduced methane selectivity, with the lowest value observed for the doubly promoted catalyst, indicating a shift toward higher molecular weight products.

#### 6.2.3 C₂⁺ Oxygenate Selectivity
- **Description:** C₂⁺ oxygenate selectivity measures the proportion of converted carbon forming oxygenated products with two or more carbons (e.g., ethanol, acetaldehyde), using:
\[
\text{C}_2^+ \text{ Oxygenate Selectivity} = \frac{\sum n_i C_i \text{ (C}_2^+ \text{ oxygenates)}}{\sum n_i C_i \text{ (all products)}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Selectivity to C₂⁺ oxygenates (notably ethanol and acetaldehyde) was:
  - Rh(1.5)/SiO₂: 15.6% (ethanol), 13.2% (acetaldehyde)
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂: 23.6% (ethanol), 11.1% (acetaldehyde)
  - Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 22.1% (ethanol), 10.7% (acetaldehyde)
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: 30.2% (ethanol), 8.7% (acetaldehyde)
- **Observations and Trends:** Both La and V promotion increased ethanol selectivity, with the doubly promoted catalyst achieving the highest C₂⁺ oxygenate selectivity (>30%), while acetaldehyde selectivity decreased, consistent with its role as an intermediate.

#### 6.2.4 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time-on-stream (TOS). Deactivation is inferred from any decline in activity.
- **Experimental Data:** Over 15 hours TOS:
  - Rh(1.5)/SiO₂: Activity remained constant.
  - Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/SiO₂ and Rh(1.5)–La(2.6)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: Slight decrease in activity during the first 8 hours, then stable.
  - Rh(1.5)/V(1.5)/SiO₂: Induction period (~8 hours) before reaching steady state.
- **Observations and Trends:** All catalysts demonstrated good stability under differential conversion conditions, with the doubly promoted catalyst showing rapid stabilization and minimal deactivation.

#### 6.2.5 Additional Performance Indicators
- **C₂/C₃ Ratio:** Used as a proxy for chain growth probability. The doubly promoted catalyst exhibited a higher C₂/C₃ ratio, indicating enhanced chain growth.
- **Observations:** The combination of La and V promoters not only increased activity and selectivity for C₂⁺ oxygenates but also favored longer-chain product formation, underscoring the importance of promoter synergy in optimizing Fischer–Tropsch catalyst performance.
