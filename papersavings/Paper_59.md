## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

#### Impregnation Method for Fe/SiO₂ Catalysts Promoted with Sb and Sn
1. • Precursor Mixing  
  - Reagents: Commercial amorphous silica (CARIACT Q‑10) is used as the support.  
  - Promoter: For FeSb/SiO₂, an aqueous solution of antimony(III) chloride (SbCl₃) is prepared; for FeSn/SiO₂, tin(II) chloride (SnCl₂) is used.  
  - The dopant ratio is maintained at Fe:promoter 100:2, ensuring an iron loading of 10 wt.% (or 21 wt.% for Fe(20%)/SiO₂ samples).

2. • Promoter Incorporation & Precursor Impregnation  
  - Procedure: The support is first impregnated with the promoter precursor (SbCl₃ or SnCl₂) to guarantee homogeneous dispersion of the promoter. After promoter incorporation, the support is subsequently impregnated with an aqueous solution of iron nitrate (Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O).  
  - Equipment: Standard laboratory mixers and pipetting systems are used to achieve accurate solution dosing.

3. • Drying  
  - Conditions: The impregnated samples are dried in an oven at 100 °C for 12 hours.  
  - Observation: Consistent weight increase is monitored to ensure complete solvent removal.

4. • Calcination  
  - Procedure: The dried samples are calcined in air at a target temperature of 400 °C for 6 hours with a controlled heating ramp of 1 °C/min.  
  - Equipment: A calibrated furnace provides the necessary temperature control.

5. • Post‑calcination Activation  
  - Treatment: Catalysts are activated by treatment in a CO flow at 350 °C and 1 bar to transform iron oxide into active iron carbide phases.

#### Mechanical Mixing Approach
• Preparation: Pre‑calcined Fe/SiO₂ is mechanically mixed with separately prepared promoter/SiO₂ powders.  
• Method: This physical blending bypasses the aqueous impregnation step, resulting in lower interaction between iron and promoter phases.  
• Outcome: Although simpler, this method gives lower catalytic activity and stability compared to the impregnation method.

Both methods precisely control reagent concentrations, process conditions, and equipment usage to ensure reproducibility and relevance for high‐temperature Fischer–Tropsch synthesis applications.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal phases and structural evolution of the catalysts were determined by X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) using a Bruker AXS D8 diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 0.1538 nm). Patterns were collected over 2θ = 20–70°, and crystallite sizes were estimated via the Scherrer equation.
- **Findings:** Calcined catalysts exhibited distinct peaks corresponding to hematite (Fe₂O₃, JCPDS13-0534). After activation and Fischer–Tropsch (FT) reaction, the disappearance of hematite peaks and the emergence of new peaks at 2θ ≈ 44° indicated the formation of iron carbide phases (notably χ-Fe₅C₂). No crystalline phases of Sn or Sb promoters were detected by XRD.
- **Observations:** The transformation from hematite to iron carbide is essential for FT activity. Promoter addition did not significantly alter the width of iron carbide peaks, and the apparent iron carbide nanoparticle size remained around 5 nm, though this may be underestimated due to peak overlap.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition and Valence States
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and X-ray fluorescence (XRF) were used to determine surface and bulk elemental composition and oxidation states. XPS spectra provided binding energy assignments for Fe, Sn, and Sb species.
- **Findings:** XRF confirmed Fe content at ~10 wt% and Sn/Sb at 0.7–0.8 wt%. XPS Fe 2p₃/₂ peaks at ~711.2 eV (Fe³⁺), with a shoulder at ~710 eV (FeCx) after CO activation, indicated the presence of both iron oxide and iron carbide. In FeSn/SiO₂, a Sn 3p₃/₂ peak at 716.5 eV increased after CO treatment, reflecting high tin dispersion. For Sb, the Sb 4d peak shifted from ~35 eV (Sb₂O₃) to ~32.3 eV (metallic Sb) after CO exposure.
- **Observations:** The proximity and electronic interaction of highly dispersed promoter species with iron nanoparticles are linked to enhanced FT activity and selectivity.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen physisorption (BET method) using a Micromeritics Tristar II PLUS analyzer assessed surface area, pore volume, and average pore size. Samples were degassed at 250 °C for 2 h prior to measurement at −196 °C.
- **Findings:** Fe/SiO₂ exhibited a surface area of 268.5 m²/g, pore volume of 1.045 cm³/g, and average pore size of 17 nm. Promoted catalysts showed slightly lower surface areas (FeSn/SiO₂: 230.1 m²/g; FeSb/SiO₂: 275.2 m²/g) and similar pore volumes (0.263–0.275 cm³/g) with average pore sizes of 11–22 nm.
- **Observations:** The mesoporous structure facilitates reactant diffusion and accessibility to active sites, supporting efficient FT catalysis.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Scanning and transmission electron microscopy (STEM-HAADF/EDX) provided insights into particle morphology and size distribution. Crystallite sizes were corroborated by XRD.
- **Findings:** Fe nanoparticles in Fe/SiO₂ were uniformly distributed, with an average size of 14.2 nm after activation. Promoter addition increased average Fe nanoparticle sizes to 26.7 nm (FeSn/SiO₂) and 29.0 nm (FeSb/SiO₂). STEM-EDX mapping showed homogeneous distribution of Sn and Sb near iron particles.
- **Observations:** While promoter addition increased particle size and decreased iron dispersion, it also stabilized the catalyst against sintering and maintained uniform morphology.

#### 6.1.5 Reducibility and Phase Evolution
- **Description:** Hydrogen temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) and in-situ Mössbauer spectroscopy tracked reduction behavior and phase transitions.
- **Findings:** TPR profiles revealed step-wise reduction: Fe₂O₃ → Fe₃O₄ (340–420 °C), Fe₃O₄ → FeO, and FeO → Fe (650–700 °C), with an additional peak above 1000 °C for iron silicate. Promoters shifted most reduction peaks to lower temperatures, except the first, which shifted higher. Mössbauer spectroscopy confirmed increased iron carbide fraction with Sb and more wüstite with Sn.
- **Observations:** Promoters facilitate reduction and favor the formation of active phases, correlating with improved catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.6 Carbon Deposition
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) quantified carbon deposition by measuring weight loss between 350–550 °C under air.
- **Findings:** Sn- and Sb-promoted catalysts exhibited approximately 60% and 30% less carbon deposition, respectively, compared to Fe/SiO₂.
- **Observations:** Reduced carbon buildup contributes to enhanced catalyst stability and longevity during FT synthesis.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Under fixed-bed reactor conditions (10 bar, 350 °C, WHSV 3.6 L/g·h, H₂/CO = 1/1), the following CO conversion values were observed:
  - Fe/SiO₂: 11%
  - FeSn/SiO₂: 53%
  - FeSb/SiO₂: 47%
  - Fe(20%)/SiO₂: 14%
  - FeSn/SiO₂ (mechanically mixed): 8%
  - FeSb/SiO₂ (mechanically mixed): 14%
- **Observations and Trends:** Impregnated Sb- and Sn-promoted catalysts exhibit a 4–5 fold increase in CO conversion compared to the non-promoted sample. Mechanical mixing does not yield similar improvements, underscoring the importance of promoter distribution.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, defined as:
  \[
  \text{CO₂ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO₂,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe/SiO₂: 15%
  - FeSb/SiO₂: 47%
  - FeSn/SiO₂: 49%
- **Observations and Trends:** Both Sb and Sn promotion substantially increase CO₂ selectivity, indicating enhanced water–gas shift activity alongside Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  \text{CH₄ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH₄,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe/SiO₂: 24%
  - FeSb/SiO₂: 28%
  - FeSn/SiO₂: 23%
- **Observations and Trends:** Sb promotion slightly increases methane selectivity, while Sn promotion maintains values similar to the unpromoted catalyst.

#### 6.2.4 C₅⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₅⁺ selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with five or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C₅⁺ Selectivity} = \frac{\sum F_{\text{C}_n,\ n\geq5}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe/SiO₂: 40%
  - FeSb/SiO₂: 59%
  - FeSn/SiO₂: 47%
- **Observations and Trends:** Promotion with Sb and Sn increases selectivity toward longer-chain hydrocarbons, with Sb showing the most pronounced effect.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor, derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory model, indicates the probability of chain growth:
  \[
  \alpha = \frac{\text{moles of C}_{n+1}}{\text{moles of C}_n}
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe/SiO₂: 6.20
  - FeSb/SiO₂: 1.70
  - FeSn/SiO₂: 1.31
- **Observations and Trends:** Both Sb and Sn promotion decrease the α factor, favoring the formation of lighter hydrocarbons and olefins, consistent with increased selectivity to short-chain products.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time and quantifying carbon deposition.
- **Experimental Data:** Promoted catalysts (FeSb/SiO₂, FeSn/SiO₂) maintain stable CO conversion over 24 h on stream, while non-promoted and mechanically mixed samples show gradual deactivation. TGA reveals up to 60% lower carbon deposition for Sn-promoted and 30% lower for Sb-promoted catalysts.
- **Observations and Trends:** The significant reduction in carbon deposition and suppression of sintering in promoted catalysts directly correlates with enhanced stability and sustained activity.

#### 6.2.7 Fischer–Tropsch Reaction Rate (FTY) and Turnover Frequency (TOF)
- **Description:** FTY and TOF quantify intrinsic catalytic activity:
  - FTY (10⁻⁴ mol CO gFe⁻¹ s⁻¹): Moles of CO converted per gram of Fe per second.
  - TOF (mol CO s⁻¹ per surface Fe atom): Moles of CO converted per surface Fe atom per second.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - FTY: Fe/SiO₂: 0.20; FeSn/SiO₂: 0.98; FeSb/SiO₂: 0.87
  - TOF: 7–10 times higher for promoted catalysts than for Fe/SiO₂
- **Observations and Trends:** The dramatic increase in FTY and TOF for impregnated promoted catalysts is attributed to electronic promotion effects, not to changes in iron dispersion or carbidization.
