## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The catalyst synthesis follows a sequential impregnation approach designed for reproducibility and compositional precision. The procedure is organized in three key impregnation cycles, each followed by aging, drying, and calcination steps.

#### • First Impregnation: Potassium Loading
1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   - Reagent: 1.77 g potassium nitrate dissolved in 15 ml de‑ionized water.
   - Support: Approximately 10 g high‑surface‑area SiO₂.
2. Process Steps  
   - Age: The impregnated SiO₂ is held at room temperature for 12 h.
   - Dry: The material is dried in an oven at 373 K for 12 h.
   - Calcine: Heat treatment in a furnace at 773 K for 5 h.
3. Observations  
   - Ensures even adsorption of K species without additional pH adjustments.

#### • Second Impregnation: MoP Precursor Deposition  
1. Precursor Mixing  
   - Reagents: 1.39 g ammonium heptamolybdate and 1.04 g diammonium hydrogen phosphate dissolved in 15.7 ml de‑ionized water (maintaining a Mo:P ratio of 1).
2. Precipitation–Deposition  
   - The solution is added dropwise onto the K–SiO₂ support with continuous mixing.
3. Process Steps  
   - Age: The mixture is aged at room temperature for 12 h.
   - Dry: The composite is dried at 373 K for 12 h.
   - Calcine: The dried sample is calcined at 773 K for 5 h.
4. Equipment  
   - Use of a controlled mixing setup and drying oven maintains consistency.

#### • Third Impregnation: Rhodium Incorporation  
1. Precursor Mixing  
   - Reagent: 0.215 g rhodium(III) acetate dissolved in 15 ml de‑ionized water with a few drops of nitric acid to ensure complete dissolution.
2. Process Steps  
   - Age: The Rh solution is added to the calcined K–MoP–SiO₂ and aged at room temperature for 12 h.
   - Dry: The sample is dried at 373 K for 12 h.
   - Calcine: Final calcination at 773 K for 5 h.
3. Follow‑up Reduction and Passivation  
   - Temperature‑programmed reduction (TPR) is carried out by ramping at 1 K/min to 923 K and holding for 2 h.
   - Subsequent passivation is performed in an O₂/He mixture.

This detailed sequential impregnation process—emphasizing precise reagent amounts, controlled aging, drying, and calcination—ensures uniform dispersion and stabilization of the active phases (Rh, K, and MoP), thereby maximizing catalyst performance for Fischer‑Tropsch synthesis.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

##### 3.1.1.1 1 wt% Rh – 5 wt% K – 10 wt% MoP/SiO₂ catalyst  
- **Potassium nitrate (KNO₃)**: Weighed 1.77 g.  
- **Ammonium heptamolybdate ((NH₄)₆Mo₇O₂₄·4H₂O)**: Weighed 1.39 g.  
- **Diammonium hydrogen phosphate ((NH₄)₂HPO₄)**: Weighed 1.04 g.  

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

##### 3.1.2.1 1 wt% Rh – 5 wt% K – 10 wt% MoP/SiO₂ catalyst  
- **Aqueous solution for KNO₃ impregnation**: Deionized water (15 mL) was poured using a graduated cylinder to dissolve the potassium nitrate prior to impregnation of the SiO₂ support.  
- **MoP precursor solution**: Deionized water (15.7 mL) was poured using a graduated cylinder to dissolve ammonium heptamolybdate and diammonium hydrogen phosphate ensuring a Mo:P ratio of 1.  
- **Rhodium(III) acetate solution**: A solution was prepared by dissolving 0.215 g of rhodium(III) acetate in 15 mL of deionized water with a few drops of nitric acid to aid dissolution; the solution was subsequently added dropwise into the precursor-soaked support.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.1 1 wt% Rh – 5 wt% K – 10 wt% MoP/SiO₂ catalyst  
- **Initial impregnation mixture**: During the dropwise addition of the aqueous solutions onto the SiO₂ support, the mixture was continuously stirred at room temperature to ensure uniform dispersion of the precursors.  
- **Post-addition mixing**: Following the complete transfer of all liquid reagents, the impregnated support was maintained under continuous stirring, which facilitated homogeneous mixing before proceeding to the subsequent aging, drying, and calcination steps.

This detailed description of reagent preparation provides clarity on solid weighing, liquid transfers, and mixing operations, ensuring full reproducibility of the synthesis process for the Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂ catalyst under Fischer–Tropsch synthesis conditions.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂ Catalyst

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation – K–SiO₂  
- **Reagents**: 1.77 g potassium nitrate (KNO₃) dissolved in 15 mL deionized water; high‐surface‐area SiO₂ support.  
- **Conditions**: Impregnation conducted at ambient temperature.  
- **Process**:  
  - Dissolve 1.77 g KNO₃ in 15 mL deionized water to form a clear solution.  
  - Impregnate the SiO₂ support with the solution and ensure uniform distribution through continuous stirring.  
  - Allow the impregnated support to age at room temperature for 12 hours.  
  - Dry the sample at 373 K for 12 hours.  
  - Calcine the dried K–SiO₂ at 773 K for 5 hours in air.  
- **Key Steps**: Precise reagent measurement and controlled aging are critical to ensure reproducibility.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly – MoP Precursor Formation  
- **Reagents**: 1.39 g ammonium heptamolybdate and 1.04 g diammonium hydrogen phosphate, each dissolved in 15.7 mL deionized water (maintaining a Mo:P ratio of 1:1).  
- **Conditions**: Dropwise addition with continuous mixing at room temperature.  
- **Process**:  
  - Prepare both solutions separately and combine by adding dropwise onto the pre-calcined K–SiO₂.  
  - Age the resulting mixture for 12 hours at ambient temperature.  
  - Dry the material at 373 K for 12 hours and then calcine at 773 K for 5 hours in air.  
- **Key Steps**: Ensure uniform distribution and precise dropwise addition to maintain the stoichiometry.

##### Step 3: Catalyst Assembly – Rh Incorporation  
- **Reagents**: 0.215 g rhodium(III) acetate dissolved in 15 mL deionized water with several drops of HNO₃ to facilitate dissolution.  
- **Conditions**: Continuous mixing and dropwise addition to the calcined K–MoP/SiO₂ precursor.  
- **Process**:  
  - Add the rhodium solution gradually to the precursor, ensuring uniform contact.  
  - Age the mixture for 12 hours at room temperature, dry at 373 K for 12 hours, and perform a final calcination at 773 K for 5 hours.  
- **Key Steps**: Controlled pH adjustment with HNO₃ and consistent mixing to avoid local oversaturation.

##### Step 4: Catalyst Activation  
- **Conditions**: Temperature-programmed reduction in a quartz U-tube using a 9.5% H₂/Ar gas mixture at 50 mL/min.  
- **Process**:  
  - Ramp the temperature at 1 K/min to 923 K and hold for 2 hours to ensure complete reduction.  
  - Follow the TPR with passivation in 2 vol% O₂ in He for 2 hours at room temperature to stabilize the catalyst.  
- **Key Steps**: Maintain precise ramp rates, gas flow, and passivation to secure the desired oxidation state, ensuring reproducibility and catalyst performance.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 ICP-AES  
- **Operational Description**: The chemical composition of the catalyst was determined by ICP-AES to quantify the elemental contents, particularly Mo, P, Rh, and K.  
- **Instruments and Models**: Analysis was carried out at CANTEST Laboratories (Burnaby, BC).  
- **Characterization Conditions**: Approximately 0.1 g of the passivated catalyst was mixed with 0.7 g of LiBO₂ and fused at 1273 K. The fused sample was then dissolved in 100 mL of 4% nitric acid.  
- **Test Parameters**: Accurate elemental quantification was obtained by following the standard ICP-AES calibration procedures.  
- **Key Notes**: This method is critical for validating the nominal loadings and to detect any phosphorus loss resulting in slight Mo enrichment.

### 4.2 EDX Analysis  
- **Operational Description**: Complementary to ICP-AES, EDX is used to confirm the elemental ratios (such as the Mo:P ratio) through spot analyses on the catalyst surface.  
- **Instruments and Models**: A Hitachi S-3000N electron microscope operated at 20 kV acceleration voltage was employed.  
- **Characterization Conditions**: Multiple (at least 10) data points were captured to ensure statistical reliability of the elemental distribution.  
- **Test Parameters**: EDX settings were optimized for detecting minor variations in elemental content.  
- **Key Notes**: This analysis helps in cross-validating the chemical composition data obtained by ICP-AES.

### 4.3 BET Surface Area Analysis  
- **Operational Description**: Measurement of the surface area was performed to assess the extent of MoP deposition and carbon formation.  
- **Instruments and Models**: Measurements were conducted using a Micromeritics FlowSorbII 2300 analyzer.  
- **Characterization Conditions**: Approximately 0.1 g of the passivated catalyst was degassed at 473 K for 2 hours. A gas mixture consisting of 30% N₂ and 70% He was used for adsorption.  
- **Test Parameters**: Standard BET parameters were applied to quantify the effective surface area despite the reduced value after MoP deposition.  
- **Key Notes**: Proper degassing is crucial for eliminating adsorbed species that could skew the results.

### 4.4 H₂ Temperature‑Programmed Reduction (H₂‑TPR)  
- **Operational Description**: H₂‑TPR was used to study the reduction behavior of the catalyst, identifying characteristic reduction peaks and comparing profiles between MoP/SiO₂ and Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂.  
- **Instruments and Models**: A micro fixed-bed reactor equipped with a thermal conductivity detector (TCD) was used.  
- **Characterization Conditions**: A 9.5% H₂/Ar mixture was flowed at 50 mL/min. The temperature was ramped at 5 K/min up to 1023 K and held for 4 hours.  
- **Test Parameters**: Prior catalyst pretreatment involved drying at 373 K for 12 hours and calcination at 773 K for 5 hours.  
- **Key Notes**: Post-TPR, catalyst passivation was performed by cooling in He followed by a 2-hour treatment in a 2 vol% O₂/He mixture to stabilize the reduced species.

### 4.5 X‑ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)  
- **Operational Description**: XPS was utilized to determine the oxidation states of Rh, Mo, and P after reduction and passivation.  
- **Instruments and Models**: Analysis was conducted using a Leybold Max200 XPS system with an Al K photon source.  
- **Characterization Conditions**: Catalyst samples were cooled to room temperature in He then passivated in a 2 vol% O₂/He mixture for 2 hours.  
- **Test Parameters**: Care was taken to minimize air exposure by transferring the samples under vacuum or N₂ atmosphere.  
- **Key Notes**: The technique enables differentiation between metallic and oxidized species, ensuring accurate surface chemical state analysis.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature is critical for achieving complete reduction of catalyst precursors, thereby exposing the active sites necessary for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The calcined catalyst precursor underwent temperature‑programmed reduction (TPR) in H₂, with a temperature ramp of 1 K/min up to a peak of 923 K. The catalyst was held at this temperature for 2 hours.
- **Observations:** This controlled heating regime ensures uniform reduction of both the MoP phase and Rh species. The gradual temperature increase minimizes thermal shock and prevents sintering, ensuring that the formation of active sites is optimized.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Gas and Flow
- **Definition:** The use of an appropriate reducing gas is key to converting oxidized catalyst species into their active metallic or phosphide forms.
- **Experimental Conditions:** High‐purity hydrogen (H₂) was utilized as the reducing agent during the TPR process, delivered at a flow rate of 120 cm³ (STP)/min per gram of catalyst.
- **Observations:** The hydrogen-rich environment facilitates the complete reduction of metal oxides (including Rh₂O₃ and Mo-oxide species) to their active forms. The uniform H₂ flow across the catalyst bed supports reproducible activation results.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Time
- **Definition:** The duration at peak temperature is essential for ensuring that the reduction reaction reaches completion, yielding a homogenous active phase.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst was maintained at 923 K for a period of 2 hours during the reduction step.
- **Observations:** This dwell time is sufficient to convert all reducible species and is correlated with stable and reproducible catalytic performance during subsequent Fischer–Tropsch reactions.

#### 5.1.4 Post-Reduction Passivation
- **Definition:** Passivation is performed immediately after the reduction process to form a protective oxide layer that prevents uncontrolled air oxidation during catalyst handling.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Following TPR and subsequent cooling in helium, the catalyst was passivated in a 2 vol% O₂/He mixture at room temperature for 2 hours.
- **Observations:** This step establishes a thin, controlled oxide layer on the catalyst surface, preserving the active reduced phases and contributing to the overall stability and reproducibility of the catalytic performance.

Note: The activation pressure was not specified; standard atmospheric conditions are assumed during TPR.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature  
- **Definition:** The reaction temperature is a critical parameter in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis as it directly affects syngas activation and product distribution.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalytic evaluation was performed over a temperature range from 548 K to 613 K. Notably, the highest C₂⁺ oxygenate selectivity (44 C atom%) was achieved at 598 K.  
- **Observations:** An increase in temperature generally influenced hydrocarbon formation, with higher temperatures leading to increased methane and total hydrocarbon selectivity while reducing the fraction of larger oxygenates.

#### 5.2.2 Reaction Pressure  
- **Definition:** Reaction pressure determines the concentration of syngas in the reactor and plays a significant role in shifting equilibrium and influencing product selectivity.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The synthesis was conducted at a pressure of 8.27 MPa.  
- **Observations:** Operating at this elevated pressure helped maximize catalyst performance while maintaining the desired reaction kinetics under Fischer–Tropsch conditions.

#### 5.2.3 Reaction H₂/CO Ratio  
- **Definition:** The H₂/CO ratio in the feed gas is pivotal in directing the hydrocarbon product distribution; it is defined as the ratio of hydrogen to carbon monoxide.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** Three H₂/CO ratios were investigated—1, 1.5, and 2—to assess their influence on catalyst performance.  
- **Observations:** At higher H₂/CO ratios, there was a propensity toward methane formation, whereas lower ratios promoted olefin production. The selectivity trends shifted with temperature variations; for instance, at a lower temperature (548 K), a slight increase in C₂⁺ oxygenate selectivity was observed with a higher ratio, in contrast to the behavior at 613 K.

#### 5.2.4 Reaction Space-Time Velocity  
- **Definition:** Space-time velocity, expressed as gas hourly space velocity (GHSV), relates the syngas flow rate per catalyst mass and is crucial for ensuring isothermal conditions and reproducible kinetics.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** GHSV values were set at 3960 h⁻¹ at 573 K, 7920 h⁻¹ at 598 K, and 15,840 h⁻¹ at 613 K by adjusting the catalyst weight to ensure CO conversion remained below 25%.  
- **Observations:** This careful adjustment minimized mass and heat transfer resistances and maintained consistent reaction conditions, thereby enhancing the reliability of the catalytic performance evaluation.



## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Elemental Composition and Stoichiometry
- **Description:** The elemental composition and stoichiometry of the catalyst were determined using inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX). These techniques are essential for verifying the intended loading of active components and dopants, which directly influence catalytic performance.
- **Findings:** ICP-AES analysis confirmed that the Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂ catalyst closely matched the nominal composition of 1 wt% Rh, 5 wt% K (as K₂O), and 10 wt% MoP on a high-surface-area SiO₂ support. The Mo:P ratio was found to be near unity (e.g., 1.04 for Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂) after reaction, indicating successful stoichiometric control, though slight Mo enrichment was observed due to phosphorus loss during thermal treatment. EDX analysis tended to overestimate phosphorus content, attributed to spectral overlap with silicon.
- **Observations:** The compositional integrity of the catalyst was largely maintained through synthesis and reaction, with minor deviations attributed to phosphorus volatility.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) surface area measurements were performed to assess the textural properties of the catalyst, which affect reactant accessibility and diffusion. Measurements were conducted using a Micromeritics FlowSorbII 2300 analyzer.
- **Findings:** The bare SiO₂ support exhibited a high surface area of 330 m²/g and a pore volume of 1.2 cm³/g. Upon sequential impregnation and MoP deposition, the surface area decreased significantly to 64 m²/g for K–MoP/SiO₂ and further to 56 m²/g for Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂. After catalytic reaction, the surface area of Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂ dropped to 24 m²/g, primarily due to carbon deposition.
- **Observations:** The marked reduction in surface area and porosity is attributed to pore blockage by MoP particles and subsequent carbon accumulation during reaction, which may limit reactant diffusion and influence catalytic activity.

#### 6.1.3 Redox Properties and Reducibility
- **Description:** Temperature-programmed reduction (TPR) was employed to investigate the reducibility of the catalyst components, providing insight into the activation and stability of active phases.
- **Findings:** The TPR profile of Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂ displayed a distinct reduction peak at 505 K, assigned to the reduction of Rh₂O₃, in addition to peaks at 773 K and 976 K corresponding to Mo and P species. The reduction was performed at a ramp rate of 5 K/min up to 1023 K, with a 4-hour hold at the final temperature, ensuring complete reduction of the Rh precursor to metallic Rh.
- **Observations:** The presence of a low-temperature reduction feature for Rh indicates facile reducibility, while the MoP phase retains its characteristic reduction behavior, suggesting minimal interaction between Rh and MoP in terms of reducibility.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Chemical States
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to determine the oxidation states and surface composition of Rh, Mo, and P after reduction and passivation. This information is critical for correlating surface chemistry with catalytic function.
- **Findings:** XPS analysis revealed that Rh existed predominantly as metallic Rh⁰ (3d₅/₂ at 307.2 eV) with a minor fraction of Rh³⁺ (3d₅/₂ at 309.2 eV). The Mo3d spectrum showed both MoP (228 eV) and oxidized Mo⁵⁺/Mo⁶⁺ species, while the P2p region indicated the presence of both phosphide (126.6 eV) and phosphate (133 eV) species. These oxidized species are attributed to surface re-oxidation during passivation in O₂/He.
- **Observations:** The coexistence of reduced and oxidized surface species reflects the impact of passivation and suggests that the active MoP phase is partially preserved, with Rh well-dispersed as metallic nanoparticles.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed into products, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** For the 1 wt% Rh–5 wt% K–10 wt% MoP/SiO₂ catalyst, CO conversion was maintained below 25% for kinetic measurements. At H₂:CO = 1.0, CO conversion increased with temperature: 9.7% at 548 K, 13.6% at 573 K, 13.9% at 598 K, and 19.7% at 613 K. The average CO conversion was 17.4 ± 4.3%. Rh promotion increased CO conversion compared to the unpromoted catalyst (e.g., from 8.4% to 9.7% at 548 K).
- **Observations and Trends:** Higher temperatures and H₂:CO ratios led to increased CO conversion. Rh addition improved both activity and stability, as evidenced by sustained conversion over extended operation.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity represents the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, defined as:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\% C atom)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** At H₂:CO = 1.0, CO₂ selectivity decreased with temperature: 51.4% at 548 K, 44.2% at 573 K, 38.7% at 598 K, and 32.1% at 613 K.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing temperature and H₂:CO ratio reduced CO₂ selectivity, consistent with the water-gas-shift equilibrium shifting left at higher temperatures.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\% C atom)} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** At 548 K and H₂:CO = 1.0, CH₄ selectivity was 11.6% for the Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂ catalyst, compared to 9.7% for the K–MoP/SiO₂ catalyst. CH₄ selectivity increased with temperature and Rh addition.
- **Observations and Trends:** Rh promotion led to higher methane formation, and selectivity to CH₄ rose with increasing temperature.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Oxygenate Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ oxygenate selectivity is the percentage of converted CO forming oxygenates with three or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_3^+ \text{ Selectivity (\% C atom)} = \frac{\sum F_{\text{C}_3^+ \text{oxy},\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** At H₂:CO = 1.0, C₃⁺ oxygenate selectivity was 27.3% at 548 K, peaking at 44% at 598 K, and decreasing to 12.7% at 613 K with H₂:CO = 2.0.
- **Observations and Trends:** Maximum C₂⁺ oxygenate selectivity (44%) was achieved at 598 K and H₂:CO = 1. Selectivity decreased at higher temperatures and H₂:CO ratios.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability reflects the ability to maintain CO conversion over time. While a quantitative deactivation rate is not provided, stability is assessed by monitoring conversion during extended operation.
- **Experimental Data:** The Rh–K–MoP/SiO₂ catalyst maintained stable CO conversion over 60 h on-stream, with data averaged over the 20–60 h period after an initial 20 h stabilization. In contrast, the K–MoP/SiO₂ catalyst exhibited significant deactivation within the first 20 h.
- **Observations and Trends:** Rh addition markedly improved catalyst stability, preventing the rapid deactivation observed in the unpromoted catalyst and sustaining performance during prolonged operation.

---

**Summary:**  
The 1 wt% Rh–5 wt% K–10 wt% MoP/SiO₂ catalyst demonstrated enhanced CO conversion, high C₂⁺ oxygenate selectivity (peaking at 44% at 598 K, H₂:CO = 1), and improved stability due to Rh promotion. CO₂ selectivity decreased with temperature and H₂:CO ratio, while CH₄ selectivity increased with both. The catalyst’s performance metrics were consistently extracted and validated, with carbon balances above 98% and reproducibility within ±10%.
