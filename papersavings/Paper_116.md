### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 Turnover Frequency (TOF)
- **Description:** Turnover frequency (TOF) quantifies the number of catalytic cycles completed per unit time per catalyst molecule, serving as a primary indicator of catalytic activity. It is defined as:
\[
\text{TOF} = \frac{\text{moles of product formed}}{\text{moles of catalyst} \times \text{time}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Under a 1:1 CO₂/H₂ atmosphere at 21 °C, the cobalt hydride catalyst Co(dmpe)₂H achieved TOFs of approximately 3,400 h⁻¹ at 1 atm and up to 74,000 h⁻¹ at 20 atm. Standard conditions (1.8 atm, 21 °C, 0.5 mM catalyst, 0.5 M Verkade’s base) yielded a TOF of 6,400 ± 600 h⁻¹. The TOF was independent of H₂ pressure and base concentration when using Verkade’s base, but showed first-order dependence on pressure and base strength with weaker bases (e.g., DBU).
- **Observations and Trends:** Higher total pressure and the use of strong bases (Verkade’s base, pKₐ = 33.6) significantly increased TOF, while weaker bases resulted in lower activity and induction periods. The catalyst maintained high activity under mild, ambient conditions.

#### 6.2.2 Turnover Number (TON)
- **Description:** Turnover number (TON) represents the total number of substrate molecules converted per catalyst molecule before deactivation. It is calculated as:
\[
\text{TON} = \frac{\text{total moles of product formed}}{\text{moles of catalyst}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Under optimized conditions, the system achieved complete conversion of CO₂ to formate within 60 minutes, with TONs corresponding to the amount of formate produced relative to catalyst loading. For example, with 0.5 mM catalyst and 0.5 M base, full conversion was observed, indicating high TONs.
- **Observations and Trends:** TONs were maximized under conditions minimizing water content and using strong bases. High base concentrations or water contamination led to incomplete conversion due to precipitation or side reactions.

#### 6.2.3 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring the persistence of catalytic activity and the formation of inactive species over time. Deactivation is tracked by the conversion of active cobalt hydride to species such as [Co(dmpe)₂CO]⁺.
- **Experimental Data:** After 24 hours under catalytic conditions, [Co(dmpe)₂CO]⁺ was the only observable cobalt phosphine species, and after 60 days, over 95% of the cobalt complex was converted to this inactive form. Deactivation was accelerated under CO₂-rich conditions (CO₂:H₂ > 1), which promoted the formation of carbonyl and dimeric cobalt species.
- **Observations and Trends:** The choice of base and gas ratio critically influenced catalyst longevity. Verkade’s base minimized induction periods and deactivation, while weaker bases and high CO₂ partial pressures increased the rate of deactivation.

#### 6.2.4 Product Selectivity
- **Description:** Product selectivity refers to the fraction of CO₂ converted to the desired product, formate, as determined by NMR spectroscopy.
- **Experimental Data:** The system exhibited high selectivity for formate under standard conditions, with negligible formation of side products when water content was below 30 ppm. Increased water content led to bicarbonate formation, reducing selectivity.
- **Observations and Trends:** Maintaining low water content and using strong bases ensured high selectivity for formate. No significant formation of CO, CH₄, or higher hydrocarbons was detected under the reported conditions.
