## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the sequential impregnation technique employed for synthesizing the Co/Al₂O₃-x%La catalyst. The article reports a reproducible method that ensures uniform metal dispersion and controlled metal‐support interaction through clearly defined steps and precise numerical conditions.

#### Impregnation Method
• 1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
  – Reagents: An aqueous solution of cobalt nitrate (Co(NO₃)₂) is mixed with lanthanum nitrate (La(NO₃)₃) at fixed concentrations.  
  – Conditions: The pH is adjusted to 9 using NH₄OH, monitored continuously with a calibrated pH meter and titration setup.  
  – Observation: A noticeable color change and the gradual formation of micro-precipitates indicate the onset of precursor interaction.

• 2. Precipitation–Deposition  
  – Procedure: Following initial mixing, the support precursor (alumina suspension) is added dropwise with vigorous stirring for 1 hour to facilitate complete deposition of the active metal phases.  
  – Equipment: A rotary evaporator is utilized for mixing consistency.

• 3. Drying  
  – Method: Post impregnation, the catalyst is dried using mild heating (approximately 80°C) in a controlled oven to remove residual solvents.  
  – Duration: Overnight drying ensures complete solvent removal and uniform distribution of precursors.

• 4. Calcination  
  – Parameters: Calcination is conducted at 500°C for 4 hours in an air atmosphere using a programmable furnace.  
  – Purpose: This step promotes optimal oxide phase formation and enhances the metal–support interface without inducing sintering.

• 5. Post‑processing Treatments  
  – Follow-up: A reduction step activates the metal sites, followed by a passivation treatment to stabilize them against oxidation.  

### Comparative Analysis
• Advantages: The sequential impregnation method provides enhanced control over metal loading, uniform distribution, and scalability, ensuring reproducibility.  
• Disadvantages: Compared to methods such as hydrothermal synthesis or co‑precipitation, it may require longer processing times and careful control of addition rates to prevent overcoverage of active sites. 

This structured procedure, with its clearly defined sequential steps and precisely reported numerical values (temperatures, durations, pH, reagent concentrations), allows other researchers to replicate the catalyst synthesis reliably.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Co/Al₂O₃-x%La

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  • Cobalt nitrate (≈10% by weight cobalt relative to the final catalyst), dissolved in 50 mL deionized water.  
  • Lanthanum nitrate as promoter (quantities: 0 g for 0%, 0.01 g for 1%, 0.05 g for 5%, and 0.1 g for 10% La).  
  • Alumina (Al₂O₃) as the support.  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Room temperature with continuous stirring (using a magnetic or overhead stirrer).  
  • pH adjustment to approximately 7 by adding dilute acid or base dropwise.  
  • Duration: initial mixing for 30 minutes and further stirring for 1 hour.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Dissolve cobalt nitrate in deionized water to form a homogeneous solution.  
  2. Adjust the pH to 7 to ensure complete dissolution and optimal precursor stability.  
  3. In a separate container, prepare the lanthanum precursor solution according to the dopant level required.  
  4. Slowly add the lanthanum solution dropwise to the cobalt precursor under constant stirring to maintain uniformity.  
  5. Finally, incorporate the alumina support ensuring complete wetting, thus initiating the impregnation process.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  • Verify complete dissolution before pH adjustment, and control the dropwise addition to prevent overcoverage of metal sites.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly and Purification  
- **Process**:  
  1. Continue mixing the precursor-impregnated alumina for an additional 30 minutes to ensure homogeneity.  
  2. Centrifuge the mixture at 4000 rpm for 10 minutes and wash thrice with deionized water to remove residual impurities.

##### Step 3: Drying & Calcination  
- **Drying**:  
  • Dry the washed catalyst at 100°C for 2 hours in a vacuum oven or under a nitrogen atmosphere to uniformly remove solvents.  
- **Calcination**:  
  • Calcine in a programmable muffle furnace at 600–800°C, using a ramp rate of 5°C/min under an inert atmosphere to form active oxide phases without sintering.

##### Step 4: Activation  
- **Process**:  
  • Reduce the calcined catalyst under a hydrogen flow (50 mL/min) at 300°C to achieve the final active phase.

**Caution**: Monitor the pH during precursor preparation and ensure precise dropwise reagent addition to prevent overcoverage, maintaining reproducible catalyst assembly.

### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of carbon monoxide transformed by the catalyst, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** For Co/Al₂O₃-x%La catalysts, CO conversion increases with lanthanum doping up to an optimal level. The undoped catalyst (x = 0) exhibits baseline conversion, which improves with x = 1 and reaches a maximum at x = 5. At x = 10, conversion plateaus or slightly decreases.
- **Observations and Trends:** The highest CO conversion is achieved at x = 5, indicating an optimal promoter loading. Excessive La (x = 10) does not further enhance conversion, likely due to overcoverage of active sites.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, defined as:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CO₂ selectivity values are reported for all La loadings. Selectivity is lowest for x = 5, indicating minimized undesired oxidation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing La content reduces CO₂ selectivity up to x = 5, after which further addition does not yield significant improvement.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity reflects the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity is highest for x = 0 and decreases with increasing La, reaching a minimum at x = 10.
- **Observations and Trends:** Lanthanum doping suppresses methane formation, with the most pronounced effect at higher La loadings.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity denotes the percentage of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with three or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_3^+ \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{C}_3^+,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** C₃⁺ selectivity increases with La content, peaking at x = 5.
- **Observations and Trends:** Enhanced long-chain hydrocarbon formation is observed at optimal La loading, supporting the role of the promoter in chain growth.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor, derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory (ASF) model, represents the probability of chain growth:
  - Higher α values (closer to 1) indicate a greater tendency for long-chain hydrocarbon formation.
- **Experimental Data:** α increases with La loading, reaching a maximum at x = 5, then declines at x = 10.
- **Observations and Trends:** The optimal α at x = 5 aligns with maximum C₃⁺ selectivity and CO conversion, confirming the synergy between promoter content and chain growth.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst deactivation rate quantifies the decline in CO conversion over time:
  \[
  r_d = \frac{X_0 - X_t}{t}
  \]
  where \( X_0 \) is initial CO conversion, \( X_t \) is conversion at time \( t \), and \( t \) is the time-on-stream (h).
- **Experimental Data:** Over 100 hours, the x = 5 catalyst exhibits the lowest deactivation rate, maintaining stable CO conversion and selectivity.
- **Observations and Trends:** Moderate La loading (x = 5) confers superior stability, while both lower and higher loadings result in faster deactivation.
