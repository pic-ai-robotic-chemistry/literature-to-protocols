## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The catalyst synthesis for Co/AC and Mn-promoted CoxMn/AC systems employs an incipient wetness impregnation technique that ensures precise metal loading and homogeneous dispersion on the activated carbon (AC) support. The process is organized into a series of reproducible steps as detailed below:

#### 1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation
• An aqueous solution is prepared by dissolving cobalt(II) nitrate hexahydrate, Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, and manganese(II) acetate tetrahydrate, Mn(CH₃COO)₂·4H₂O, in water.  
• The metal precursor quantities are adjusted so that the Co loading is fixed at 15 wt% while Mn is varied between 0 and 3 wt%, allowing controlled promotional effects.  
• The AC support is pre-treated by washing with HNO₃ and deionized water, followed by drying at 120 °C for 24 hours, and sieving to obtain a particle size of 180–250 μm for optimal impregnation.

#### 2. Precipitation–Deposition
• Although the standard step order includes this stage, in this synthesis the incipient wetness impregnation directly achieves uniform deposition, and no additional precipitation–deposition stage is employed.

#### 3. Drying
• The impregnated AC is dried overnight at 60 °C in air. This step serves to remove excess moisture, ensuring that the precursor solution fills the pores without residue.

#### 4. Calcination
• The dried catalyst precursor is calcined in an argon atmosphere at 350 °C for 16 hours. This thermal treatment decomposes the metal precursors into stable oxide phases while preventing unwanted oxidation.

#### 5. Additional Post‑treatment
• Prior to catalytic testing, an in situ reduction is executed in a fixed-bed reactor. The catalyst is reduced under an H₂ atmosphere at 430 °C for 10 hours, converting cobalt oxides to active metallic cobalt.

This method—validated by precise temperature, time, and loading controls—provides reproducible results and facilitates comparative analysis against other synthesis approaches, highlighting advantages such as uniform metal dispersion, scalability, and operational simplicity in Fischer–Tropsch catalyst preparation.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

In this section, detailed preparation steps for both the unpromoted Co/AC catalyst and the Mn‐promoted CoxMn/AC catalysts are provided. The process is organized into three core operations: weighing of solid reagents, liquid reagent transfer, and mixing to achieve a uniform precursor distribution.

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

##### 3.1.1.1 Co/AC Catalyst
- **Activated Carbon (AC)**: Weighed 1 g to serve as the catalyst support.
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed in an amount calculated to deliver a fixed 15 wt % Co loading relative to the total mass of the catalyst.

##### 3.1.1.2 Mn‐Promoted CoxMn/AC Catalyst
- **Activated Carbon (AC)**: Weighed 1 g following the same support preparation protocol.
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed to maintain a 15 wt % Co loading.
- **Mn(CH₃COO)₂·4H₂O**: Weighed to incorporate Mn at loadings of 0.5, 1, 2, or 3 wt %, depending on the target catalyst composition.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

##### 3.1.2.1 Co/AC Catalyst
- **Aqueous solution of Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Poured 1.4 mL onto the weighed AC support using a graduated cylinder. This step ensures that the Co precursor is uniformly distributed onto the support.

##### 3.1.2.2 Mn‐Promoted CoxMn/AC Catalyst
- **Mixed aqueous solution of Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O and Mn(CH₃COO)₂·4H₂O**: Poured 1.4 mL onto the weighed AC support using a graduated cylinder. The solution is prepared to achieve the desired Co and Mn ratios for effective promoter integration.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.1 Co/AC Catalyst
- **Precursor-impregnated AC**: Mixed thoroughly under ambient conditions to ensure a uniform coating of the Co precursor over the AC support. This homogeneous mixing is critical prior to the subsequent drying and calcination procedures.

##### 3.1.3.2 Mn‐Promoted CoxMn/AC Catalyst
- **Mixed precursor-impregnated AC**: Mixed adequately at room temperature to guarantee that both the Co and Mn precursors are evenly distributed across the AC support. This step is essential for achieving consistent impregnation, which in turn improves reproducibility during drying, calcination under Ar, and later in situ reduction steps.

The described procedure, employing precise reagent weighing and controlled liquid transfer followed by thorough mixing, underpins the replicability of the incipient wetness impregnation method used in these Fischer–Tropsch catalyst preparations.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

  • Impregnate the support by adding the precursor solution gradually until incipient wetness is achieved.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 X‑ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Operational Description**: XRD is utilized to identify crystalline phases and confirm Co₂C formation in the catalysts. The method provides insights into the phase composition of both unpromoted and Mn‑promoted catalysts.
- **Instruments and Models**: PANalytical X’Pert Powder diffractometer.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Cu Kα radiation is used at an accelerating voltage of 40 kV and 40 mA, with a 2θ scan range from 30° to 70°.
- **Test Parameters**: A step size of 0.02° and a scanning rate of 2°/min; samples are packed directly into a glassy sample cell to ensure proper alignment.
- **Key Notes**: Accurate sample mounting is crucial for reliable phase identification, particularly for distinguishing Co₂C from overlapping phases.

### 4.2 High-Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy (HRTEM) & Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (EDS)
- **Operational Description**: HRTEM is applied to assess the catalyst morphology and particle size. It distinguishes the Co₂C@Co structure in unpromoted Co/AC from the Co@Co₂C structure in Mn‑promoted catalysts.
- **Instruments and Models**: Tecnai G2 F30 instrument.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Operated at an accelerating voltage of 300 kV.
- **Test Parameters**: Catalyst samples are prepared by drop-casting an ethanol suspension of ground material onto lacey carbon Cu grids.
- **Key Notes**: This technique provides detailed microstructural information critical for understanding active site distribution.

### 4.3 X‑ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS), XANES, and EXAFS
- **Operational Description**: These spectroscopic methods assess the oxidation state and local coordination environment of cobalt and manganese. XPS confirms the catalyst surface composition post in situ reduction, while XANES and EXAFS offer insights into electronic structure changes.
- **Instruments and Models**: XPS is conducted in a multimethod UHV system (base pressure of 7.1×10⁻⁵ Pa); XANES/EXAFS measurements are performed at the Co K-edge (7712 eV) under ambient conditions.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Prior to analysis, catalyst samples are reduced in flowing H₂ at 430 °C for 120 minutes and pressed in silver foil.
- **Test Parameters**: High-resolution scans for Co 2p, Mn 2p, C 1s, and O 1s are acquired.
- **Key Notes**: This combined approach is vital for monitoring the transition from metallic Co to carbide phases.

### 4.4 BET Surface Area Analysis
- **Operational Description**: N₂ physisorption measurements are performed to determine the BET surface area and pore characteristics.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Measurements are conducted at 77 K after degassing the catalyst at 200 °C overnight.
- **Key Notes**: Decreasing BET areas with increasing Mn loading correlate with textural changes of the support.

### 4.5 H₂ Temperature‑Programmed Reduction (H₂‑TPR)
- **Operational Description**: H₂‑TPR evaluates the reducibility of the catalysts.
- **Instruments and Models**: A micro fixed-bed reactor coupled with a thermal conductivity detector (TCD) is used.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Approximately 100 mg of calcined catalyst is pretreated by flushing with Ar at 120 °C for 2 hours, then heated to 800 °C at 10 °C/min in a 10% H₂/Ar gas mixture.
- **Test Parameters**: The reduction profile is recorded via H₂ consumption.
- **Key Notes**: Uniform catalyst loading and precise temperature control are essential for reproducibility.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The catalyst activation step is a critical pre-reaction treatment designed to convert the cobalt oxide species into active metallic cobalt, setting the stage for effective Fischer-Tropsch synthesis. This procedure is carried out in a fixed-bed reactor under well-controlled conditions to ensure reproducibility and optimal catalyst performance.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature is pivotal for driving the reduction of cobalt oxides, ensuring the formation of a metallic phase that can later undergo carburization to form Co₂C under reaction conditions.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst is subjected to in situ reduction at 430 °C.
- **Observations:** Operating at 430 °C guarantees a complete reduction of precursor oxides, facilitating a uniform active phase distribution essential for high catalytic activity.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** Activation pressure influences the kinetics of reduction, affecting how effectively the hydrogen gas interacts with the catalyst surface.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The reduction is performed at a pressure of 0.1 MPa.
- **Observations:** Maintaining a pressure of 0.1 MPa under a hydrogen atmosphere ensures efficient conversion of cobalt oxides without inducing undesirable agglomeration.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
- **Definition:** The selection of the activating gas sets the chemical environment needed for reduction. A hydrogen-rich atmosphere is fundamental to reduce the oxide species.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Hydrogen (H₂) is employed as the sole reducing gas.
- **Observations:** The use of H₂ ensures that cobalt oxides are fully reduced to metallic cobalt, forming the ideal precursor for the subsequent formation of active carbide phases during the Fischer-Tropsch reaction.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time
- **Definition:** Adequate activation time is necessary to achieve complete reduction while avoiding excessive exposure that may lead to sintering.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst is maintained at 430 °C under H₂ for 10 hours.
- **Observations:** A 10-hour reduction period has been optimized to ensure complete transformation of cobalt oxide to metallic cobalt, thereby promoting catalyst stability and optimal dispersion of active sites prior to the reaction.

These activation conditions—specifically the precise temperature, pressure, gas composition, and time—are integral for setting the catalyst in an optimal state, enabling effective performance during Fischer-Tropsch synthesis.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature  
- **Definition:** The reaction temperature is the thermal condition at which the Fischer-Tropsch synthesis is conducted. It significantly influences the catalyst activity, product distribution, and reaction kinetics.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The reactor is maintained at 220 °C during the catalytic evaluation.  
- **Observations:** Operating at 220 °C provided optimal catalytic performance, with a stable reaction profile. In tests with the Co1Mn/AC catalyst, the temperature was effective in sustaining the catalytic activity over prolonged operation (with minimal deactivation observed over extended time on stream).

#### 5.2.2 Reaction Pressure  
- **Definition:** The total system pressure governs the concentration of syngas in the reactor, directly impacting both the reaction rate and the selectivity toward heavier hydrocarbons (C₅+).  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The reaction is performed at a pressure of 3.0 MPa.  
- **Observations:** This elevated pressure enhances the syngas concentration, favoring the formation of carbon-rich products. The consistent use of 3.0 MPa has been linked with improved catalyst performance and a notable increase in higher hydrocarbon selectivity.

#### 5.2.3 Reaction H₂/CO Ratio  
- **Definition:** The molar ratio of hydrogen (H₂) to carbon monoxide (CO) in the feed gas is critical, as it affects hydrogenation dynamics and product distribution during the Fischer-Tropsch reaction.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** A fixed H₂/CO ratio of 2.0 is maintained throughout the evaluation.  
- **Observations:** This ratio creates a balanced syngas environment, promoting the formation of olefins and higher alcohols while suppressing excessive methane production. Furthermore, it contributes to establishing a C-rich and H-lean catalyst surface in Mn-promoted systems.

#### 5.2.4 Reaction Space-Time Velocity  
- **Definition:** The gas hourly space velocity (GHSV) represents the volumetric flow rate of the reactants per unit mass of catalyst, affecting the contact time between syngas and the active sites.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The GHSV is set at approximately 2000 h⁻¹.  
- **Observations:** This flow rate is carefully optimized to balance CO conversion efficiency and catalyst stability, ensuring that while reactant exposure is sufficiently high to maintain activity, it avoids rapid catalyst deactivation during the Fischer-Tropsch synthesis.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystalline phases and structural evolution of the catalysts were determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD), with further insights from high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM).
- **Findings:** XRD analysis of the spent unpromoted Co/AC catalyst revealed the coexistence of face-centered cubic metallic Co (fcc-Co), Co₃O₄, and Co₂C phases. In contrast, Mn-promoted CoxMn/AC catalysts (0.5–3 wt% Mn) exhibited only Co₂C diffraction peaks after reaction, with no detectable metallic Co or cobalt oxide phases. HRTEM confirmed that unpromoted Co/AC features a Co₂C@Co structure, where Co₂C forms on the surface of Co nanoparticles. Upon Mn promotion, a distinct Co@Co₂C structure emerges, characterized by ~20 nm Co₂C nanoparticles with highly dispersed Co⁰ ensembles (<6 nm) on their surfaces.
- **Observations:** The transformation from metallic Co to Co₂C is facilitated by syngas treatment, and Mn promotion drives the complete carburization of cobalt, stabilizing the Co@Co₂C interface critical for catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition and Valence State
- **Description:** Surface and bulk chemical states were probed using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES), and extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS).
- **Findings:** XPS spectra of calcined precursors showed Co³⁺ and Co²⁺ (779.7, 781.3, 782.7 eV) and Mn⁴⁺ (641.9 eV). After reduction and reaction, unpromoted Co/AC surfaces were dominated by Co²⁺ (mainly Co₂C and some CoO), with no metallic Co detected. In Mn-promoted catalysts, both Co²⁺ (781.5 eV, attributed to Co₂C) and a stable Co⁰ component (778.2 eV) were present, indicating ultrastable metallic Co on Co₂C nanoparticles. XANES and EXAFS confirmed the evolution from metallic Co to Co₂C under syngas, with Mn facilitating this transformation and stabilizing the Co–Co₂C interface.
- **Observations:** The coexistence of surface Co⁰ and Co₂C in Mn-promoted catalysts is linked to enhanced olefin selectivity and catalyst stability.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** N₂ physisorption (BET and BJH/HK methods) was used to assess surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution.
- **Findings:** Increasing Mn loading from 0.5 to 3 wt% led to a decrease in BET surface area (from 825 to 700 m²/g) and total pore volume (from 0.47 to 0.60 cm³/g). The average mesopore size remained relatively constant (3.1–3.8 nm), and micropore size was stable at ~5.5–5.6 Å across all samples.
- **Observations:** The reduction in surface area and pore volume with higher Mn content is attributed to pore blockage by carbonaceous species formed during calcination, while pore size distribution remains largely unaffected.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Morphological features and particle size distributions were characterized by HRTEM and supported by XRD.
- **Findings:** Unpromoted Co/AC exhibited spherical nanoparticles with an average size of ~11 nm. Mn-promoted CoxMn/AC catalysts displayed larger Co₂C nanoparticles (17–20 nm) with highly dispersed Co⁰ ensembles (<6 nm) on their surfaces. STEM-EDS mapping confirmed uniform Mn dispersion.
- **Observations:** Mn promotion leads to a uniform Co@Co₂C structure, with increased particle size and enhanced dispersion of active Co⁰ sites, which are essential for the observed catalytic behavior.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction. It is calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the molar flow rates of CO into and out of the reactor, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard Fischer-Tropsch synthesis (FTS) conditions (220 °C, 3.0 MPa, H₂/CO = 2, GHSV = 2000 h⁻¹), the unpromoted Co/AC catalyst achieved a CO conversion of 47.5%. Mn-promoted catalysts exhibited lower conversions: Co0.5Mn/AC at 40.5%, Co1Mn/AC at 29.1%, Co2Mn/AC at 14.3%, and Co3Mn/AC at 10.5%.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing Mn loading leads to a progressive decrease in CO conversion, indicating that Mn promotion, while beneficial for selectivity, suppresses overall CO hydrogenation activity.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO that forms CO₂, calculated as:
\[
\text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** CO₂ selectivity values increase with Mn content: Co/AC (0.7%), Co0.5Mn/AC (1.8%), Co1Mn/AC (2.4%), Co2Mn/AC (3.1%), and Co3Mn/AC (2.9%).
- **Observations and Trends:** The rise in CO₂ selectivity with Mn addition suggests enhanced CO dissociation and disproportionation, consistent with a more C-rich, H-lean catalyst surface.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
\[
\text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** CH₄ selectivity is highest for Co/AC (22.9%) and significantly reduced in Mn-promoted catalysts: Co0.5Mn/AC (8.6%), Co1Mn/AC (8.1%), Co2Mn/AC (9.7%), Co3Mn/AC (10.3%).
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn promotion markedly suppresses methane formation, favoring the production of longer-chain hydrocarbons and oxygenates.

#### 6.2.4 Olefin Selectivity
- **Description:** Olefin selectivity is the percentage of converted CO forming C₂–C₄ olefins, calculated on a carbon basis.
- **Experimental Data:** Olefin selectivity increases from 14.7% (Co/AC) to 41.7% (Co2Mn/AC and Co3Mn/AC), with Co0.5Mn/AC and Co1Mn/AC also exceeding 40%.
- **Observations and Trends:** The most pronounced increase occurs with initial Mn addition (0.5 wt %), after which further Mn increases do not substantially affect olefin selectivity. This enhancement is attributed to the C-rich, H-lean surface environment and the formation of the Co@Co₂C structure.

#### 6.2.5 Alcohol Selectivity
- **Description:** Alcohol selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO forming alcohols (mainly higher alcohols), calculated as a carbon percentage.
- **Experimental Data:** Alcohol selectivity is 14.6% for Co/AC and rises to 19–21% for Mn-promoted catalysts, with the ratio of higher alcohols to total alcohols exceeding 90% across all samples.
- **Observations and Trends:** Alcohol selectivity remains relatively stable with Mn promotion, indicating that the Co–Co₂C interface, essential for alcohol synthesis, is preserved.

#### 6.2.6 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor, derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory model, indicates the probability of chain growth versus termination:
\[
\alpha = \frac{\text{Probability of chain growth}}{\text{Probability of chain growth} + \text{Probability of termination}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** All catalysts, regardless of Mn content, exhibit α values of approximately 0.65.
- **Observations and Trends:** The invariance of α with Mn loading suggests that chain-lengthening characteristics and product distribution patterns are not significantly altered by Mn promotion.

#### 6.2.7 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion and product selectivity over extended time on stream.
- **Experimental Data:** For Co1Mn/AC, CO conversion decreased slightly from 29% to 25% after 120 hours, with negligible change in selectivity.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalysts demonstrate excellent stability, with minimal deactivation and sustained product distribution, highlighting the robustness of the Co@Co₂C structure under reaction conditions.
