## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

#### Incipient Wetness Impregnation Method for 15Co–xCa/AC Catalyst

1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   • The catalyst synthesis begins with the preparation of the activated carbon (AC) support. Coconut shell‑derived AC is first washed with deionized water until its pH reaches approximately 7 to remove impurities, then crushed to a 40–60 mesh size to maximize surface area and pore accessibility.  
   • An aqueous solution is prepared using Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O as the cobalt precursor. For CaO-promoted samples, calcium nitrate is added at controlled amounts to yield CaO loadings of 0, 0.05, 0.10, and 0.50 wt%.  
   • Standard laboratory equipment such as pH meters and glassware are utilized to ensure precise measurement and mixing.

2. Precipitation–Deposition  
   • Although some synthesis methods include a precipitation–deposition step, the current procedure omits this step, relying solely on the incipient wetness impregnation approach to achieve uniform metal distribution.

3. Drying  
   • Immediately following impregnation, the impregnated AC is dried in air at 333 K for 12 hours. This drying stage is critical to remove excess moisture and to ensure that the metal precursors are firmly fixed on the support.  
   • Observations during drying include a uniform color and texture of the impregnated AC, with no formation of distinct precipitates.

4. Calcination and Post‑treatment  
   • Post‑drying, an in situ reduction is performed. The catalyst is subjected to a hydrogen atmosphere at 703 K for 10 hours under a pressure of 0.1 MPa and a flow rate of 900 h⁻¹ in a fixed-bed reactor. This step converts the cobalt precursor into its active metallic form and promotes the formation of Co–CaO interactions.  
   • The incipient wetness impregnation method is favored for its simplicity, precise control of metal precursor loading, scalability, and reproducibility compared to other synthesis techniques like hydrothermal or co‑precipitation methods.

This structured approach ensures that every step—from mixing to reduction—is carefully controlled, allowing for reproducibility and a clear understanding of how the active Co and CaO phases are developed, which is essential for achieving optimal Fischer–Tropsch catalytic performance.

- **Instruments and Models**: PANalytical X’Pert3 Powder diffractometer.

### 4.2 H₂ Temperature‑Programmed Reduction (H₂‑TPR)
- **Operational Description**: The reduction behavior of the catalytic samples was analyzed by subjecting 100 mg of calcined catalyst to a pretreatment with argon, followed by heating in a controlled 10% H₂/Ar atmosphere.
- **Instruments and Models**: A micro fixed-bed reactor equipped with a thermal conductivity detector (TCD) was used.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Initially flushed with argon at 393 K for 1 hour, cooled to 323 K, and then heated to 1173 K at a ramping rate of 10 K/min with a gas flow of 30 mL/min.
- **Test Parameters**: The procedure revealed that CaO incorporation shifted the reduction temperature to a range of 626–630 K.
- **Key Notes**: This higher reduction temperature indicates a stronger Co–CaO interaction, affecting the reduction of cobalt oxide species.

### 4.3 N₂ Physisorption (BET Analysis)
- **Operational Description**: BET measurements were used to assess the surface area, pore volume, and average pore size of the catalysts.
- **Instruments and Models**: Measurements were conducted on an Autosorb‑1 instrument.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Samples (30 mg each) were out-gassed under vacuum at 473 K for 3 hours and analyzed at 77 K.
- **Test Parameters**: Observations include a significant decrease in surface area and mesopore volume upon Co and CaO impregnation.
- **Key Notes**: Precise out-gassing ensures removal of adsorbed species for accurate surface determination.

### 4.4 CO Chemisorption
- **Operational Description**: Pulsed CO adsorption experiments were performed to evaluate cobalt dispersion and reduction degree.
- **Instruments and Models**: The setup involves an in-situ reduction system with a TCD for detection.
- **Characterization Conditions**: 100 mg samples were reduced in a 10% H₂/Ar flow at 703 K for 4 hours, purged with helium for 10 minutes, then cooled to 323 K for CO adsorption using a 10% CO/He mixture.
- **Test Parameters**: The adsorption was continued until the TCD signal stabilized, indicating complete adsorption.
- **Key Notes**: This method is essential for quantifying active cobalt sites affected by CaO doping.

### 4.5 High‑Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy (HRTEM)

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Composition (XRD)
- **Description:** X-ray diffraction (XRD) was employed to identify crystalline phases and monitor phase transformations in both fresh and spent 15Co–xCa/AC catalysts. The analysis distinguishes between hexagonal close-packed (hcp) and face-centered cubic (fcc) metallic cobalt, as well as cobalt carbide (Co₂C), using characteristic diffraction peaks and the JCPDS library.
- **Findings:** In fresh catalysts, both hcp and fcc Co phases coexist, with the intensity of hcp Co peaks increasing as CaO loading rises, indicating that CaO promotes hcp Co formation. After CO hydrogenation, hcp Co peaks nearly disappear while fcc Co peaks persist, and new peaks corresponding to Co₂C (e.g., at 37.0°, 41.3°, 42.6°, 45.8°, and 56.6° 2θ) emerge, confirming the transformation of hcp Co to Co₂C. The Co₂C/Co intensity ratio increases from 0.44 to 0.78 as CaO content increases from 0 to 0.50 wt%.
- **Observations:** CaO addition facilitates the formation of hcp Co and its subsequent conversion to Co₂C under syngas, leading to the coexistence of metallic Co (mainly fcc) and Co₂C in spent catalysts.

#### 6.1.2 Reduction Behavior (H₂-TPR)
- **Description:** H₂-temperature programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) assesses the reducibility of cobalt species and the strength of metal-support interactions. Reduction temperatures reflect the ease of converting cobalt oxides to metallic Co.
- **Findings:** H₂-TPR profiles display two main reduction peaks for cobalt oxides, with reduction temperatures increasing slightly (626–630 K) as CaO loading increases. This shift indicates stronger Co–CaO interactions and more difficult reduction of cobalt oxides, particularly CoOₓ.
- **Observations:** CaO acts to inhibit the reduction of cobalt oxides, which is consistent with the observed increase in hcp Co and Co₂C formation.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity (N₂ Physisorption, BET)
- **Description:** N₂ physisorption (BET method) quantifies surface area, pore volume, and pore size, providing insight into catalyst textural properties.
- **Findings:** The BET surface area decreases from 976 m²/g (AC) to 890 m²/g (15Co/AC) and further to 695 m²/g (15Co0.50Ca/AC) with increasing CaO. Total pore volume drops from 0.70 cm³/g (AC) to 0.46 cm³/g (15Co0.50Ca/AC), and average pore size decreases from 1.4 nm to 1.0 nm. These changes are mainly due to mesopore filling by metal particles, while micropore area and volume decrease only slightly.
- **Observations:** The reduction in mesopore volume suggests that active sites are primarily located in mesopores and on the external surface, supporting efficient reactant diffusion.

#### 6.1.4 Cobalt Dispersion and Surface Sites (CO Chemisorption)
- **Description:** CO chemisorption quantifies the amount of exposed metallic Co sites and cobalt dispersion, reflecting the catalyst’s active surface.
- **Findings:** The amount of adsorbed CO decreases from 112.9 to 98.5 μmol/g as CaO increases from 0 to 0.50 wt%, indicating reduced cobalt dispersion and fewer exposed metallic Co sites. The reduction degree also slightly decreases with CaO addition (from 74% to 71%).
- **Observations:** Higher CaO loading leads to lower metallic Co dispersion, correlating with increased Co₂C formation and altered catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.5 Morphology and Lattice Structure (HRTEM)
- **Description:** High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) provides direct visualization of particle morphology and lattice fringes, confirming phase coexistence.
- **Findings:** HRTEM images reveal lattice fringes of 2.25 Å and 2.21 Å at a 91° angle, characteristic of Co₂C (200) and (020) planes, alongside fcc Co (111) planes. The average cobalt particle size exceeds 10 nm, consistent with XRD estimates.
- **Observations:** The coexistence of metallic Co and Co₂C in close contact is visually confirmed, supporting the XRD findings and highlighting the structural evolution induced by CaO promotion.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction, typically expressed as a percentage.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard Fischer–Tropsch synthesis conditions (H₂/CO = 2, 3.0 MPa, 493 K, GHSV = 900 h⁻¹), the CO conversion values for 15Co–xCa/AC catalysts with varying CaO loadings were:
  - 0 wt% CaO: 64.3%
  - 0.05 wt% CaO: 56.2%
  - 0.10 wt% CaO: 49.0%
  - 0.50 wt% CaO: 24.7%
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing CaO content led to a progressive decrease in CO conversion. This trend is attributed to a reduction in the number of exposed metallic Co sites and lower cobalt dispersion, as confirmed by CO chemisorption and XRD analyses.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity represents the proportion of converted CO that forms CO₂, reflecting the extent of the water–gas shift and oxidation side reactions.
- **Experimental Data:** Reported CO₂ selectivity values were:
  - 0 wt% CaO: 1.5%
  - 0.05 wt% CaO: 1.4%
  - 0.10 wt% CaO: 0.7%
  - 0.50 wt% CaO: 1.3%
- **Observations and Trends:** CO₂ selectivity remained low across all catalysts, with a slight minimum at 0.10 wt% CaO, indicating that CaO promotion does not significantly enhance CO₂ formation.

#### 6.2.3 Alcohol Yield and Selectivity
- **Description:** Alcohol yield is the percentage of total product carbon present as alcohols, while selectivity refers to the fraction of converted CO forming alcohols.
- **Experimental Data:** The alcohol yield increased from 9.9% (0 wt% CaO) to 15.0% (0.10 wt% CaO), with selectivity to C₁–C₁₆ alcohols rising from 15.4% to 30.6% over the same range.
- **Observations and Trends:** Moderate CaO doping (0.10 wt%) significantly enhanced both alcohol yield and selectivity, while further CaO addition (0.50 wt%) did not provide additional benefit and reduced overall conversion.

#### 6.2.4 Hydrocarbon Selectivity (HC₅⁺ and HC₁–₄)
- **Description:** Selectivity to HC₅⁺ (C₅ and higher hydrocarbons) and HC₁–₄ (C₁–C₄ hydrocarbons) indicates the distribution of hydrocarbon products.
- **Experimental Data:** HC₅⁺ selectivity decreased from 45.3% (0 wt% CaO) to 38.8% (0.10 wt% CaO), while HC₁–₄ selectivity dropped from 37.7% to 29.9% over the same range.
- **Observations and Trends:** CaO addition suppressed hydrocarbon formation, especially for heavier fractions, favoring alcohol synthesis.

#### 6.2.5 Chain Growth Factor (α Factor)
- **Description:** The chain growth factor (α) reflects the probability of carbon chain propagation, derived from Anderson–Schulz–Flory (ASF) kinetics. Values closer to 1 indicate a higher likelihood of forming longer chains.
- **Experimental Data:** The α factor for alcohols, olefins, and paraffins was consistently around 0.66 ± 0.01 for all catalysts.
- **Observations and Trends:** Slight decreases in α with increasing CaO suggest mild inhibition of chain growth, but ASF distribution remains valid, supporting the observed product profiles.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring performance after 36 hours of reaction stabilization.
- **Experimental Data:** All performance metrics were reported after 36 h on stream, with no significant deactivation observed within this period.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalysts demonstrated stable operation under the tested conditions, with no explicit deactivation rates reported.

---

**Summary of Key Findings:**  
Moderate CaO promotion (0.10 wt%) on Co/AC catalysts optimizes the balance between metallic Co and Co₂C phases, resulting in enhanced alcohol yield and selectivity, suppressed hydrocarbon formation, and stable performance. Excessive CaO reduces available active sites, lowering CO conversion. The synergy between metallic Co and Co₂C, facilitated by the AC support and CaO promoter, is critical for efficient higher alcohol synthesis from syngas.
