### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
  where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the inlet and outlet CO flow rates, respectively, determined via gas chromatography.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions (220°C, 2.0 MPa, H₂/CO = 2:1, GHSV = 12,000 mL·g⁻¹·h⁻¹), CO conversion for Co/Al₂O₃-x%La catalysts increased with La content, peaking at an optimal dopant level (x = 5%), then declining at higher La ratios. For example, the undoped catalyst (x = 0) exhibited lower conversion, while the catalyst with 5% La showed the highest conversion.
- **Observations and Trends:** Moderate La doping enhances CO conversion, attributed to improved metal dispersion and reducibility. Excessive La content leads to diminished activity, likely due to over-coverage or phase segregation.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CO₂ selectivity decreased as La content increased. The undoped catalyst produced the highest CO₂ selectivity, while the 10% La-doped sample exhibited the lowest.
- **Observations and Trends:** Higher La levels suppress CO₂ formation, indicating a shift away from the water-gas shift reaction and favoring hydrocarbon synthesis.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CH₄ selectivity initially increased with La addition, reaching a maximum at intermediate doping (x = 1–5%), then decreased at higher La content.
- **Observations and Trends:** The trend mirrors CO conversion, suggesting that optimal La levels promote both activity and methane formation, while excessive La suppresses methane selectivity.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity represents the percentage of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with three or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_3^+ \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = 100 - \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}} + F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_6,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** C₃⁺ selectivity increased with La content, with the highest values observed for catalysts containing 5–10% La.
- **Observations and Trends:** La promotion favors longer-chain hydrocarbon formation, enhancing the yield of desired C₃⁺ products.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Deactivation Rate
