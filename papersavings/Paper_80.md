## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the conventional incipient wetness impregnation method used for preparing the 15Co–xGa/AC catalyst for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. The method emphasizes precise reproducibility and uniform metal distribution on the coconut shell‐derived activated carbon support.

#### Conventional Incipient Wetness Impregnation Method

Step 1: Catalyst Support Preparation  
• Activated carbon (AC) is obtained from coconut shells.  
• The AC is thoroughly washed using hot deionized water (approximately 10 repeated washes) to effectively remove impurities.  
• Post‑washing, the AC is subjected to an initial drying process at 100 °C for 12 hours.  
• Equipment used: stirring apparatus for washing, filtration systems, and a drying oven with precise temperature control.

Step 2: Precursor Mixing  
• A mixed aqueous solution is prepared by dissolving metal nitrates: Co(NO₃)₂ and Ga(NO₃)₃.  
• The weight percentage is controlled to achieve 15 wt% cobalt and Ga dopant levels ranging from 1.5 to 3.5 wt%.  
• The solution is then applied evenly to the pre-dried AC via impregnation, ensuring complete infiltration into the porous structure.

Step 3: Precipitation–Deposition  
• While no explicit precipitation–deposition step is reported, the homogeneous incorporation of metal ions during impregnation serves a similar purpose in ensuring uniform dispersion.

Step 4: Drying  
• After impregnation, the catalyst is dried again at 100 °C for 12 hours to remove residual moisture.

Step 5: Calcination and Post‑Treatment  
• The dried catalyst is calcined at 350 °C for 5 hours under an argon (Ar) atmosphere, converting the impregnated precursors to their oxide forms.  
• An additional reduction step is optionally performed at 450 °C for 5 hours in pure hydrogen (H₂) to activate the catalyst by converting Co₃O₄/CoO species to metallic Co, while modulating the Co²⁺/Co⁰ ratio.

Each step emphasizes controlled parameters such as temperature, duration, reagent concentrations, and equipment usage to ensure consistency and reproducibility, providing clear guidelines for replication by other researchers.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 X-ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Operational Description**: XRD analysis was performed to confirm the crystalline phases present in the catalyst. The primary goal was to detect Co₃O₄ and assess the impact of Ga doping through the observation of diffraction peak broadening, which is indicative of enhanced cobalt dispersion.
- **Instruments and Models**: Measurements were carried out using a Rigaku Ultima IV diffractometer.
- **Characterization Conditions**: The system utilized Cu Kα radiation (λ = 0.15406 nm) at a tube voltage of 40 kV and a current of 40 mA. The scan was performed over a 2θ range of 20°–70°.
- **Test Parameters**: A scanning speed of 10°/min was used; sample mounting followed calcination to ensure no additional pretreatment steps were necessary.
- **Key Notes**: Broadened diffraction peaks post-Ga addition suggest that Ga improves cobalt dispersion without introducing new crystalline phases.

### 4.2 Temperature Programmed Reduction (TPR)
- **Operational Description**: TPR was employed to investigate the reduction behavior of cobalt species in the catalyst. The method identifies three reduction steps: Co₃O₄ to CoO, CoO to Co, and a further process attributed to the support.
- **Instruments and Models**: A micro fixed-bed reactor integrated with a thermal conductivity detector (TCD) was used.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Approximately 0.1 g catalyst was pretreated in Ar at 150 °C for 0.5 h, cooled to 50 °C, and then subjected to a flow of 10% H₂/90% Ar while heating from 50 °C to 800 °C at a ramp rate of 10 °C/min.
- **Test Parameters**: Precise control of gas flow and temperature ramp was maintained to clearly resolve the reduction peaks.
- **Key Notes**: Calibration using a reference (CuO) helped ensure accurate integration of the reduction peaks.

### 4.3 H₂-Chemisorption
- **Operational Description**: This method quantified cobalt dispersion by measuring hydrogen adsorption on the catalyst.
- **Instruments and Models**: The analysis was conducted in a U-shaped tube reactor.
- **Characterization Conditions**: The catalyst (~0.20 g) was reduced at 450 °C for 5 hours in pure H₂, followed by evacuation at the same temperature for 2 hours. Measurements were performed at 150 °C.
- **Test Parameters**: Hydrogen uptake was recorded, assuming a one-to-one stoichiometry between hydrogen atoms and surface cobalt atoms.
- **Key Notes**: The test provides insights into the effect of Ga doping on available active sites.

### 4.4 High-Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy (HRTEM)
- **Operational Description**: HRTEM was used to image the catalyst at the nanoscale, revealing lattice fringes corresponding to CoO and metallic Co.
- **Instruments and Models**: Analysis was performed on a FEI Tecnai F30 electron microscope operating at 300 kV.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Catalysts were reduced at 450 °C for 5 hours under H₂, ultrasonically dispersed in ethanol for 15 minutes, and deposited onto carbon-coated copper grids.
- **Test Parameters**: Exposure settings ensured clear resolution of lattice spacings for accurate phase identification.
- **Key Notes**: Imaging confirms the coexistence of Co²⁺ and Co⁰ species.

### 4.5 X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)
- **Operational Description**: XPS analysis examined the surface composition, particularly monitoring the Co²⁺/(Co²⁺ + Co⁰) ratio as affected by Ga content.
- **Instruments and Models**: An Omicron Sphera II spectrometer with an Al Kα X-ray source (hv = 1486.6 eV) was used.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Samples were pressed into thin disks and reduced at 450 °C for 5 hours under H₂ prior to analysis under high-vacuum conditions.
- **Test Parameters**: Although specific scan ranges and pass energies were not detailed, binding energies were calibrated using the C 1s peak at 284.6 eV.
- **Key Notes**: This method effectively reflects the electronic interactions and surface modifications induced by Ga doping, despite the omission of certain instrumental details.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structural Dispersion
- **Description:** The crystal phase composition and dispersion of cobalt species were analyzed using X-ray diffraction (XRD) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM). XRD identifies crystalline phases and assesses peak broadening, which reflects particle size and dispersion.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns of the as-calcined 15Co–xGa/AC catalysts revealed Co₃O₄ as the sole crystalline phase, with characteristic peaks at 2θ = 36.8°, 44.7°, and 59.3°. Upon Ga doping, these peaks became noticeably broadened, indicating enhanced cobalt dispersion. No diffraction peaks corresponding to Co–Ga alloys or Co₂C were detected, even after reaction.
- **Observations:** The broadening of Co₃O₄ peaks with increasing Ga content demonstrates that Ga promotes finer dispersion of cobalt species. The absence of new phases confirms that Ga does not induce alloy or carbide formation under the synthesis and reaction conditions.

#### 6.1.2 Reducibility and Metal–Support Interaction
- **Description:** Temperature-programmed reduction (TPR) was employed to evaluate the reducibility of cobalt oxides and the influence of Ga on reduction behavior.
- **Findings:** TPR profiles exhibited three main reduction peaks: the first for Co₃O₄ → CoO, the second for CoO → Co⁰ (overlapping with Ga₂O₃ reduction in Ga-doped samples), and a broad third peak (400–600 °C) attributed to AC support gasification. Ga addition shifted the second reduction peak to higher temperatures and reduced its intensity, indicating that Ga retards the CoO → Co⁰ transition.
- **Observations:** The delayed reduction of CoO in the presence of Ga suggests a stronger metal–support interaction, resulting in a higher proportion of unreduced Co²⁺ species.

#### 6.1.3 Cobalt Dispersion
- **Description:** H₂–chemisorption was used to quantify the dispersion of cobalt on the catalyst surface, which is critical for catalytic activity.
- **Findings:** Cobalt dispersion decreased from 6.2% to 3.7% as Ga content increased from 1.5 wt% to 3.5 wt%.
- **Observations:** While moderate Ga doping enhances dispersion (as seen in XRD), excessive Ga leads to agglomeration or coverage effects, reducing accessible cobalt sites.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Chemical States and Elemental Composition
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) provided insights into the surface oxidation states of cobalt and the effect of Ga doping on surface composition.
- **Findings:** XPS analysis showed an increase in the Co²⁺/(Co²⁺ + Co⁰) surface ratio from 43.8% (Ga-free) to 67.1% (3.5 wt% Ga). No shifts in binding energies for Co or Ga were observed, and C 1s spectra confirmed the absence of Co₂C.
- **Observations:** The higher Co²⁺ fraction with increasing Ga content is consistent with TPR results and supports the formation of dual active sites (Co²⁺–Co⁰). The lack of binding energy shifts or new peaks further confirms that no Co–Ga alloy or carbide phases are present.

#### 6.1.5 Morphology and Lattice Structure
- **Description:** HRTEM was utilized to directly observe the morphology and lattice structure of the catalysts.
- **Findings:** HRTEM images revealed lattice fringes of 0.213 nm (CoO, (200) plane) and 0.204 nm (metallic Co, (111) plane), confirming the coexistence of Co²⁺ and Co⁰ species within the same catalyst particles.
- **Observations:** The simultaneous presence of both lattice spacings substantiates the dual-site hypothesis, which is linked to enhanced higher alcohol selectivity.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction, typically expressed as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (mol\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
  where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the molar flow rates of CO at the reactor inlet and outlet, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions (220 °C, 3.0 MPa, GHSV = 4000 mL/(g·h)), the CO conversion values for the 15Co–xGa/AC catalysts are:
  - 15Co/AC (0% Ga): 25.9 mol%
  - 15Co–1.5Ga/AC: 28.9 mol%
  - 15Co–2.0Ga/AC: 21.8 mol%
  - 15Co–2.5Ga/AC: 13.1 mol%
  - 15Co–3.0Ga/AC: 10.6 mol%
  - 15Co–3.5Ga/AC: 5.2 mol%
- **Observations and Trends:** A slight increase in CO conversion is observed at low Ga loading (1.5 wt%), but further increases in Ga content result in a marked decline. This trend is attributed to Ga-induced changes in cobalt dispersion and reduction behavior, which reduce the number of available metallic Co active sites at higher Ga levels.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO that forms CO₂, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (mol\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Reported CO₂ selectivity values are:
  - 15Co/AC: 81.5 mol%
  - 15Co–1.5Ga/AC: 77.2 mol%
  - 15Co–2.0Ga/AC: 74.3 mol%
  - 15Co–2.5Ga/AC: 70.1 mol%
  - 15Co–3.0Ga/AC: 69.0 mol%
  - 15Co–3.5Ga/AC: 67.8 mol%
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing Ga content consistently lowers CO₂ selectivity, indicating suppression of the water–gas shift and total oxidation pathways. This effect correlates with a higher surface Co²⁺/(Co²⁺ + Co⁰) ratio, as revealed by XPS.

#### 6.2.3 Alcohol Selectivity (SROH)
- **Description:** Alcohol selectivity (SROH) is defined as the molar percentage of alcohols in the total converted CO, representing the catalyst’s efficiency for higher alcohol synthesis.
- **Experimental Data:** SROH values under standard conditions:
  - 15Co/AC: 17.0 mol%
  - 15Co–1.5Ga/AC: 20.2 mol%
  - 15Co–2.0Ga/AC: 24.7 mol%
  - 15Co–2.5Ga/AC: 30.3 mol%
  - 15Co–3.0Ga/AC: 28.7 mol%
  - 15Co–3.5Ga/AC: 27.1 mol%
- **Observations and Trends:** SROH increases significantly with Ga addition, peaking at 2.5 wt% Ga. This enhancement is attributed to the formation of dual Co²⁺–Co⁰ active sites, which favor associative CO adsorption and alcohol formation. Excessive Ga, however, reduces overall conversion without further improving selectivity.

#### 6.2.4 Product Distribution (C₂⁺–OH/ROH Ratio)
- **Description:** The C₂⁺–OH/ROH ratio indicates the fraction of higher alcohols (C₂ and above) among total alcohol products, reflecting chain growth efficiency.
- **Experimental Data:** For 15Co/AC, the C₂⁺–OH/ROH ratio is 45.6%; for 15Co–2.5Ga/AC, it decreases to 24.5%.
- **Observations and Trends:** Ga doping shifts the alcohol product distribution toward shorter-chain alcohols, as evidenced by the declining C₂⁺–OH/ROH ratio with increasing Ga content.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time at steady-state conditions.
- **Experimental Data:** All performance data were collected after 20 hours on stream, with no significant deactivation observed during this period.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalysts exhibit stable performance under the tested conditions, with product selectivity and conversion remaining consistent after initial stabilization.

**Summary:**  
Moderate Ga doping in 15Co–xGa/AC catalysts enhances alcohol selectivity and suppresses CO₂ formation, with optimal performance at 2.5 wt% Ga. The improvements are linked to the formation of dual Co²⁺–Co⁰ active sites and modified surface properties, though excessive Ga reduces CO conversion and chain growth. The catalysts demonstrate stable operation over extended reaction times.
