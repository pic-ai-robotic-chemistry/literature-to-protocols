## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The synthesis of the KCuFe/mAl₂O₃ catalyst and its promoted variants (Ni-, Mn-, Mg-, and Co-KCuFe/mAl₂O₃) relies on a sequential incipient wetness impregnation protocol. Detailed steps extracted from the article are outlined below, ensuring reproducibility and clear operational guidance.

#### • Iron Precursor Impregnation
1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   - Dissolve the iron precursor (targeting 25 wt % Fe) in a precise ethanol/water mixture.
   - Equipment: Standard glass reactor with magnetic stirring.
   - Observation: Homogeneous solution formation, indicated by uniform coloration.
   
2. Precipitation–Deposition  
   - Upon impregnation onto the mesoporous alumina (initial surface area ≈430 m²/g), a precipitation stage ensures even deposition of the metal ions.
   - Note: The method emphasizes controlled deposition before initiating drying.

#### • Cu Impregnation and Intermediate Treatment
3. Wet Impregnation of Cu  
   - Introduce a 0.5 wt % Cu solution onto the dried iron-loaded alumina.
   - Conditions: The impregnated system should display a modest color change confirming precursor interaction.
   
4. Drying and Calcination  
   - Dry the material at 120 °C (duration as optimized by the procedure).
   - Calcination Parameters: 400 °C for 4 hours with a ramp rate of 1 °C/min.
   - Equipment: Controlled atmosphere furnace.

#### • K and Promoter Incorporation
5. Potassium Addition  
   - Wet impregnate the catalyst with a potassium precursor to achieve 1.0 wt % K.
   - Follow with a similar drying/calcination cycle (as above) to integrate the K seamlessly.
   
6. Promoter Impregnation (Ni, Mn, Mg, or Co)  
   - Introduce the promoter at 1.0 wt % via wet impregnation onto the established KCuFe/mAl₂O₃ base.
   - Subsequent Processing: Repeat drying at 120 °C and calcination at 400 °C for 4 hours (ramp rate: 1 °C/min).
   
Each step is designed to ensure uniform dispersion and effective metal-support interaction. The sequential impregnation followed by standardized drying and calcination conditions promotes consistent catalyst structure, as corroborated by observations like color transitions and precipitate formation. This comprehensive procedure guarantees that another researcher can replicate the synthesis with precision, maintaining the catalyst’s reproducibility and performance under Fischer–Tropsch operational conditions.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

This section details the systematic procedures for preparing the catalyst precursors via incipient wetness impregnation. The process is organized into three distinct steps: weighing solid reagents, transferring liquid solutions, and mixing to achieve homogeneous precursor solutions. The procedures are defined separately for the base catalyst (KCuFe/mAl₂O₃) and its promoted variants (Ni-, Mn-, Mg-, and Co-KCuFe/mAl₂O₃).

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

##### 3.1.1.1 KCuFe/mAl₂O₃  
- **Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O**: Weighed to achieve a 25 wt% Fe loading on the mesoporous alumina.  
- **Cu(NO₃)₂·3H₂O**: Weighed to provide a 0.5 wt% Cu content as required for the impregnation step.  
- **KNO₃**: Weighed for incorporation at a 1.0 wt% level, establishing the base KCuFe composition.

##### 3.1.1.2 Promoted KCuFe/mAl₂O₃  
- **Ni(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed to achieve a 1.0 wt% Ni addition.  
- **Mn Nitrate**: Weighed for promoter incorporation at 1.0 wt%, with additional trials also considering 0.5 and 2.0 wt% loadings for dopant effect studies.  
- **Mg Nitrate**: Weighed to introduce 1.0 wt% Mg.  
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed for a 1.0 wt% Co addition.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

##### 3.1.2.1 KCuFe/mAl₂O₃  
- **Ethanol/Water Mixture**: Poured into the vessel containing the Fe precursor to dissolve it effectively prior to impregnation onto the alumina support.

##### 3.1.2.2 Promoted KCuFe/mAl₂O₃  
- **Ethanol/Water Mixture**: Transferred using standard glassware into the container to prepare dilute promoter nitrate solutions for wet impregnation.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.1 KCuFe/mAl₂O₃  
- **Fe Precursor Solution**: Mixed by magnetic stirring to ensure complete dissolution and uniform distribution over the mesoporous alumina support prior to drying and calcination.

##### 3.1.3.2 Promoted KCuFe/mAl₂O₃  
- **Promoter Solution**: Stirred continuously during wet impregnation to secure a homogeneous solution, setting the stage for subsequent drying and calcination that finalize the catalyst synthesis.

This organized approach underpins the reproducibility of the catalyst preparation, ensuring that each synthesis step—from precise weighing to controlled mixing—is clearly delineated for both the base and promoted variants.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: KCuFe/mAl₂O₃ Base Catalyst

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**:  
  • Iron (III) nitrate nonahydrate (to yield 25 wt % Fe)  
  • Ethanol and deionized water (solvent mixture)  
  • Mesoporous alumina (support, ~430 m²/g)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Dissolution at ambient temperature under stirring until a homogeneous solution is obtained  
  • Impregnation by incipient wetness onto the mesoporous alumina  
- **Process**:  
  1. Dissolve the measured iron nitrate in an ethanol/water mixture ensuring complete solubilization.  
  2. Load the solution onto the alumina support; allow for uniform distribution.  
  3. Dry the impregnated sample at 120 °C to remove solvents.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  • Ensure the iron precursor is evenly distributed to maximize contact with the support.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly and Intermediate Processing
- **Reagents**:  
  • Cu precursor (cupric nitrate trihydrate for 0.5 wt % Cu)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Wet impregnation with gentle stirring (specific mixing conditions adjusted as per equipment availability)  
- **Process**:  
  1. Impregnate the Fe-loaded support with the Cu solution.  
  2. Dry at 120 °C, then calcine at 400 °C for 4 hours with a ramp rate of 1 °C/min to form the mixed oxide framework.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  • Maintain consistent drying and calcination to prevent precursor cross-contamination.

##### Step 3: Assembly of K and Promoted Variants
- **Reagents**:  
  • Potassium nitrate (to achieve 1.0 wt % K)  
  • For promoted variants: 1.0 wt % of Ni, Mn, Mg, or Co nitrate (Mn variants further explored at 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 wt %)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Sequential incipient wetness impregnation, with intermediate drying at 120 °C and calcination at 400 °C for 4 hours (ramp rate 1 °C/min).  
- **Process**:  
  1. First, impregnate with K solution, ensuring proper distribution before drying and calcination.  
  2. Subsequently, add the chosen promoter under identical processing steps to obtain the final catalyst.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  • Follow drying and calcination consecutively to lock in the layered structure and minimize cross-contamination.  
  • Record and control temperature, duration, and ramp rate for reproducibility.

This stepwise protocol, from raw precursor dissolution to final promoted catalyst formation, ensures reproducibility and provides a clear pathway for replicating the catalytic synthesis in Fischer–Tropsch experiments.

- **Definition:** The activation temperature is critical for achieving an effective in situ reduction of the catalyst precursor, ensuring the formation of the active phase necessary for optimal Fischer–Tropsch performance.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst is subjected to an in situ reduction at 380 °C for 16 hours in a fixed-bed tubular reactor housed within a vertical furnace.
- **Observations:** Operating at 380 °C promotes complete reduction without compromising the catalyst’s integrity, thereby facilitating the development of well-dispersed active sites.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** Activation pressure influences the interaction between the activation gas and the catalyst, potentially enhancing the adsorption and reduction processes.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Following the reduction step, the reactor is pressurized to 300 psi prior to the introduction of the reaction gas mixture.
- **Observations:** The controlled pressurization ensures that the catalyst remains in an optimal state for the subsequent Fischer–Tropsch reaction, balancing reduction efficiency with gas diffusion.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
- **Definition:** The gas composition plays an essential role in achieving the required reduction and in setting the stage for the reaction phase.
- **Experimental Conditions:** During the reduction, hydrogen is used—flowing at 30 mL/min—to effectively reduce the catalyst precursor. Subsequently, the reaction utilizes a syngas mixture with an H₂/CO ratio of 1.25.
- **Observations:** The hydrogen-rich environment ensures thorough reduction, while the specific syngas composition is crucial for achieving desired Fischer–Tropsch product distributions.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Space-Time Velocity
- **Definition:** The space-time velocity (STV) quantifies the gas flow relative to the catalyst volume, ensuring uniform gas–solid contact during activation.
- **Experimental Conditions:** A gas hourly space velocity of 2000 h⁻¹ is maintained, ensuring efficient exposure of catalyst surfaces to the reactive gases.
- **Observations:** This STV supports effective mass transfer and thorough activation, providing consistency across different catalyst formulations.

#### 5.1.5 Activation Time
- **Definition:** The activation time determines the duration over which the catalyst is exposed to the reducing environment, ensuring complete transformation of the precursor.
- **Experimental Conditions:** A dedicated reduction period of 16 hours is applied.
- **Observations:** This duration is optimized to prevent over-reduction or sintering, thereby maintaining catalyst stability and promoting enhanced performance during the subsequent Fischer–Tropsch reaction.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal phases of the catalysts were analyzed using wide-angle powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) with a Bruker Advance D8 diffractometer (Cu Kα radiation, 2θ = 20°–80°). XRD identifies the presence and transformation of iron oxide and mixed oxide phases, which are critical for Fischer–Tropsch (FT) activity.
- **Findings:** After sequential impregnation and calcination at 400 °C, the base KCuFe/mAl₂O₃ catalyst retained the mesoporous alumina structure, with iron present primarily as Fe₂O₃. Promoter addition did not significantly alter the main crystal phases, though the formation of mixed oxides (e.g., cobalt aluminate in Co-promoted samples) was observed.
- **Observations:** The preservation of mesoporosity and the presence of iron oxide phases indicate successful incorporation of active components without major phase loss. Promoters influence crystallite dispersion and may form additional mixed phases, affecting catalytic behavior.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Textural properties were determined by N₂ adsorption–desorption isotherms using BET (surface area) and BJH (pore size distribution) analyses (ASAP2020 instrument). These parameters influence reactant diffusion and catalyst accessibility.
- **Findings:** The mesoporous alumina support exhibited a high surface area (430 m²/g), pore volume (0.9 cm³/g), and pore diameter (7.0 nm). After KCuFe impregnation, these values decreased to 344 m²/g, 0.4 cm³/g, and 5.0 nm, respectively. Promoted catalysts showed further slight decreases, with surface areas of 305–337 m²/g, pore volumes of 0.35–0.4 cm³/g, and pore diameters of 4.4–4.8 nm.
- **Observations:** The mesoporous structure was retained post-impregnation, ensuring effective reactant diffusion. The minor reduction in surface area and pore volume is attributed to metal loading rather than structural collapse.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition and Valence States
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to determine surface elemental composition and iron oxidation states. High-resolution Fe 2p spectra provide insight into the chemical environment of iron, while survey scans quantify promoter and support elements.
- **Findings:** The Fe 2p₃/₂ binding energy for all promoted catalysts was ~711 eV, characteristic of Fe³⁺ (Fe₂O₃), with satellite features at ~719 eV and ~733 eV confirming this state. The Mn-promoted catalyst exhibited the highest surface Fe content, while Mg-promoted samples had about 40% less surface Fe. XPS also confirmed the presence of promoter elements (Ni, Mn, Mg, Co) and support (Al).
- **Observations:** Variations in surface Fe concentration correlate with promoter type, influencing FT performance. The high Fe content in Mn-promoted catalysts is linked to enhanced activity and selectivity.

#### 6.1.4 Reducibility and Iron Speciation
- **Description:** Hydrogen temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) and Fe K-edge X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) analyses were employed to assess iron reducibility and speciation, which are crucial for catalytic function.
- **Findings:** H₂-TPR revealed two main reduction peaks: Fe₂O₃ to Fe₃O₄ (290–350 °C) and Fe₃O₄ to FeO/Fe (400–500 °C). Promoter addition lowered the second reduction peak by ~70 °C, with the order of reducibility: Ni ≈ Mn > Mg > Co. XANES confirmed these trends, showing the highest degree of Fe reduction in Ni- and Mn-promoted catalysts.
- **Observations:** Enhanced reducibility, especially in Mn-promoted samples, is associated with improved FT activity and selectivity, as more active iron phases are generated under reaction conditions.

#### 6.1.5 Elemental Distribution and Promoter Effects
- **Description:** XPS survey scans and high-resolution spectra were used to evaluate the distribution of promoters and their interaction with the support and active phase.
- **Findings:** The sequential impregnation method ensured uniform promoter distribution. XPS indicated possible formation of mixed oxides (e.g., cobalt aluminate) in Co-promoted catalysts and mixed Fe–Mn phases in Mn-promoted samples.
- **Observations:** The nature and distribution of promoters directly impact surface composition and the electronic environment of iron, thereby modulating FT catalytic performance.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. It is calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
  where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the molar flow rates of CO entering and exiting the reactor, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions (270 °C, 300 psi, GHSV 2000 h⁻¹, H₂/CO = 1.25), the Mn-promoted KCuFe/mAl₂O₃ catalyst exhibited a marginally higher CO conversion than the unpromoted base catalyst. The Ni-promoted catalyst showed an 18% decrease in CO conversion relative to the base catalyst. Mg- and Co-promoted catalysts demonstrated intermediate conversions, with Mg outperforming Ni and Co but not Mn.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn promotion enhances CO conversion, while Ni addition suppresses it. The base catalyst benefits from K and Cu addition, but further improvement is most pronounced with Mn.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, indicating the extent of the water–gas shift reaction:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The Mn-promoted catalyst reduced CO₂ selectivity by approximately 5% compared to the base catalyst. The Ni-promoted catalyst also showed a decrease (about 2.5%), while Mg promotion resulted in CO₂ selectivity similar to the base catalyst.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn and Ni suppress CO₂ formation, indicating reduced water–gas shift activity, whereas Mg has minimal effect.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The Ni-promoted catalyst exhibited the highest CH₄ selectivity (~29%). The Co-promoted catalyst increased CH₄ selectivity by ~5% over the base, while Mn promotion led to a ~2% decrease. Mg promotion resulted in lower CH₄ selectivity than Ni and Co.
- **Observations and Trends:** Ni strongly favors methane formation, while Mn and Mg suppress it, shifting product distribution toward heavier hydrocarbons.

#### 6.2.4 C₅⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₅⁺ selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with five or more carbon atoms, a key indicator of chain growth:
  \[
  \text{C}_5^+ \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = 100 - \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}} + F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_6,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The base KCuFe/mAl₂O₃ catalyst achieved ~77% C₅⁺ selectivity, which increased to ~81% with Mn promotion. Mg, Ni, and Co promoters resulted in lower C₅⁺ selectivities, with Ni being the least effective.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn is the most effective promoter for maximizing C₅⁺ selectivity, correlating with enhanced chain growth.

#### 6.2.5 Chain Growth Factor (α Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor quantifies the probability of chain propagation versus termination, influencing the distribution of hydrocarbon products. Higher α values favor longer-chain hydrocarbons.
- **Experimental Data:** Mn and Co promoters increased α, as evidenced by higher C₅⁺ selectivity and heavier product distribution. Ni decreased α, favoring methane.
- **Observations and Trends:** The choice of promoter directly impacts α, with Mn providing the most significant improvement.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst deactivation rate is determined by the decline in CO conversion over time:
  \[
  \text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}} \quad (\text{h}^{-1})
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The Mn-promoted catalyst exhibited the slowest deactivation, maintaining higher activity over time. Other promoters, particularly Ni, showed faster deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn enhances catalyst stability, attributed to improved iron dispersion and carbide stabilization.

#### 6.2.7 Olefin/Paraffin Ratio
- **Description:** The olefin/paraffin (O/P) ratio indicates the relative production of unsaturated to saturated hydrocarbons, relevant for fuel quality.
- **Experimental Data:** The O/P ratio increased from 0.71 (base catalyst) to 0.85 with Mn promotion, while Co and Ni promoters reduced this ratio.
- **Observations and Trends:** Mn and Mg favor olefin production, while Ni and Co shift selectivity toward paraffins.

---
**Summary:**  
Mn is the most effective promoter for KCuFe/mAl₂O₃, enhancing CO conversion, C₅⁺ selectivity, α factor, and catalyst stability, while suppressing methane and CO₂ formation. Ni increases methane selectivity and deactivation, while Mg and Co offer intermediate performance. The observed trends are closely linked to changes in surface iron content, reducibility, and promoter–support interactions.
