## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the synthesis procedures for both the cobalt–manganese carbide (CoMnC) catalyst and the hydrophobic PDVB support, with each method described step‐by‐step to allow precise reproducibility.

#### Co‑precipitation Method for CoMnC Catalyst

1. • Precursor Mixing:  
   – Prepare Solution A by dissolving 29.4 g of Co(NO₃)₂•6H₂O and 17.9 g of 50 wt% Mn(NO₃)₂ in 75 mL of deionized water.  
   – Prepare Solution B with 24.0 g of Na₂CO₃ in 75 mL of deionized water.  
   – Under vigorous stirring at 30°C, add both solutions dropwise into a beaker containing 200 mL of deionized water while maintaining pH ~8.

2. • Precipitation–Deposition:  
   – Continue stirring for 1 hour to promote homogeneous precipitation–deposition of metal carbonates, as indicated by the formation of a distinct precipitate.

3. • Aging:  
   – Allow the suspension to age at room temperature for 2 hours to complete the deposition process.

4. • Drying:  
   – Centrifuge and wash the precipitate with deionized water. Dry the collected material in a drying oven at 100°C for 10 hours.

5. • Calcination:  
   – Calcine the dried precursor in a furnace at 330°C for 3 hours to achieve the active CoMnC phase, ensuring the intrinsic catalyst structure is preserved.

#### Free‑Radical Polymerization Method for PDVB Synthesis

1. • Initiation and Polymerization:  
   – Mix divinylbenzene (DVB) with azobis isobutyronitrile (AIBN) as the radical initiator.  
   – Transfer the mixture to a Teflon‑lined autoclave and polymerize at 100°C for 24 hours; for nanoporous PDVB variants (PDVB-DMF or PDVB-EA), incorporate solvents DMF or EA to tailor pore structure.

2. • Post‑processing:  
   – Wash the resulting PDVB with methanol to remove unreacted monomers and residual solvents.  
   – Dry the polymer at 100°C overnight.

A comparative analysis indicates that the co‑precipitation method is both cost-effective and scalable, with simpler equipment needs compared to the controlled autoclave setup required for PDVB synthesis. The precise reporting of reagent amounts, numerical values for temperatures and durations, and sequential processing steps ensures that these procedures can be replicated accurately in independent studies.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

##### 3.1.1.1 CoMnC/PDVB catalyst system  
- **Co(NO₃)₂•6H₂O**: Weighed 29.4 g using an analytical balance.  
- **Mn(NO₃)₂ (50 wt%)**: Weighed 17.9 g with precision appropriate for catalyst synthesis.  
- **Na₂CO₃**: Weighed 24.0 g to be used for the precipitation step.  
- **Divinylbenzene**: Weighed 10 g for the polymer precursor in PDVB synthesis.  
- **AIBN**: Weighed 0.5 g and set aside as the free‑radical initiator for polymerization.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

##### 3.1.2.1 CoMnC/PDVB catalyst system  
- **Deionized water for Solution A**: Poured 75 mL into a beaker containing the metal nitrate precursors to prepare the CoMnC precursor solution.  
- **Deionized water for Solution B**: Poured an additional 75 mL into a separate vessel to dissolve Na₂CO₃, ensuring separate solution preparation.  
- **Solvent for PDVB synthesis (DMF or EA)**: Transferred 50 g using a graduated cylinder into the reaction vessel, where the divinylbenzene and AIBN are dissolved under stirring.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.1 CoMnC/PDVB catalyst system  
- **Metal precursor mixture**: The Co(NO₃)₂•6H₂O and Mn(NO₃)₂ solution (Solution A) and the Na₂CO₃ solution (Solution B) are combined dropwise into a reaction beaker while stirring vigorously at room temperature for 1 hour, ensuring a controlled co‑precipitation environment.  
- **Polymer precursor solution**: The divinylbenzene and AIBN in DMF or EA are stirred continuously for 1 hour at room temperature to form a homogeneous reaction mixture prior to autoclave polymerization at 100°C.  
- **Final CoMnC/PDVB mixture**: After individual preparation, the CoMnC catalyst and PDVB promoter are physically mixed until a uniform distribution is achieved, ensuring reproducibility in catalyst performance during subsequent CO hydrogenation testing.

This detailed, catalyst‑specific grouping—addressing weighing, liquid transfer, and mixing—ensures clarity and reproducibility in synthesizing the CoMnC/PDVB catalyst system for Fischer–Tropsch experiments while incorporating precise quantitative details provided in the experimental protocol and QA responses.

### 3.2 Detailed Synthesis Process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: CoMnC Catalyst

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  • 29.4 g Co(NO₃)₂•6H₂O  
  • 17.9 g of 50 wt% Mn(NO₃)₂  
  • 24.0 g Na₂CO₃  
  • Deionized water  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Temperature: 30 °C  
  • Stirring: Vigorous for dissolution and pH control  
  • Target pH: ~8  
- **Process**:  
  1. Dissolve 29.4 g Co(NO₃)₂•6H₂O and 17.9 g Mn(NO₃)₂ separately in 75 mL deionized water while stirring at 30 °C for 1 hour.  
  2. In parallel, dissolve 24.0 g Na₂CO₃ in 75 mL deionized water.  
  3. Add the Na₂CO₃ solution dropwise to the mixed metal nitrate solution under vigorous stirring while monitoring the pH, ensuring it remains around 8.  
  4. Continue stirring for an additional hour and then allow the mixture to age at 30 °C for 2 hours.

##### Step 2: Purification  
- **Process**:  
  • Subject the precipitate to centrifugation followed by thorough washing with deionized water to remove residual ions.  
  • (Centrifugation speeds and durations are managed according to standard laboratory protocols.)

##### Step 3: Drying & Calcination  
- **Drying**:  
  • Dry the purified material in an oven at 100 °C overnight, ensuring stable temperature maintenance.  
- **Calcination**:  
  • Calcine the dried precursor at 330 °C for 3 hours in air, with a controlled ramp rate as per furnace settings.

##### Step 4: Activation  
- **Process**:  
  • Activate the calcined catalyst by reduction under a hydrogen flow (30 mL/min) at 300 °C for 5 hours to transform the oxide to the carbide phase during subsequent CO hydrogenation.

#### Catalyst Preparation [2]: PDVB Synthesis and Assembly

##### Step 1: PDVB Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  • 10 g Divinylbenzene (DVB)  
  • 0.5 g AIBN (radical initiator)  
  • Selected solvent (for nanoporous PDVB, use 50 g DMF or EA; for nonporous PDVB, polymerize directly)  
- **Process**:  
  1. Mix DVB and AIBN; for nonporous PDVB, conduct free-radical polymerization in an autoclave at 100 °C for 24 hours.  
  2. For nanoporous PDVB (PDVB-DMF or PDVB-EA), dissolve the mixture in the solvent and heat at 100 °C for 12 hours.

##### Step 2: Physical Mixing  
- **Process**:  
  • Combine the CoMnC catalyst with PDVB at weight ratios of 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 to assess performance effects.  
  • Ensure thorough physical mixing to promote optimal dehydration during CO hydrogenation.

**Key Steps & Caution**: Maintain precise temperature and pH control, particularly during the co-precipitation and aging steps, to ensure reproducibility and optimal catalyst formation.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature is critical for reducing the CoMn oxide precursor and converting it into the active carbide phase of the CoMnC catalyst.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The catalyst pre‐reaction treatment is performed at 300 °C in a fixed-bed flow reactor.  
- **Observations:** This temperature is optimized to ensure complete reduction without excessive heat that could lead to sintering. The transformation at 300 °C is verified by the catalyst’s consistent performance during Fischer–Tropsch testing.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** Activation pressure influences gas–solid interactions during the reduction process.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The activation process is carried out under atmospheric pressure (0.1 MPa), ensuring a controlled environment for the in‑situ reduction step.  
- **Observations:** Operating at ambient pressure facilitates a reproducible reduction without apparent catalyst agglomeration, which supports stable long-term performance.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
- **Definition:** The activation gas composition is essential for achieving the desired chemical transformation of the catalyst precursor.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** A pure hydrogen (H₂) atmosphere is utilized during the catalyst activation step, ensuring that the CoMn oxide is fully reduced into its active carbide form.  
- **Observations:** The exclusive use of H₂ prevents undesired side reactions such as over‐carbiding, ensuring effective metal reduction and optimal catalyst surface preparation.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Gas Flow Rate and Time
- **Definition:** The flow rate of the activation gas determines the contact time and reaction kinetics, while the activation time ensures the reduction process reaches completion.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The hydrogen gas is introduced at a flow rate of 20 mL/min during the activation, and the treatment is maintained for 5 hours.  
- **Observations:** This duration is sufficient to achieve complete reduction of the catalyst precursor, as demonstrated by the stable catalyst activity and selectivity observed during prolonged Fischer–Tropsch reaction tests.

The detailed activation protocol—employing a 300 °C treatment in pure H₂ at atmospheric pressure with a controlled gas flow—ensures that the CoMnC catalyst is properly pre-treated, leading to enhanced CO conversion and high reliability in subsequent Fischer–Tropsch synthesis experiments.

### 5.2 Reaction

- **Definition:** The reaction temperature is the controlled thermal condition under which the CO hydrogenation takes place. It directly influences the kinetics of reactant activation and product formation in Fischer-Tropsch synthesis.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structural Stability
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase composition of the catalysts were analyzed using X-ray diffraction (XRD), with phase identification referenced to standard PDF patterns for Co₂C and CoO. This analysis is critical for confirming the formation and stability of the active carbide phase under reaction conditions.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns of the as-synthesized CoMn oxide and steady-state CoMnC after 20 hours of syngas conversion confirm the presence of CoMnC phases. Comparison of XRD patterns for CoMnC and CoMnC/PDVB catalysts at various reaction periods reveals negligible differences, indicating that the physical mixing with hydrophobic PDVB does not alter the catalyst’s structure. After water treatment at 250°C, the CoMnC/PDVB catalyst maintains its carbide phase, while bare CoMnC shows oxidation to CoO.
- **Observations:** The structural integrity of CoMnC is preserved in the presence of PDVB, supporting the conclusion that the hydrophobic promoter does not chemically modify the active sites.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption isotherms (BET analysis) were employed to determine the surface area and pore volume of the catalysts and PDVB promoters. These parameters are essential for understanding reactant diffusion and accessibility to active sites.
- **Findings:** Nanoporous PDVB-EA exhibits a high BET surface area of 488.2 m²/g and a pore volume of 0.74 cm³/g, while nonporous PDVB has a surface area below 5 m²/g and negligible pore volume. The CoMnC/PDVB composite inherits the porosity characteristics of the PDVB component.
- **Observations:** The substantial difference in surface area and porosity between nanoporous and nonporous PDVB highlights the role of solvent choice during PDVB synthesis. Enhanced porosity facilitates improved mass transport, contributing to superior catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Microstructure
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were used to examine the morphology and microstructure of the catalyst and promoter particles. These techniques provide insight into particle distribution and physical stability.
- **Findings:** SEM images reveal tightly packed, disordered mixtures of CoMnC and PDVB, with intergranular distances ranging from nanometers to micrometers. TEM analysis confirms that the morphology of CoMnC remains unchanged after reaction and physical separation from PDVB.
- **Observations:** The uniform distribution and physical stability of the catalyst and promoter granules ensure consistent catalytic activity and prevent agglomeration or channeling during operation.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Hydrophobicity
- **Description:** Water contact angle measurements were performed to assess the hydrophobicity of PDVB, which is relevant for water management during CO hydrogenation.
- **Findings:** PDVB exhibits a water contact angle of 86°, confirming its hydrophobic nature. Increasing the content of hydrophilic comonomers reduces the contact angle, demonstrating tunable surface properties.
- **Observations:** The hydrophobicity of PDVB is crucial for promoting rapid water desorption from the catalyst surface, thereby minimizing water-induced inhibition of CO adsorption.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis coupled with mass spectrometry and differential scanning calorimetry (TG–MS/DSC) was used to evaluate the thermal stability of PDVB under reaction conditions.
- **Findings:** PDVB remains stable with no detectable weight loss or decomposition at 250°C. Decomposition and significant weight loss are observed only above 370°C.
- **Observations:** The high thermal stability of PDVB ensures its structural integrity and hydrophobic function during Fischer-Tropsch synthesis.

#### 6.1.6 Surface Adsorption Properties (FTIR)
- **Description:** Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), including in-situ studies, was conducted to probe CO adsorption and water desorption behavior on the catalyst surface.
- **Findings:** FTIR spectra show that, for CoMnC/PDVB, CO adsorption signals remain strong even in the presence of water, while bare CoMnC exhibits diminished CO adsorption when water is present. Water signals are weak for CoMnC/PDVB, indicating rapid water desorption.
- **Observations:** The hydrophobic PDVB effectively prevents water from occupying active sites, maintaining efficient CO adsorption and supporting enhanced catalytic activity.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed into products, calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions (250°C, 0.1 MPa, H₂/CO = 2, GHSV = 1800 mL·g⁻¹·h⁻¹), the bare CoMnC catalyst achieves a CO conversion of 32.2%. Physical mixing with PDVB significantly enhances conversion: at a PDVB/CoMnC weight ratio of 0.5, conversion is 56.4%; at 1.0, 63.5%; and at 1.5, 70.5%.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing PDVB loading correlates with higher CO conversion, attributed to the hydrophobic PDVB promoting rapid water desorption and minimizing water-induced inhibition of CO adsorption.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, calculated as:
\[
\text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** For CoMnC/PDVB (1:1), CO₂ selectivity is 46.3% at H₂/CO = 2. Adjusting the feed to include CO₂ (H₂/CO/CO₂/Ar = 68/19/10/3) reduces CO₂ selectivity to 23.7%.
- **Observations and Trends:** The hydrophobic PDVB does not increase CO₂ selectivity; instead, selectivity can be tuned by feed composition. Hydrophilic modifications do not further reduce CO₂ selectivity, underscoring the unique effect of hydrophobic PDVB.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
\[
\text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** For CoMnC/PDVB, CH₄ selectivity is 4.8% (1:1 ratio), 9.8% (0.5:1), and 8.9% (1.5:1). The bare CoMnC catalyst exhibits higher CH₄ selectivity (e.g., 12.9%).
- **Observations and Trends:** PDVB addition efficiently suppresses methane formation, with the lowest CH₄ selectivity at the 1:1 ratio, coinciding with peak olefin selectivity.

#### 6.2.4 C₂–C₄ Olefin Selectivity
- **Description:** Selectivity to C₂–C₄ olefins (light olefins) is defined as the percentage of these products among total hydrocarbons (CO₂-free carbon basis).
- **Experimental Data:** For CoMnC/PDVB, C₂–C₄ olefin selectivity is 62.8% (0.5:1), 71.4% (1:1), and 64.5% (1.5:1). The bare catalyst achieves ~60%.
- **Observations and Trends:** The 1:1 PDVB/CoMnC ratio maximizes light olefin selectivity, demonstrating the promotional effect of hydrophobic PDVB on desired product distribution.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor represents the probability of chain propagation in hydrocarbon formation, influencing product distribution. Lower α favors light olefins.
- **Experimental Data:** The addition of PDVB reduces the α value, as evidenced by increased light olefin selectivity and decreased C₅⁺ fraction (e.g., C₅⁺ selectivity drops to 16.9% at 1:1 PDVB/CoMnC).
- **Observations and Trends:** Hydrophobic PDVB promotes rapid water removal, suppressing secondary hydrogenation and favoring shorter-chain olefins, thus lowering α.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time; deactivation rate is the decline in conversion per hour:
\[
\text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Time (h)}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The CoMnC/PDVB catalyst maintains CO conversion at ~62–65% and light olefin selectivity at ~70% over 120 hours, with negligible decline.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalyst demonstrates excellent stability and a very low deactivation rate, attributed to the hydrophobic PDVB’s ability to prevent water accumulation and active site blockage.

**Summary:**  
The CoMnC/PDVB catalyst system exhibits markedly improved CO conversion, suppressed methane formation, enhanced light olefin selectivity, and outstanding stability under mild reaction conditions. These enhancements are directly linked to the hydrophobicity of PDVB, which accelerates water desorption and preserves active sites, as confirmed by comprehensive experimental data.
