## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the synthesis procedures employed for Fischer–Tropsch catalysts, including both the base (unpromoted Fe/Si) and promoted variants (potassium- and Group II alkali‑earth metal promoted catalysts). The synthesis methods are reported using precise reagent concentrations, reaction conditions, and equipment details to ensure reproducibility.

#### Unpromoted Iron Catalyst (Fe/Si Base)
1. **Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation:**
   - Reagents: Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O and tetraethyl orthosilicate are mixed to achieve an Fe:Si atomic ratio of 100:4.6.
   - Equipment: Continuous stirred tank reactor with pH meter.
2. **Precipitation–Deposition:**
   - Procedure: Concentrated ammonium hydroxide is added to adjust the pH to 9, with an average residence time of 6 minutes, triggering precipitate formation.
   - Observation: A distinct precipitate forms, indicating successful deposition.
3. **Separation and Washing:**
   - Technique: Precipitate is collected using a vacuum drum filter and washed twice with deionized water to remove impurities.
4. **Drying and Particle Sizing:**
   - Conditions: Dried at 110 °C for 24 hours.
   - Post‑processing: The dried catalyst is crushed to approximately 60 μm to enhance dispersion.
5. **Calcination:**
   - Details: Calcined at 350 °C for 4 hours under air flow to stabilize the catalyst structure.

#### Promoted Catalysts

**Potassium Promoted Catalyst:**
- After preparing the base catalyst, an aqueous potassium nitrate solution is impregnated onto the catalyst to achieve a promoter:Fe atomic ratio of 1.44:100.
- Drying is carried out at 110 °C overnight followed by the same calcination step (350 °C for 4 hours), ensuring proper integration of the promoter.

**Group II Alkali‑earth Metal Promoted Catalysts:**
- Following the base catalyst synthesis, each catalyst is impregnated with an aqueous nitrate solution of Be, Ba, Ca, or Mg (maintaining the same 1.44:100 promoter:Fe ratio).
- The impregnation process and subsequent drying at 110 °C overnight, along with the calcination (350 °C for 4 hours), mirror those of the potassium-promoted catalyst.

### Comparative Analysis
- The precipitation method provides rapid formation of the precipitate with controlled pH and short residence time, which is advantageous for scalability. However, this method requires precise pH control and rapid processing.
- Impregnation offers flexibility in promoter introduction. Although it requires an additional drying step, it ensures uniform promoter distribution and enables direct comparison among different promoter types under standardized conditions.

In addition, trap temperatures are tightly controlled (100 °C, 0 °C, and 200 °C) to segregate the product phases effectively. The system configuration promotes precise control of gas flow rates and maintenance of standard reaction conditions, which is critical for reproducible product analysis.

- **Test Parameters**:

### 5.1 Activation

The catalyst activation process is a critical pre‐reaction treatment that ensures the iron-based catalyst is transformed into its active species for effective Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. In this study, activation parameters were carefully controlled to optimize catalyst reduction and maintain performance.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
• Definition: The temperature during activation is pivotal for initiating the reduction of iron oxide species to the active phases, thereby promoting the formation of iron carbide that enhances long-chain hydrocarbon production.  
• Experimental Conditions: Activation was conducted at 270 °C.  
• Observations: This temperature was selected to drive the two-step reduction process effectively, facilitating the transition from precursor oxides to an active catalyst while balancing the risk of sintering.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
• Definition: The pressure applied during activation influences the kinetics of the reduction reaction by improving gas–solid interactions.  
• Experimental Conditions: The catalyst was activated under an elevated pressure of 1.3 MPa.  
• Observations: This pressure aids in pushing the reduction reactions forward, ensuring efficient formation of the active sites necessary for sustained catalytic performance.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
• Definition: The composition of the activation gas critically determines the reduction pathway and extent, affecting both the rate and selectivity of catalyst activation.  
• Experimental Conditions: Pure CO was introduced during the catalyst activation phase. Following this initial reduction, a mixture of CO and H₂ was introduced via mass controllers to maintain a controlled environment.  
• Observations: CO was observed to be particularly effective for reducing the iron oxide catalyst through a two-step process, leading to enhanced selectivity towards long-chain hydrocarbon products.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Space–Time Velocity
• Definition: The space–time velocity quantifies the flow rate of the activation gas relative to the catalyst mass, ensuring thorough contact and uniform reduction.  
• Experimental Conditions: The activation gas was applied at 24 SL/h per gram of Fe.  
• Observations: This flow rate strikes a balance by ensuring sufficient gas exposure without causing over-reduction or localized temperature gradients.

#### 5.1.5 Activation Time
• Definition: The duration of the activation step ensures complete transformation of the catalyst without inducing deactivation effects such as sintering.  
• Experimental Conditions: The catalyst was maintained under these activation conditions for 24 hours.  
• Observations: The extended activation period supports the formation of a stable, active phase that is critical for optimal performance during Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase Evolution
- **Description:** The crystal phase composition of the iron-based catalysts was monitored under Fischer–Tropsch synthesis (FTS) conditions to assess phase stability and transformation, which are critical for catalytic performance.
- **Findings:** The unpromoted iron catalyst (Fe/Si) exhibited a progressive phase transition from iron carbide (Fe₅C₂) to magnetite (Fe₃O₄) during FTS operation. Complete oxidation to Fe₃O₄ was observed at the end of the reaction run.
- **Observations:** The transformation from Fe₅C₂ to Fe₃O₄ indicates catalyst deactivation via oxidation under reaction conditions. The presence of Fe₅C₂ is associated with active FTS phases, while the formation of Fe₃O₄ correlates with diminished catalytic activity.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition and Promoter Incorporation
- **Description:** Elemental composition was controlled during synthesis by adjusting the Fe:Si atomic ratio and the promoter (K or Group II metal) loading. The impact of promoter addition on catalyst composition was evaluated through stoichiometric calculations and synthesis protocols.
- **Findings:** The base catalyst was synthesized with a Fe:Si atomic ratio of 100:4.6. Promoted catalysts were prepared by impregnating the base material with potassium or Group II metal nitrates to achieve a fixed promoter:Fe atomic ratio of 1.44:100.
- **Observations:** The consistent promoter loading across samples ensures comparability in catalytic testing. The transition from carbide to oxide phases during FTS also reflects changes in the oxidation state of iron, from lower (carbide) to higher (oxide) valence.

#### 6.1.3 Product Analysis via Gas Chromatography
- **Description:** Product distributions, including CO, H₂, CO₂, and hydrocarbon fractions, were analyzed using a suite of gas chromatographs (HP Quad Series Micro GC, HP 5890 Series II, Agilent 6890, HP 5890 Series II Plus). This analysis provides indirect insight into catalyst selectivity and activity.
- **Findings:** The potassium-promoted catalyst exhibited the highest CO₂ yield and lowest methane selectivity at high space velocities, with an alpha value of 0.80, indicating enhanced chain growth probability. Group II promoted catalysts generated higher water partial pressures and varied CO₂ selectivity, with barium showing the highest CO₂ yield at low space time, while calcium and magnesium promoted better carbon utilization.
- **Observations:** The product analysis highlights the influence of promoter type on FTS selectivity, water-gas shift activity, and hydrocarbon chain growth. The alpha value trend (K > Group II > unpromoted) underscores the role of potassium in promoting longer hydrocarbon chains.

#### 6.1.4 Water Partial Pressure and Carbon Utilization
- **Description:** Water partial pressure and carbon usage ratio were monitored as indicators of catalyst performance and selectivity, particularly in relation to the water-gas shift reaction and hydrocarbon formation.
- **Findings:** All Group II promoted catalysts produced higher water partial pressures than the unpromoted iron catalyst, with maxima at 40–50% CO conversion. The potassium-promoted catalyst maintained a lower water partial pressure and exhibited a lower carbon usage ratio compared to Group II promoted catalysts.
- **Observations:** Enhanced water-gas shift activity and improved carbon utilization are linked to promoter identity, with calcium and magnesium promoters delivering superior carbon efficiency relative to potassium.

### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of inlet CO transformed into products, reflecting catalyst activity. It is calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{N_{\text{in}} - N_{\text{out}}}{N_{\text{in}}} \times 100\%
  \]
  where \(N_{\text{in}}\) and \(N_{\text{out}}\) are the molar flow rates of CO entering and exiting the reactor, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Across space times of 0.02–0.33 SL/h·g‑Fe, the potassium-promoted catalyst consistently achieves the highest CO conversion, particularly at high space velocity (low space time). The unpromoted Fe/Si catalyst exhibits lower CO conversion at low space time but surpasses promoted catalysts at higher space times (>0.1 SL/h·g‑Fe). Group II-promoted catalysts (Be, Ba, Ca, Mg) display CO conversions similar to or slightly below the unpromoted catalyst, with Ca-promoted always lower.
- **Observations and Trends:** Potassium promotion enhances initial CO conversion, while the unpromoted catalyst demonstrates stable conversion with negligible deactivation over time.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, indicating water-gas shift (WGS) activity:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{\text{Moles of CO}_2 \text{ produced}}{\text{Moles of CO converted}} \times 100\%
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The potassium-promoted catalyst exhibits the highest CO₂ selectivity across all space times, especially at high space velocity. Among Group II-promoted catalysts, Ba yields the most CO₂ at low space time, while Ca and Mg produce less CO₂ than the unpromoted catalyst.
- **Observations and Trends:** Enhanced WGS activity is evident in potassium- and Ba-promoted catalysts, while Ca and Mg suppress CO₂ formation, improving carbon utilization.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO resulting in methane:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{\text{Moles of CH}_4 \text{ produced}}{\text{Moles of CO converted}} \times 100\%
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The unpromoted catalyst shows the highest methane selectivity at low space time. Potassium promotion results in the lowest methane selectivity at high space velocity. Group II-promoted catalysts display similar methane selectivity, except at low space velocity.
- **Observations and Trends:** Potassium suppresses methane formation, favoring longer-chain hydrocarbons, while the unpromoted catalyst tends toward methane at low space time.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity reflects the fraction of CO converted to hydrocarbons with three or more carbon atoms, indicating chain growth efficiency.
- **Experimental Data:** Potassium promotion increases C₃⁺ selectivity, correlating with higher α values. Group II-promoted catalysts also enhance C₃⁺ selectivity compared to the unpromoted catalyst, though to a lesser extent.
- **Observations and Trends:** Higher C₃⁺ selectivity is associated with improved chain growth and liquid hydrocarbon yield.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor represents the probability of chain propagation versus termination, derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory distribution:
  \[
  W_n = (1-\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}
  \]
  where \(W_n\) is the mole fraction of hydrocarbons with n carbon atoms.
- **Experimental Data:** The potassium-promoted catalyst achieves the highest α value (0.80), indicating strong chain growth. Group II-promoted catalysts exhibit α values from 0.75 upward, while the unpromoted catalyst is lower.
- **Observations and Trends:** Potassium enhances chain growth probability, favoring longer-chain hydrocarbon production.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Deactivation rate quantifies the decline in CO conversion over time:
  \[
  \text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}} \quad (\%\,\text{per day})
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The unpromoted catalyst shows negligible deactivation. Beryllium and barium promoted catalysts have low deactivation rates (0.07 and 0.15%/day), potassium and calcium moderate (0.88 and 0.91%/day), and magnesium the highest (2.24%/day).
- **Observations and Trends:** Promotion increases initial activity but can accelerate deactivation, with potassium and Group II metals showing higher rates than the unpromoted catalyst.

#### 6.2.7 Carbon Usage Ratio
- **Description:** The carbon usage ratio is the fraction of CO converted to hydrocarbons (not CO₂), reflecting carbon efficiency.
- **Experimental Data:** The unpromoted catalyst achieves the highest carbon usage ratio. All promoted catalysts show lower ratios, with potassium the lowest and Ca/Mg higher than Ba/Be.
- **Observations and Trends:** Potassium promotion, while enhancing chain growth and WGS activity, reduces overall carbon efficiency compared to the unpromoted catalyst.
