## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

- **Overview of Catalyst Synthesis Techniques**  
  - The catalysts were prepared by supporting cobalt nanoparticles of controlled size (4.5–18.1 nm) on hierarchical ZSM-5 (Z5) zeolite. The Z5 support was obtained by treating H-ZSM-5 with TPAOH to introduce mesoporosity. Cobalt loading and particle size were tuned by varying the amount of cobalt precursor and adjusting drying and calcination conditions. This approach allows for systematic investigation of cobalt particle size effects on Fischer–Tropsch performance.
  - **Advantages:** The method enables precise control of cobalt particle size and support porosity, which is critical for tuning catalytic activity and selectivity. The use of hierarchical ZSM-5 improves mass transfer and reduces diffusion limitations.
  - **Disadvantages:** The process requires careful optimization of cobalt loading and thermal treatments to achieve desired particle sizes. The method may be less scalable due to the need for multiple post-synthesis treatments and precise control of synthesis parameters.

## 6 Results

### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** Crystal phase composition and structural integrity assessed by XRD.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns show that the crystalline structure of Z5 is preserved after TPAOH treatment. Fresh Co-x/Z5 catalysts contain Co₃O₄ and Z5 phases. The relative crystallinity of Z5 decreases with increasing cobalt particle size, indicating partial framework destruction after cobalt loading.
- **Observations:** Larger cobalt particles tend to locate on the external surface of Z5, as indicated by increased surface area for Co-18/Z5.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** BET surface area, pore volume, and mesoporosity determined by N₂ adsorption–desorption.
- **Findings:** Specific surface area and pore volume decrease from 312 m² g⁻¹ and 0.36 cm³ g⁻¹ (Co-4.5/Z5) to 273 m² g⁻¹ and 0.30 cm³ g⁻¹ (Co-9.9/Z5), then increase to 297 m² g⁻¹ for Co-18/Z5. Mesopore volume decreases from 0.29 cm³ g⁻¹ (Co-4.5/Z5) to 0.21 cm³ g⁻¹ (Co-18/Z5).
- **Observations:** Mesopores are partially blocked by cobalt species for larger particles; micropore volume changes slightly.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Morphology and cobalt particle size distribution observed by SEM, TEM, and HRTEM.
- **Findings:** SEM shows partial breaking of ZSM-5 crystals and formation of mesopores. TEM confirms cobalt particle sizes from 4.5 to 18.1 nm. HRTEM shows 0.205 nm lattice spacing (Co(111) plane).
- **Observations:** Larger cobalt particles are located on the external surface of Z5.

#### 6.1.4 Acidic Properties
- **Description:** Lewis and Brønsted acid site quantification by Py-FTIR and NH₃-TPD.
- **Findings:** Py-FTIR: Lewis acid sites decrease from 443.7 mmol g⁻¹ (Co-4.5/Z5) to 316.9 mmol g⁻¹ (Co-18/Z5); Brønsted acid sites decrease from 141.5 to 80.1 mmol g⁻¹. NH₃-TPD: Two desorption peaks at 250°C and 400°C; intensity decreases with increasing cobalt particle size.
- **Observations:** Highest acidity for Co-4.5/Z5 due to high crystallinity.

#### 6.1.5 Reduction and Adsorption Properties
- **Description:** Reduction behavior (H₂-TPR), CO and H₂ adsorption (CO-TPD, H₂-TPD).
- **Findings:** H₂-TPR: Four peaks corresponding to nitrate decomposition, Co₃O₄ to CoO, CoO to Co, and reduction of cobalt silicate/aluminate. CO-TPD: CO desorption peak shifts to lower temperature with increasing particle size up to 9.9 nm, then increases. H₂-TPD: H₂ adsorption increases from 22.6 mmol g⁻¹ (Co-4.5/Z5) to 124.9 mmol g⁻¹ (Co-9.9/Z5), then decreases to 77.9 mmol g⁻¹ (Co-18/Z5).
- **Observations:** Degree of cobalt reduction increases from 55.2% (Co-4.5/Z5) to 92.0% (Co-9.9/Z5), then remains ~90% for larger particles. Cobalt dispersion decreases with increasing particle size.

#### 6.1.6 Surface Elemental Composition and Valence
- **Description:** Surface composition and cobalt valence states by XPS.
- **Findings:** Co 2p₃/₂ binding energy increases from 780.3 eV (Co-4.5/Z5) to 780.9 eV (Co-18/Z5); Co 2p₁/₂ from 796.2 to 796.7 eV. Satellite peaks at 786.4 and 803.1 eV.
- **Observations:** Enhanced interaction between cobalt and Z5 with increasing particle size.

### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** The ratio of CO molecules converted by the catalyst to the CO molecules in the reactant feed.
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CO conversion increases from 16.8% (Co-4.5/Z5) to 58.7% (Co-9.9/Z5), then decreases to 50.0% (Co-18/Z5).
- **Observations and Trends:** Maximum CO conversion at 9.9 nm cobalt particle size due to higher reduction degree and appropriate dispersion.

#### 6.2.2 CH₄ and C₂–C₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** Selectivity toward methane and light hydrocarbons.
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CH₄ selectivity decreases with increasing cobalt particle size; Co-4.5/Z5 shows highest CH₄ (28.3%) and C₂–C₄ (15.2%) selectivity.
- **Observations and Trends:** Smaller cobalt particles favor undesired light hydrocarbon formation.

#### 6.2.3 Liquid Hydrocarbon Selectivity (Gasoline, Jet Fuel, Diesel)
- **Description:** Selectivity toward C₅–C₁₁ (gasoline), C₈–C₁₆ (jet fuel), and C₁₀–C₂₀ (diesel) hydrocarbons.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Gasoline (C₅–C₁₁): Selectivity peaks at 44.5% (Co-9.9/Z5), then declines to 40.1% (Co-18/Z5).
  - Jet fuel (C₈–C₁₆): Selectivity peaks at 43.5% (Co-14/Z5), then declines to 41.8% (Co-18/Z5).
  - Diesel (C₁₀–C₂₀): Selectivity increases linearly with particle size, reaching 35.4% (Co-18/Z5).
- **Observations and Trends:** Optimal selectivity for gasoline and jet fuel at intermediate cobalt sizes; diesel selectivity increases with larger particles.

#### 6.2.4 α Factor (Chain Growth Probability)
- **Description:** Probability of hydrocarbon chain growth relative to termination.
- **Experimental Data:** α value for Co-4.5/Z5 is 0.67; for other samples (Co-9.9/Z5, Co-14/Z5, Co-18/Z5), α = 0.77–0.81.
- **Observations and Trends:** Lower α for small cobalt particles leads to higher CH₄ and C₂–C₄ selectivity; higher α for larger particles favors long-chain hydrocarbons.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Stability of CO conversion and hydrocarbon selectivity over time.
- **Experimental Data:** C₅+ selectivity remains stable over 72 h; slight decrease in CO conversion observed. Gasoline selectivity for Co-4.5/Z5 and Co-9.9/Z5 decreases slightly over 72 h; jet fuel selectivity for Co-14/Z5 remains at 43.3%; diesel selectivity for Co-18/Z5 remains above 35%.
- **Observations and Trends:** All catalysts show good stability for liquid hydrocarbon production over extended operation.
