## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the preparation routes for the ZnCrOx–GeAPO-18 catalyst system, including the active ZnCrOx phase and the zeotype support (GeAPO-18). Each method is presented step‑by‑step to ensure reproducibility and clarity.

#### ZnCrOx via Co‑precipitation Method
1. **Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation**  
   - Reagents: Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, Cr(NO₃)₃·9H₂O, Al(NO₃)₃·9H₂O dissolved in distilled water (e.g., 100 mL) in fixed concentrations (e.g., 14.56 g Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, 5.6 g Cr(NO₃)₃·9H₂O, 5.25 g Al(NO₃)₃·9H₂O).  
   - Reaction Condition: Temperature maintained at 70 °C.
2. **Precipitation–Deposition**  
   - Precipitant: Addition of (NH₄)₂CO₃ solution to trigger precipitate formation.  
   - Placement: This step follows the precursor mixing, ensuring uniform deposition of the metals.
3. **Aging**  
   - Conditions: 3 hours at 70 °C to stabilize the precipitate.
4. **Drying**  
   - Parameters: Drying at 110 °C after thorough washing with distilled water.
5. **Calcination**  
   - Parameters: Calcination at 500 °C for 1 hour to develop the final oxide phase.

#### GeAPO-18 via Hydrothermal Method
1. **Precursor Mixing**  
   - Reagents: Aluminum isopropoxide, 85% phosphoric acid, GeO₂ (for GeAPO-18) mixed as per a molar composition of Al₂O₃:P₂O₅:GeO₂:DPA:H₂O = 1:1:0.5:1.4:50.
2. **Hydrothermal Crystallization**  
   - Equipment: Teflon‑lined autoclave to provide controlled high‑pressure conditions.
   - Reaction Conditions: Crystallization at 200 °C for 1 day.
3. **Post‑processing**  
   - Washing: Thorough washing with distilled water to remove impurities.
   - Drying: Overnight drying at approximately 110 °C.
   - Calcination: Conducted at 600 °C for 5 hours to achieve the desired zeotype phase.
4. **Comparative Adjustments**  
   - For SAPO‑18 and MgAPO‑18, similar procedures are followed with modifications in precursor types (e.g., silica sol for SAPO‑18, magnesium nitrate for MgAPO‑18) and adjusted precursor ratios to meet specific compositional targets.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: ZnCrOx

##### Step [1]: Precursor Preparation – Co‐precipitation of ZnCrOx  
- **Reagents**:  
  - 14.56 g Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O  
  - 5.6 g Cr(NO₃)₃·9H₂O  
  - 5.25 g Al(NO₃)₃·9H₂O  
  - 100 mL distilled water  
  - (NH₄)₂CO₃ aqueous solution (as precipitant)  
- **Conditions**:  
  - Reaction temperature: 70°C  
  - Total reaction time: 3 hours of co‐precipitation  
  - pH adjustments: Not explicitly required under the provided conditions  
- **Process**:  
  - Dissolve the three metal nitrates in 100 mL distilled water to form a homogeneous solution.  
  - At 70°C, add (NH₄)₂CO₃ solution dropwise to induce precipitation while stirring continuously.  
  - Maintain the temperature and stirring for 3 hours to ensure complete co‐precipitation.  
  - Age the formed precipitate for an additional 3 hours to allow maturation before purification.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  - Ensure uniform mixing during reagent dissolution and careful control of the addition rate for (NH₄)₂CO₃ to achieve reproducible precipitation.

##### Step [2]: Purification, Drying & Calcination  
- **Process**:  
  - Filter and thoroughly wash the aged precipitate with distilled water to remove residual ions.  
  - Dry the washed precipitate overnight at approximately 110°C.  
  - Calcine the dried material at 500°C for 1 hour (ramp rate not specified) under an oxidizing atmosphere.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  - Reliable washing and drying are essential to eliminate impurities and to prevent structural defects during calcination.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature  
The reaction temperature in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis is critical for activating syngas and promoting CO conversion. In these experiments, the reactor was operated within a temperature range of 410 °C to 480 °C. It is observed that lower temperatures (around 410 °C) result in lower CO conversion and light-olefins selectivity, whereas at higher temperatures (up to 480 °C), CO conversion reached approximately 85% with light-olefins selectivity maintained near 83%. This underscores the importance of selecting an optimal temperature for ensuring enhanced catalyst performance.

#### 5.2.2 Reaction Pressure  
Pressure influences both the concentration of syngas and the formation of hydrocarbon chains. The experimental setup utilized a fixed-bed reactor configuration, operating under pressures ranging from 4 MPa to 8 MPa. While the detailed influence of these different pressures on product selectivity is not exhaustively elaborated, the tests indicated that higher pressures generally enhance the conversion of syngas and facilitate the formation of longer-chain hydrocarbons.

#### 5.2.3 Reaction H₂/CO Ratio  
The H₂/CO ratio is vital in balancing the availability of hydrogen and carbon monoxide, which ultimately affects product distribution. A fixed H₂/CO ratio of 2.5 was employed across all catalytic tests. This ratio is instrumental in steering the reaction toward the targeted production of light olefins while controlling the formation of unwanted byproducts.

#### 5.2.4 Reaction Space-Time Velocity  
The gas hourly space velocity (GHSV) defines the contact time between reactants and the catalyst, impacting overall conversion results. The experiments reported GHSV values ranging from 1500 mL/(g·h) to 6000 mL/(g·h), with specific tests demonstrating values of 1500, 3000, and 4500 mL/(g·h). Higher GHSV values, indicating shorter contact times, were associated with lower CO conversion; however, they also contributed to improved catalyst stability over extended tests (up to 150 hours).  

These meticulously controlled conditions ensure the reproducibility of the Fischer–Tropsch synthesis process and highlight the delicate balance required between temperature, pressure, H₂/CO ratio, and GHSV to optimize both activity and selectivity in catalyst performance.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of the zeotype supports and composite catalysts were assessed using X-ray diffraction (XRD). This analysis is critical for confirming the formation of the desired AEI framework and ensuring the absence of impurity phases, which can impact catalytic performance.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns of GeAPO-18, SAPO-18, and MgAPO-18 zeotypes exhibited the typical AEI crystal structure with no detectable impurity phases. The GeAPO-18 samples, with Ge/Al ratios ranging from 0.012 to 0.027, consistently showed phase purity and structural integrity. Post-reaction XRD analysis of GeAPO-18 after up to 150 hours of syngas conversion revealed retention of the AEI framework, indicating excellent structural stability under reaction conditions.
- **Observations:** The robust crystal structure of GeAPO-18, even after prolonged catalytic testing, underscores its suitability as a stable support for Fischer–Tropsch catalysis.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition and Dopant Distribution
- **Description:** Elemental composition and dopant homogeneity were determined by X-ray fluorescence (XRF) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping. Accurate control of dopant levels is essential for tuning acid site properties and catalytic behavior.
- **Findings:** XRF analysis confirmed that the Ge/Al ratios in GeAPO-18 samples matched the intended doping levels (0.012–0.027), with homogeneous Ge distribution throughout the crystals as evidenced by EDS mapping. The successful incorporation of Ge into the AlPO-18 framework was further supported by XAS data.
- **Observations:** Precise dopant control enables systematic modulation of acid site density and strength, directly influencing catalytic selectivity.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Microstructure
- **Description:** Morphological features and microstructure were examined using high-resolution scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and integrated differential phase contrast scanning transmission electron microscopy (iDPC-STEM). These techniques provide insights into particle size, shape, and dispersion, which affect catalyst accessibility and stability.
- **Findings:** SEM images revealed well-defined, crystalline particles for GeAPO-18, with no observable phase separation. iDPC-STEM, performed at 300 kV with a spherical-aberration corrector, confirmed uniform dispersion of ZnCrOx nanoparticles on the zeotype support. The microstructure remained unchanged after extended reaction times.
- **Observations:** The uniform morphology and stable microstructure contribute to the catalyst’s durability and consistent performance.

#### 6.1.4 Acid Site Density and Strength
- **Description:** The density and strength of Brønsted acid sites were quantified using NH₃ temperature-programmed desorption (NH₃-TPD) and microcalorimetric measurements. Acid site properties are pivotal in governing product selectivity and suppressing undesired secondary reactions.
- **Findings:** NH₃-TPD profiles showed that GeAPO-18 possesses weaker and less dense acid sites compared to SAPO-18 and MgAPO-18, with desorption temperatures for medium-strength sites between 229–413°C. Microcalorimetry revealed a differential NH₃ adsorption heat of 143 kJ/mol for GeAPO-18, lower than SAPO-18 (156 kJ/mol) and MgAPO-18 (193 kJ/mol). These results were consistent with DFT-calculated NH₃ binding energies.
- **Observations:** The weaker acidity of GeAPO-18 effectively suppresses secondary hydrogenation and oligomerization, enabling high selectivity for light olefins.

#### 6.1.5 Oxidation State and Coordination Environment
- **Description:** The oxidation state and coordination of Ge centers in the zeotype framework were probed by X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) at the Ge K-edge. This characterization ensures proper dopant incorporation and framework integrity.
- **Findings:** XAS data indicated that Ge is present as isolated, fourfold-coordinated species within the AlPO-18 framework, confirming successful framework substitution and absence of GeO₂ domains.
- **Observations:** The well-defined coordination environment of Ge is essential for achieving the desired acid site properties and catalytic function.

#### 6.1.6 Adsorption Properties and Surface Reactivity
- **Description:** Ethylene temperature-programmed desorption (TPD) and gravimetric analysis were used to evaluate the adsorption strength and capacity of Brønsted acid sites, providing insight into the catalyst’s propensity for secondary reactions.
- **Findings:** Ethylene-TPD profiles and DFT calculations showed that GeAPO-18 exhibits significantly weaker ethylene adsorption (mean adsorption energy ~0.56 eV) than SAPO-18 and MgAPO-18. This is consistent with the lower acid strength measured by microcalorimetry.
- **Observations:** The reduced olefin adsorption on GeAPO-18 supports high olefin selectivity by minimizing undesired side reactions.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction. It is calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100\%
  \]
  where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the molar flow rates of CO at the reactor inlet and outlet, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** For ZnCrOx–GeAPO-18, CO conversion reached up to 85% at 430°C, 6 MPa, and GHSV 1500 mL/(g·h). Across a Ge/Al ratio range of 0.012–0.027, conversion increased from 60% to 85%. In comparison, SAPO-18 exhibited CO conversion from 27% (with >80% olefin selectivity) to 50% (with ~50% selectivity) as Si content increased. MgAPO-18 showed lower conversion and selectivity.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing Ge content in GeAPO-18 led to a near-linear rise in CO conversion while maintaining high selectivity, a performance unattainable with SAPO-18 or MgAPO-18, which suffer from a trade-off between activity and selectivity.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO that forms CO₂, reflecting the extent of the water-gas shift and oxidation reactions:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100\%
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** For ZnCrOx–GeAPO-18, CO₂ selectivity ranged from 32% to 40% under varying conditions. For example, at 430°C, 4 MPa, and GHSV 1500 mL/(g·h), selectivity was 37%. SAPO-18 showed similar CO₂ selectivity (32–41%).
- **Observations and Trends:** The CO₂ selectivity remained relatively stable across different Ge/Al ratios and reaction conditions, indicating effective suppression of excessive oxidation.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100\%
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** While explicit CH₄ selectivity values are not tabulated, the high light-olefins selectivity (>80%) for ZnCrOx–GeAPO-18 indicates that CH₄ selectivity is low.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalyst’s design, featuring weaker Brønsted acid sites, effectively suppresses methane formation, favoring olefin production.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with three or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_3^+ \text{ Selectivity} = 100 - \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}} + F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_6,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100\%
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** The hydrocarbon distribution is dominated by light olefins (C₂–C₄=), with C₃⁺ selectivity reflecting a high yield of propylene and minimal heavier hydrocarbons.
- **Observations and Trends:** The suppression of secondary reactions in GeAPO-18 ensures that C₃⁺ selectivity is primarily due to propylene, with minimal formation of heavier, less valuable products.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion and selectivity over extended reaction times.
- **Experimental Data:** ZnCrOx–GeAPO-18 maintained stable CO conversion and light-olefins selectivity over 150 hours on stream, with the light-olefins yield remaining at 48% and the olefin/paraffin ratio at 16.
- **Observations and Trends:** Post-reaction characterizations (XRD, NH₃-TPD, ICP-OES, XAS) confirmed structural and compositional stability, with negligible deactivation observed.

---

**Summary:**  
The ZnCrOx–GeAPO-18 catalyst system achieves a unique combination of high CO conversion (up to 85%), exceptional light-olefins selectivity (up to 83%), and outstanding stability, breaking the conventional activity–selectivity trade-off. The introduction of Ge into the zeotype framework enables increased acid site density without enhancing acid strength, thereby suppressing side reactions and maximizing target olefin yields. These results are robust across a wide range of reaction conditions and sustained over prolonged operation.
