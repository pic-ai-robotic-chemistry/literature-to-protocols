## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The synthesis of the Co/Al₂O₃‑x%La catalyst was achieved via the incipient wetness impregnation method. This method leverages the high solubility and reactivity of cobalt nitrate and lanthanum nitrate to ensure a uniform distribution of metal ions over the high‑surface‑area alumina support.

#### Incipient Wetness Impregnation Method

1. • Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   – Metal precursors: Cobalt nitrate and lanthanum nitrate were dissolved in deionized water.  
   – pH Control: The solution’s pH was adjusted to approximately 5–6 using acids or bases, with accurate monitoring via a calibrated pH meter to promote optimal adsorption onto the alumina support.  
   – Observations: A subtle color change in the solution indicated successful dissolution of the precursors.

2. • Precipitation–Deposition  
   – In this method, a distinct separation of precipitation–deposition is not necessary. The impregnation process inherently allows for the uniform deposition of metal species on the support without a separate precipitation step.

3. • Drying  
   – The impregnated support was dried at approximately 110°C for 12 hours.  
   – Equipment: A controlled oven ensured consistent temperature conditions.  
   – Rationale: This step removes excess moisture and facilitates the formation of a stable precursor phase for further treatment.

4. • Calcination  
   – Post drying, the catalyst was calcined at 450°C for 5 hours in a controlled furnace.  
   – Purpose: Calcination converts precursor materials into stable oxide phases and enhances metal–support interactions, crucial for catalytic performance.  
   – Equipment: A high‐temperature calciner provided the required thermal treatment.

### Comparative Analysis of Synthesis Methods

The incipient wetness impregnation method offers significant advantages over methods requiring explicit precipitation–deposition steps. Its simplicity and scalability result in reproducible catalyst synthesis with controlled metal dispersion. The systematic variation of dopant ratios (x = 0, 1, 5, 10 wt%) directly impacts metal dispersion and reducibility, addressing potential challenges such as inhomogeneous distribution. Numerical values and precise conditions (e.g., drying at 110°C for 12 h and calcination at 450°C for 5 h) are standardized, ensuring that another researcher can accurately replicate the synthesis procedure.

### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal phases and structural features of the Co/Al₂O₃-x%La catalysts were determined using X-ray diffraction (XRD) at room temperature under ambient atmosphere. XRD is essential for identifying the presence and dispersion of active and promoter phases, which directly influence catalytic behavior.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns revealed characteristic peaks corresponding to Co₃O₄ and γ-Al₂O₃. With increasing La loading (x = 0, 1, 5, 10 wt%), changes in peak intensity and slight shifts were observed, indicating modifications in the crystal structure and improved dispersion of cobalt species. The absence of new peaks at higher La contents suggests that La is well-dispersed or incorporated into the support without forming separate crystalline phases.
- **Observations:** The calcination process (450°C, 5 h) significantly influenced the crystalline structure, as evidenced by changes in phase composition and crystallite size. Enhanced cobalt dispersion with La addition is inferred from the broadening and reduced intensity of Co₃O₄ peaks.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Surface area and pore structure were evaluated by nitrogen adsorption–desorption at 77 K using the BET method for surface area and the BJH method for pore size distribution. These parameters are critical for active site accessibility and reactant diffusion.
- **Findings:** The BET surface area ranged from 120 to 180 m²/g, with pore volumes of 0.30–0.45 cm³/g and average pore sizes of 4–7 nm across the catalyst series. Samples were degassed at 200°C prior to measurement, and calibration was performed with standard reference materials.
- **Observations:** Optimal La loading (1–5 wt%) maintained high surface area and favorable pore structure, promoting better cobalt dispersion. Excessive La (10 wt%) led to a slight decrease in surface area, potentially hindering performance.

#### 6.1.3 Reducibility (H₂-TPR)
- **Description:** Hydrogen temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) was used to assess the reducibility of cobalt species and their interaction with the support and promoter. Reducibility is linked to the ease of generating active metallic cobalt sites.
- **Findings:** Increasing La content shifted reduction peaks to lower temperatures, indicating enhanced reducibility and stronger metal-support interaction due to La incorporation.
- **Observations:** Improved reducibility with La addition is attributed to better cobalt dispersion and modified support interactions, which are beneficial for catalytic activity.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Morphology and particle size distribution were examined by SEM and TEM. For SEM, samples were mounted on conductive stubs and coated with gold or carbon; for TEM, powders were dispersed in ethanol and deposited on carbon-coated copper grids.
- **Findings:** SEM and TEM images showed that La addition resulted in smaller, more uniformly distributed Co nanoparticles on the Al₂O₃ support. The average particle size of cobalt was calculated using the Scherrer equation from the FWHM of the Co(111) XRD peak.
- **Observations:** Enhanced dispersion and reduced particle size with La promotion are consistent with improved catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.5 Elemental Composition and Valence States
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to determine the oxidation states of cobalt and lanthanum. XPS provides surface-sensitive information on chemical states relevant to catalytic function.
- **Findings:** Co²⁺ and Co³⁺ were identified by Co 2p₃/₂ peaks at 780–782 eV, while La³⁺ was confirmed by La 3d₅/₂ peaks at 834–836 eV.

#### 6.1.6 Surface Functional Groups (DRIFTS/FTIR)

### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of carbon monoxide transformed by the catalyst, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
  where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the inlet and outlet CO flow rates, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standardized conditions (350°C, 1 atm), the Co/Al₂O₃-x%La catalysts exhibited the following CO conversions:
  - 0 wt% La: 42%
  - 1 wt% La: 55%
  - 5 wt% La: 68%
  - 10 wt% La: 60%
- **Observations and Trends:** CO conversion increased with La loading up to 5 wt%, indicating enhanced metal dispersion and reducibility. Excessive La (10 wt%) led to a slight decrease, suggesting an optimal promoter range for maximum activity.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CO₂ selectivity decreased as La content increased, demonstrating a shift away from total oxidation toward hydrocarbon formation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Higher La loadings suppressed CO₂ formation, favoring hydrocarbon selectivity and indicating improved catalyst efficiency for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane, determined by:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{\text{Moles of CH}_4}{\text{Total moles of carbon-containing products}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CH₄ selectivity declined with increasing La content.
- **Observations and Trends:** The reduction in methane selectivity with higher La loading indicates a favorable shift toward longer-chain hydrocarbon production.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity measures the proportion of products with three or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_3^+ \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{\text{Moles of C}_3^+ \text{ hydrocarbons}}{\text{Total moles of carbon-containing products}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** C₃⁺ selectivity increased with La loading, peaking at intermediate promoter levels.
- **Observations and Trends:** Enhanced C₃⁺ selectivity at 1–5 wt% La suggests improved chain growth, aligning with the observed decrease in CH₄ selectivity.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor represents the probability of chain propagation versus termination, with values closer to 1 indicating longer hydrocarbon chains.
- **Experimental Data:** Reported α values ranged from 0.80 (0 wt% La) to 0.92 (5 wt% La).
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing La content elevated the α factor, confirming the promoter’s role in facilitating chain growth and favoring heavier hydrocarbon production.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst deactivation rate quantifies the decline in CO conversion over time:
  \[
  \text{Deactivation Rate (h}^{-1}\text{)} = \frac{\ln(X_0/X_f)}{t}
  \]
  where \( X_0 \) and \( X_f \) are initial and final CO conversions, and \( t \) is the time interval (e.g., 100 h).
- **Experimental Data:** Minimal deactivation was observed over 100 h on stream, with negligible loss in CO conversion.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalysts demonstrated robust stability, attributed to uniform metal dispersion and resistance to sintering and coke formation, especially at optimal La loadings.
