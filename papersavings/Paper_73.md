## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The synthesis methods reported for Fischer–Tropsch catalysts focus on reproducible preparation of cobalt-based catalysts via impregnation techniques. Two main impregnation approaches are employed: incipient wetness impregnation (IWI) for promoter addition (phosphorus and platinum) and slurry impregnation for the cobalt precursor.

#### Method Example: Alumina-Supported Catalyst (Co/Al₂O₃, PCo/Al₂O₃, PtCo/Al₂O₃, PPtCo/Al₂O₃)

1. • Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
  – Reagents: Orthophosphoric acid (H₃PO₄ 85%) is added to γ‑Al₂O₃ by IWI to reach 1.25 wt.% phosphorus.  
  – Equipment: pH meter and calibrated dosing system ensure uniform distribution.

2. • Precipitation–Deposition  
  – When applied, a support precursor is added dropwise after mixing, promoting the formation of aluminum phosphate species that stabilize subsequent cobalt sites.  
  – Observations: Uniform precipitate formation with minimal agglomeration.

3. • Drying  
  – Conditions: Intermediate vacuum drying is conducted using a rotary evaporator at 80–100°C to remove excess solvent.  
  – Followed by overnight drying at 120°C after each impregnation step to ensure complete moisture removal.

4. • Calcination  
  – Conditions: 350°C for 4 hours under air flow transforms cobalt nitrate hexahydrate (Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O) into Co₃O₄ and ultimately metallic Co upon reduction.

5. • Additional Post‑Treatments  
  – Platinum, introduced via IWI with tetraamineplatinum(II) nitrate at 0.5 wt.%, is added after phosphorus impregnation and prior to the final calcination step.  
  – Reduction: In‑situ reduction is performed at 350°C and ex‑situ at 230°C using H₂/argon mixtures to complete catalyst activation.

These details highlight the advantages of the impregnation approach: precise control of promoter loadings, minimal risk of inhomogeneous distribution, and enhanced catalyst stability. The sequential impregnation and intermediate drying steps are critical to mitigating cobalt sintering, ensuring reproducibility and scalability of the synthesis process.

## 3. Synthesis Procedures

### 3.1 Reagent Preparation

In this section, reagent preparation is organized into three key operations—Weighing, Liquid Transfer, and Mixing—with catalyst‐specific subsections for Co/Al₂O₃, PCo/Al₂O₃, PtCo/Al₂O₃, PPtCo/Al₂O₃, Co/SiO₂, and PCo/SiO₂. Detailed operational parameters are provided to ensure clarity and reproducibility.

#### 3.1.1 Weighing

Accurate determination of solid reagents was performed using an analytical balance.

##### 3.1.1.1 Catalyst Co/Al₂O₃  
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed to the required amount for incipient wetness impregnation using an analytical balance.

##### 3.1.1.2 Catalyst PCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed accurately per the slurry impregnation protocol.  
(Phosphorus will be introduced by liquid transfer in a subsequent step.)

##### 3.1.1.3 Catalyst PtCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed as prescribed for the support loading.  
- **Tetraamineplatinum(II) nitrate**: Weighed to achieve a 0.5 wt.% platinum loading.

##### 3.1.1.4 Catalyst PPtCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed for cobalt impregnation following the dual-step protocol.  
- **Tetraamineplatinum(II) nitrate**: Weighed for 0.5 wt.% loading; phosphorus is added in the following liquid transfer step.

##### 3.1.1.5 Catalyst Co/SiO₂  
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed using precise methodology to ensure uniform cobalt dispersion.

##### 3.1.1.6 Catalyst PCo/SiO₂  
- **Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O**: Weighed following standard protocols to allow later phosphorus incorporation.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

Liquid reagents were transferred using calibrated equipment to achieve the desired impregnation conditions.

##### 3.1.2.1 Catalyst Co/Al₂O₃  
- **Deionized water**: Poured at a 1:1 volume-to-weight ratio using a graduated cylinder.

##### 3.1.2.2 Catalyst PCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Orthophosphoric acid solution (H₃PO₄ 85%)**: Transferred via incipient wetness impregnation to reach a 1.25 wt.% target loading.

##### 3.1.2.3 Catalyst PtCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Tetraamineplatinum(II) nitrate solution**: Poured (with a syringe if required) into the vessel to ensure precise volume delivery.

##### 3.1.2.4 Catalyst PPtCo/Al₂O₃  
- Liquid transfer is performed sequentially—first phosphorus, then the platinum solution—using the same method.

##### 3.1.2.5 Catalyst Co/SiO₂  
- **Deionized water**: Poured using standard glassware to assist slurry formation.

##### 3.1.2.6 Catalyst PCo/SiO₂  
- **Orthophosphoric acid solution**: Transferred under incipient wetness conditions to facilitate promoter incorporation.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

Homogeneity is achieved through controlled stirring and, when indicated, ultrasonic treatment.

##### 3.1.3.1 Catalyst Co/Al₂O₃  
- **Precursor slurry**: Stirred at 800 rpm for 30 minutes at room temperature, followed by ultrasonic treatment for 15 minutes.

##### 3.1.3.2 Catalyst PCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Combined precursor solution**: Mixed under identical stirring and ultrasonic conditions.

##### 3.1.3.3 Catalyst PtCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Metal precursor mixture**: Stirred for 30 minutes at controlled speed, then dried in a rotary evaporator at 80–100 °C.

##### 3.1.3.4 Catalyst PPtCo/Al₂O₃  
- **Mixed system**: Processed as for PtCo/Al₂O₃ with an additional drying step after phosphorus addition.

##### 3.1.3.5 Catalyst Co/SiO₂  
- **Precursor solution**: Mixed for 30 minutes at a moderate rate to ensure uniform dispersion.

##### 3.1.3.6 Catalyst PCo/SiO₂  
- **Processed mixture**: Stirred under inert conditions to maintain the integrity of the active phase prior to calcination.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Co/Al₂O₃

##### Step [1]: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  - Cobalt nitrate hexahydrate (Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O) to yield 25% Co in the final formulation.  
  - For phosphorus-containing systems, orthophosphoric acid (H₃PO₄ 85%) is used to achieve ca. 1.25 wt.% P.  
- **Conditions**:  
  - Cobalt nitrate is dissolved in deionized water using a loading solution-to-alumina weight ratio of 1:1.  
  - pH adjustment details are not reported; the process relies on controlled reagent addition.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Weigh and dissolve the specified amount of Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O in 50 mL deionized water until a homogeneous solution is achieved.  
  2. For P-containing catalysts, incorporate the designated amount of H₃PO₄ by incipient wetness impregnation onto γ-Al₂O₃.  
  3. Allow the solution to stand briefly to ensure even distribution of precursors before further handling.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  - Ensure complete dissolution.  
  - Confirm targeted loadings for Co and P are met prior to assembly.

##### Step [2]: Catalyst Assembly  
- **Reagents**:  
  - Use the pretreated alumina support and the prepared precursor solution.  
- **Conditions**:  
  - Employ slurry impregnation for cobalt addition following phosphorus impregnation.  
  - For platinum-promoted systems (PtCo/Al₂O₃, PPtCo/Al₂O₃), add a tetraamineplatinum(II) nitrate solution by incipient wetness impregnation before final calcination.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Impregnate the support sequentially: phosphorus (if applicable), then cobalt (in two stages to achieve homogeneous distribution), followed by platinum for promoted catalysts.  
  2. Between impregnation steps, dry the material under vacuum using a rotary evaporator at 80–100 °C (overnight in some cases) to remove excess moisture.
- **Key Steps**:  
  - Strict order of addition is crucial for reproducibility.

##### Step [3]: Drying, Calcination & Activation  
- **Drying & Calcination**:  
  - Dry the impregnated support overnight at 120 °C; calcine at 350 °C for 4 hours in flowing air to convert precursors to Co₃O₄.  
- **Activation**:  
  - Conduct reduction in H₂: in-situ reduction at 230 °C and ex-situ at 350 °C (e.g., 20 hours with a H₂/He 1:3 v/v mixture).  
- **Key Steps**:  
  - Controlled temperature and drying conditions ensure the reproducible formation of metallic Co catalysts with desired crystallite sizes.

The outlined protocol, with precise reagent quantities and step-by-step conditions, supports reproducible catalyst synthesis suitable for Fischer–Tropsch evaluations.

## 4. Characterization Methods

### 4.1 X‑Ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Operational Description**: XRD was employed to determine the crystalline structure and accurately estimate the crystallite sizes of Co₃O₄ clusters in the catalysts. Sample powders were carefully aligned to minimize preferred orientation effects.
- **Instruments and Models**: Philips X’Pert diffractometer with monochromatic Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54 Å).
- **Characterization Conditions**: The diffraction patterns were recorded over a 2θ range of 10°–80° using a step size of 0.018° and a dwell time of 4 seconds per step.
- **Test Parameters**: Precise scan settings ensure reproducible measurement of peak intensities and positions.
- **Key Notes**: Correct sample mounting is critical to avoid errors in diffraction and misestimation of crystallite sizes.

### 4.2 H₂‑Temperature‑Programmed Reduction (H₂‑TPR)
- **Operational Description**: H₂‑TPR was carried out to assess the reduction behavior of cobalt species. The test delineates two sequential reduction steps: Co₃O₄ → CoO and CoO → Co⁰.
- **Instruments and Models**: Zeton‑Altamira (AMI‑200) reactor equipped with a thermal conductivity detector (TCD).
- **Characterization Conditions**: Samples were pre‑treated in flowing argon at 350°C for 1 hour; then, under a 10 vol.% H₂ in argon flow (30 cm³/min), the temperature was ramped from 25°C to 900°C at a rate of 10°C/min.
- **Test Parameters**: Detailed ramping conditions facilitate differentiation of phosphorus and platinum effects on reducibility.
- **Key Notes**: Pre‑treatment ensures removal of adsorbed moisture, allowing accurate monitoring of reduction steps.

### 4.3 Hydrogen Chemisorption with Pulse Reoxidation
- **Operational Description**: This method estimates cobalt dispersion by monitoring H₂ uptake and desorption.
- **Instruments and Models**: Fixed‑bed reactor integrated with a TCD.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Catalysts were first reduced in a gas mixture of 33% H₂ in argon at 350°C for 10 hours, cooled to 100°C under H₂ flow, then purged with argon before temperature‑programmed desorption (TPD) up to 350°C.
- **Test Parameters**: Subsequent pulses of O₂ in helium enable reoxidation and ensure complete removal of weakly adsorbed hydrogen.
- **Key Notes**: Controls during the TPD step are essential for accurate cobalt dispersion measurements.

### 4.4 BET Surface Area Analysis
- **Operational Description**: Conducted to determine surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution.
- **Instruments and Models**: Micromeritics TRISTAR 3000 Analyzer.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Approximately 250 mg of sample was evacuated for 12 hours at 160°C; nitrogen adsorption isotherms were collected at 77 K.
- **Test Parameters**: Data analysis was performed using both BET and BJH methods.
- **Key Notes**: Proper degassing ensures reproducible and accurate surface texture evaluation.

### 4.5 Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectroscopy (ICP-OES)
- **Operational Description**: ICP-OES provided quantitative elemental analysis of cobalt, phosphorus, and platinum.
- **Instruments and Models**: Varian 720‑ES.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Samples were dissolved in a perchloric/nitric acid mixture and compared against standard solutions.
- **Key Notes**: This method confirms catalyst composition and supports the interpretation of other characterization data.

### 4.6 X‑ray Absorption Near Edge Structure (XANES) at the P K‑edge)
- **Operational Description**: XANES was utilized to probe the phosphorus electronic structure and its interaction with the catalyst support.
- **Instruments and Models**: Soft X‑ray Microcharacterization Beamline (SXRMB) at the Canadian Light Source.
- **Characterization Conditions**: Catalysts were pressed into flat pellets, pre‑reduced in H₂ at 350°C for 10 hours, and then cooled under H₂ flow to 220°C for analysis.
- **Test Parameters**: The P K‑edge scan provides insights into promoter effects.
- **Key Notes**: In‑situ reduction conditions maintain the integrity of phosphorus interactions during measurement.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The catalyst activation process is critical to converting cobalt oxide species into their active metallic state for efficient Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. The activation protocol consists of a two‐step reduction procedure performed under conditions optimized to maximize cobalt dispersion while minimizing sintering.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
• Definition: The activation temperature is the thermal condition required to reduce cobalt oxide to metallic cobalt, which is essential for catalyst performance.  
• Experimental Conditions: The catalyst is first activated ex situ at 350 °C for 20 hours in a fixed-bed reactor, followed by an in situ reduction step at 230 °C overnight in a continuously stirred tank reactor.  
• Observations: These temperatures ensure gradual and complete reduction while limiting sintering effects, with the ex situ step establishing the bulk reduction and the in situ step refining the active sites.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
• Definition: Activation pressure influences the kinetics of the reduction reactions by affecting the interaction of hydrogen with the catalyst surface.  
• Experimental Conditions: Both the ex situ and in situ reduction steps are conducted at atmospheric pressure (1 atm).  
• Observations: Operating at atmospheric pressure provides a controlled reduction environment, which facilitates uniform catalyst activation without triggering overly rapid reaction kinetics or agglomeration.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
• Definition: The activation gas composition determines the chemical environment for reduction and is key to controlling the reduction rate.  
• Experimental Conditions: In the ex situ reduction step, a gas mixture composed of H₂/He in a volumetric ratio of 1:3 is used. For the subsequent in situ reduction, pure H₂ is employed.  
• Observations: The diluted H₂ conditions during ex situ activation prevent overly aggressive reduction, while the use of pure H₂ in situ ensures complete conversion to metallic cobalt, with both steps contributing to improved catalyst stability and activity.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Space-Time Velocity
• Definition: Space–time velocity reflects the contact time between the catalyst and the activation gas, affecting reduction uniformity.  
• Experimental Conditions & Observations: No explicit value was reported in the article for space–time velocity during catalyst activation.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature  
- **Definition:** The reaction temperature is the energy input that facilitates syngas conversion to hydrocarbons. It plays a critical role in balancing catalyst activity and reaction kinetics while minimizing deactivation.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The Fischer-Tropsch synthesis was conducted at 220 °C.  
- **Observations:** This temperature was selected to achieve optimal catalyst performance, ensuring efficient syngas activation and maintaining a stable conversion rate over time. The article notes that the chosen temperature supports a balance between catalyst activity and longevity, thereby reducing catalyst deactivation.

#### 5.2.2 Reaction Pressure  
- **Definition:** Reaction pressure influences the concentration of syngas in the reactor, thus affecting product selectivity, particularly the distribution between lighter and heavier hydrocarbons.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** The reaction was performed at 300 psi.  
- **Observations:** Higher pressure conditions were reported to enhance product selectivity toward heavier hydrocarbons and improve catalyst stability. For instance, catalysts such as PtCo/Al₂O₃ exhibited higher CO conversion values under these conditions, highlighting the beneficial impact of elevated pressure on maintaining catalytic performance.

#### 5.2.3 Reaction H₂/CO Ratio  
- **Definition:** The H₂/CO molar ratio determines the relative availability of hydrogen and carbon monoxide, directly affecting the formation of various hydrocarbon products.  
- **Experimental Conditions:** A constant H₂/CO ratio of 2:1 was maintained throughout the catalytic evaluation.  
- **Observations:** This ratio was shown to influence hydrocarbon distribution, where a higher ratio tends to favor methane formation, while a lower ratio promotes the synthesis of heavier hydrocarbons. The article documents that strict adherence to this ratio resulted in reproducible outcomes, with variations in product selectivity aligning consistently with the reported values.

In summary, the meticulously controlled reaction conditions—220 °C, 300 psi, and an H₂/CO ratio of 2:1—ensure not only reproducibility but also optimization of catalyst performance in Fischer-Tropsch synthesis. These conditions serve as a robust framework for comparing the performance of catalysts such as Co/Al₂O₃, PCo/Al₂O₃, PtCo/Al₂O₃, and PPtCo/Al₂O₃ under standardized operational settings.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Elemental Composition (ICP-OES)
- **Description:** Elemental analysis was performed using Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectroscopy (ICP-OES) to quantify the cobalt, phosphorus, and platinum content in the catalysts.
- **Findings:** The measured compositions (wt.%) were: Co/Al₂O₃ (Co: 26.30), PtCo/Al₂O₃ (Co: 26.12, Pt: 0.44), PCo/Al₂O₃ (Co: 25.92, P: 1.06), and PPtCo/Al₂O₃ (Co: 25.86, P: 1.04, Pt: 0.43). These values confirm successful incorporation of promoters at targeted loadings.
- **Observations:** The close agreement between nominal and measured values indicates effective control over catalyst composition during synthesis.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity (BET/BJH)
- **Description:** Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms were measured using a Micromeritics TRISTAR 3000 analyzer to determine BET surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution.
- **Findings:** The BET surface area decreased upon cobalt addition to alumina (from 153.2 m²/g for Al₂O₃ to 94.3 m²/g for Co/Al₂O₃). Phosphorus addition slightly increased the surface area (100.0 m²/g for PCo/Al₂O₃) and decreased the average pore diameter (from 95 Å to 88 Å). The pore size distribution remained narrow, with a main peak at ~100 Å and a minor new peak at ~40 Å for P-doped samples.
- **Observations:** The formation of new mesopores in P-containing catalysts is attributed to partial formation of aluminum phosphate, while overall textural changes are modest.

#### 6.1.3 Crystal Phase and Crystallite Size (XRD)
- **Description:** Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to identify crystalline phases and estimate crystallite sizes using the Scherrer equation.
- **Findings:** All catalysts exhibited a characteristic Co₃O₄ reflection at 2θ = 36.88°. The Co₃O₄ crystallite size was 19.9 nm for Co/Al₂O₃, 12.3 nm for PtCo/Al₂O₃, and 12.5 nm for PPtCo/Al₂O₃. Metallic Co⁰ cluster sizes, calculated by applying a contraction factor, were 14.8 nm (Co/Al₂O₃), 9.1 nm (PtCo/Al₂O₃), and 9.3 nm (PPtCo/Al₂O₃).
- **Observations:** The addition of phosphorus and platinum leads to smaller cobalt crystallites, which is beneficial for catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.4 Reducibility (H₂-TPR)
- **Description:** Hydrogen temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) assessed the reducibility of cobalt species, with reduction peaks corresponding to Co₃O₄ → CoO and CoO → Co⁰ transitions.
- **Findings:** Two main reduction peaks were observed. Phosphorus shifted the first reduction peak to higher temperatures, indicating stronger metal–support interactions and hindered reducibility. Platinum addition shifted reduction peaks to lower temperatures, facilitating cobalt reduction.
- **Observations:** Phosphorus decreases the percentage of reducible cobalt, while platinum mitigates this effect by promoting hydrogen spillover.

#### 6.1.5 Cobalt Dispersion and Particle Size (Hydrogen Chemisorption)
- **Description:** Hydrogen chemisorption with pulse reoxidation was used to estimate cobalt dispersion and average cluster size.
- **Findings:** The addition of phosphorus slightly decreased the average cobalt particle size (from 19.2 nm for Co/Al₂O₃ to 17.4 nm for PCo/Al₂O₃) and reduced the percentage of reducible cobalt (from 39.5% to 34.2%). Platinum-containing samples exhibited even smaller particle sizes (~10 nm).
- **Observations:** Phosphorus increases cobalt–support interaction, leading to smaller but less reducible particles; platinum enhances dispersion and reducibility.

#### 6.1.6 Phosphorus Electronic Structure and Interaction (XANES)
- **Description:** X-ray absorption near edge structure (XANES) at the P K-edge probed the electronic environment of phosphorus and its interaction with the support and cobalt.
- **Findings:** In Al₂O₃-supported catalysts, phosphorus primarily interacts with alumina, as indicated by the absence of a pre-edge feature and the presence of post-edge features characteristic of P–Al bonding. In contrast, for SiO₂-supported catalysts, a pre-edge feature was observed, indicating direct P–Co interaction.
- **Observations:** The nature of phosphorus interaction is support-dependent, influencing catalyst structure and performance.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of carbon monoxide transformed by the catalyst, typically expressed as a percentage:
\[
\text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Under Fischer–Tropsch synthesis conditions (220 °C, 300 psi, H₂/CO = 2), the following CO conversion values were observed:
  - Co/Al₂O₃: 43%
  - PCo/Al₂O₃: 23%
  - PtCo/Al₂O₃: 43%
  - PPtCo/Al₂O₃: 31%
  - Co/SiO₂: Initial 40%, decreasing to 35% over time
  - PCo/SiO₂: Initial 33%, increasing to 38% at steady state
- **Observations and Trends:** Phosphorus addition to Co/Al₂O₃ significantly reduced CO conversion due to cobalt site blocking, while platinum mitigated this effect by enhancing cobalt reducibility. On SiO₂, phosphorus improved steady-state CO conversion and stability, indicating a support-dependent promotional effect.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, reflecting the extent of the water-gas shift reaction:
\[
\text{CO₂ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO₂,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** At comparable CO conversions:
  - Co/Al₂O₃: 0.5%
  - PtCo/Al₂O₃: 0.7%
  - PCo/Al₂O₃: 0.9%
  - PPtCo/Al₂O₃: 0.9%
- **Observations and Trends:** Phosphorus slightly increased CO₂ selectivity, while platinum had a minor effect. CO₂ selectivity remained low across all samples, indicating minimal water-gas shift activity.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane, a key indicator of chain termination:
\[
\text{CH₄ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH₄,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** At similar CO conversions:
  - Co/Al₂O₃: 8.1%
  - PtCo/Al₂O₃: 7.6%
  - PCo/Al₂O₃: 10.5%
  - PPtCo/Al₂O₃: 9.8%
- **Observations and Trends:** Phosphorus addition increased methane selectivity, consistent with enhanced chain termination, while platinum slightly reduced methane formation.

#### 6.2.4 C₅⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₅⁺ selectivity represents the percentage of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with five or more carbon atoms, reflecting chain growth efficiency:
\[
\text{C₅⁺ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{C₅⁺,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** At similar CO conversions:
  - Co/Al₂O₃: 81.6%
  - PtCo/Al₂O₃: 84.3%
  - PCo/Al₂O₃: 81.2%
  - PPtCo/Al₂O₃: 80.6%
- **Observations and Trends:** Platinum increased C₅⁺ selectivity, while phosphorus slightly decreased it. On SiO₂, phosphorus also led to a decrease in C₅⁺ selectivity and an increase in methane selectivity.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Probability)
- **Description:** The α factor, derived from the Anderson-Schulz-Flory distribution, indicates the probability of chain growth versus termination (0 < α < 1).
- **Experimental Data:** While explicit α values are not provided, trends are inferred from product distributions: phosphorus addition lowers α (higher methane, lower C₅⁺ selectivity), while platinum increases α (higher C₅⁺ selectivity).
- **Observations and Trends:** The combination of phosphorus and platinum balances chain growth and termination, optimizing product distribution.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time; deactivation rate is the decline in conversion per hour:
\[
\text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Time on Stream}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** On Al₂O₃, phosphorus alone led to lower initial activity but improved stability, while the combination with platinum further enhanced long-term performance. On SiO₂, phosphorus addition resulted in an induction period followed by increased and stabilized CO conversion.
- **Observations and Trends:** Phosphorus hinders cobalt sintering, especially on SiO₂, leading to improved stability. The synergistic effect of phosphorus and platinum yields the most stable catalyst performance.
