## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The synthesis of the Co/MnOx@quasi‑MOF‑74 catalyst is achieved using a hydrothermal method combined with controlled pyrolysis. This method was chosen for its reproducibility, scalability, and ability to retain a high surface area while generating active metallic sites.

#### Hydrothermal Synthesis Method

1. • Precursor Mixing/Co‑precipitation:  
   - Reagents: 0.52 g Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, 0.26 g MnCl₂·4H₂O, and 0.20 g H₄DOT are dissolved in a solvent mixture comprising 55.0 mL DMF, 3.5 mL ethanol, and 3.5 mL water.  
   - Equipment: Magnetic stirring ensures homogeneity.  
   - Observations: A homogeneous solution is obtained, setting the stage for nucleation.

2. • Precipitation–Deposition:  
   - The homogeneous mixture is transferred into a Teflon‑lined autoclave; this step facilitates both controlled precipitation and deposition necessary for crystalline framework formation.  

3. • Hydrothermal Reaction:  
   - Conditions: The autoclave is sealed and heated at 135 °C for 24.0 h.  
   - Observations: Successful formation of deep‑purple CoMn‑MOF‑74 crystals is noted, confirming the formation of the desired precursor.

4. • Drying (Activation):  
   - The synthesized product is washed, then vacuum dried at 40 °C for 12.0 h to remove residual solvents and prepare the material for thermal treatment.

5. • Calcination (Thermal Treatment):  
   - Process: The activated CoMn‑MOF‑74 is subjected to controlled pyrolysis in a quartz tubular reactor under a nitrogen atmosphere using a ceramic fiber oven.  
   - Conditions: Pyrolysis is carried out at 400 °C to yield the Co/MnOx@quasi‑MOF‑74 catalyst.  
   - Observations: FT‑IR and Raman spectroscopy confirm that key aromatic vibrations are retained, indicating partial deligandation while forming active metallic Co° nanoparticles alongside Co²⁺ and Co₂C phases.

### Comparative Analysis

The hydrothermal method offers a high degree of control over crystal formation and framework retention compared to other synthesis techniques. Its simplicity, use of common laboratory equipment (Teflon‑lined autoclave, quartz reactor), and reproducible conditions make it attractive for scale‐up. However, alternative methods like impregnation may simplify metal introduction but often lack the precision in controlling nanoparticle distribution and framework integrity achieved by the hydrothermal approach.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents:**  
  • 0.52 g Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O  
  • 0.26 g MnCl₂·4H₂O  
  • An appropriate stoichiometric amount of 2,5-dihydroxyterephthalic acid (H₄DOT)  
  • A solvent mixture of DMF, ethanol, and water (total volume ≈50 mL)
- **Conditions:**  
  • Continuous stirring at room temperature for 30 minutes  
  • Conducted in a 100 mL Teflon-lined autoclave
- **Process:**  
  1. Dissolve Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, MnCl₂·4H₂O, and H₄DOT in the solvent mixture until a homogeneous solution is achieved.  
  2. Transfer the solution into the autoclave and seal it tightly.  
  3. Place the autoclave in an oven and perform hydrothermal synthesis at 135 °C for 24 hours to form the bimetallic CoMn-MOF-74 precursor.
- **Key Steps:** Ensure complete reagent dissolution and stable hydrothermal conditions for optimal crystallization.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly and Purification
- **Reagents:**  
  • No additional chemicals; uses the as-synthesized CoMn-MOF-74
- **Conditions:**  
  • Wash with fresh methanol repeatedly over a 3-day period until the supernatant is transparent  
  • Vacuum drying at 40 °C for 12 hours
- **Process:**  
  1. Recover the crystalline CoMn-MOF-74 from the reaction mixture.  
  2. Wash the crystals thoroughly with methanol (six sequential washes) to remove unreacted species.  
  3. Dry the purified crystals under vacuum at 40 °C, ensuring all residual solvents are removed.
- **Key Steps:** Emphasize thorough washing and controlled drying to maintain crystallinity and prevent contamination.

##### Step 3: Drying & Calcination (Thermal Treatment)
- **Conditions:**  
  • Heat in a nitrogen atmosphere to prevent oxidation  
  • Ramp rate of 1.0 °C/min until 400 °C is reached; dwell at this temperature for 4 hours  
  • Nitrogen flow maintained at 50 mL/min through a quartz tubular reactor inside a ceramic fiber oven
- **Process:**  
  1. Transfer the dried CoMn-MOF-74 powder into the quartz tubular reactor.  
  2. Initiate the controlled pyrolysis under nitrogen, promoting partial deligandation to in situ generate Co/MnOx nanoparticles while largely preserving the MOF structure.  
  3. Upon completion, allow the material to cool under continuous nitrogen flow.
- **Key Steps:** Precise temperature control and inert atmosphere are critical to retaining high surface area and achieving uniform active nanoparticle formation.

This step-by-step procedure, following the defined workflow (precursor preparation → purification → drying → calcination), ensures reproducibility and confirms the conversion of the MOF precursor to the active Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 catalyst.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The catalyst activation for the Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 synthesis involves a two‐step process that ensures controlled removal of solvents and partial deligandation of the MOF precursor, which is essential for the subsequent formation of well‐dispersed metallic Co nanoparticles along with MnOx components.

#### 5.1.1 Solvent Removal by Vacuum Drying
- **Definition:** This initial activation step is designed to eliminate residual guest and coordinated solvent molecules from the freshly synthesized CoMn‐MOF‐74 precursor, preparing it for controlled thermal treatment.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The precursor is washed thoroughly with fresh methanol and then activated by vacuum drying at 40 °C for 12 hours.
- **Observations:** This gentle drying process retains the integrity of the MOF framework while ensuring that no interfering solvent molecules are present during pyrolysis.

#### 5.1.2 Thermal Treatment (Pyrolysis)
- **Definition:** Pyrolysis functions as the controlled deligandation process whereby a portion of the organic ligands is removed, enabling the in situ formation of Co/MnOx nanoparticles while largely retaining the MOF‐74 framework.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The dried precursor is heated in a quartz tubular reactor housed within a ceramic fiber oven. A heating ramp of 1 °C/min is applied until 400 °C is reached. The catalyst is then maintained at 400 °C for a dwell time of 4 hours under a continuous flow of nitrogen.
- **Observations:** The selected pyrolysis temperature of 400 °C is optimal for generating the quasi‐MOF‐74 structure. This temperature enables sufficient partial deligandation to induce the formation of active Co° nanoparticles while preserving the porosity and framework characteristics critical for higher alcohol synthesis.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Atmosphere and Pressure
- **Definition:** An inert atmosphere is critical during thermal treatment to prevent unwanted oxidation and to control the reduction process of cobalt species.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Activation is performed under a nitrogen atmosphere, with a controlled flow rate of approximately 50 mL/min, and at near‐ambient (atmospheric) pressure.
- **Observations:** The inert gas environment facilitates controlled partial reduction of Co²⁺ to metallic Co° while minimizing particle sintering; the atmospheric conditions ensure reproducibility and uniform activation.

All reported parameters are extracted directly from the catalyst synthesis protocol, ensuring that the activation process is reproducible and directly relevant to the Fischer–Tropsch performance of Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature  
The reaction temperature is a critical parameter that governs the activation of syngas and the conversion pathways in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. In these experiments, the catalytic tests were conducted at an exact temperature of 200 °C. This moderate temperature is chosen to balance the conversion of CO and the selectivity towards higher alcohols while minimizing over-cracking and undesired side reactions.

#### 5.2.2 Reaction Pressure  
Reaction pressure directly influences the concentration of syngas in the reactor and its interaction with the catalyst's active sites. The experiments were performed at a pressure of 3.0 MPa within a fixed-bed reactor configuration. Such a high pressure is essential for enhancing the CO conversion rate and achieving a favorable product distribution, as it ensures adequate reactant adsorption and efficient chain growth mechanisms.

#### 5.2.3 Reaction H₂/CO Ratio  
The H₂/CO ratio is intrinsically linked to the pathway of product formation in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. A ratio of 2:1 was maintained throughout the catalytic evaluation of the Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 catalyst. This stoichiometry is critical for promoting the dissociative activation of CO on the metallic sites while simultaneously facilitating non‐dissociative CO insertion processes. Consequently, it accelerates the formation of the desired C₂+ alcohols, in line with the observed high alcohol selectivity.

#### 5.2.4 Reaction Space-Time Velocity  
Space-time velocity (often quantified as the gaseous hourly space velocity, GHSV) determines the contact time between the gas-phase reactants and the catalyst. A GHSV of 4500 mL/(g_cat·h) was applied during the experiments. This parameter is optimized to provide sufficient residence time for the syngas to convert effectively while limiting the formation of lighter byproducts such as methane. The chosen flow rate supports consistent CO conversion and maintains the steady performance of the catalyst over extended operating periods.

Collectively, these precise reaction conditions—200 °C, 3.0 MPa, an H₂/CO ratio of 2:1, and a GHSV of 4500 mL/(g_cat·h)—ensure that the catalytic performance of the Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 is reproducible, facilitating high selectivity toward higher alcohols under controlled Fischer–Tropsch synthesis conditions.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Composition
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase evolution of the catalyst were investigated using powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5418 Å). XRD is essential for confirming the retention or transformation of the MOF-74 framework and identifying the formation of new crystalline phases during thermal treatment.
- **Findings:** The XRD patterns of Co/MnOx@quasi-MOF-74 (pyrolyzed at 400 °C) closely match those of the parent CoMn-MOF-74, indicating that the MOF-74 framework is largely preserved at this temperature. At higher pyrolysis temperatures (≥500 °C), the framework decomposes, and new peaks corresponding to graphitic carbon, metallic Co, and MnO appear.
- **Observations:** The preservation of the MOF-74 structure at 400 °C is attributed to controlled deligandation under a nitrogen atmosphere, while higher temperatures induce complete carbonization and phase transformation.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms were measured using a Micrometrics ASAP 2460 analyzer to determine the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution. These parameters are critical for assessing the accessibility of active sites and mass transport properties.
- **Findings:** The Co/MnOx@quasi-MOF-74 sample exhibits a BET surface area of approximately 553 m²/g, retaining about 86% of the parent MOF’s surface area. The average pore size and total pore volume are consistent with a mesoporous structure.
- **Observations:** The high surface area and preserved porosity after partial pyrolysis facilitate efficient reactant diffusion and exposure of active sites, which are advantageous for catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.3 Chemical State and Elemental Composition
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to analyze the oxidation states and surface composition of cobalt and manganese species. The evolution of Co species is particularly relevant to catalytic activity.
- **Findings:** XPS spectra reveal that the Co 2p region contains contributions from Co²⁺ and metallic Co⁰. The proportion of metallic Co increases from 18.6% at 400 °C to over 50% at higher pyrolysis temperatures. The presence of Co₂C is also detected, indicating partial carburization.
- **Observations:** The coexistence of metallic Co, Co²⁺, and Co₂C provides a balance between CO dissociation and non-dissociative CO adsorption, which is crucial for selective higher alcohol synthesis.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size Distribution
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), including high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping, were used to examine the morphology, particle size, and elemental distribution.
- **Findings:** The catalyst displays a core–shell morphology with homogeneously distributed Co/MnOx nanoparticles embedded within the quasi-MOF-74 matrix. TEM analysis shows an average nanoparticle size of approximately 8.3 nm, with EDS confirming uniform distribution of Co and Mn.
- **Observations:** The uniform and nanoscale dispersion of active nanoparticles is attributed to the in situ deligandation strategy, which prevents aggregation and ensures high catalytic accessibility.

#### 6.1.5 Framework Integrity and Deligandation
- **Description:** Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) and Raman spectroscopy were utilized to monitor the retention of organic linkers and the extent of carbonization during pyrolysis.
- **Findings:** FT-IR and Raman spectra of the 400 °C sample show characteristic vibrations of the aromatic rings and MOF-74 framework, while samples treated at ≥500 °C exhibit the disappearance of these features and the emergence of D and G bands associated with graphitic carbon.
- **Observations:** Controlled deligandation at 400 °C preserves the quasi-MOF-74 structure, whereas higher temperatures result in complete carbonization and loss of framework features.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction. It is calculated as:
  \[
  \chi_{CO} = \left[1 - \frac{A_{CO,out} \times A_{N2,in}}{A_{CO,in} \times A_{N2,out}}\right] \times 100\%
  \]
  where \(A_{CO,in}\) and \(A_{N2,in}\) are the peak areas of CO and N₂ in the feed, and \(A_{CO,out}\) and \(A_{N2,out}\) are those in the exit gas.
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions (230 °C, 3.0 MPa, GHSV 4500 mL g⁻¹ h⁻¹, H₂/CO = 2), Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 achieves a CO conversion of 21.4%. Increasing the pyrolysis temperature of the precursor enhances CO conversion, with Co/MnOx@C-700 reaching 55.9% and Co@quasi-MOF-74 up to 61.98%. Higher GHSV values reduce conversion (e.g., 10.49% at 15,000 mL g⁻¹ h⁻¹). Stability tests show conversion stabilizing at ~12% (230 °C) and ~30% (250 °C) over 120 hours.
- **Observations:** Enhanced metallic Co content correlates with increased CO conversion, but may compromise selectivity for higher alcohols.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, calculated as:
  \[
  LSC_2 = \frac{CO_{2,out}}{CO_{in} - CO_{out}} \times 100\%
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** For Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74, CO₂ selectivity is exceptionally low at 0.8%. CO₂ is described as "hardly detectable," indicating minimal water–gas shift activity.
- **Observations:** Low CO₂ selectivity demonstrates efficient carbon utilization and minimal side reactions.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  S_{C(i=1)} = \frac{nC_1}{CO_{in} - CO_{out}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CH₄ selectivity for Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 is 10.0%. This value increases with higher calcination temperatures (e.g., 29.8% for Co/MnOx@C-600).
- **Observations:** Low CH₄ selectivity is crucial for maximizing higher alcohol production.

#### 6.2.4 C₂⁺OH and C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₂⁺OH selectivity denotes the fraction of alcohols with two or more carbons among total alcohols, while C₃⁺ selectivity refers to hydrocarbons with three or more carbons. Selectivity is calculated as:
  \[
  S_{C(i=1,2,3,4)} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^4 nC_l}{CO_{in} - CO_{out}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 achieves a total alcohol (ROH) selectivity of 48.7 wt%, with 93.1 wt% of these as C₂⁺OH. C₃⁺ hydrocarbon selectivity is not explicitly quantified but is included in the product distribution tables.
- **Observations:** High C₂⁺OH selectivity underscores the catalyst’s effectiveness for higher alcohol synthesis.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Probability)
- **Description:** The α factor represents the probability of chain growth versus termination, derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory equation:
  \[
  \ln(W_n/n) = n \cdot \ln(\alpha) + \ln\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{\alpha}\right)
  \]
  where \(n\) is the carbon number and \(W_n\) is the mass fraction.
- **Experimental Data:** For Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74, α_ROH (alcohols) ranges from 0.62 to 0.66, and α_RH (hydrocarbons) from 0.70 to 0.78, depending on conditions (e.g., α_ROH = 0.64, α_RH = 0.72 at 230 °C).
- **Observations:** Higher α values are associated with increased chain growth, but excessive metallic Co can shift selectivity toward hydrocarbons.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time. The deactivation rate is:
  \[
  \text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}} \quad (\text{h}^{-1})
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** After an initial drop within 10 hours, CO conversion remains stable (~12% at 230 °C, ~30% at 250 °C) over 120 hours, with negligible further deactivation.
- **Observations:** The catalyst demonstrates excellent long-term stability under continuous operation.

#### 6.2.7 Catalytic Turnover Yield (CTY)
- **Description:** CTY quantifies the amount of CO converted per gram of catalyst per hour.
- **Experimental Data:** Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 achieves a CTY of 117.8 mg_CO g_cat⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 200 °C.
- **Observations:** High CTY reflects the catalyst’s superior activity for syngas conversion.

---

**Summary:**  
Co/MnOx@quasi‐MOF‐74 exhibits a balanced profile of moderate CO conversion, exceptionally high selectivity for higher alcohols (C₂⁺OH), low CH₄ and CO₂ formation, favorable chain growth probability, and robust operational stability. These metrics collectively highlight the effectiveness of the controlled deligandation strategy and the synergy between metallic Co and CUSs in optimizing Fischer–Tropsch–type higher alcohol synthesis.
