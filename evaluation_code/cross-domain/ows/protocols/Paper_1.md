# Crystal Facet Engineering on SrTiO3 Enhances Photocatalytic Overall Water Splitting

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Formula**: SrTiO₃, designed as the primary photocatalyst material.
- **Crystal Structure**: Engineered to form a pure perovskite phase, confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis.
- **Doping & Defect Engineering**: Incorporation of Al dopants is used to influence crystal facet development; controlled calcination (notably at 1423 K) promoted the formation of well-defined facets while potentially inducing oxygen vacancies. (Rationale: To optimize anisotropic charge separation and enhance catalytic efficiency.)
- **Synthesis Environment**: Calcination under oxidizing conditions facilitates the pure perovskite structure and controlled facet exposure, with experimental observations indicating that lower (1123 K) or higher (1523 K) temperatures lead to suboptimal crystal growth and facet morphology.

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **Band Gap & Optical Properties**: Optical measurements via UV-Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (DRS) revealed a slight band gap shift attributed to facet engineering, thereby enhancing visible-light absorption. (Method: Tauc plot analysis, typical of DRS data.)
- **Band Edge Alignment**: Adjustments in band edge positions ensure proper alignment with water redox potentials to drive water splitting, although specific numerical values are not provided.
- **Semiconductor Character**: Mott-Schottky analysis confirms predominantly n-type behavior, which supports efficient charge separation. (Method: Electrochemical impedance techniques measuring flat-band potential characteristics.)

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Nanoparticle Morphology**: Synthesized particles exhibit a cuboctahedron shape with well-defined morphological features as confirmed by Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM).
- **Exposed Facets**: The targeted exposure of {100}, {110}, and {111} facets is achieved by precise control over synthesis parameters – calcination temperature plays a key role in dictating facet evolution. (Observation: Poor control at 1123 K and irregular curvature at 1523 K highlight the importance of selecting 1423 K for optimal morphology.)
- **Heterostructure Formation**: The catalyst system is enhanced by the deposition of co-catalysts employing a two-step photodeposition method, with Rh facilitating hydrogen evolution and metal oxide species (e.g., Cr₂O₃ or CoOOH) promoting oxygen evolution. (Rationale: To create anisotropic charge separation by selective deposition on specific facets.)

## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. **Sol-gel synthesis**: Dissolve **0.1 M** strontium nitrate (Sr(NO₃)₂) and **0.1 M** titanium isopropoxide (Ti(OiPr)₄) in **50 mL** of anhydrous ethanol. (This controlled mixing enhances the exposure of high-energy crystal facets.)
2. **Gelation & Aging**: Stir the precursor solution at **300 rpm** and **60 °C for 6 h** to achieve uniform gelation. Allow the gel to age at room temperature for **24 h** for improved homogeneity.
3. **Drying & Calcination**: Dry the aged gel at **100 °C for 12 h**. Grind the dried gel into a fine powder and calcine it in static air at **1000 °C for 4 h** to crystallize the perovskite SrTiO₃ structure.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. **Annealing for Defect Engineering**: Subject a portion of the calcined SrTiO₃ powder to annealing in a tube furnace under a **forming gas (10% H₂/Ar)** atmosphere at **800 °C for 2 h**. (This thermal treatment is applied to induce oxygen vacancies and optimize charge carrier dynamics.)
2. **Acid Washing**: If required, perform an acid washing step using a dilute acid solution to remove surface impurities before further processing.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. **HER Co-catalyst Deposition (Rh)**: Prepare an **ethanol-based 0.1 M** solution of Rh precursor. Deposit **1 wt.%** Rh on the annealed SrTiO₃ by photodeposition under a **300 W Xe lamp** for **4 h**. (This step is designed to improve hydrogen evolution kinetics.)
2. **OER Co-catalyst Deposition (Cr₂O₃)**: Apply a **5 wt.%** loading of Cr₂O₃ via an incipient wetness impregnation method using a chromium nitrate solution. Dry the impregnated material at **100 °C for 12 h** and subsequently calcine at **300 °C for 2 h in air**. (This treatment facilitates oxygen evolution and enhances overall stability.)

## **3. Characterization Methods & Settings**

### 3.1 Structural & Compositional Analysis

#### X-ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Sample Preparation**: Powder samples were pressed onto a zero-background holder to ensure a flat, uniform surface.
- **Instrument & Model**: A standard lab diffractometer was used; specific model details were not disclosed.
- **Instrument Parameters**: Utilized Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å) typical for such measurements.
- **Measurement Conditions**: Experiments were carried out under ambient temperature and atmospheric pressure.
- **Test/Acquisition Parameters**: A standard 2θ scan range (e.g., from 10° to 80°) with a fine step size was employed.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Load and align the sample on the holder.
  2. Execute a preliminary alignment scan.
  3. Conduct a full scan to capture the diffraction pattern.
- **Purpose**: To identify the crystalline phase, determine lattice parameters, and assess overall phase purity of the facet-engineered SrTiO₃.
- **Data Analysis & Calibration**: Data is processed with Rietveld refinement methods, with calibration done using a silicon standard.
- **Key Notes**: Critical for confirming the formation of the perovskite structure and correlating facet exposure with calcination and annealing conditions.

#### Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)
- **Sample Preparation**: Nanoparticles were dispersed and deposited on carbon-coated TEM grids.
- **Instrument & Model**: High-resolution TEM with HAADF-STEM imaging and EDS mapping capabilities was employed.
- **Measurement Conditions**: Measurements were performed under high vacuum with an accelerating voltage optimized for nanoparticle imaging.
- **Purpose**: To visualize morphological features and confirm the formation of well-defined cubic nanoparticles with high-energy facets.
- **Key Notes**: Complementary EDS mapping provided elemental distribution, verifying homogeneous co-catalyst incorporation.

### 3.2 Photophysical & Electrical Analysis

#### UV-Vis-NIR Diffuse Reflectance Spectroscopy (DRS)
- **Sample Preparation**: Powder samples were packed into a quartz holder, ensuring minimal scattering.
- **Instrument & Model**: Shimadzu UV-3600i Plus spectrophotometer with an integrating sphere attachment was used.
- **Measurement Conditions**: Conducted under ambient conditions using BaSO₄ as the 100% reflectance standard.
- **Test/Acquisition Parameters**: Wavelength range set from 250 to 1200 nm with an acquisition interval of 1 nm.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Record a baseline with BaSO₄.
  2. Replace the reference with the sample and acquire the reflectance spectrum.
- **Purpose**: To determine the optical absorption edge and estimate the band gap energy.
- **Data Analysis & Calibration**: Data converted via the Kubelka-Munk function and band gap evaluated using a Tauc plot.
- **Key Notes**: The method provides insights into facet-induced band gap narrowing, correlating with enhanced photocatalytic performance.

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: One-step overall water splitting.
  
  **For One-step System:**
  - **Photocatalyst**: Facet-engineered SrTiO₃ acts as the single multifunctional catalyst managing both hydrogen and oxygen evolution.
  - **Co-catalyst Roles**: 
    - **Rh**: Serves as the electron collector to promote efficient hydrogen evolution.
    - **Cr₂O₃**: Functions as the hole collector to facilitate oxygen evolution.
  - **Synthesis & Pre-treatment**: The catalyst is synthesized via a controlled hydrothermal process. Key precursors include titanium dioxide and strontium carbonate, followed by calcination at 1000°C in an oxygen atmosphere. Subsequent reduction and annealing treatments help induce targeted defects and optimize crystal facet exposure for improved charge separation.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Absorption Parameters**: The system is irradiated with a 300 W Xe lamp outfitted with an AM 1.5G filter, providing an intensity of approximately 100 mW cm⁻². This setup is designed to optimize visible light absorption critical for photocatalytic efficiency.
- **Reactor and Gas Management**: A top-irradiation reactor configuration is employed under an inert atmosphere (e.g., Argon) to ensure consistent illumination and to minimize any risks associated with gas reactivity. Although specific strategies such as membrane separators were not explicitly detailed, the setup is carefully designed to mitigate the potential for H₂/O₂ mixing.
- **Electrolyte Environment**: A neutral electrolyte, exemplified by 0.05 M Na₂SO₄, supports stable reaction conditions during overall water splitting, ensuring balanced ion transport and system durability.

This integrated design strategy, combining precise facet engineering of SrTiO₃ and the strategic placement of co-catalysts, underpins the system’s capability to achieve efficient overall water splitting in a one-step operational mode.

## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: The facet-engineered SrTiO₃ photocatalyst achieved hydrogen evolution rates in the range of several hundred µmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ under simulated solar irradiation (300 W Xe lamp, AM 1.5G filter, 100 mW cm⁻², 0.05 M Na₂SO₄, Ar atmosphere).
- **O₂ Evolution Rate**: Oxygen evolution rates were consistently half those of hydrogen, maintaining the expected stoichiometry for overall water splitting.
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: The molar ratio of evolved H₂ to O₂ was maintained at approximately 2:1 throughout the experiments, confirming the selectivity for overall water splitting.
- **Apparent Quantum Yield (AQY)**: AQY values were reported at specific wavelengths (e.g., 420 nm), calculated using the formula: `AQY(%) = (2 × number of evolved H₂ molecules / number of incident photons) × 100`, demonstrating efficient photon-to-hydrogen conversion.
- **Solar-to-Hydrogen (STH) Efficiency**: The system exhibited STH efficiencies competitive with leading powder suspension systems, calculated based on total evolved H₂ under AM 1.5G illumination and the incident light energy.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Facet-Dependent Activity**: SEM and TEM analyses confirmed the presence of high-energy {111} and {110} facets, which, according to DFT calculations, promote efficient charge separation and enhance water oxidation activity.
- **Mechanistic Support (DFT Calculations)**: Theoretical studies indicated that the {111} facets of SrTiO₃ possess favorable hole-trapping abilities, correlating with observed facet-selective photooxidation and supporting the proposed reaction pathway.
- **Suppression of Side Reactions**: The use of Cr₂O₃ as a co-catalyst was designed to suppress the reverse reaction (H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O), ensuring high selectivity for water splitting.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability

- **Post-Reaction Characterization**:

  - **Morphological Integrity**: SEM and TEM imaging after reaction showed no significant particle sintering or facet degradation.
