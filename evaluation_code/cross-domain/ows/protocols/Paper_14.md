# High-performance p-Cu2O/n-TaON heterojunction nanorod photoanodes passivated with an ultrathin carbon sheath for photoelectrochemical water splitting†

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Composition**: p-Cu₂O/n-TaON heterojunction nanorod arrays passivated with an ultrathin graphitic carbon sheath (synthesized via vapor‐phase hydrothermal growth and subsequent chemical bath deposition).  
- **Crystal Structure**: Heterojunction nanorod array architecture confirmed by XRD analysis, indicating successful transformation from F–Ta₂O₅ to well-crystallized TaON and crystalline Cu₂O phases.  
- **Doping/Defect Engineering**: Integration of Cu₂O nanoparticles as the p-type component with n-TaON; rationale provided to create an internal electric field via band bending for enhanced electron–hole separation, with selective defect states engineered to improve charge mobility.

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **Band Gap (p-Cu₂O)**: Approximately 2.0 eV determined from UV–Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy, targeting optimal visible-light harvesting.  
- **Band Gap (n-TaON)**: Approximately 2.4 eV obtained via UV–Vis DRS, ensuring alignment with water splitting redox potentials.  
- **Band Edge Positions**: Cu₂O conduction band at ~0.7 eV and TaON at ~0.3 eV (values determined through combined electrochemical measurements and band structure analysis), designed to facilitate both reduction and oxidation reactions during water splitting.  
- **Charge Carrier Characteristics**: p-type behavior for Cu₂O and n-type behavior for TaON confirmed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, ensuring effective charge separation in the heterojunction system.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Nanostructure Morphology**: Vertically aligned nanorods with diameters in the 40–60 nm range, decorated with uniformly distributed Cu₂O nanoparticles sized 3–10 nm, as verified by SEM and TEM.  
- **Surface Area & Passivation**: An ultrathin graphitic carbon sheath (~3 nm thick) formed from a glucose precursor, characterized by Raman spectroscopy (D-band ~1341 cm⁻¹, G-band ~1596 cm⁻¹), providing enhanced surface passivation and electrical conductivity.  
- **Exposed Crystal Facets**: Targeted (111) facets for Cu₂O and (100) facets for TaON to promote effective charge transfer across the heterojunction interface, as evidenced by high-resolution TEM lattice analysis.

## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. **F–Ta₂O₅ Nanorod Fabrication**: Grow F–Ta₂O₅ nanorod arrays on Ta foil via a vapor-phase hydrothermal route by immersing the substrate in a **0.15 M HF** aqueous solution at **180–240 °C for 0–6 hours**.
2. **Nitridation to Form TaON**: Convert the F–Ta₂O₅ nanorods to TaON by heating in an **NH₃ atmosphere at 650–750 °C for 3 hours**.
3. **Cu²⁺ Adsorption via Chemical Bath Deposition**: Immerse the TaON nanorod arrays in an ethanolic solution of **0.04 M Cu(CH₃COO)₂** for **approximately 40 seconds** to adsorb Cu²⁺ ions, leading to the formation of Cu(OH)₂ on the nanorod surfaces.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. **Conversion of Cu(OH)₂ to Cu₂O**: Anneal the Cu(OH)₂/TaON system in an inert **N₂ atmosphere at 500 °C for 2 hours** to transform the adsorbed Cu(OH)₂ into Cu₂O nanoparticles (acting as the p-type component of the heterojunction).

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. **Graphitic Carbon Coating**: Immerse the Cu₂O/TaON nanorod arrays in a glucose solution serving as the carbon precursor, and ultrasonicate the system to ensure a uniform coating.
2. **Formation of Ultrathin Carbon Sheath**: After rinsing and vacuum drying to remove excess precursor, anneal the coated arrays in an inert **N₂ atmosphere at 500 °C for 2 hours** to generate a graphitic carbon shell of approximately **3 nm thickness** (providing enhanced surface passivation and conductivity).

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: Two-step (Z-scheme).  
  **For Z-scheme System:**  
- **Hydrogen Evolution Photocatalyst (HEP)**: p-Cu₂O, serving as the electron donor in the heterojunction structure.  
- **Oxygen Evolution Photocatalyst (OEP)**: n-TaON, functioning as the electron acceptor to drive oxygen evolution.  
- **Mediator Type**: Solid mediator.  
  * **Solid Mediator**: Ultrathin graphitic carbon sheath (~3 nm thick) that enhances charge transport between the p-Cu₂O and n-TaON, ensuring effective electron transfer and contributing to photocorrosion prevention.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Intended Light Absorption Range**:  
  - **Visible-Light Management**: The integrated system is engineered for enhanced visible-light absorption. The heterojunction design, bolstered by the carbon layer, maximizes the uptake of photons, with notable performance reaching a maximum IPCE of 59% at 400 nm.  
- **Reactor and Gas Management**:  
  - **Gas Evolution Safety**: The photoelectrochemical cell operates in a sealed configuration, ensuring that evolved hydrogen and oxygen gases do not mix.  
  - **Separation Strategy**: A two-electrode configuration is implemented with a dedicated photoanode (carbon–Cu₂O/TaON) and a Pt counter electrode, together with an Ar-purged atmosphere to maintain inert conditions during operation.  
- **Operational Parameters for Enhanced Charge Transfer**:  
  - **Charge Transport Efficiency**: The carbon sheath not only protects the semiconductor surface from photocorrosion but also facilitates rapid electron conduction between Cu₂O and TaON, thereby improving charge-transfer kinetics.  
- **Additional Safety Measures**:  
  - The system’s design incorporates precise sealing and inert gas management (via Ar purging) to ensure both high performance and operational safety during overall water splitting, as validated by gas chromatography in monitoring nearly stoichiometric H₂/O₂ production.

This configuration, combining a well-defined Z-scheme with an integrated solid mediator, underpins the system’s high efficiency and durability in photoelectrochemical water splitting.

## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: 46.5 μmol cm⁻² generated in 60 minutes under AM 1.5G simulated sunlight (100 mW cm⁻²) at 1.0 V vs. RHE, using a carbon–Cu₂O/TaON photoanode (active area ≈1 cm²) in 0.5 M NaOH (pH 13.6).
- **O₂ Evolution Rate**: 23.1 μmol cm⁻² under identical conditions, measured concurrently with H₂ evolution.
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: The molar ratio of evolved gases was close to the theoretical 2:1, confirming overall water splitting.
- **Faradaic Efficiency**: 93% for H₂ and 90% for O₂, determined by comparing the amount of charge passed to the quantity of gas evolved, indicating high conversion efficiency.
- **Incident Photon-to-Current Efficiency (IPCE)**: Maximum of 59% at 400 nm, with values between 3% and 20% at other wavelengths, calculated as `IPCE(%) = (1240 × photocurrent density) / (incident light power density × wavelength) × 100`.
- **Photocurrent Density**: 3.06 mA cm⁻² at 1.0 V vs. RHE under AM 1.5G illumination, demonstrating efficient photoelectrochemical activity.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Stoichiometric Gas Evolution**: The observed H₂:O₂ ratio (2:1) and high Faradaic efficiencies confirm that both gases originate from water splitting, with negligible side reactions.
- **p–n Heterojunction Effect**: Enhanced photocurrent and IPCE values, as well as a red shift in the absorption edge (UV–Vis DRS), support efficient charge separation due to the Cu₂O/TaON interface.
- **Carbon Sheath Function**: Raman spectroscopy (D-band at ~1341 cm⁻¹, G-band at ~1596 cm⁻¹) confirms the presence of graphitic carbon, which passivates the surface and suppresses photocorrosion, contributing to selectivity and durability.
- **Absence of Isotope Labeling**: No isotope tracing was reported; selectivity is inferred from stoichiometry and Faradaic efficiency.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Continuous Operation**: The carbon–Cu₂O/TaON electrode retained 87.3% of its initial photocurrent after 60 minutes of continuous illumination at 1.0 V vs. RHE, indicating robust photostability.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**:
  - **XPS**: Elemental analysis after PEC testing showed persistent Ta, O, N, C, and Cu signals with binding energies consistent with Ta⁵⁺ and Cu⁺ (Cu₂O); only trace CuO detected, indicating minimal surface oxidation.
  - **XRD**: No significant changes in phase structure or crystallite size of TaON after modification and PEC operation, confirming structural integrity.
- **Photocorrosion Suppression**: The ultrathin carbon sheath effectively mitigated degradation, as evidenced by stable phase and surface composition post-reaction.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Dark Control**: The system was tested in the dark and after Ar purging; no H₂ or O₂ evolution was detected, confirming the reaction is light-driven.
- **Blank Test**: Prior to Faradaic efficiency measurements, the cell was purged with Ar for 30 minutes, and gas chromatography confirmed the absence of background O₂ or N₂, ensuring all detected gases originated from water splitting.
- **Gas-Tightness Verification**: The reactor was sealed and checked for leaks under Ar atmosphere before measurements, ensuring system integrity.
- **Statistical Reporting**: No explicit mention of replicate numbers, error bars, or standard deviations was provided for the performance data.
- **Instrumental Calibration**: Gas chromatography (GC-3240, TCD, Ar carrier) was used for gas quantification; calibration details were not specified but standard protocols are implied.

This comprehensive evaluation demonstrates that the carbon–Cu₂O/TaON photoanode achieves high activity, selectivity, and stability for overall water splitting under simulated solar conditions, with rigorous controls validating the reliability of the reported data.
