2. Heat the precursor mixture in an alumina crucible under flux-Y conditions at temperatures of **900 °C, 1000 °C, or 1100 °C**. (Although the exact duration is not specified, heating is performed sufficiently to crystallize the SrTiO₃ phase.)
3. Cool the material and subsequently perform a drying step to remove any residual solvents, followed by grinding to yield a uniform Al-doped SrTiO₃ powder suitable for further modification.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. Subject the calcined powder to an additional thermal treatment to adjust the defect state. Perform a post-synthesis calcination at **800 °C for 2–6 h** in an inert atmosphere (e.g., argon or nitrogen) to limit oxidation while enhancing charge separation properties. (This post-treatment aids in achieving the desired structural and electronic modifications.)

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. Deposit the secondary catalytic phase via photodeposition to create the Rh₂Ycryo₃ co-catalyst layer. Prepare an aqueous solution containing Rh and Cr precursors designed for **0.1 wt% Rh and 0.1 wt% Cr** loading. 
2. Disperse the post-treated powder in the solution and irradiate it using a **300 W Xe lamp (λ > 300 nm)** at room temperature for **2–4 h**. (This step ensures uniform photodeposition, enhancing effective electron/hole separation and catalytic activity.)
3. Following photodeposition, dry the material under controlled conditions to stabilize the co-catalyst layers, ensuring optimal interface formation between the Al-doped SrTiO₃ base and the Rh/Cr sites.
