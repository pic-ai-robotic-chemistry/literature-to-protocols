# Photocatalyst releasing hydrogen from water

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Formula**: (Ga₁–ₓZnₓ)(N₁–ₓOₓ) with sample compositions corresponding to x values of 0.12, 0.16, and 0.42 (as described for samples A, B, and C).
- **Crystal Structure/Phase**: A solid solution of GaN and ZnO confirmed by powder X-ray diffraction; the formation of this structure supports the desired visible-light absorption.
- **Doping Strategy & Defects**: Zn doping is employed and combined with engineered defects achieved via a solid-state nitridation of Ga₂O₃ and ZnO under flowing NH₃. This approach modifies the photocatalyst’s electronic structure to enhance the hydrogen evolution site.
- **Rationale**: The controlled doping and defect engineering optimize light absorption and improve charge separation, critical for efficient water splitting.

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **Band Gap**: Tunable band gap ranging from 2.68 eV to 2.43 eV, determined through diffuse reflectance spectroscopy. The reduction in band gap with increased Zn content enhances visible light absorption.
- **Band Edge Position Adjustment**: While specific potentials are not detailed, the adjustment in band structure through Zn doping naturally shifts the absorption edge (approximately 510 nm for sample A), tailoring the material for optimal photovoltaic performance.
- **Electronic Properties**: The engineered structure supports enhanced charge separation and visible-light activity, contributing to overall improved water splitting efficiency.
- **Rationale**: Tuning the band gap aligns the photocatalyst’s electronic properties with the solar spectrum, ensuring effective utilization of visible light.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Physical Form & Support**: The photocatalyst is prepared as a powder to maximize surface area and promote effective reactant interaction.
- **Co-catalyst Deposition**: Uniformly deposited Rh–Cr mixed oxide nanoparticles (10–20 nm) are integrated via post-synthesis impregnation followed by heating at 350°C for 1 hour; SEM and TEM confirm their even distribution.
- **Composite Architecture**: The heterostructured interface between the (Ga₁–ₓZnₓ)(N₁–ₓOₓ) matrix and the Rh–Cr nanoparticles enables synergistic promotion of both hydrogen and oxygen evolution.
- **Synthesis Conditions**: The controlled nitridation process (e.g., 950°C for 0.25 h, 850°C for 15 h, and 950°C for 1.5 h for samples A, B, and C respectively) followed by cooling in an NH₃ atmosphere ensures phase purity and optimized morphology.
- **Rationale**: The tailored morphology and precise nanoparticle deposition enhance charge separation and photocatalytic efficiency for overall water splitting.


## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. Combine precursor powders by mixing Ga₂O₃ and ZnO in the required stoichiometric ratio.  
2. Conduct solid‐state nitridation by heating the powder mixture under a continuous flow of **NH₃ (250 mL/min)**. Employ distinct conditions for different samples:  
   • For Sample A: heat at **950 °C for 0.25 h**.  
   • For Sample B: heat at **850 °C for 15 h**.  
   • For Sample C: heat at **950 °C for 1.5 h**.  
3. Cool the nitrided samples to room temperature while maintaining the **NH₃** atmosphere to preserve the desired phase and prevent oxidation.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. No intermediate chemical or thermal post-treatment was applied to alter the defect state or morphology of the as-prepared (Ga₁–ₓZnₓ)(N₁–ₓOₓ) solid solution prior to surface functionalization.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. Prepare an impregnation solution by dissolving **Na₃RhCl₆·2H₂O** and **Cr(NO₃)₃·9H₂O** in **3–4 mL** of distilled water.  
2. Introduce **0.3–0.4 g** of the as-synthesized photocatalyst into the solution and stir continuously with a glass rod to ensure homogeneous mixing.  
3. Evaporate the solvent under constant stirring until a uniform solid mixture is obtained.  
4. Calcine the dried mixture in air at **350 °C for 1 h** to decompose the precursors and form uniformly distributed rhodium–chromium mixed oxide nanoparticles (with a target particle size of **10–20 nm**) on the photocatalyst surface.

## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **Hydrogen Evolution Rate**: Sustained stoichiometric H₂ evolution observed over a 35-hour period, with a total evolved gas (H₂ + O₂) of 16.2 mmol, exceeding the catalyst amount (3.7 mmol) under visible-light irradiation (300 W Xe lamp with cutoff filter; 0.3 g catalyst in 370 mL pH 4.5 H₂SO₄).
- **Oxygen Evolution Rate**: O₂ evolution tracked concurrently with H₂, maintaining the expected stoichiometry throughout the reaction.
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: Maintained at 2:1 from the onset of the reaction, as confirmed by gas chromatography, indicating true overall water splitting.
- **Quantum Efficiency (QE)**: 2.5% at 420–440 nm (measured with a calibrated Si photodiode and monochromatic light), and 3.0 ± 0.2% at 400 nm (band pass filter), calculated as Φ (%) = (2 × number of evolved H₂ molecules / number of incident photons) × 100.
- **Enhanced O₂ Evolution (Control)**: In the presence of AgNO₃ as a sacrificial electron acceptor, QE for O₂ evolution increased to 51% at 420–440 nm, demonstrating the system’s intrinsic capability for high activity when electron–hole recombination is suppressed.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Product Origin Confirmation**: Stoichiometric evolution of H₂ and O₂ in a 2:1 ratio throughout the reaction period supports water as the sole source of both gases.
- **Suppression of Side Reactions**: No evidence of undesired side reactions or byproduct formation was detected; the use of Rh–Cr mixed oxide co-catalyst was critical, as individual Rh or Cr oxides did not yield comparable activity.
- **Mechanistic Controls**: The dramatic increase in O₂ evolution with AgNO₃ addition confirms that the observed O₂ in standard OWS arises from water oxidation, not from catalyst decomposition or other sources.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Long-Term Operation**: Photocatalyst maintained activity without noticeable degradation over repeated 35-hour runs, indicating robust photostability under continuous visible-light irradiation.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**: Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD), energy-dispersive X-ray analysis (EDAX), and diffuse reflectance spectroscopy performed after catalysis confirmed retention of the solid solution structure, composition, and optical properties (band gap and absorption edge unchanged).
- **Morphological Integrity**: Scanning and transmission electron microscopy (SEM/TEM) revealed that Rh–Cr mixed oxide nanoparticles (10–20 nm) remained uniformly distributed on the catalyst surface post-reaction.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Mass Balance Validation**: The total evolved gas volume exceeded the catalyst amount, and the H₂:O₂ ratio was monitored throughout, confirming accurate mass balance and reaction stoichiometry.
- **Air Removal Protocol**: The reaction system was evacuated multiple times prior to irradiation to eliminate atmospheric gases, ensuring that all detected H₂ and O₂ originated from water splitting.
- **Gas Analysis**: Evolved gases were quantified using gas chromatography in a closed gas circulation system, providing reliable and reproducible measurements.
- **Statistical Reporting**: Quantum efficiency at 400 nm is reported as 3.0 ± 0.2%, indicating inclusion of standard deviation; however, the number of replicates (n) is not specified.
- **Comparative Controls**: Use of AgNO₃ as a sacrificial acceptor served as a positive control, highlighting the system’s potential and confirming the limiting step in OWS is water oxidation under standard conditions.
