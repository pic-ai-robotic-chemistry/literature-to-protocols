# Photocatalytic water splitting with a quantum efficiency of almost unity

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

- **Nominal Formula**: Al-doped SrTiO₃ (SrTiO₃:Al).
- **Target Crystal Structure**: Perovskite structure, designed to provide robust framework and favorable charge transport properties.
- **Doping Strategy**: Substitution of Al for Ti in the SrTiO₃ lattice, intended to tailor the electronic structure and improve charge carrier dynamics.
- **Engineered Defects**: Potential presence of oxygen vacancies to promote charge separation, as indicated by defect engineering in the material design.
- **Rationale**: Al doping is employed to optimize photocatalytic properties by modifying the band structure and enhancing the efficiency of water splitting through improved charge separation.

- **Target Electronic Properties**: Aimed at achieving a reduced band gap to improve visible-light absorption and align the band edge positions with water redox potentials.
- **Band Edge Alignment**: Designed to support efficient separation of photoexcited electrons and holes, facilitating effective hydrogen and oxygen evolution.
- **Measurement Approaches**: Intended use of methods such as Tauc plot analysis (from UV-Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy data) and Mott-Schottky measurements to determine the band gap and flat-band potentials (specific values are not explicitly provided).
- **Charge Carrier Optimization**: The electronic tuning via Al doping is expected to enhance carrier mobility and minimize recombination losses, contributing to the nearly unity external quantum efficiency reported.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Crystallographic Facets**: Emphasis on well-defined {100} facets for the selective deposition of core–shell Rh/Cr₂O₃ structures, with CoOOH deposited predominantly on ⟨110⟩ facets.
- **Morphological Control**: The design ensures uniform particle shape and controlled size distribution to maximize active surface area and enhance catalytic performance.
- **Heterostructure Formation**: The formation of Rh/Cr₂O₃ core–shell architectures is critical for selective electron accumulation, while facet-specific CoOOH deposition promotes the oxygen evolution reaction.
- **Characterization Techniques**: Electron microscopy (SEM and STEM) confirms the optimized distribution of cocatalysts on designated facets, underpinning the strategic material design for effective overall water splitting.

## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. Disperse **0.1 g** of the Al-doped SrTiO₃ (SrTiO₃:Al) photocatalyst in **100 mL** of distilled water using sonication to ensure a homogeneous suspension.
2. Prepare aqueous precursor solutions with a concentration of **2 mg·mL⁻¹** for each metal source: RhCl₃·6H₂O for Rh, K₂CrO₄ for Cr, and Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O for Co. Use high-purity reagents as referenced in the Materials chapter.
3. Establish a controlled photodeposition environment by keeping the suspension under ambient laboratory conditions, ensuring that the catalyst remains well dispersed throughout the process.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. In this protocol, no additional bulk post-synthesis thermal or chemical treatments are performed on the base SrTiO₃:Al material. The approach relies on the direct implementation of photodeposition for catalyst functionalization without a separate annealing or chemical modification step.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. Initiate the co-catalyst deposition by adding **50 μL** of the freshly prepared RhCl₃·6H₂O solution to the dispersion to target a **0.1 wt%** Rh loading. Immediately stir and irradiate the suspension with a **300 W Xe lamp for 10 minutes** to reduce Rh³⁺ to Rh⁰ and deposit it on selected facets.
2. Introduce **25 μL** of the K₂CrO₄ solution, corresponding to a **0.05 wt%** Cr target loading, and continue magnetic stirring. Irradiate the mixture for an additional **5 minutes** so that Cr₂O₃ forms a conformal shell around the Rh nanoparticles.
3. Add the Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O precursor solution in a predetermined volume sufficient for the desired Co loading. Maintain continuous stirring and irradiate for a further **5 minutes** to achieve the selective deposition of CoOOH on distinct facets.
4. Ensure that all photodeposition steps occur sequentially in the same vessel without any solvent exchange to preserve the controlled deposition environment and to promote the formation of a well-defined heterostructure beneficial for overall water splitting performance.

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: One-step overall water splitting.
  - **For One-step System:**  
    - **Photocatalyst Material**: Al-doped SrTiO₃ (SrTiO₃:Al), serving as the sole platform for both half-reactions.  
    - **Modification Strategy**: Sequential photodeposition was employed to load co-catalysts.  
    - **Rh Deposition**: Rh is introduced first (using an RhCl₃·6H₂O solution at 1% loading) to form the active sites that promote hydrogen evolution.  
    - **Cr₂O₃ Formation**: Subsequent deposition of Cr precursor (via K₂CrO₄ solution at 0.05 wt% Cr) facilitates the formation of a core–shell structure with Rh, which enhances charge separation by preventing unwanted oxygen reduction on the metal core.  
    - **CoOOH Addition**: Final photodeposition of Co precursor (Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O) forms cobalt oxyhydroxide, which is specifically responsible for catalyzing the oxygen evolution reaction.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Source & Management**:  
  - **Light Source**: A 300 W Xe lamp (full arc) is used to simulate sunlight.  
  - **Targeted Light Absorption Range**: The system is optimized for effective absorption in the visible region, approximately 400–700 nm, ensuring high external quantum efficiency.
- **Reactor and Safety Features**:  
  - **Reaction Medium**: The photocatalytic reaction is conducted in a vessel containing 100 ml of distilled water, ensuring a homogeneous dispersion of the modified photocatalyst achieved via sonication.  
  - **Gas Evolution and Analysis**: Evolved H₂ and O₂ gases are monitored using gas chromatography.  
  - **Safety and Gas Separation Approach**: The reactor is designed with measures to maintain distinct collection zones for H₂ and O₂, using controlled gas flow and sealed reaction conditions to prevent gas crossover and ensure stoichiometric production without the need for sacrificial agents.

This detailed configuration underscores a one-step, single-photocatalyst strategy that integrates precise material modifications with optimized light management and reactor safety, contributing to efficient overall water splitting.


## **5. Testing System Configuration**

### 5.1 Reactor & Gas Handling
- **Reactor Type & Volume**: The photocatalytic overall water splitting (OWS) experiments were conducted in a **quartz reactor** with a total internal volume of **1.2 liters**, inclusive of all gas circulation loops. The use of quartz ensures high optical transparency for UV-visible irradiation and chemical inertness.
- **Temperature Control**: The reactor temperature was precisely maintained at **25 °C** using a **water jacket** connected to a thermostatic circulator, ensuring consistent reaction kinetics and gas solubility.
- **Pressure Control**: The system operated at **atmospheric pressure** throughout the measurements.
- **Gas Handling Protocol**:
  1. **Purging**: Prior to each experiment, the reactor and circulation lines were purged **three times** with **high-purity argon gas** to eliminate residual air and moisture.
  2. **Circulation**: Gas within the system was circulated using a **diaphragm pump** at a flow rate of **100 mL min⁻¹**, promoting homogeneous gas distribution and efficient sampling.
  3. **Sampling**: **Gas samples** were withdrawn from a dedicated sampling port using a **gas-tight syringe** at **30-minute intervals** for quantitative analysis.
- **Gas Analysis (GC)**: Evolved **H₂ and O₂** were quantified by **gas chromatography (GC)**. The GC system was equipped with a suitable column and detector for hydrogen and oxygen analysis; calibration was performed using standard gas mixtures to ensure accurate quantification.
- **GC Calibration**: Calibration curves were established with certified standard mixtures of H₂ and O₂ in argon, enabling precise determination of gas evolution rates.

### 5.3 Reaction Medium

## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **Hydrogen Evolution Rate**: The Al-doped SrTiO₃ (SrTiO₃:Al) photocatalyst, sequentially loaded with Rh, Cr₂O₃, and CoOOH, achieved overall water splitting with H₂ and O₂ evolution at a stoichiometric ratio under full arc illumination from a 300 W Xe lamp (100 mL distilled water, no sacrificial agents).
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: The evolved gases consistently exhibited a molar ratio of approximately 2:1 (H₂:O₂), directly confirming the expected stoichiometry for overall water splitting.
- **External Quantum Efficiency (EQE)**: Achieved 95–96% at 350–360 nm, measured under monochromatic irradiation; this corresponds to an internal quantum efficiency (IQE) approaching unity, calculated as `EQE (%) = (number of electrons contributing to H₂ and O₂ evolution / number of incident photons) × 100`.
- **Solar-to-Hydrogen (STH) Efficiency**: Reported under simulated sunlight, with the system demonstrating high efficiency, though the exact STH value is not specified.
- **Comparative Performance**: Sequential photodeposition of cocatalysts resulted in 2–2.5 times higher reaction rates compared to traditional coimpregnation methods, highlighting the impact of precise cocatalyst placement.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Isotope Labeling Evidence**: H₂¹⁸O isotope labeling experiments, analyzed by mass spectrometry, confirmed that evolved O₂ originated exclusively from water, as indicated by the detection of ¹⁸O₂ in the product gas.
- **Facet-Selective Catalysis**: Electron microscopy (SEM, STEM, EDX) and selected-area electron diffraction demonstrated that Rh/Cr₂O₃ nanoparticles were deposited on {100} facets (promoting HER), while CoOOH was localized on ⟨110⟩ facets (promoting OER), supporting spatial separation of reduction and oxidation sites.
- **Suppression of Back-Reaction**: The Cr₂O₃ shell on Rh nanoparticles inhibited the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR), preventing backward electron transfer and ensuring selectivity for water splitting.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Continuous Operation**: The photocatalyst maintained 94% of its initial activity after 12.5 hours of continuous illumination, indicating robust operational stability.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**: X-ray diffraction (XRD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed that the crystal structure and morphology of the catalyst remained unchanged after prolonged reaction, evidencing material integrity.
- **Leaching Analysis**: No significant leaching of Sr, Ti, or cocatalyst components was detected post-reaction, as determined by solution analysis (e.g., ICP-MS).

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Dark Control**: No H₂ or O₂ evolution was observed in the absence of light, confirming the strictly light-driven nature of the reaction.
- **Blank Reactor Control**: Experiments conducted without the photocatalyst yielded negligible gas evolution, further validating the necessity of the catalyst for activity.
- **Gas Analysis**: Real-time monitoring of H₂ and O₂ was performed using gas chromatography equipped with a thermal conductivity detector and argon carrier gas, ensuring accurate quantification.
- **Data Reproducibility**: All performance metrics were averaged over at least three independent experiments, with error bars representing standard deviation, underscoring the reliability and reproducibility of the reported data.
