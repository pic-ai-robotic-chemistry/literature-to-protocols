# An electron-hole rich dual-site nickel catalyst for efficient photocatalytic overall water splitting

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Composition**: Ni₂P/NiS@PCOS, representing a dual-site system combining nickel phosphide (Ni₂P), nickel sulfide (NiS), and a polymeric carbon–oxygen semiconductor (PCOS) (as confirmed by XRD analysis).  
- **Crystalline Phases**: Distinct phases of crystalline Ni₂P and NiS identified through XRD, ensuring well-defined structural properties.  
- **Doping and Defects**: Incorporation of nitrogen and sulfur dopants into the PCOS layer, introduced via calcination of L‑cysteine (rationale: to enhance charge density and catalytic performance).  
- **Design Rationale**: The heterojunction design with electron‑rich Ni₂P–NiS sites and hole‑rich Ni₂P–PCOS sites is intended to promote efficient charge separation for overall water splitting.

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **Electronic Characterization Methods**: Band structure and energetics assessed using ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS with He I, 21.22 eV), Mott–Schottky plots, and cyclic voltammetry (CV) to determine band edge positions.  
- **Charge Carrier Features**: The dual‑site design strategically exploits electron‑rich and hole‑rich domains to facilitate hydrogen evolution at Ni₂P–NiS sites and oxygen evolution at Ni₂P–PCOS sites.  
- **Band Gap Analysis**: While an explicit numerical band gap is not provided, standard techniques such as UV–vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (DRS) and Mott–Schottky analysis were employed to investigate the semiconductor’s electronic properties.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Morphological Features**: Transformation from NiS nanosheets to nanoparticles (~30 nm diameter) observed by TEM post‑phosphorization, indicating significant morphological evolution.  
- **Heterostructure Formation**: High‑resolution TEM revealed a coherent interface between Ni₂P and the PCOS overlayer, confirming effective heterojunction integration.  
- **Surface and Structural Analysis**: Raman spectroscopy indicated typical D (defect) and G (graphitization) bands with an average I_D/I_G ratio near 0.80, while FT‑IR spectroscopy detected C–N, C–S, and C–O groups; these results collectively validate a defect‑rich, doped carbon structure.  
- **Design Rationale**: Engineered morphology and heterostructuring are critical for optimizing charge transport pathways and enhancing overall photocatalytic activity.

## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. **Hydrothermal Preparation**: Dissolve **2 mmol NiCl₂** and **2 mmol L‑cysteine** in **25 mL deionized water**. Stir the solution for **6 h** at room temperature.  
2. **Nanosheet Formation**: Slowly inject a pre‐prepared Na₂S solution into the mixture over **30 minutes** while continuing the stirring. Maintain the stirring for an additional **10 h** to ensure complete reaction.  
3. **Autoclave Reaction**: Transfer the resulting mixture into a Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave and heat at **110 °C for 2 h** (forming NiS@L‑cysteine nanosheets). After the reaction, perform filtration and multiple washings with deionized water to remove impurities.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. **Phosphorization Process**: Mix the washed NiS@L‑cysteine nanosheets with a precise amount of sodium hypophosphite hydrate (ranging from **0.1 g to 0.9 g** to tune the catalyst composition).  
2. **Calcination for Defect Engineering**: Load the mixture into a porcelain boat and wrap it in aluminum foil. Heat it in a tube furnace under nitrogen flow (**20 mL/min**) with a constant ramp rate of **5 °C/min** until reaching **350 °C**, then maintain the temperature for **180 minutes**. This step transforms the NiS@L‑cysteine nanosheets into Ni₂P/NiS with simultaneous defect engineering.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. **PCOS Formation via Pyrolysis**: During the calcination step, the incorporated L‑cysteine simultaneously converts into a polymeric carbon–oxygen semiconductor (PCOS) which serves as a protective and charge-regulating surface layer.  
2. **Integrated Functionalization**: The process results in the formation of a Ni₂P/NiS@PCOS heterojunction, where the PCOS layer enhances both HER and OER activities by providing abundant interfacial active sites and facilitating charge separation.

These sequential procedures—starting with the hydrothermal synthesis of NiS@L‑cysteine nanosheets, followed by phosphorization under controlled thermal conditions, and culminating in the formation of the PCOS layer—ensure a robust, dual-site nickel catalyst tailored for efficient overall water splitting.

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: One-step overall water splitting.
  - **For One-step System:**  
    - **Photocatalyst Material**: Ni₂P/NiS@PCOS, integrating nickel phosphides (Ni₂P), nickel sulfide (NiS), and a polymeric carbon–oxygen semiconductor (PCOS).  
    - **Functional Design**: The catalyst is engineered to provide dual-site functionality where NiS serves as the electron-rich site for hydrogen evolution and Ni₂P with PCOS functions as the hole-rich site for oxygen evolution.  
    - **Synthesis Background**: The catalyst is prepared via a one-step hydrothermal and subsequent phosphorization (calcination) process, ensuring the formation of crystalline Ni₂P alongside residual NiS and a doped carbon overlayer (PCOS).

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Management**:  
  - **Target Wavelength Range**: Visible-light irradiation with wavelengths greater than 420 nm, ensuring optimal absorption for effective photocatalysis.
  - **Light Source Details**: Simulated solar conditions (using a Xe lamp under AM conditions) facilitate efficient activation of the Ni₂P/NiS@PCOS system.
- **Gas Handling & Safety Measures**:  
  - **Gas Production and Separation**: The structural design naturally allocates separate reaction sites for H₂ and O₂ evolution. This inherent separation minimizes gas mixing, providing a nearly stoichiometric production ratio (approximately 2:1 H₂:O₂).  
  - **Reactor Configuration**: The photocatalytic experiments are conducted in a single, sealed reactor under a neutral aqueous environment, preventing cross-interference between the gaseous products without the need for additional physical membranes.
- **Operational Parameters**:  
  - **System Setup**: Approximately 100 mg of photocatalyst is dispersed in 100 mL of deionized water, with continuous stirring for uniform light exposure and reaction kinetics enhancement.  
  - **Performance Metrics**: H₂ and O₂ evolution rates are recorded at approximately 150.7 μmol·h⁻¹ and 70.2 μmol·h⁻¹, respectively, which are validated by gas chromatography techniques.

This detailed extraction encapsulates the overall design strategy of the one-step system, directly addressing the integration of functional materials, targeted operational conditions, and inherent safety measures by ensuring spatial separation of evolved gases through the catalyst’s dual-site configuration.


## **5. Testing System Configuration**

### 5.1 Reactor & Gas Handling
- **Reactor System**: Photocatalytic overall water splitting was conducted in a Labsolar-6A all-glass automatic inline trace gas analysis system. The reaction vessel accommodated 100 mg of photocatalyst dispersed in 100 mL deionized water, with 10 mg MnO₂ added to decompose any H₂O₂ byproducts.
- **Atmosphere Control**: Prior to irradiation, the reactor was degassed by vacuum-drawing for 10 minutes at a vacuum pressure of 13 kPa to remove residual O₂, ensuring an inert environment. Argon (Ar) was used as the carrier and purge gas throughout the experiment.
- **Gas Handling Protocol**:
  1. **Degassing**: The system was evacuated and purged with Ar to eliminate atmospheric gases.
  2. **Sealing**: The reactor was sealed post-degassing to maintain an inert atmosphere during irradiation.
  3. **Sampling & Analysis**: Evolved gases (H₂ and O₂) were continuously transported from the reactor headspace by the Ar carrier gas directly to an on-line gas chromatograph (Beifen-Ruili, SP-2100A, China) for real-time quantification.
- **Gas Chromatography**: The GC system was integrated inline with the reactor, utilizing Ar as the carrier gas. Quantification of H₂ and O₂ was performed automatically, with three independent experiments conducted to ensure reproducibility. Error bars in reported data reflect standard deviations.
- **Contamination Control**: The closed, all-glass system and inline GC analysis minimized atmospheric contamination and gas loss, supporting accurate and reproducible gas quantification.

### 5.2 Illumination Conditions
- **Light Source**: A 300 W xenon (Xe) arc lamp was employed as the irradiation source.
- **Spectral Output**: Simulated solar illumination was achieved using a total reflection filter and an AM 1.5G filter, delivering a spectral range of λ > 420 nm for overall water splitting. For AQE measurements, bandpass filters at 420, 475, and 550 nm were used.
- **Irradiance**: The incident light intensity at the reactor window was set to 100 mW cm⁻². The illumination area was precisely adjusted to 10 cm² to ensure uniform exposure and minimize measurement error.
- **Uniformity Verification**: Light intensity was measured at multiple points across the reactor surface using a calibrated photodetector or radiometer, confirming consistent irradiance over the catalyst suspension.
- **Illumination Geometry**: The reactor was irradiated from the top, ensuring even light distribution across the photocatalyst.

### 5.3 Reaction Medium
- **Aqueous Phase**: The reaction medium consisted of 100 mL deionized water (high resistivity), with 100 mg of the Ni₂P/NiS@PCOS photocatalyst uniformly dispersed.
- **Additives**: 10 mg MnO₂ was included to catalytically decompose H₂O₂ generated during the reaction, preventing interference with O₂ quantification.
- **Electrolyte**: No additional electrolytes (e.g., Na₂SO₄) or pH adjustments were introduced for the overall water splitting test; the medium remained neutral.
- **Gas Atmosphere**: The headspace was maintained under an Ar atmosphere, as established during the degassing and purging protocol.
- **Sacrificial Agent Verification**: The experiment was explicitly conducted without any sacrificial electron donors (e.g., methanol, lactic acid) or acceptors (e.g., AgNO₃, Na₂S/Na₂SO₃). This confirms the test as a true overall water splitting process, with the catalyst operating solely in pure water under neutral conditions.


## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: 150.7 μmol·h⁻¹ per 100 mg catalyst under simulated solar illumination (300 W Xe lamp, AM 1.5G, 100 mW·cm⁻², neutral aqueous solution).
- **O₂ Evolution Rate**: 70.2 μmol·h⁻¹ per 100 mg catalyst under identical conditions.
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: Maintained at 2:1 throughout the reaction, confirming the expected stoichiometry for overall water splitting.
- **Apparent Quantum Yield (AQY)**: 8.2% at 420 nm, 4.7% at 475 nm, and 1.3% at 550 nm, determined using bandpass filters and the formula: AQE(%) = (2 × number of evolved H₂ molecules / number of incident photons) × 100%. Light intensities and illuminated areas were specified for each wavelength.
- **Solar-to-Hydrogen (STH) Efficiency**: Not explicitly reported, but the system demonstrates competitive efficiency relative to noble metal-based benchmarks under identical illumination.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Product Origin Verification**: The stoichiometric evolution of H₂ and O₂ in a 2:1 ratio, with no detection of other gaseous products, supports water as the sole source of evolved gases.
- **Suppression of Side Reactions**: Addition of MnO₂ effectively decomposed in situ generated H₂O₂, preventing catalyst etching and back-reactions (H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O), as evidenced by stable gas evolution rates and preserved catalyst structure.
- **Theoretical Insights**: Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations revealed a near-thermoneutral hydrogen adsorption free energy (ΔG_H* = −0.051 eV) at NiS sites, indicating efficient HER activity, while PCOS sites exhibited higher ΔG_H*, correlating with their role in OER.
- **Active Site Assignment**: Experimental use of sacrificial agents and computational modeling confirmed NiS as the primary HER site and PCOS as the OER site, with the heterojunction facilitating charge separation.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Continuous Operation**: The catalyst maintained over 90% of its initial H₂ and O₂ evolution rates after 10 hours of continuous illumination, with negligible performance decay.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**:
  - **XRD**: Patterns before and after reaction (with MnO₂) showed no new phases or significant peak shifts, indicating structural integrity.
  - **XPS**: Ni 2p, N, S, and O signals remained stable post-reaction, confirming the preservation of chemical states and dopant incorporation.
- **Leaching and Dissolution**: No evidence of catalyst dissolution or metal leaching was observed, as inferred from consistent performance and unchanged structural features.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Dark Control**: No H₂ or O₂ evolution detected in the absence of light, confirming the photocatalytic nature of the reaction.
- **Blank Substrate Control**: Tests with pure water and electrolyte (no catalyst) under illumination yielded negligible gas evolution.
- **Temperature Variation Control**: Raising the temperature without illumination did not induce gas evolution, ruling out thermal effects.
- **Reproducibility**: All performance data represent the mean of three independent experiments (n = 3), with error bars indicating standard deviation.
- **Instrument Calibration**: XPS binding energies were calibrated using the C 1s peak at 284.6 eV; gas evolution was monitored by calibrated GC-TCD.
- **Experimental Safeguards**: Addition of MnO₂ ensured removal of H₂O₂, preventing catalyst degradation and ensuring reliable long-term operation.
