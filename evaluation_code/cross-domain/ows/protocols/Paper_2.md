## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. Dissolve **phosphatidylcholine (PC)** and **cholesterol** in **chloroform** and dry the solution into a thin film (to form a uniform lipid matrix).
2. Hydrate the dried film in the presence of a preformed aqueous dispersion of HER-MOF precursors, namely **HfCl₄** and **[(H4TCPP)Zn]**, along with WOR-MOF components and **FeCl₃** (for liposome interior stabilization). This hydration occurs with gentle agitation and the inclusion of glass beads.
3. Subject the hydrated mixture to **ultrasonication for 10 minutes** to ensure proper dispersion.
4. Perform **10 freeze-thaw cycles** (using liquid nitrogen for freezing) to further promote uniformity in the multilamellar vesicle suspension.
5. Immerse the suspension in a **55 °C water bath** (to enhance liposome formation) before proceeding to the final extrusion step with a mini-extruder.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. Wash the as-synthesized Hf-[(TCPP)Zn]-MOF thoroughly with **DMF** and **acetone** to remove residual impurities, followed by drying under **vacuum at 50 °C**.
2. Modify the HER-MOF surface by treating it with **pentafluoropropionic acid** (to replace formate capping groups) which increases the hydrophobicity of the MOF, thereby facilitating tighter integration with the lipid bilayer.
3. Monitor and confirm the integrity of the modified MOF using UV-Vis spectroscopy and fluorescence measurements (excitation at **430 nm**) to verify retention of [(TCPP)Zn] emission.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. Introduce co-catalyst components by adding **TCBQ** (at a concentration of **0.018 mM**) alongside a **3+ ion (1.4 eq.) of Fe** into the suspension, achieving a target Pt-TON of **223** (to enhance both charge separation and redox shuttling).
2. Ensure the controlled deposition of the co-catalyst occurs during the hydration process, securing intimate contact between the MOF surfaces and the lipid bilayer.
3. Confirm the co-catalyst loading through spectroscopic methods and assess its impact on charge transfer dynamics, thereby establishing the dual-MOF architecture’s efficacy in overall water splitting.

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: One-step overall water splitting, where both hydrogen and oxygen generation occur simultaneously without sacrificial reagents.
- **For One-step System:**  
  - **Photocatalyst Material**: LP-HER-WOR-MOF, a metal–organic framework embedded in a liposomal matrix. This system integrates two components within a single architecture: one based on the [(TCPP)Zn] unit and another involving TCBQ in its reduced (HTCBQ) form.
  - **Functional Integration**: The MOF components are embedded into the liposome via a controlled hydration process, ensuring intimate contact between the aqueous and lipid phases. This setup promotes effective catalyst dispersion and assists in the redox shuttle process.
  - **Redox Shuttle Involvement**: Although operating as a one-step system, the design incorporates the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ redox couple to regulate electron transfer dynamics during water splitting, with TCBQ facilitating the oxidation process.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Absorption Range**:  
  - The system employs LED irradiation with distinct wavelengths—approximately **400 nm** for hydrogen evolution and **450 nm** for water oxidation—to align with the absorption profiles of the photocatalyst components. This targeted illumination ensures optimal activation and charge separation.
- **Gas Management and Safety**:  
  - **Separation Strategy**: The unique liposomal architecture inherently maintains spatial separation between the generated H₂ and O₂ gases, reducing recombination and back-reactions. This structural separation contributes to both enhanced efficiency and operational safety.
  - **Operational Conditions**:  
    - The reaction is conducted in an aqueous medium that supports efficient electron transfer while stabilizing the redox shuttle (Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺).  
    - The sealed experimental setup, monitored using gas chromatography, ensures that gas products are accurately quantified and safely managed.
- **Additional Considerations**:  
  - **Charge Transfer Optimization**: The close integration and proximity of the MOF components within the liposome facilitate rapid quenching of the [(TCPP)Zn] emission by TCBQ, underscoring an effective electron management strategy during overall water splitting.

This integrated system-level design leverages the benefits of liposomal encapsulation and dual MOF architecture, illustrating a sophisticated approach to one-step overall water splitting with a focus on effective light management, charge separation, and gas safety.


## **5. Testing System Configuration**

### 5.1 Reactor & Gas Handling
- **Reactor Type & Configuration**: The overall water splitting (OWS) experiments were conducted in a septum-sealed quartz cuvette or a sealed glass container, designed to prevent gas leakage and enable accurate headspace sampling. For certain measurements, a home-built three-neck cell was employed, accommodating electrodes and facilitating gas exchange.
- **Atmosphere & Purging Protocol**:
  1. The reactor was thoroughly purged with high-purity N₂ for 20–30 minutes prior to illumination to remove residual oxygen and establish an inert atmosphere.
  2. After purging, the vessel was sealed with a septum to maintain the N₂ environment throughout the reaction.
- **Temperature & Pressure Control**: The reaction was performed at ambient temperature, with no active temperature or pressure regulation during catalysis. The sealed design ensured stable internal pressure for the duration of the test.
- **Gas Sampling & Analysis**:
  - **Sampling Method**: At defined time points (typically after 24 hours of illumination), headspace gas was withdrawn using a gastight syringe through the septum.
  - **Gas Chromatography (GC) System**: Product quantification was performed using a GC7900 equipped with a **Thermal Conductivity Detector (TCD)**. The system utilized both **TDX-01** and **Molecular Sieve 5Å** columns to separate H₂ and O₂.
  - **Calibration**: Quantification was based on calibration with standard gas mixtures, ensuring accurate determination of H₂ and O₂ evolution.

### 5.2 Illumination Conditions
- **Light Source**: A **Xenon (Xe) arc lamp** integrated into a fluorimeter with a grating monochromator was used for OWS, with the excitation wavelength set to **430 nm**. For half-reactions, **400 nm** and **450 nm LEDs** were employed.
- **Spectral Output**: Monochromatic light at the specified wavelength was delivered to the reactor, ensuring selective excitation of the photocatalyst.
- **Irradiance Measurement**: The incident light intensity at **436 nm** was determined using **potassium ferrioxalate actinometry**, a chemical method that quantifies photon flux based on photochemical yield.
- **Illumination Geometry**: The light beam was directed onto the sealed cuvette or container, ensuring the catalyst suspension received uniform irradiation through the optical window.

### 5.3 Reaction Medium
- **Aqueous Phase**: The reaction medium consisted of pure water, with the catalyst (LP–HER-WOR-MOF) dispersed within the aqueous phase. The specific water source (e.g., deionized) and electrolyte composition were not detailed, but the system operated under neutral conditions.
- **Gas Atmosphere**: The headspace was maintained under a N₂ atmosphere, established and preserved as described in the gas handling protocol.
- **Sacrificial Agent Verification**: The OWS tests were explicitly conducted **without any sacrificial electron donors or acceptors**. Control experiments confirmed that neither the liposome nor any added component acted as a sacrificial agent, and the observed 2:1 H₂:O₂ evolution ratio, as well as D₂ detection with D₂O, verified that water was the sole source of electrons and protons.



## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: 836 μmol g⁻¹ for LP–HER–WOR–MOF under 400 nm LED irradiation for 24 hours in water (catalyst loading: 0.07 mg mL⁻¹).
- **Apparent Quantum Efficiency (AQE)**: 1.5 ± 1% at 436 nm, calculated using the formula:  
  \( AQE = \frac{4 \times n_{H_2} \times N_A \times h \times c}{S \times P \times t \times \lambda} \times 100\% \),  
  where \( n_{H_2} \) is the amount of H₂ generated, \( S \) is irradiation area, \( P \) is light intensity, \( t \) is reaction time, and \( \lambda \) is wavelength.
- **O₂ Evolution Rate**: Not explicitly reported for LP–HER–WOR–MOF; thus, the H₂:O₂ stoichiometric ratio cannot be directly determined for this system.
- **Reference Stoichiometry**: For comparison, benchmark systems (e.g., Ru/SrTiO₃:Rh/BiVO₄) achieved H₂:O₂ ratios close to the theoretical 2:1, confirming proper water splitting in analogous architectures.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Product Origin Confirmation**: Use of D₂O as solvent resulted in the evolution of D₂ and a small amount of HD, confirming that hydrogen evolved from water splitting.
- **Suppression of Back-Reaction**: Spatial separation of HER-MOF (in lipid bilayer) and WOR-MOF (in aqueous core) within the liposome, combined with redox shuttles (TCBQ in bilayer, Fe³⁺ in aqueous phase), was designed to prevent H₂/O₂ recombination. Fluorescence quenching experiments demonstrated that TCBQ efficiently quenched [(TCPP)Zn] emission only when co-localized, supporting effective compartmentalization.
- **Charge Transfer Evidence**: Fluorescence quenching of [(TCPP)Zn] by TCBQ (Supplementary Figure 31) and retention of emission post-reaction (Supplementary Figure 63) indicate efficient electron transfer and redox cycling within the system.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Continuous Operation**: The liposomal structure remained intact and catalytically active for up to 36 hours under continuous illumination; degradation was observed after 36 hours, attributed to reactive oxygen species.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**:  
  - **TEM & Confocal Imaging**: Confirmed the presence and spatial arrangement of MOFs within liposomes before and after reaction.
  - **Dye Release Assay**: Detected liposome degradation after extended operation, corroborated by confocal microscopy.
  - **ICP-MS & PXRD**: Showed negligible leaching of metal centers and no significant structural changes in MOFs post-catalysis.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Dark Control**: No H₂ or O₂ evolution detected in the absence of light, confirming photocatalytic origin of gas evolution.
- **Component Omission Controls**: Removal of TCBQ or Fe³⁺ from the system led to a marked decrease in H₂ and O₂ production, demonstrating the necessity of both redox shuttles.
- **Isotopic Control**: D₂O experiments confirmed water as the hydrogen source.
- **Error Analysis**: Performance data are reported with standard deviations (e.g., AQE: 1.5 ± 1%), and error bars represent standard deviations from multiple measurements; the number of replicates is not specified.
- **Gas Quantification**: O₂ measured using a fluorescence oxygen sensor; H₂ quantified for AQE and turnover calculations. No explicit mention of mass balance or reactor gas-tightness checks, but systematic controls and repeated measurements underpin data reliability.
