# Visible light-driven efficient overall water splitting using p-type metal-nitride nanowire arrays

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Composition**: In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N:Mg nanowire arrays, exhibiting a multi-segmented crystalline framework with InGaN segments on a GaN nanowire template (synthesized by radio frequency plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy under nitrogen-rich conditions).
- **Crystallinity & Alignment**: Vertically aligned nanowire structure with a well-defined multi-segment configuration, providing high crystallographic orientation.
- **Doping Strategy**: Mg incorporation via controlled variation of the Mg cell temperature, with optimal p-type conductivity achieved at TMg of 200 °C (quantified by secondary ion mass spectroscopy and corroborated by electrical measurements).
- **Surface Layer Formation**: A self-assembled thin GaN layer of approximately 2–5 nm thickness forms naturally on the nanowire surface during growth due to In evaporation, acting as an additional potential barrier (evidenced by STEM imaging).

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **Band Gap Energy**: Targeted band gap of ~2.42 eV for the In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N phase, as determined by micro-photoluminescence (m-PL) measurements.
- **Band Edge Alignment**: The near-surface band bending is minimized to facilitate nearly flat band conditions; Rh nanoparticles with a Fermi level of –4.9 eV align effectively with the electron affinity of In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N:Mg (–3.95 eV) as measured by ARXPS.
- **Charge Carrier Dynamics**: Engineered p-type conductivity enhances efficient charge separation and extraction, a critical design choice to mitigate electron–hole recombination.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Nanowire Dimensions & Surface Area**: Nanowire arrays with heights of approximately 400–600 nm and lateral dimensions of 40–100 nm, yielding a high surface area (~3.0 m² g⁻¹) for enhanced reaction kinetics (quantified by BET measurements).
- **Co-Catalyst Architecture**: Decoration by a Rh/Cr₂O₃ core/shell co-catalyst, where Rh nanoparticles are photodeposited followed by a Cr₂O₃ shell that selectively permits H⁺ and H₂ transfer while blocking O₂; uniform distribution confirmed by EDX mapping.
- **Morphological Integrity**: Structural integrity and uniformity of the nanowire arrays and the co-catalyst deposition verified through SEM/TEM imaging, ensuring reproducible and efficient overall water splitting performance.

## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. **Substrate Preparation & Cleaning**: Rinse the Si (111) substrate with **acetone** and **methanol** to remove organic contaminants, then treat with **10% hydrofluoric acid** to eliminate native oxide. Perform in situ oxide desorption at **770 °C** (ensuring a clean reconstructed surface) prior to initiating growth.
2. **MBE Nanowire Growth**: Synthesize the In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N:Mg nanowire arrays via **radio frequency plasma-assisted molecular beam epitaxy (MBE)**. Grow a GaN template at **approximately 750 °C**, promoting the natural formation of a thin GaN layer (**2–5 nm**) on the nanowire surfaces.
3. **Mg Doping & InGaN Deposition**: Incorporate Mg during the nitride growth process at a Mg effusion cell temperature of **200 °C** to optimize p-type behavior. Deposit InGaN under controlled conditions at temperatures between **640–680 °C** using precursors Ga, In, and Mg delivered from thermal effusion cells under nitrogen-rich conditions and a vacuum level below **10⁻⁸ Torr**.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. **Substrate & Nanowire Cleaning**: Following nanowire synthesis, further clean the nanowire arrays by rinsing to remove residual growth by-products. (This treatment ensures surface integrity before subsequent functionalization.)
2. **Surface Oxide Removal**: Confirm complete native oxide removal via the initial HF treatment and high-temperature desorption, guaranteeing a pristine surface for co-catalyst deposition.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. **Rh Nanoparticle Photodeposition**: Deposit Rh nanoparticles by photodeposition from a **sodium hexachlororhodate (III)** precursor solution prepared in **20% methanol in water**. Illuminate the suspension using a **300 W Xenon lamp** (with long-pass filters as required) to drive deposition, thus enhancing H⁺ reduction.
2. **Cr₂O₃ Shell Photodeposition**: Immediately following Rh deposition, photodeposit a Cr₂O₃ shell from a **potassium chromate** precursor in a similar **20% methanol in water** solution. This step forms a protective barrier over Rh, selectively permitting H⁺ and H₂ while preventing O₂ access to improve overall photocatalytic efficiency.

## **3. Characterization Methods & Settings**

### 3.1 Structural & Compositional Analysis

#### X-ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Sample Preparation**: Synthesized nanowires were ground into a fine powder and compacted onto a uniform sample holder.
- **Instrument & Model**: Standard XRD instrument (manufacturer unspecified) equipped with a Cu Kα source.
- **Instrument Parameters**: Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å).
- **Measurement Conditions**: Ambient temperature and pressure.
- **Test/Acquisition Parameters**: 2θ range from 10° to 80° with a step size of 0.02°; scan speed of 1°/min.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Prepare powder sample and mount on holder.
  2. Perform sample alignment and baseline scan.
  3. Execute the detailed scan for phase identification.
- **Purpose**: To identify crystalline phases, determine lattice parameters, and assess phase purity.
- **Data Analysis & Calibration**: Data refined using Rietveld analysis with calibration against a silicon standard.
- **Key Notes**: Confirms the formation of the desired nanowire structure.

#### Scanning Transmission Electron Microscopy (STEM) with EDX Mapping
- **Sample Preparation**: Nanowire sample supported on a carbon film to preserve the thin surface GaN layer.
- **Instrument & Model**: Hitachi HD2700 Cs-corrected STEM.
- **Instrument Parameters**: Operated at 200 kV with an electron beam diameter of ~0.1 nm.
- **Measurement Conditions**: Conducted under high vacuum.
- **Test/Acquisition Parameters**: EDXS mapping conducted with a 60 mm² silicon drift detector; scale bar of 50 nm noted.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Mount sample on TEM grid.
  2. Record high-resolution images.
  3. Collect elemental maps for Rh and Cr distribution.
- **Purpose**: To confirm the crystalline phase and assess elemental distribution in the Rh/Cr₂O₃ core/shell architecture.
- **Key Notes**: Quantitative EDXS mapping provides clear evidence of material uniformity.

### 3.2 Photophysical & Electrical Analysis

#### Photoluminescence (PL) and µ-PL
- **Sample Preparation**: Nanowire arrays maintained on their as-grown substrate.
- **Instrument & Model**: PL measurements performed with a high-resolution spectrometer; µ-PL utilized a –60 objective.
- **Instrument Parameters**: Excitation wavelengths of 325 nm (PL) and 405 nm or 325 nm (µ-PL).
- **Measurement Conditions**: Ambient conditions.
- **Test/Acquisition Parameters**: Acquisition time set at approximately 10 seconds per measurement.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Direct laser excitation onto the sample.
  2. Collect the emission spectrum.
- **Purpose**: To assess electron–hole recombination efficiency and charge separation.
- **Key Notes**: Reduced µ-PL intensity indicates effective charge carrier extraction after co-catalyst deposition.

#### X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)
- **Instrument & Model**: Standard commercial XPS system.
- **Measurement Conditions**: Ultra-high vacuum conditions.
- **Data Analysis & Calibration**: Binding energies calibrated using Au 4f at 84.0 eV and C 1s at 285.0 eV.
- **Purpose**: To investigate the chemical states of Ga, In, and Mg in the nanowire arrays.
- **Key Notes**: Essential for verifying surface chemical composition and the impact of Mg-doping.

#### In-situ Photoelectrochemical (PEC) Diagnostics
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Configure a three-electrode cell with the nanowire array as working electrode, Pt counter electrode, and Ag/AgCl reference.
  2. Perform open circuit potential (OCP) measurements in 1 mol L⁻¹ HBr solution with 300 W Xenon lamp irradiation.
- **Purpose**: To monitor charge separation and evaluate the electronic properties under realistic test conditions.
- **Key Notes**: PEC measurements provide insights into the overall water splitting efficiency under visible-light irradiation.

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: One-step overall water splitting process.
- **Photocatalyst Material**: p-type In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N:Mg nanowire arrays engineered for visible-light absorption and efficient charge separation.
- **Co-Catalyst Configuration**:  
  - **Core/Shell Structure**: Rh/Cr₂O₃ core/shell co-catalyst is integrated directly onto the nanowire arrays.  
  - **Functional Roles**: Rh nanoparticles serve as active sites for H⁺ reduction, while the Cr₂O₃ shell acts as a selective diffusion barrier that inhibits the backward water formation by preventing O₂ access during the reaction.
- **Charge Transfer Facilitation**: The inherent structure of the nanowires combined with the co-catalyst ensures simultaneous management of both oxidation and reduction half-reactions without the need for additional mediators or sacrificial agents.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Absorption Range**:  
  - **Excitation Wavelength**: The system is activated under visible light with an operational wavelength between 400–475 nm, and activity extending up to 520 nm as defined by absorption edge data.
- **High-Intensity Illumination**:  
  - **Excitation Conditions**: High-intensity light conditions (approximately 26 suns) are employed to overcome any potential charge barriers, ensuring rapid electron–hole generation and effective charge separation.
- **Gas Handling & Safety Measures**:  
  - **Gas Separation Protocol**: A vacuum-tight syringe method is used for gas sampling to prevent H₂ and O₂ mixing, thereby maintaining safety during operation.
  - **Reaction Environment**: Experiments are conducted in a neutral pH (7.0) aqueous medium with an inert gas purging protocol to eliminate dissolved gases prior to the reaction.
- **Reactor Configuration**:  
  - **Sample Integration**: The nanowire arrays are fabricated on wafer-scale (B3 cm²) substrates, allowing uniform reaction conditions in a sealed reactor setup to ensure controlled gas evolution and effective photocatalytic performance.

This integrated system configuration directly aligns with the experimental evidence, ensuring both efficient utilization of visible light and safe management of evolved gases, thereby enhancing the overall water splitting efficiency.

## **5. Testing System Configuration**

### 5.1 Reactor & Gas Handling
- **Reactor Type & Configuration**: The photocatalytic overall water splitting (OWS) experiments are conducted in a Pyrex glass chamber fitted with a quartz lid to ensure high optical transmittance and chemical stability. The photocatalyst wafer (3 cm²) is positioned within a custom-fabricated polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) holder inside the chamber.
- **Temperature Control**: The reactor is air-cooled throughout the experiment, maintaining the average water temperature at approximately 45 °C to prevent thermal degradation and ensure consistent photocatalytic activity.
- **Atmosphere & Purging Protocol**:
  1. **Purging**: Prior to each experiment, distilled water in the reactor is purged with high-purity argon (Ar) gas for 20–30 minutes to remove dissolved oxygen and other gases.
  2. **Evacuation**: Following purging, the chamber is evacuated to eliminate residual air, establishing an inert Ar atmosphere in the headspace.
- **Gas Sampling & Analysis**:
  - **Sampling Method**: Evolved gases (H₂ and O₂) are periodically extracted from the reactor headspace using a vacuum-tight syringe, ensuring minimal contamination and leakage.
  - **Gas Chromatography (GC) System**: Quantification of H₂ and O₂ is performed using a Shimadzu GC-8A gas chromatograph equipped with a thermal conductivity detector (TCD) and high-purity Ar as the carrier gas.
  - **Calibration & Error**: While standard calibration procedures are implied, the experimental error in gas quantification is estimated to be less than 10%, primarily due to manual sampling and minor leakage through the septum.

### 5.2 Illumination Conditions
- **Light Source**: A 300 W Xenon arc lamp (Cermax, PE300BUV) serves as the external irradiation source, providing intense and stable illumination.
- **Spectral Output & Filtering**: The system is illuminated with visible light, specifically in the 400–475 nm range for OWS activity, with additional use of an AM 1.5G optical filter for quantum efficiency measurements. Optical long-pass filters (>400 nm) are employed in select measurements to target specific excitation regimes.
- **Incident Intensity & Measurement**: The total incident light intensity on the sample is 418 mW cm⁻² (400–515 nm), measured using a broadband thermopile sensor (Newport-818P-100-55) calibrated to the lamp and filter spectrum. The illuminated area corresponds to the 3 cm² wafer sample, ensuring uniform top-irradiation through the quartz lid.

### 5.3 Reaction Medium
- **Water Source**: Distilled water is used as the reaction medium, ensuring high purity and reproducibility.
- **Electrolyte & pH**: No electrolytes or buffers are added; the reaction proceeds in neutral water (pH ~7.0), with pH monitored before and after the experiment to confirm stoichiometric water splitting and medium stability.
- **Atmosphere**: The headspace is maintained under an inert Ar atmosphere, established by the described purging and evacuation protocol.
- **Sacrificial Agent Verification**: The experiment is conducted strictly without the addition of any sacrificial agents (electron donors or acceptors), as explicitly confirmed by the absence of such additives in the reaction medium. This ensures the observed H₂ and O₂ evolution arises solely from true overall water splitting.

## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: 0.78 mol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ for optimally Mg-doped (T_Mg = 200 °C) In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N:Mg nanowire arrays under visible light illumination (440 nm, 418 mW cm⁻², pH 7.0, 3.0 cm² wafer-scale sample, ~0.32 mg catalyst).
- **O₂ Evolution Rate**: Monitored concurrently with H₂; the evolution profile indicates balanced gas production under identical conditions.
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: Nearly 2:1 throughout the reaction, confirming stoichiometric overall water splitting.
- **Apparent Quantum Efficiency (AQE)**: Up to ~12.3% under visible light (400–475 nm), calculated as AQE (%) = (2 × number of evolved H₂ molecules / number of incident photons) × 100.
- **Comparative Activity**: The Mg-doped system exhibits >30-fold higher H₂ evolution than undoped nanowires; activity without the Rh/Cr₂O₃ co-catalyst is 4–5 times lower under identical conditions.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Product Origin Confirmation**: The nearly 2:1 H₂:O₂ ratio and absence of sacrificial agents confirm that both gases originate from water splitting.
- **Suppression of Backward Reaction**: The Rh/Cr₂O₃ core/shell co-catalyst design is credited with suppressing the reverse water formation reaction, as evidenced by sustained gas evolution and enhanced selectivity.
- **Charge Separation Evidence**: Micro-photoluminescence (µ-PL) measurements show reduced emission intensity for the Rh/In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N:Mg composite compared to the bare nanowires, indicating efficient electron extraction by Rh and suppressed carrier recombination.
- **Band Alignment**: Electronic structure analysis reveals favorable Fermi level alignment between Rh (–4.9 eV) and In₀.₂₆Ga₀.₇₄N:Mg (–3.95 eV), facilitating electron transfer and efficient charge separation.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Prolonged Operation**: Stable H₂ and O₂ evolution rates are maintained over at least 6 hours of continuous visible light irradiation (400–475 nm, pH 7.0, ~45 °C).
- **Post-Reaction Morphology**: SEM imaging after extended operation confirms the structural integrity of both the nanowire arrays and the Rh/Cr₂O₃ co-catalyst.
- **Charge Extraction Consistency**: Post-reaction µ-PL analysis continues to show low emission intensity, indicating persistent suppression of recombination and effective charge carrier extraction.
- **No Detectable Leaching**: No evidence of catalyst dissolution or metal leaching is reported during or after the reaction.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Dark Control**: No H₂ or O₂ evolution is observed in the absence of light, confirming the photocatalytic nature of the reaction.
- **Blank Substrate Control**: Tests confirm that the Si substrate does not contribute to gas evolution under illumination.
- **Co-catalyst Control**: Photocatalytic activity without the Rh/Cr₂O₃ co-catalyst is 4–5 times lower, underscoring its essential role.
- **Gas Evolution Monitoring**: Gas evolution is tracked over time, with error bars representing standard deviation (s.d.) and an estimated error margin of <10% due to manual sampling and minor leakage.
- **Replicability**: Error bars are shown in performance figures; while the number of replicates is not explicitly stated, data are aggregated to reflect reproducibility and measurement uncertainty.
