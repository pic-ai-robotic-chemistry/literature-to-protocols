# Synergistic Promotion of Single-Atom Co Surrounding a PtCo Alloy Based On a g‑C3N4 Nanosheet for Overall Water Splitting

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Composition**: CoSAs/PtCo on high‐surface-area g‑C₃N₄ nanosheets (CNN), where the CNN substrate is derived from graphitic carbon nitride.
- **Synthesis Method & Precursor Formation**: Bulk g‑C₃N₄ was synthesized via the calcination of melamine at 823 K, followed by thermal exfoliation at 773 K (5 K/min for 5 hours) to yield porous nanosheets.
- **Integrated Catalytic Components**: Engineered integration of isolated Co single atoms (Co SAs) and PtCo alloy nanoparticles onto the CNN substrate; this configuration is chosen to maximize active site dispersion and foster synergistic effects for HER and OER.
- **Design Rationale**: The coupling of g‑C₃N₄ with distinct metal species enhances atomic utilization, where the substrate’s inherent graphitic structure supports effective dispersion and anchoring of metal species via abundant defect sites.

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **Electronic Property Targeting**: The catalyst design aims for an optimal band gap to enable visible-light absorption, determined by Tauc plot analysis of UV–vis DRS data.
- **Band Edge Alignment**: Measurements via Mott-Schottky analysis ensure that the semiconductor’s conduction and valence bands are well aligned with water redox potentials to facilitate efficient charge carrier separation.
- **Charge Carrier Characteristics**: The n‑type nature of the g‑C₃N₄ substrate is strategically chosen to funnel electrons toward CoSAs and PtCo alloy regions, thereby enhancing hydrogen evolution and oxygen evolution reaction kinetics.
- **Rationale for Electronic Structure**: Tailoring the band structure through defect engineering (e.g., enriched amino (NHₓ) sites) assists in improving water adsorption and charge separation.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Physical Configuration**: The obtained CNN nanosheets exhibit a high BET surface area of 277.86 m²/g (measured by BET), which promotes abundant active sites for water molecule activation.
- **Nanostructure & Defect Layout**: Exfoliation yields a thin, porous morphology with engineered NHₓ sites that serve as anchoring points for metal species.
- **Heterostructure Architecture**: Uniform dispersion of Co single atoms and PtCo alloy nanoparticles is confirmed via TEM and HAADF-STEM, creating a distinct heterostructure that is critical for efficient bifunctional catalysis.
- **Design Integration Rationale**: The morphological features ensure enhanced interaction between the semiconductor substrate and metal nanoparticles, thereby optimizing charge separation and catalytic performance in overall water splitting.


## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. **Bulk g‑C₃N₄ Formation**: Calcine melamine at **823 K for 4 h** in a solid‐state reaction to yield bulk g‑C₃N₄ (ensuring crystallinity).
2. **Thermal Exfoliation**: Subject the calcined bulk g‑C₃N₄ to a rapid heating protocol at a rate of **5 K/min** up to an exfoliation temperature of **773 K** to obtain high-surface-area nanosheet g‑C₃N₄ (CNN) with enhanced BET surface area and pore volume.

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. **Dispersion & Sonication**: Disperse **0.2 g** of the as‐prepared CNN in **20 mL water** and subject the suspension to high-power sonication for **6 h** (to ensure uniform particle separation).
2. **Preparation of Metal Precursor Solution**: Dissolve **3 mg H₂PtCl₆** and **1 mg Co(NO₃)₂** in **10 mL ethylene glycol (EG)** to prepare a homogeneous metal precursor solution.
3. **Mixing under Controlled Atmosphere**: Combine the CNN dispersion with the metal precursor solution and stir at **323 K under high vacuum** for **2 h** (to promote intimate contact and minimize oxygen exposure) while adding **10 vol% triethanolamine (TEOA)** to serve as a photoreductant.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. **Photoreduction Deposition**: Irradiate the mixed solution with a **300 W Xe lamp (λ >300 nm)** for **2 h** (ensuring the effective reduction of noble metal cations and formation of PtCo alloy nanoparticles alongside single‐atom Co sites).
2. **Final Drying Step**: Dry the resulting material at **333 K overnight** to remove residual solvents and secure the functionalized catalyst structure.

- **Test/Acquisition Parameters**: Standard counting time per step (e.g., ~1 s per step) ensuring sufficient signal-to-noise.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Load the sample and perform alignment.

- **Purpose**: To identify crystalline phases, determine lattice parameters, and assess crystallite size for the PtCo alloy phase.

- **Key Notes**: Confirms phase purity and structural integrity of the alloy nanoparticles.

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: Two-step (Z-scheme), as indicated by the separation of catalytic roles.
  
**For Z-scheme System:**
- **Hydrogen Evolution Photocatalyst (HEP)**: Single-atom cobalt (CoSAs) sites deposited on nanosheet g‑C₃N₄, which serve as the active centers for the hydrogen evolution reaction.
- **Oxygen Evolution Photocatalyst (OEP)**: PtCo alloy nanoparticles uniformly distributed on the same g‑C₃N₄ support, providing active sites for the oxygen evolution reaction.
- **Mediator Functionality**: The g‑C₃N₄ nanosheet (CNN) not only acts as a high surface area support but also facilitates interfacial charge transfer between the CoSAs and PtCo alloy nanoparticles, effectively fulfilling the role of a solid-state mediator.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Absorption Range**: The system is activated by a 300 W Xe lamp delivering UV–visible light (λ > 300 nm), with long-pass filters applied (e.g., filters at 380±10, 400±10, and 420±10 nm) to narrow the irradiation spectrum for selective activation.
- **Reactor Configuration**: The photocatalytic reactions are conducted in a Pyrex top-irradiation vessel, ensuring uniform light exposure while the catalyst disperses in pure water.
- **Gas Handling and Safety**: Prior to irradiation, the reaction solution is evacuated to thoroughly remove air, thereby minimizing the risk of H₂ and O₂ mixing. The evolved gases are collected via a closed gas system and analyzed using gas chromatography equipped with a thermal conductivity detector.
- **Operational Environment**: The photocatalytic process is carried out under tightly controlled conditions to ensure catalyst stability and effective overall water splitting, with measures in place to maintain temperature stability and prevent unintended recombination reactions.

This detailed extraction encapsulates both the integrated system configuration and the operational strategies that underlie the photocatalytic performance in overall water splitting.

## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: 300.9 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ under UV–visible light irradiation (λ > 300 nm, 3.83 W, 300 W Xe lamp, 20 mg catalyst in 50 mL pure water, Pyrex top-irradiation vessel, room temperature).
- **O₂ Evolution Rate**: 65.6 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ under identical conditions.
- **Visible-Light Performance**: Under visible-light irradiation (λ > 400 nm, 2.65 W), H₂ and O₂ evolution rates were 85.7 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ and 21.3 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹, respectively.
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: The average product ratio over 4 hours was H₂:H₂O₂:O₂ = 2:1.05:0.44. The total H:O ratio of all products was 2:0.97, closely matching the theoretical 2:1 stoichiometry for water splitting, with H₂O₂ formation quantified by UV–vis spectrophotometry (Fe²⁺/KSCN method, λ = 476 nm).
- **Apparent Quantum Efficiency (AQE)**: Measured under monochromatic illumination at λ = 380±10, 400±10, and 420±10 nm using a fiber spectrometer to determine incident photons. AQE values tracked the optical absorption spectrum, confirming photoexcitation-driven activity.
- **Metal-Normalized Activity**: The system achieved a normalized metal activity exceeding 48.30 μmol h⁻¹ mg⁻¹, outperforming many amorphous g‑C₃N₄-based catalysts.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Synergistic Bifunctionality**: Only the bifunctional CoSAs/PtCo@CNN catalyst produced both H₂ and O₂ in overall water splitting; single-component catalysts (CoSAs@CNN or PtCo@CNN) and reference samples (Pt/PtCo@CNN, Pt/PtCo@CN) showed negligible activity.
- **Hydroxyl Radical Detection**: In situ EPR detected •OH radicals exclusively for CoSAs/PtCo@CNN, indicating active water oxidation.
- **Interfacial Reactivity**: NAP-XPS revealed a positive shift in Co²⁺ binding energy upon H₂O exposure, evidencing Co single atoms as active sites for H₂O activation and hydroxyl spillover to PtCo alloy.
- **Charge Separation**: Photoluminescence and photocurrent measurements demonstrated suppressed electron–hole recombination and enhanced charge transfer in CoSAs/PtCo@CNN relative to controls.
- **Product Origin**: No isotope labeling was reported; selectivity was inferred from stoichiometry and control experiments.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Continuous Operation**: The catalyst maintained high H₂ evolution rates over three consecutive cycles (each with 20 mg catalyst, 50 mL water, λ > 300 nm irradiation), indicating robust operational stability.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**:
  - **XRD**: Retained diffraction peaks for the PtCo alloy phase after three cycles, confirming crystalline stability.
  - **XPS**: The proportion of Co⁰ species (14.3%) and Pt oxidation states remained unchanged post-reaction, indicating preserved chemical states.
- **Structural Integrity**: TEM and HAADF-STEM confirmed the nanosheet morphology and uniform metal dispersion were maintained after cycling.
- **Leaching**: No explicit evidence of metal leaching was reported; catalyst integrity was inferred from consistent post-reaction composition.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Component Controls**: Single-component catalysts and reference samples exhibited no measurable H₂ or O₂ evolution, confirming the necessity of the synergistic CoSAs/PtCo configuration.
- **Blank and Dark Controls**: No gas evolution or •OH signal was detected in the absence of catalyst or under dark conditions, verifying photocatalytic origin.
- **Gas-Tightness and Air Exclusion**: The reactor was evacuated multiple times before irradiation to eliminate air, ensuring accurate gas quantification.
- **Gas Analysis**: H₂ and O₂ were quantified by gas chromatography (GC7920, TCD, 5Å molecular sieve column, Ar carrier).
