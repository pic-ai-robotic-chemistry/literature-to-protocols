# Au@CdS Core–Shell Nanoparticles-Modifi ed ZnO Nanowires Photoanode for Effi cient Photoelectrochemical Water Splitting

## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Composition**: Au@CdS core–shell nanoparticles anchored on ZnO nanowires grown on FTO glass (Au core with CdS shell; ZnO as support) (Characterized by TEM/HRTEM).
- **Core–Shell Architecture**: Au core of approximately 5 nm encased in a CdS shell of about 2.5 nm thickness; Cd/S molar ratio maintained at ~1 (Determined by HRTEM and sequential chemical bath deposition).
- **Synthesis Conditions**: Au nanoparticles deposited via chemical reduction of chloroauric acid using sodium citrate at pH 9.0; CdS shell grown using sequential immersion cycles (~0.3 nm per cycle, 8 cycles total) to ensure uniformity.
- **Rationale**: The CdS shell is employed to enhance visible-light absorption and to establish a well-defined nanojunction with ZnO for improved charge transfer.

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **CdS Band Gap**: Approximately 2.7 eV, as extracted from optical absorption spectroscopy and validated via the Brus equation (Ensures activation under visible light).
- **Band Edge Alignment**: Conduction band positioned around 3.8 eV versus vacuum, facilitating electron injection from CdS into ZnO (Inferred from optical and electrochemical measurements).
- **Plasmonic Effect**: Au core exhibits a plasmon resonance band shift from 520 nm (in Au–ZnO) to 570 nm (in Au@CdS–ZnO) (Determined by UV-Vis absorption spectroscopy), indicating strong electronic interaction.
- **Charge Carrier Dynamics**: Designed for efficient electron transfer from photoexcited CdS (and plasmonic Au) to ZnO, as supported by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) data.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Nanoparticle Size & Shape**: Au@CdS nanoparticles have an overall size near 10 nm with clearly defined core–shell morphology (Characterized by TEM/HRTEM).
- **ZnO Nanowire Dimensions**: ZnO nanowires approximately 2 µm long and 100 nm in diameter, ensuring a high surface area for nanoparticle anchoring (Verified by TEM/HRTEM).
- **Exposed Facets**: Au components exhibit lattice fringes corresponding to the (111) planes (HRTEM analysis); structural orientation aids efficient charge transfer.
- **Composite Heterostructure**: The intimate contact between Au@CdS and ZnO nanowires creates an engineered nanojunction, optimizing interfacial charge separation (Corroborated by microscopy and optical measurements).

## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. **Au Nanoparticle Deposition**: Immerse the ZnO nanowire–coated FTO substrates in a **1 × 10⁻³ M chloroauric acid** solution. Adjust the solution pH to **9.0** using **sodium citrate** as the reducing agent to initiate chemical reduction and generate Au nanoparticles (~5 nm core size).  
2. **ZnO Nanowire Support Formation**: Prior to Au deposition, ensure ZnO nanowires are grown on FTO via immersion in a solution containing **0.025 M zinc nitrate hydrate** and **0.025 M hexamethylenetetramine** at **95 °C for 6 hours** (parameters optimized for nanowire length ~2 µm and diameter 100 nm).

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. **Annealing Treatment**: Heat the Au-decorated substrates at **350 °C for 30 minutes in air** to enhance adhesion, promote crystallinity of the ZnO nanowires, and stabilize the Au nanoparticles on the support.  
2. **Plasma Treatment**: Expose the annealed substrates to oxygen plasma in a plasma cleaner for **10 minutes** to improve the surface reactivity and uniformity, further preparing the interface for subsequent shell deposition.

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. **Sequential CdS Shell Deposition**: Deposit a CdS shell on the Au nanoparticles via sequential chemical bath deposition. Immerse the annealed and plasma‐treated substrates in a **0.05 M CdCl₂** solution for **30 seconds**, rinse thoroughly with water, then immerse in a **0.05 M Na₂S** solution for **30 seconds**, followed by a second water rinse.  
2. **Cycle Control for Shell Thickness**: Repeat the immersion–rinse sequence for a total of **8 cycles**, achieving an average CdS shell thickness of approximately **2.5 nm** (with an estimated growth rate of **0.3 nm per cycle**). This controlled deposition ensures uniform shell formation and optimal charge transfer properties.

These step-by-step procedures precisely reproduce the synthetic pathway required to fabricate the Au@CdS-ZnO photoanode, ensuring enhanced visible-light absorption and efficient photoelectrochemical water splitting.

## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: One-step process.
  
**For One-step System:**
- **Single Photocatalyst Material**: Au@CdS core–shell nanoparticles anchored on ZnO nanowires. The CdS component is primarily responsible for driving both the oxidation and reduction half-reactions.
- **Support Scaffold**: ZnO nanowires grown on F-doped SnO₂ (FTO) glass provide an efficient electron conduction pathway.  
- **Component Dimensions**:  
  - **Au Core**: ~5 nm in diameter, providing plasmonic enhancement for charge separation.  
  - **CdS Shell**: ~2.5 nm thick (achieved at the optimal eighth deposition cycle).  
  - **ZnO Nanowires**: Approximately 2 µm in length and 100 nm in diameter.
- **Integration Strategy**: The nanojunction formed between Au, CdS, and ZnO facilitates direct electron transfer, thereby enhancing the photoelectrochemical performance in overall water splitting.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Absorption Range**:  
  - **Target Wavelength**: Primarily visible light with effective absorption starting from 400 nm, as indicated by the optical absorption spectra.  
- **Illumination Source**:  
  - **Light Source**: A 150 W Xe lamp under AM1.5 conditions with an intensity of 100 mW cm⁻².
- **PEC Cell Configuration**:  
  - **Electrode Setup**: A two-electrode configuration comprising the Au@CdS-ZnO photoanode and a platinum foam counter electrode. This design simplifies the reaction pathway by employing a one-step process while maintaining efficient charge separation.
- **Safety and Gas Management**:  
  - **Gas Analysis**: Evolved hydrogen is quantified using an on-line gas chromatograph (GC) with a thermal conductivity detector.  
  - **Preventing Gas Mixing**: Although no dedicated membrane or compartmentalization is described, the two-electrode PEC cell design (with well-defined electrode areas) inherently minimizes risks associated with H₂/O₂ cross-contamination.

This detailed extraction directly addresses the system-level strategy, emphasizing the integrated design of the photocatalytic device where Au@CdS core–shell nanoparticles on ZnO nanowires enable both enhanced visible-light absorption and efficient charge separation—all within a simplified one-step overall water splitting process.

- **Photoelectrochemical Cell Configuration**: Two-electrode setup with an Au@CdS-ZnO photoanode (active area: 1.2 cm²) and a platinum foam counter electrode (area: 9.0 cm²), both immersed in the electrolyte within the quartz cell.
- **Temperature Conditions**: All experiments conducted at ambient room temperature; no active temperature control or cooling mechanism was employed.
- **Gas Handling & Analysis**:
  - **Gas Sampling**: Evolved H₂ was monitored in real time using an online gas chromatograph (GC), directly interfaced with the reactor headspace for continuous or periodic sampling.
  - **GC Instrumentation**: The GC was equipped with a **thermal conductivity detector (TCD)** and utilized high-purity nitrogen as the carrier gas, ensuring sensitive and selective detection of hydrogen.
  - **Quantification Protocol**: H₂ evolution was quantified based on the GC response; specific calibration procedures or standard gas mixtures are not detailed, but the use of an online GC-TCD setup supports reliable measurement.
- **System Integration**: The PEC cell was fully assembled within the quartz reactor, with electrodes positioned to maximize exposure to both the electrolyte and incident light.

### 5.2 Illumination Conditions
- **Light Source**: 150 W xenon (Xe) arc lamp, providing a broad spectral output suitable for simulating solar irradiation.
- **Spectral Filtering**: For general PEC testing, the lamp output was filtered to transmit wavelengths greater than 350 nm; AM1.5 irradiation was also employed for select experiments, though the specific filter type is not specified.
- **Irradiance**: The incident light intensity at the photoanode surface was precisely set to **100 mW cm⁻²**. While the exact sensor used for measurement is not specified, this value reflects standard solar simulation conditions.

- **Monochromatic Measurements**: For IPCE analysis, monochromatic light was provided by the Xe lamp through a Cornerstone monochromator, enabling wavelength-resolved efficiency assessment.

### 5.3 Reaction Medium
- **Electrolyte Composition**: Aqueous solution containing **0.25 M Na₂S** and **0.35 M Na₂SO₃** served as the electrolyte, prepared with deionized water. This composition facilitates efficient charge separation and supports high photocatalytic activity.
- **Water Quality**: Deionized water was used for all solution preparations and electrode rinsing, ensuring minimal contamination and reproducibility.

- **Sacrificial Agent Status**: The presence of Na₂S and Na₂SO₃ as hole scavengers confirms that the system operates in a **sacrificial agent-assisted mode**. There is no explicit absence of sacrificial agents; thus, the test does not represent true overall water splitting.


## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: 11.5 µmol h⁻¹ under AM1.5 illumination (100 mW cm⁻²) with an applied bias of 0.4 V in a two-electrode PEC cell using 0.25 M Na₂S and 0.35 M Na₂SO₃ as the electrolyte.
- **Incident Photon-to-Current Conversion Efficiency (IPCE)**: 14.8% at 400 nm, measured without external bias using monochromatic light and a calibrated silicon diode (IPCE calculated as: `IPCE(%) = (1240 × photocurrent density (A cm⁻²)) / (incident light power density (W cm⁻²) × wavelength (nm)) × 100`).
- **Comparative Performance**: The Au@CdS-ZnO photoanode outperformed CdS-ZnO (5.0 µmol h⁻¹) and Au-CdS-ZnO (8.3 µmol h⁻¹) controls under identical conditions.
- **Stoichiometry**: O₂ evolution was suppressed due to the presence of sacrificial agents; thus, the H₂:O₂ ratio was not directly measured or reported.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Reaction Pathway Evidence**: The use of Na₂S/Na₂SO₃ as hole scavengers ensured that H₂ evolution originated from water reduction, while O₂ evolution was intentionally suppressed, precluding direct stoichiometric confirmation.
- **Electronic Interaction Confirmation**: Optical absorption spectroscopy revealed a plasmon resonance shift from 520 nm (Au-ZnO) to 570 nm (Au@CdS-ZnO), indicating strong electronic coupling between Au and CdS, which facilitates efficient charge separation and transfer.
- **Structural Validation**: High-resolution TEM confirmed the integrity of the core–shell structure and well-defined interfaces, supporting the mechanistic rationale for enhanced PEC activity.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Continuous Operation**: The photoanode retained 98.5% of its initial photocurrent after 10 hours of continuous AM1.5 illumination at 0.4 V bias, with a stable H₂ evolution rate throughout.
- **Cycling Stability**: Performance remained nearly unchanged after 50 consecutive PEC cycles, demonstrating robust operational durability.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**: TEM imaging after prolonged testing showed that the Au@CdS core–shell structure and ZnO nanowire scaffold remained intact, confirming excellent structural stability.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Comparative Controls**: Performance was benchmarked against CdS-ZnO and Au-CdS-ZnO photoanodes, establishing the unique advantage of the core–shell nanojunction.
- **Error Analysis**: Error bars representing standard deviation were reported for photocurrent density as a function of CdS shell thickness (see Figure S6), though the number of replicates (`n`) was not specified for all metrics.
- **Measurement Reliability**: H₂ evolution was quantified using an online gas chromatograph, ensuring accurate and real-time detection.
- **Experimental Safeguards**: While explicit dark or blank controls were not detailed, the use of sacrificial agents and comparative analysis with control electrodes provided indirect validation of the light-driven nature and specificity of the observed activity.
