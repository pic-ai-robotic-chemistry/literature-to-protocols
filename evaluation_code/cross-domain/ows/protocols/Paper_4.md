## **1. Material Design & Fundamental Properties**

### 1.1 Target Composition & Crystal Chemistry
- **Nominal Composition**: Metal‐free carbon nanodot–carbon nitride (CDots–C3N4) nanocomposite comprising graphitic carbon dots embedded within a porous C3N4 matrix.
- **Synthesis Precursors & Strategy**: C3N4 was synthesized via thermal polymerization of urea (10 g heated to 550 °C with a ramp rate of 0.5 °C/min for 3 hours), while CDots were produced using an electrochemical method followed by hydrothermal treatment with ammonia at 150 °C for 7 hours.
- **Design Rationale**: Incorporation of CDots is intended to enhance visible light absorption through subband state formation and to catalyze H2O2 decomposition, thereby preventing catalyst poisoning and improving water splitting efficiency.

### 1.2 Band Structure & Energetics
- **Band Gap**: 2.77 eV, determined from Tauc plot analysis of UV–vis spectroscopy data.
- **Band Edge Positions**: Valence band at approximately 1.8 eV and conduction band at about -0.9 eV versus RHE, as assessed by ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) and complementary electrochemical techniques.
- **Electronic Properties**: The composite exhibits an ionization potential of 6.96 eV and a conduction band energy of 4.19 eV, which aligns with the necessary energy levels for efficient photogenerated charge separation during water splitting.

### 1.3 Morphology, Facets & Heterostructures
- **Morphological Characteristics**: The C3N4 matrix is highly porous with grain sizes ranging from 90 to 400 nm, as revealed by TEM/HRTEM analysis.
- **Embedded CDots**: Carbon dots sized between 2 and 10 nm showing a hexagonal crystalline structure (interplanar spacing ~0.202 nm) confirmed by high-resolution TEM.
- **Surface Area**: A specific surface area of 97 m²/g measured by the BET method enhances the water–catalyst interface.
- **Heterostructure Design**: The strategic embedding of CDots within the porous C3N4 network fosters efficient charge separation and rapid decomposition of H2O2, facilitating an effective overall water splitting process.

## **2. Synthesis & Post-Processing**

### 2.1 Synthesis Protocol
1. **CDots Pre-Synthesis**: Prepare carbon nanodots via a **typical electrochemical method** followed by **hydrothermal treatment with ammonia** (to modify and enhance the CDots properties).
2. **C3N4 Synthesis**: Mix **10 g of urea powder** with **5 mL of a 2 mg/L CDots solution** (resulting in a concentration ranging from **1.6×10⁻⁵ to 4.8×10⁻³ gCDots per gram of catalyst**) in an alumina crucible to form a homogeneous mixture.
3. **Thermal Polymerization**: Heat the mixture in a muffle furnace by ramping the temperature at **0.5 °C/min** until reaching **550 °C**, then maintain this temperature for **3 hours** (ensuring complete polymerization of urea into C3N4 and incorporation of CDots).

### 2.2 Post-Treatments & Modification
1. **Cooling**: Allow the composite to cool naturally to room temperature immediately after the thermal polymerization step.
2. **Washing**: Rinse the cooled powder with **ultrapure water** to remove soluble impurities and unreacted precursors.
3. **Drying**: Dry the washed composite at **room temperature** (with optional drying in a vacuum oven at **60 °C** for enhanced removal of moisture, if required).

### 2.3 Co-catalyst & Surface Functionalization
1. **Intrinsic Catalytic Function**: No additional noble-metal co-catalyst deposition is performed. The integrated CDots provide the catalytic functionality necessary for the **decomposition of H₂O₂** (thereby preventing catalyst poisoning and enhancing overall water splitting efficiency).

## **3. Characterization Methods & Settings**

### 3.1 Structural & Compositional Analysis

#### X-ray Diffraction (XRD)
- **Sample Preparation**: The synthesized composite was ground into a fine powder and mounted onto a zero-background silicon holder.
- **Instrument & Model**: X'Pert-Pro MPD D/max-γAX-ray diffractometer.
- **Instrument Parameters**: Cu Kα radiation (λ = 0.154178 nm) was used.
- **Measurement Conditions**: Measurements were conducted under ambient temperature and atmospheric pressure.
- **Test/Acquisition Parameters**: While the specific 2θ range and counting time were not detailed, the system was set up for phase purity analysis.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Mount the powder sample.
  2. Level the sample and perform alignment.
  3. Acquire the diffraction pattern.
- **Purpose**: To assess the phase purity and crystalline structure of the C₃N₄ matrix.
- **Data Analysis & Calibration**: Data are typically analyzed via Rietveld refinement and calibrated using standard silicon references.
- **Key Notes**: The measurements confirm the formation of the porous C₃N₄ framework.

#### Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM & HRTEM)
- **Sample Preparation**: A diluted dispersion of the composite was drop-cast onto a copper grid with a polyvinyl support film and dried in air.
- **Instrument & Model**: FEI-Tecnai F20.
- **Instrument Parameters**: HRTEM imaging was performed at an accelerating voltage of 200 kV.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Drop-cast the solution and dry the grid.
  2. Image the sample to visualize morphology.
- **Purpose**: To resolve the porous structure of C₃N₄ and confirm the CDots sizes (2–10 nm) with a hexagonal crystalline structure.
- **Key Notes**: HRTEM provided high-resolution insight into the nanostructural details.

#### Raman Spectroscopy
- **Instrument & Model**: HR 800 Raman spectroscope (JY, France) equipped with a synapse CCD detector and a confocal Olympus microscope.
- **Instrument Parameters**: Employed a 633 nm He–Ne laser with 600 g mm⁻¹ gratings.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Record spectra of the composite before and after water splitting cycles.
- **Purpose**: To assess chemical bonding and confirm the structural stability of the CDots–C₃N₄ composite.

#### Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) Spectroscopy
- **Sample Preparation**: The sample was mixed with KBr to form a pellet.
- **Instrument & Model**: Spectrum One (Perkin Elmer).
- **Purpose**: To identify functional groups and validate chemical integrity.

#### X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)
- **Instrument & Model**: KRATOS Axis ultra-DLD spectrometer using a monochromatized Mg Kα source (hν = 1283.3 eV).
- **Calibration**: Calibrated using the C 1s peak at 284.8 eV.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Load and align the sample.
  2. Acquire elemental composition data.
- **Purpose**: To determine surface compositions and chemical states.

### 3.2 Photophysical & Electrical Analysis

#### UV–vis Absorption Spectroscopy and Tauc Plot Analysis
- **Sample Preparation**: Dispersed powder samples were used in a quartz cuvette.
- **Instrument & Model**: Lambda 750 (PerkinElmer) with a 300 W Xe lamp.
- **Instrument Parameters**: Data were acquired over a wavelength range of 300–800 nm.
- **Operational Procedure**:

  2. Measure the absorption spectrum.

- **Purpose**: To determine the optical band gap (≈2.77 eV) and assess enhanced visible light absorption due to CDots incorporation.
- **Key Notes**: The formation of sub-band states is linked to the improved light harvesting capabilities of the composite.

- **Sample Preparation**: Powder sample placed in a quartz sample holder with a transparent window.
- **Operational Procedure**:
  1. Record diffuse reflectance spectra.
  2. Apply Kubelka–Munk analysis for band gap estimation.
- **Purpose**: To complement absorption measurements and ensure consistency in optical properties across the visible–NIR range.


## **4. Photocatalyst System Architecture**

### 4.1 System Configuration
- **OWS Mode**: One-step overall water splitting via a stepwise two-electron/two-electron process.
  
**For One-step System:**
- **Photocatalyst Material**: Metal‐free CDots–C3N4 nanocomposite, where the porous C3N4 acts as the primary light absorber and initiates water oxidation (producing H₂O₂ and H₂), while the embedded CDots catalyze the rapid decomposition of H₂O₂ to release O₂.
- **Mediator**: Not applicable; the intrinsic proximity between CDots and C3N4 ensures efficient electron/hole transfer without an external redox or solid mediator.

### 4.2 Operational Design
- **Light Source & Absorption Range**: Utilizes a 300 W Xenon lamp fitted with a long-pass filter allowing wavelengths λ > 420 nm, ensuring that the composite efficiently harvests visible light. The target intensity (approximately 2.84 mW/cm²) supports optimal excitation of the photocatalyst.
- **Reactor Configuration**: Conducted in a batch reactor where 150 mL of ultrapure water serves as the reaction medium. The catalyst (approximately 80 mg) is homogenously dispersed, ensuring uniform light irradiation and optimal photocatalytic performance.
- **Gas Handling & Safety Measures**: The system employs a closed gas-circulation setup, which facilitates continuous monitoring and safe collection of the evolved H₂ and O₂ gases. Prior to irradiation, the reaction suspension is thoroughly degassed to eliminate residual air, thus minimizing the risk of hazardous gas mixing and back-reaction. This design supports steady and stoichiometrically balanced gas evolution, as evidenced by a nearly ideal H₂:O₂ ratio.


In this configuration, the CDots–C3N4 system leverages its unique architecture to perform overall water splitting without reliance on external mediators, aligning with the experimental evidence and QA insights provided in the article.


## **5. Testing System Configuration**

### 5.1 Reactor & Gas Handling
- **Reactor Type & Volume**: Outer irradiation-type photoreactor constructed from Pyrex glass, connected to a closed gas-circulation system; standard reaction volume of 150 mL ultrapure water with 0.080 g photocatalyst dispersed.
- **Temperature & Stirring**: Experiments conducted at ambient temperature with continuous magnetic stirring at 400 rpm to ensure homogeneous suspension and prevent catalyst sedimentation.
- **Degassing Protocol**:
  1. The catalyst suspension is thoroughly degassed to remove air prior to irradiation, ensuring an inert, oxygen-free environment. This is achieved by repeated evacuation and purging, typically with N₂, though the protocol is described as “thoroughly degassed to remove air.”
  2. After degassing, the reactor is sealed to maintain an inert atmosphere at ambient pressure throughout the test.
- **Gas Handling & Analysis**:
  - **Gas Circulation**: The system is fully closed, allowing evolved gases (H₂ and O₂) to circulate directly from the reactor to the analysis unit.
  - **Sampling Method**: Online, continuous gas analysis is performed without manual intervention, enabling real-time monitoring of gas evolution.
  - **Gas Chromatograph (GC)**: SP-6890 model equipped with a **5 Å molecular sieve column** and a **thermal conductivity detector (TCD)**; N₂ is used as the carrier gas.
  - **GC Calibration**: Quantitative calibration is performed using standard gas mixtures of H₂ and O₂, ensuring accurate measurement of evolved gases.
  - **Gas Verification**: Only H₂ and O₂ are detected during the reaction; no CO₂ or N₂ is observed, confirming the gases originate from water splitting. For isotope labeling studies, GC-MS is employed to verify O₂ evolution from H₂¹⁸O.

### 5.2 Illumination Conditions
- **Light Source**: 300 W xenon (Xe) lamp (PLS-SXE 300, Beijing Trusttech Co. Ltd, China) positioned 3 cm above the reactor window for top-irradiation.
- **Spectral Filtering**: Kenko L-42 cutoff filter is used to achieve visible-light irradiation (λ > 420 nm). For solar simulation, an AM 1.5G solar simulator is employed to replicate the standard solar spectrum.
- **Irradiance/Photon Flux**: The incident light intensity at the reactor window is **70 mW cm⁻²**, determined by measuring the total incident power (0.63 W) over the illuminated area (9 cm²) using a calibrated photodetector or power meter.
- **Illuminated Area & Geometry**: The illuminated area is precisely defined as 9 cm², matching the cross-sectional area of the reactor window to ensure uniform and direct illumination of the catalyst suspension.
- **Alignment & Stability**: The lamp and filter are aligned perpendicular to the reactor window and fixed in place to maintain consistent illumination throughout the experiment.

### 5.3 Reaction Medium
- **Water Source**: Ultrapure water (resistivity ≥18.2 MΩ·cm) is used as the reaction medium; for isotope experiments, H₂¹⁸O is employed.
- **Electrolyte/Buffer**: No electrolytes or buffers are added for standard photocatalytic water splitting tests; the medium is neutral.
- **Atmosphere During Test**: The headspace is thoroughly degassed and sealed to maintain an inert, air-free environment at ambient pressure.
- **Confirmation of No Sacrificial Agents**: The reaction is conducted exclusively in ultrapure water, with **no sacrificial agents (electron donors or acceptors) added**. This is explicitly confirmed by the absence of any organic or nitrogenous additives and by the detection of only H₂ and O₂ as evolved gases, validating the system as a true overall water splitting test.



## **6. Performance Evaluation & Validation**

### 6.1 Activity Metrics
- **H₂ Evolution Rate**: ~8.4 mmol h⁻¹ under visible light irradiation (300 W Xe lamp, λ > 420 nm, 150 mL ultrapure water, 80 mg catalyst).
- **O₂ Evolution Rate**: ~4.1 mmol h⁻¹ under identical conditions.
- **H₂:O₂ Stoichiometric Ratio**: 2.02:1, closely matching the theoretical 2:1 ratio for overall water splitting, as confirmed by gas chromatography.
- **Apparent Quantum Yield (AQY)**: 16% at 420 ± 20 nm, 6.29% at 580 ± 15 nm, and 4.42% at 600 ± 10 nm, calculated as QE = [2 × (number of evolved H₂ molecules) / (number of incident photons)] × 100%.
- **Solar-to-Hydrogen (STH) Efficiency**: 2.0% under AM 1.5G solar simulator (0.63 W over 9 cm², 6 h reaction), calculated as the ratio of energy generated by H₂ to total incident solar energy.

### 6.2 Selectivity & Mechanism
- **Isotope Labeling Evidence**: H₂¹⁸O labeling with GC-MS detection showed exclusive evolution of ¹⁸O₂ (mass 36), directly confirming O₂ originates from water splitting.
- **Suppression of Side Reactions**: The embedded CDots catalyze rapid decomposition of H₂O₂, preventing its accumulation and associated catalyst poisoning, as demonstrated by the absence of H₂O₂ in the reaction medium and sustained gas evolution.
- **Mechanistic Controls**: Only the CDots–C₃N₄ composite (not physical mixtures or individual components) enabled overall water splitting, indicating the necessity of nanoscale proximity for the sequential two-electron process.

### 6.3 Stability & Durability
- **Long-Term Cycling**: The catalyst maintained consistent H₂ and O₂ evolution rates and quantum efficiency over 200 cycles spanning 200 days (low CDots concentration) and 50 one-day cycles (high CDots concentration), as well as during 45 days of continuous operation.
- **Mass Loss**: Negligible mass loss observed after repeated use (within 0.0008 g experimental error), indicating robust material retention.
- **Post-Reaction Characterization**: Raman, FTIR, and XPS spectra before and after extended cycling showed no detectable structural or chemical changes; no CO₂ or N₂ was detected, confirming absence of catalyst decomposition.

### 6.4 Controls & Data Quality
- **Dark Control**: No H₂ or O₂ evolution detected in the absence of light, confirming the reaction is strictly photocatalytic.
- **Blank Substrate Control**: Pure water and electrolyte without catalyst, as well as tests with only CDots or only C₃N₄, produced negligible gas, verifying the necessity of the composite structure.
- **Isotope Control**: Use of H₂¹⁸O confirmed the water origin of evolved O₂.
- **Mass Balance Monitoring**: Time-resolved measurement of H₂ and O₂ ensured maintenance of the 2:1 stoichiometry throughout operation.
- **Statistical Reporting**: Performance data presented with error bars indicating maximum and minimum values; average values marked, though the number of replicates and standard deviations were not explicitly stated.
