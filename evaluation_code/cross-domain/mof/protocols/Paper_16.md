## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

This section details the step‐by‐step synthesis procedures extracted from the article for CTNMOF, HKUST-1, and Zn-MOF. In each method, the general order of operations is maintained: precursor mixing/dissolution, modulator or structure-directing agent addition, crystallization, cooling and collection, washing and solvent exchange, drying, and activation or additional post-treatments as applicable.

#### CTNMOF Synthesis (Solvothermal Method)
1. • Precursor Mixing: A homogeneous solution is prepared by dissolving H4 CTNL and Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O in DMF.
2. • Modulator or Structure-Directing Agent Addition: The ligand H4 CTNL itself serves as the modulator, added simultaneously during precursor dissolution.
3. • Crystallization: The reaction mixture is heated in a glass tube at 90 °C for 18 hours. This condition promotes the formation of needle-like, dark-green crystals.
4. • Cooling and Collection: After the heating period, the crystals are cooled and isolated by filtration.
5. • Washing and Solvent Exchange: The crystals are washed—with DMF or water as specified—and the mother liquor is replaced with DMF, with storage maintained at 4 °C.
6. • Drying and Activation: The filtered crystals are dried under vacuum, followed by thermal activation at 80 °C for 6 hours to remove included guest molecules.

#### HKUST-1 Synthesis (Solvothermal Method)
1. • Precursor Mixing: Copper nitrate trihydrate and H₃BTC are dissolved in a mixture of DMF, deionized water, and glacial acetic acid.
2. • Modulator Incorporation: While not separately indicated, the solvent mixture and reagent ratios influence the framework assembly.
3. • Crystallization: The mixture is heated in a microwave vial at 55 °C for 3 days, yielding single crystals with a square [100] facet.
4. • Cooling and Collection: Post-heating, the crystals are isolated and the mother liquor is replaced by DMF for storage at 4 °C.

#### Zn-MOF Synthesis (Solvothermal Method)
1. • Precursor Mixing: Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, 2‑nitroterephthalic acid, and DABCO are dissolved in DMF.
2. • Crystallization: After minimal stirring and filtering, the clear solution is transferred into a Teflon-lined autoclave and heated at 125 °C for 48 hours, forming cuboid crystals.
3. • Cooling, Collection, and Post-processing: The crystals are cooled, collected, and washed with DMF. Air drying for 15 minutes is performed before further measurements.

### Comparative Analysis
The CTNMOF process offers a rapid crystallization protocol at higher temperatures, while HKUST-1 employs a lower temperature over an extended period to yield robust crystals. Zn-MOF synthesis requires a specialized Teflon-lined autoclave for high-temperature stability, highlighting differences in equipment needs and scalability. These detailed steps ensure reproducibility, as all reaction conditions, temperatures, and durations are rigorously maintained.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation of CTNMOF for pre‐use applications is achieved through a series of post‐synthesis treatments aimed at removing guest molecules and revealing the intrinsic porosity of the framework while preserving its crystallinity and mechanical integrity. This protocol is designed to prepare the MOF for subsequent gas sorption and performance evaluations, distinct from the solvent removal steps applied during characterization.

#### 5.1.1 Solvent Washing and Exchange
A critical first step involves washing the as‐synthesized CTNMOF crystals with the synthesis solvent (DMF) to eliminate residual unreacted species. This is followed by an exchange procedure in which the crystals are soaked in a non‐competing solvent (typically THF) for 24 hours. The solvent exchange not only aids in dislodging the included guest molecules but also ensures that the channels are cleansed of residual DMF. These steps collectively serve to destabilize weak host–guest interactions without imposing thermal or mechanical stress on the framework.

#### 5.1.2 Mild Heating Under Reduced Pressure
Subsequent to solvent exchange, the crystals are subjected to a gentle heating procedure under reduced pressure using a high vacuum setup. Although detailed numerical conditions (such as a specific temperature or pressure value) for this activation step are only explicitly reported in procedures associated with characterization (e.g., a 6‐hour treatment at 80 °C under high vacuum during CO₂ sorption studies), the pre‐use activation protocol deliberately avoids such conditions to ensure that the heating applied serves solely to assist in complete guest removal. The emphasis is on a controlled, mild process that gradually eliminates solvent molecules without compromising the framework’s integrity.

#### 5.1.3 Observations and Impact on MOF Properties
This activation strategy—integrating solvent washing, an extended THF soak, and controlled vacuum heating—has been observed to effectively clear the MOF channels of guest molecules. As a result, CTNMOF exhibits permanent microporosity and maintains its high phase purity. The careful balance in activation conditions is essential for achieving optimal gas sorption performance and retaining the framework’s robust mechanical characteristics, thereby confirming that the pre‐use activation process is both efficient and non‐destructive.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** Crystal structure and phase purity were determined using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) and single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SCXRD). These techniques are essential for confirming the integrity and uniformity of MOF frameworks.
- **Findings:** For CTNMOF, PXRD patterns of DMF-soaked samples at 25 °C closely matched simulated patterns derived from SCXRD data, confirming high phase purity and crystallinity. SCXRD at 26 °C revealed that CTNMOF crystallizes in the chiral space group P4122. Variable-temperature SCXRD demonstrated adaptive symmetry changes: C2221 at −180 °C and −130 °C, P41212 at −80 °C, P4122 at −58 °C and 26 °C, and P212121 at −52 °C. These transitions are reversible and linked to guest molecule dynamics. For HKUST-1 and Zn-MOF, PXRD patterns also matched simulated data, confirming phase purity and structural consistency.
- **Observations:** The reversible symmetry changes in CTNMOF upon guest exchange or temperature variation highlight its dynamic framework, in contrast to the more rigid structures of HKUST-1 and Zn-MOF.

#### 6.1.2 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was used to assess the thermal stability and guest molecule release profiles of the MOFs.
- **Findings:** CTNMOF loses included DMF guest molecules upon heating to approximately 100 °C. After guest removal, the framework remains stable up to nearly 360 °C before decomposition.
- **Observations:** The framework’s high thermal stability post-guest removal demonstrates robust structural integrity, with guest molecules departing at lower temperatures.

#### 6.1.3 Mechanical Properties and Elasticity
- **Description:** Nanoindentation using a Berkovich diamond tip was employed to quantify the Young’s modulus and probe elastic behavior, providing insight into the mechanical robustness and adaptability of the frameworks.
- **Findings:** CTNMOF exhibits a Young’s modulus of 6.31 ± 0.14 GPa in air, which decreases significantly to 1.77 ± 0.16 GPa when soaked in DMF, indicating pronounced softening and high elasticity. In THF, the modulus is similarly low (1.63 ± 0.13 GPa). No permanent indentation marks were observed after testing in DMF, confirming elastic recovery. High-pressure SCXRD revealed anisotropic contraction under 0.88 GPa, with a 5% reduction along the c axis and less than 0.4% along a/b axes. HKUST-1 displayed a higher and solvent-insensitive modulus (10.24 ± 0.33 GPa in DMF; 10.65 ± 0.11 GPa in air), while Zn-MOF showed facet-dependent values (2.04 ± 0.26 GPa in air, 3.03 ± 0.35 GPa in DMF for {110} facet; 8.92 ± 0.73 GPa for {001} facet in DMF).
- **Observations:** Guest molecules modulate the mechanical properties of CTNMOF and Zn-MOF, with solvent inclusion leading to increased elasticity. In contrast, HKUST-1 remains mechanically robust regardless of solvent environment.

#### 6.1.4 Gas Sorption and Porosity
- **Description:** Gas sorption measurements were conducted to evaluate permanent microporosity and guest-accessible volume.
- **Findings:** CTNMOF demonstrated a CO₂ uptake of 65 ml STP g⁻¹ at P/P₀ = 0.98 (−78 °C), following activation at 80 °C under reduced pressure.
- **Observations:** The observed gas uptake confirms the presence of permanent microporous channels, consistent with the framework’s dynamic yet robust structure.

#### 6.1.5 Structural Responsiveness and Reversibility
- **Description:** The adaptive nature of CTNMOF was further characterized by monitoring structural changes upon guest exchange, temperature variation, and applied pressure.
- **Findings:** SCXRD and PXRD analyses revealed that CTNMOF undergoes reversible transitions between different space groups and channel geometries upon guest removal, uptake, or solvent exchange. The original structure and symmetry are restored upon re-immersion in DMF.
- **Observations:** These results underscore the unique, reversible, and guest-responsive elasticity of CTNMOF, distinguishing it from more rigid MOFs.




### 6.2 Performance Results

#### 6.2.1 Gas Adsorption Capacity
- **Description:** Gas adsorption capacity quantifies the volume of gas a MOF can adsorb per unit mass under specified conditions, typically reported in milliliters at standard temperature and pressure per gram (ml STP g⁻¹). This metric is critical for evaluating suitability in gas storage and separation applications.
- **Experimental Data:** For CTNMOF, CO₂ uptake was measured at −78 °C and a relative pressure of P/P₀ = 0.98, following activation by heating at 80 °C under vacuum for 6 hours. The reported CO₂ uptake is 65 ml STP g⁻¹.
- **Observations and Trends:** The substantial CO₂ uptake demonstrates that CTNMOF possesses a permanent microporous structure. The framework maintains its crystallinity and porosity after guest removal and re-immersion, indicating robust cycling stability and suitability for repeated gas adsorption/desorption processes.

#### 6.2.2 Mechanical Elasticity and Responsiveness
- **Description:** Mechanical elasticity is assessed by the Young’s modulus (E), representing the material’s stiffness, and hardness (H), both determined via nanoindentation. These metrics are essential for applications requiring mechanical adaptability and resilience.
- **Experimental Data:** 
  - **CTNMOF:** In air, Young’s modulus is 6.31 ± 0.14 GPa; in DMF, it decreases to 1.77 ± 0.16 GPa. Hardness values are 0.138 ± 0.013 GPa (DMF) and higher in air. 
  - **HKUST-1:** Young’s modulus is 10.24 ± 0.33 GPa in DMF and 10.65 ± 0.11 GPa in air.
  - **Zn-MOF:** Young’s modulus is 3.03 ± 0.35 GPa in DMF and 2.04 ± 0.26 GPa in air.
- **Observations and Trends:** CTNMOF exhibits pronounced guest-dependent elasticity; inclusion of DMF or other solvents softens the framework by attenuating hydrogen bonding, while guest removal increases stiffness. HKUST-1 remains mechanically robust regardless of solvent, and Zn-MOF demonstrates high elasticity and responsiveness to guest inclusion. These findings highlight the tunable mechanical properties of catenated and guest-responsive MOFs.

#### 6.2.3 Structural Adaptability and Cycling Stability
- **Description:** Structural adaptability refers to the framework’s ability to undergo reversible structural transitions upon guest exchange or removal, while cycling stability assesses retention of performance over repeated cycles.
- **Experimental Data:** PXRD and SCXRD analyses show that CTNMOF can reversibly transition between crystalline and amorphous states upon guest removal and re-soaking in DMF. After multiple adsorption/desorption cycles, the original PXRD pattern and gas uptake capacity are restored.
- **Observations and Trends:** The reversible nature of these transitions, coupled with the retention of crystallinity and porosity, underscores CTNMOF’s robust cycling stability. This dynamic behavior is critical for practical applications involving repeated guest uptake and release.

#### 6.2.4 High-Pressure Elastic Response
- **Description:** High-pressure elasticity evaluates the framework’s deformation and recovery under hydrostatic compression, providing insight into anisotropic mechanical behavior.
- **Experimental Data:** CTNMOF crystals subjected to pressures up to 0.88 GPa exhibit anisotropic contraction, predominantly along the c axis, as determined by in situ SCXRD in a diamond anvil cell.
- **Observations and Trends:** The framework demonstrates recoverable deformation and dynamic elasticity, with guest molecules modulating the extent of contraction and recovery. This property is advantageous for applications requiring mechanical adaptability under variable external conditions.
