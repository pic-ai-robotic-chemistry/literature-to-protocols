## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The synthesis of crystalline lithium imidazolate COFs is achieved primarily via a solvothermal method that enables reproducible polymerization through imine condensation. The experimental procedure is outlined in clearly numbered steps as follows:

#### • Experimental Procedure for Solvothermal Synthesis

1. **Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation**  
   - Reagents: An imidazole‐substituted diamine and 1,3,5‐triformylbenzene are combined in a solvent mixture of ethanol and mesitylene.  
   - Catalyst/Modulator: A 3 M aqueous acetic acid solution is added to facilitate imine formation.  
   - Equipment: The reaction is assembled in a Schlenk tube. The mixture is flash frozen in liquid nitrogen and evacuated to approximately 200 mTorr before sealing, ensuring an inert atmosphere.

2. **Polymerization/Condensation**  
   - The sealed reaction vessel is heated to 120 °C for 3 days. This stage allows complete polymerization and imine condensation, with observations such as the formation of a pale yellow precipitate indicating successful reaction progression.

3. **Crystallization / Reaction Completion**  
   - During the sustained heating, the COF framework crystallizes. The process may include a period of uninterrupted reaction to promote uniform stacking and high crystallinity as confirmed by PXRD in subsequent characterization.

4. **Washing / Purification**  
   - Post-reaction, the precipitate is recovered using vacuum filtration. It is sequentially washed with solvents—methylene chloride, ethanol, and acetone—to remove unreacted residues and by-products.  
   - The product is further purified utilizing Soxhlet extraction in THF for 24 hours.

5. **Drying**  
   - The purified COF is dried under reduced pressure to remove any residual solvent. The drying conditions are optimized to maintain the crystalline structure without collapsing the porous network.

Additional variants, such as CH₃‑ImCOF and CF₃‑ImCOF, follow an analogous procedure with substitution effects during synthesis. Post‑synthetic lithiation (using n‑BuLi in hexane under controlled temperature conditions) is performed later to yield lithiated derivatives with enhanced ionic conductivities.

This structured approach, incorporating specific reagents, precise temperature and temporal controls, and methodical purification, ensures that the synthesis is both accurate and reproducible for subsequent applications in lithium-ion conduction studies.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: H‑ImCOF

##### Step [1]: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: 25 mg (0.075 mmol) imidazole‐substituted diamine 1‑H, 8 mg (0.05 mmol) 1,3,5‑triformylbenzene; solvents: 0.8 mL ethanol and 0.2 mL mesitylene; modulator: 0.2 mL 3 M aqueous acetic acid.
- **Conditions**: Begin at room temperature for mixing; flash freeze at 77 K, then evacuate to 200 mTorr; seal the ampule.
- **Process**:
  - Dissolve the diamine and trialdehyde in the solvent mixture.
  - Add the acetic acid modulator.
  - Flash freeze the reaction ampule in liquid nitrogen, then evacuate.
- **Key Steps**: Maintaining an inert, moisture‐free environment is critical.

##### Step [2]: COF Assembly
- **Reagents**: Prepared precursors from Step 1.
- **Conditions**: Heat at 120 °C for 3 days in a sealed Schlenk tube under a nitrogen atmosphere.
- **Process**:
  - Place the sealed ampule in a preheated oven at 120 °C.
  - Allow the imine‐condensation reaction to proceed without stirring, yielding a pale‐yellow precipitate.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure complete imine bond formation by sustained heat while avoiding agitation.

##### Step [3]: Purification and Drying
- **Process**:
  - Isolate the precipitate by vacuum filtration.
  - Wash sequentially with methylene chloride, ethanol, and acetone.
  - Perform Soxhlet extraction in THF for 24 hours if further purification is required.
  - Finally, dry the product under reduced pressure at 120 °C overnight.
- **Key Steps**: Consistent washing and thorough drying ensure reproducibility.

##### Step [4]: Post‑Synthetic Lithiation
- **Reagents**: n‑BuLi in hexane.
- **Conditions**: Maintain the dispersion in hexane in an ice bath during addition, followed by 16 hours of room temperature stirring.
- **Process**:
  - Disperse the dried H‑ImCOF powder in hexane.
  - Add n‑BuLi dropwise while keeping the reaction cold (ice bath for 10 minutes).
  - Stir the mixture at ambient temperature for 16 hours.
  - Recover the lithiated COF by centrifugation, washing the solid five times with dry hexane.
  - Dry under vacuum.
- **Key Steps**: Precise cooling during n‑BuLi addition minimizes side reactions and preserves framework integrity.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation of the COFs was performed as part of the synthesis and post‐synthesis workup to ensure a clean, porous framework ready for application testing. The process incorporated a sequence of solvent exchanges, vacuum drying, and a chemical activation step when converting the imidazole groups to their lithiated form.

#### 5.1.1 Solvent Exchange and Soxhlet Extraction
- **Definition:** Solvent exchange is employed to remove high-boiling and strongly interacting synthesis solvents, thereby preventing pore collapse and facilitating an accessible porous network.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The as‐synthesized COF powders were first washed sequentially with water, methylene chloride, ethanol, and acetone. This was followed by Soxhlet extraction using tetrahydrofuran (THF) for 24 hours to further remove residual impurities.
- **Observations:** The sequential solvent wash and extended Soxhlet extraction contributed to improved porosity and crystallinity without compromising the structural integrity of the framework.

#### 5.1.2 Vacuum Drying
- **Definition:** Vacuum drying is critical for eliminating residual moisture and solvents from the pore system, ensuring that the porous structure remains activated.
- **Experimental Conditions:** After the solvent exchange procedures, the COF samples were vacuum-dried at 120 °C. The procedure was carried out overnight (approximately 24 hours) under low-pressure conditions, with the reaction vessel initially evacuated to around 200 mTorr.
- **Observations:** This step effectively removed traces of solvent without damaging the framework, as confirmed by subsequent porosity and stability measurements.

#### 5.1.3 Chemical Activation via Lithiation
- **Definition:** Chemical activation by post-synthetic lithiation converts the COF’s imidazole groups to a lithium imidazolate form, which is essential for enhancing ionic conductivity.
- **Experimental Conditions:** In this step, a dispersion of the COF in hexane was prepared. A 1.6 M n‑BuLi solution in hexane (0.1 mL) was added dropwise in an ice bath over 10 minutes. The mixture was then stirred at room temperature for 16 hours, followed by multiple washes with hexane and a final vacuum drying.
- **Observations:** The controlled addition and extended stirring ensured thorough conversion, preserving crystallinity while introducing the desired lithium functionality.

Each activation parameter was carefully optimized to achieve complete solvent removal and controlled chemical modification, thereby maintaining the structural and functional properties necessary for high Li-ion conductivity.

### 5.2 Application/Testing

The evaluation of COF performance was carried out under carefully controlled experimental conditions to ensure reproducibility and enable direct extraction of the reported parameters. Two primary application tests were executed: ionic conductivity measurements for the lithiated frameworks and gas adsorption studies. Both tests emphasize the influence of temperature on performance while maintaining consistent ambient conditions.

#### 5.2.1 Test Temperature
The ionic conductivity evaluations and symmetric lithium cell cycling tests were conducted at room temperature. For ionic conductivity, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed in a controlled environment under a dry argon atmosphere. The temperature stability of the COFs was further confirmed by recording EIS measurements during a heating process in a small box furnace, where each temperature point was equilibrated for 1 hour. In contrast, gas adsorption tests utilized nitrogen adsorption isotherms measured at 77 K to assess pore characteristics and uptake behaviors.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of the COFs were assessed using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), with structural models refined by the Pawley method.
- **Findings:** H‑ImCOF exhibited intensive PXRD peaks at 2θ = 2.8°, 4.9°, and 5.7°, corresponding to the (100), (110), and (200) planes, respectively. The refined unit cell parameters (a = b = 36.95 Å, c = 4.15 Å, α = β = 90°, γ = 120°) confirm a fully eclipsed AA stacking mode with an interlayer distance of 4.15 Å. The experimental PXRD patterns closely matched simulated models, indicating high crystallinity and phase purity. After lithiation, H‑Li‑ImCOF retained crystallinity, with peaks at 2θ = 2.6° and 5.6°. CH₃‑ImCOF showed lower crystallinity, evidenced by a broad hump in the 15°–30° 2θ range, while CF₃‑ImCOF maintained sharp, intense peaks, reflecting higher structural order.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition
- **Description:** Elemental composition was determined by CHN analysis to verify framework stoichiometry and purity.
- **Findings:** For H‑ImCOF, the calculated values (C, 81.0%; H, 5.0%; N, 14.0%) were closely matched by experimental results (C, 77.1%; H, 5.6%; N, 12.9%), supporting successful synthesis and correct stoichiometry.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen adsorption isotherms at 77 K were used to determine BET surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution, with QSDFT analysis for pore size.
- **Findings:** H‑ImCOF displayed a BET surface area of 1120 m²/g and a pore volume of 0.87 cm³/g, with a main pore size of 2.6 nm. Lithiation reduced these values in H‑Li‑ImCOF to 350 m²/g and 0.59 cm³/g, with a pore size of 2.9 nm. CH₃‑ImCOF and CF₃‑ImCOF had lower surface areas (460 and 705 m²/g) and smaller pores (2.3 and 2.2 nm), with further reductions upon lithiation, except for CF₃‑Li‑ImCOF, which retained higher porosity.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Microstructure
- **Description:** Morphology and microstructure were examined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM).
- **Findings:** TEM images of H‑ImCOF revealed nanoscale crystallites with highly ordered hexagonal mesopores (~2.6 nm), consistent with porosity data. Both pristine and lithiated COFs maintained uniform morphology, with no significant changes observed after lithiation.

#### 6.1.5 Chemical Structure Verification
- **Description:** FT‑IR and solid-state ^13C CP‑MAS NMR were employed to confirm imine bond formation and framework integrity.
- **Findings:** FT‑IR spectra showed disappearance of aldehyde C=O peaks and emergence of strong imine (C=N) stretching at 1624–1625 cm⁻¹. ^13C CP‑MAS NMR displayed imine carbon signals at 159–160 ppm, with absence of aldehyde carbon peaks, confirming successful condensation.

#### 6.1.6 Lithium Incorporation
- **Description:** ^7Li solid-state NMR was used to verify lithium incorporation and environment in lithiated COFs.
- **Findings:** Single ^7Li resonance peaks were observed for each lithiated COF: CH₃‑Li‑ImCOF at 1.65 ppm, H‑Li‑ImCOF at 1.33 ppm, and CF₃‑Li‑ImCOF at −0.29 ppm, indicating uniform lithium environments and successful lithiation.

#### 6.1.7 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) assessed the thermal robustness of the frameworks.
- **Findings:** Pristine COFs exhibited onset decomposition temperatures around 450 °C, indicating excellent thermal stability. Lithiated COFs showed slightly reduced stability, with continuous weight losses of 8–15% up to 450 °C, but retained high thermal robustness suitable for practical applications.



### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Ionic Conductivity

- **Description:** Ionic conductivity (σ) quantifies the ability of the COF-based solid electrolyte to transport lithium ions, a critical parameter for solid-state battery applications. It is calculated using the equation:  
  $$ \sigma = \frac{L}{R \times A} $$
  where \( L \) is pellet thickness, \( R \) is resistance (from EIS Nyquist plots), and \( A \) is the cross-sectional area.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - **H‑Li‑ImCOF (with ~20 wt% propylene carbonate):** 5.3 × 10⁻³ S/cm  
  - **CH₃‑Li‑ImCOF:** 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ S/cm  
  - **CF₃‑Li‑ImCOF:** 7.2 × 10⁻³ S/cm

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The ionic conductivity is strongly influenced by the electronic nature of the imidazolate substituent. The electron-withdrawing CF₃ group in CF₃‑Li‑ImCOF yields the highest conductivity, while the electron-donating CH₃ group results in the lowest. The addition of propylene carbonate as a plasticizer further enhances conductivity.

#### 6.2.2 Lithium-Ion Transference Number

- **Description:** The lithium-ion transference number (\( t_{Li^+} \)) represents the fraction of total ionic current carried by lithium ions, determined using the Bruce–Vincent–Evans method:
  $$
  t_{Li^+} = \frac{I_S(\Delta V - I_0R_0)}{I_0(\Delta V - I_SR_S)}
  $$
  where \( I_0 \) and \( I_S \) are initial and steady-state currents, \( R_0 \) and \( R_S \) are initial and steady-state resistances, and \( \Delta V \) is the applied voltage.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - **H‑Li‑ImCOF:** ~0.88  
  - **CH₃‑Li‑ImCOF:** 0.93  
  - **CF₃‑Li‑ImCOF:** 0.81

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  All lithiated COFs exhibit high lithium-ion transference numbers, confirming effective immobilization of anionic sites and single-ion conduction. The physically blended H‑ImCOF/LiClO₄ system, by contrast, shows a much lower \( t_{Li^+} \) (0.21), highlighting the advantage of covalent immobilization.

#### 6.2.3 Activation Energy for Ion Conduction

- **Description:** Activation energy (\( E_a \)) reflects the energy barrier for lithium-ion migration, determined from the slope of Arrhenius plots of conductivity versus temperature.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - **H‑Li‑ImCOF:** 0.12 eV  
  - **CH₃‑Li‑ImCOF:** 0.27 eV  
  - **CF₃‑Li‑ImCOF:** 0.10 eV

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  Lower activation energies correspond to easier ion transport. CF₃‑Li‑ImCOF demonstrates the lowest \( E_a \), consistent with its superior conductivity.

#### 6.2.4 Electrochemical and Cycling Stability

- **Description:** Stability is assessed by the retention of ionic conductivity and voltage profile during repeated cycling in symmetric Li/Li-ImCOF/Li cells at 0.1 mA/cm².

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - **Cycling:** Stable voltage profiles over >40 cycles at room temperature  
  - **Thermal Stability:** Decomposition onset at ~450 °C (TGA)

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The COFs maintain structural integrity and performance after extended cycling, with no significant degradation observed. Lithiated COFs also exhibit robust thermal stability suitable for battery operation.

#### 6.2.5 Structure–Performance Correlation

- **Description:** The relationship between framework structure (pore size, substituent effects) and ion transport properties.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - **Pore Size:** ~2.6–3.0 nm (QSDFT analysis)  
  - **BET Surface Area:** Decreases upon lithiation (e.g., H‑ImCOF: 1120 m²/g → H‑Li‑ImCOF: 350 m²/g)

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  Electron-withdrawing substituents (CF₃) increase pore acidity and reduce Li–N binding strength, enhancing ion mobility. The well-defined, interconnected pores facilitate efficient lithium transport, as confirmed by solid-state NMR and conductivity measurements.
