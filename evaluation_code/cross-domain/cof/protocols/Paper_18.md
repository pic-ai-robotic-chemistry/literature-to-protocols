## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

This section details the reported synthesis procedures for the TPB-DMTP-COF and its subsequent post-synthetic modification, providing sufficient experimental clarity for reproducibility.

#### Solvothermal Synthesis of TPB-DMTP-COF
1. • Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
  - Reagents: 1,3,5-tri-(4-aminophenyl)benzene (TAPB, 0.080 mmol, 28.1 mg) and 2,5-dimethoxyterephthalaldehyde (DMTA, 0.120 mmol, 23.3 mg) are dissolved in an o-dichlorobenzene (o-DCB) and n-butanol (n-BuOH) solvent mixture.  
  - Catalyst: Acetic acid (6 M, 0.1 mL) is added.  
  - Preparation: The solution is degassed using three freeze–pump–thaw cycles.
2. • Polymerization/Condensation  
  - The degassed solution is sealed in a Pyrex tube (10 mL) and subjected to heating at 120 °C for three days to facilitate condensation.
3. • Crystallization/Reaction  
  - During the reaction, a dark-green precipitate forms, evidencing successful COF network formation.
4. • Washing/Purification  
  - The precipitate is collected via centrifugation, washed multiple times with THF and acetonitrile, and further purified by Soxhlet extraction with THF for one day.
5. • Drying  
  - The cleaned powder is dried under vacuum at 120 °C overnight.

*Comparative Note:* This solvothermal approach is advantageous for producing highly crystalline and porous COFs; however, the use of sealed Pyrex tubes and precise temperature control might limit scalability.

#### Post-synthetic Click Modification
1. • Reagent Incorporation  
  - A quantitative azide–alkyne “click” reaction attaches (S)-2-(azidomethyl)pyrrolidine to ethynyl sites present in intermediate [HC≡C]ₓ-TPB-DMTP-COFs.
2. • Reaction Conditions  
  - The COF is suspended in a THF/water mixture, with CuI and N,N-diisopropylethylamine added as catalyst and base, respectively.
  - Stirring is carried out at room temperature for four hours.
3. • Purification and Drying  
  - Following centrifugation, the product is washed five times with THF and acetonitrile and then dried at room temperature under vacuum, yielding the final [(S)-Py]ₓ-TPB-DMTP-COF.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation process for the TPB‐DMTP‐COF is designed to efficiently remove residual guest molecules and restore the open porosity crucial for its catalytic performance. Following initial synthesis, the COF undergoes a two-step activation process comprising a solvent exchange via Soxhlet extraction and subsequent thermal vacuum drying.

#### 5.1.1 Solvent Exchange
To eliminate trapped solvents from the framework, the as‐synthesized TPB‐DMTP‐COF is initially subjected to Soxhlet extraction. In this step, tetrahydrofuran (THF) is employed as the extraction solvent. The COF sample is continuously washed with THF for a duration of one day. This solvent exchange process is critical because it replaces high-boiling-point synthesis solvents with a more volatile solvent, thereby reducing the risk of pore collapse during subsequent thermal treatment. The repeated refreshing of THF ensures that the guest molecules, which might hinder the effective reopening of the pores, are completely removed.

#### 5.1.2 Thermal Vacuum Drying
After the Soxhlet extraction, the COF powder is dried under vacuum at 120 °C for 12 hours. This thermal activation stage serves to evaporate any residual THF and remaining high-boiling impurities from the framework. The selected temperature of 120 °C is optimal in facilitating solvent removal while preserving the structural integrity and high crystallinity of the COF. Although the drying is performed “under vacuum,” the article does not specify the exact pressure conditions; however, the approach has proven effective in ensuring that the porosity—as reflected in high BET surface areas—and the mesostructural order remain intact.

#### 5.1.3 Observations on Activation Efficiency
The combined activation parameters—Soxhlet extraction with THF followed by vacuum drying at 120 °C for 12 hours—result in complete removal of guest molecules. This protocol preserves the COF’s intrinsic porosity and crystallinity, as evidenced by consistent PXRD and BET measurements. Importantly, the activation process is well separated from the post‐synthetic click modification, ensuring that the framework is fully prepared for catalytic applications in asymmetric Michael reactions.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of the COFs were determined by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), with further confirmation via Pawley and Rietveld refinements.
- **Findings:** TPB-DMTP-COF exhibited six prominent PXRD peaks, with the most intensive at 2θ = 2.76° (assigned to the (100) facet), and additional peaks at 4.82°, 5.60°, 7.42°, 9.70°, and 25.2° corresponding to the (110), (200), (210), (220), and (001) facets, respectively. The experimental PXRD pattern closely matched the simulated AA (eclipsed) stacking model, with lattice parameters a = b ≈ 37 Å, c ≈ 3.5 Å, and space group P6.
- **Observations:** The sharpness and intensity of the (100) peak, along with low R^P and RWP values from refinements, confirm high crystallinity and phase purity. After post-synthetic modification with (S)-Py units, the main diffraction peaks and their positions were retained, indicating preservation of the crystalline framework, though peak intensity decreased with higher functionalization due to increased amorphous character.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption isotherms at 77 K were used to assess BET and Langmuir surface areas, pore volume, and pore size distribution.
- **Findings:** TPB-DMTP-COF displayed a BET surface area of 2,105 m²/g, a Langmuir surface area of 3,336 m²/g, and a pore volume of 1.28 cm³/g. The average pore size was 3.26 nm, with a type IV isotherm indicative of mesoporosity. Chiral-modified COFs showed slightly reduced pore sizes (3.07, 2.95, and 2.86 nm) as the density of (S)-Py units increased.
- **Observations:** The high surface area and uniform mesopore size reflect successful framework formation and precise pore engineering. Surface area and porosity were retained after exposure to harsh conditions (boiling water, 12 M HCl, 14 M NaOH), demonstrating exceptional chemical stability.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition and Functional Group Incorporation
- **Description:** CHN elemental analysis and FTIR spectroscopy were employed to confirm elemental composition and functional group incorporation.
- **Findings:** Elemental analysis confirmed the expected C, H, and N content for both pristine and modified COFs, with increased nitrogen content after click modification, consistent with triazole and (S)-Py incorporation. FTIR spectra showed characteristic C=N stretching bands (1658–1533 cm⁻¹) confirming imine bond formation. The disappearance of C≡C (2,120 cm⁻¹) and C≡C–H (3,300 cm⁻¹) bands after the click reaction indicated quantitative conversion of ethynyl groups.
- **Observations:** The close match between theoretical and experimental elemental values, along with FTIR changes, confirms both the successful formation of the framework and the quantitative nature of the post-synthetic modification.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Microstructure
- **Description:** High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) was used to visualize the morphology and microstructure.
- **Findings:** HR-TEM images revealed well-defined hexagonal polygon textures and ordered stacking layers, directly visualizing the periodic layered morphology and nanochannels of the COF.
- **Observations:** The uniformity and order observed in HR-TEM confirm controlled nucleation and growth. No significant morphological changes were detected after chiral modification.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal and Chemical Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and stability tests in harsh chemical environments assessed the robustness of the COFs.
- **Findings:** TPB-DMTP-COF showed no weight loss up to 400 °C under N₂, indicating high thermal stability. Complete combustion occurred above 340 °C in O₂. The framework retained crystallinity and porosity after one week in boiling water, 12 M HCl, and 14 M NaOH.
- **Observations:** The incorporation of methoxy groups reinforced interlayer interactions, resulting in outstanding thermal and chemical stability suitable for practical applications.




### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Catalytic Activity in Asymmetric Michael Addition
- **Description:** Catalytic activity was evaluated by the conversion rate and yield in the asymmetric Michael addition of cyclohexanone to β-nitrostyrene, using the chiral COF [(S)-Py]ₓ-TPB-DMTP-COF as a heterogeneous organocatalyst. The reaction was performed in neat water at room temperature, aligning with green chemistry principles.
- **Experimental Data:** For [(S)-Py]₀.₁₇-TPB-DMTP-COF, 100% conversion and 95% yield were achieved within 12 hours using 0.01 mmol catalyst in 0.4 mL water, with benzoic acid and N,N-diisopropylethylamine as additives.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalyst demonstrated high activity under mild, aqueous conditions. Increasing the chiral site loading (x = 0.34 or 0.50) resulted in longer reaction times (17 and 34 hours, respectively) to reach full conversion, attributed to overlapping catalytic sites that reduced substrate accessibility.

#### 6.2.2 Enantioselectivity and Diastereoselectivity
- **Description:** Enantioselectivity was quantified as enantiomeric excess (e.e., %) and diastereoselectivity as the diastereomeric ratio (d.r.), reflecting the catalyst’s ability to induce stereoselectivity in the product.
- **Experimental Data:** [(S)-Py]₀.₁₇-TPB-DMTP-COF afforded 92% e.e. and a d.r. of 90/10 for the Michael addition product. These values were determined by HPLC (e.e.) and ^1H NMR (d.r.).
- **Observations and Trends:** The high enantio- and diastereoselectivity are attributed to the well-defined chiral environment provided by the (S)-Py-modified channel walls. The selectivity remained high across different chiral site loadings, indicating effective asymmetric induction by the immobilized catalyst.

#### 6.2.3 Recyclability and Stability
- **Description:** Recyclability was assessed by repeated catalytic cycles, measuring retention of activity and selectivity. Stability was evaluated by monitoring structural integrity and performance after exposure to harsh conditions.
- **Experimental Data:** Over five consecutive cycles, [(S)-Py]₀.₁₇-TPB-DMTP-COF maintained 100% conversion, with yield decreasing slightly from 95% to 92% and e.e. from 94% to 93%. The diastereoselectivity remained high (d.r. 97/3). Catalyst recovery exceeded 99% after each cycle.
- **Observations and Trends:** Only minor increases in reaction time (from 6 to 13 hours) and negligible losses in yield and selectivity were observed, demonstrating excellent reusability. PXRD and BET analyses confirmed that the COF retained its crystallinity and porosity after catalysis and after exposure to boiling water, strong acid, and base, underscoring its exceptional chemical and thermal stability.

#### 6.2.4 Structure–Performance Relationship
- **Description:** The relationship between the COF’s structural features and its catalytic performance was examined.
- **Experimental Data:** The COF exhibited high crystallinity, uniform mesopores (~3 nm), and robust framework stability. Post-synthetic modification with (S)-Py introduced chiral catalytic sites without compromising porosity or crystallinity.
- **Observations and Trends:** The open, mesoporous architecture facilitated efficient substrate access and accumulation, while the immobilized chiral sites enabled high activity and selectivity. Excessive chiral site density led to reduced efficiency due to steric hindrance, highlighting the importance of optimal functionalization.
