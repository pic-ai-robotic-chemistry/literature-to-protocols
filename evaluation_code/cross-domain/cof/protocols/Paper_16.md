## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The article reports two primary COF synthesis methods—solvothermal synthesis for COF-OEt and a modified, cross-linking approach for COF-OAl. Both methods follow a similar structured workflow, detailed below.

#### • COF-OEt (Solvothermal Synthesis)

1. **Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation**  
   - Reagents: 10.0 mg 3,5-diformylphenylboronic acid (DFPBA) and 16.2 mg 2,5-diethoxyterephthalohydrazide (DETH).  
   - Solvent System: A 1:1 mixture of n-butyl alcohol and o-dichlorobenzene (total volume 1.5 mL).  
   - Process: The mixture undergoes three freeze–pump–thaw cycles to remove air and moisture.

2. **Polymerization/Condensation**  
   - The reaction mixture is sealed in a glass ampoule under vacuum.  
   - Conditions: Heated at 150 °C for 3 days, facilitating both polymerization and condensation.  
   - Observation: Formation of a yellow colloid, indicating the onset of gelation.

3. **Crystallization / Reaction**  
   - With continued heating, crystallization occurs within the sealed environment.

4. **Washing / Purification**  
   - Post-reaction, the resultant material is washed sequentially with ethanol and methyl alcohol.

5. **Drying**  
   - Drying is performed under dynamic vacuum at 120 °C, ensuring removal of residual solvents.

#### • COF-OAl (Solvothermal Synthesis with Post-synthetic Cross-linking)

1. **Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation**  
   - Reagents: 10.0 mg DFPBA and 17.2 mg 2,5-bis(allyloxy)terephthalohydrazide (BATH).  
   - Solvent System: A 1:1 mixture of n-butyl alcohol and mesitylene.  
   - The same freeze–pump–thaw procedure is applied prior to sealing.

2. **Polymerization/Condensation**  
   - Sealed under vacuum in a glass ampoule and heated at 120 °C for 3 days.

3. **Crystallization, Washing, and Drying**  
   - The product is filtered and washed with ethanol then dried to obtain the COF-OAl.

4. **Additional Post-treatment – Covalent Cross-linking**  
   - Process: COF-OAl is stirred with Grubbs catalyst (1st generation) in dichloromethane.  
   - Conditions: Reflux at 45 °C for 36 hours, enabling alkene metathesis and cross-link formation.

This structured extraction provides a reproducible framework, with explicit numerical values and step ordering—from monomer mixing through polymerization/condensation to subsequent purification and post-treatment—ensuring clarity and comparability for researchers aiming to replicate these COF syntheses.

### 5.2 Application/Testing

#### 5.2.1 Hydrolysis Testing Conditions for COF-OEt  
The application test for COF-OEt involves a controlled hydrolysis process designed to transform the lamellar COF into hollow organic nanotubes. In this procedure, COF-OEt is suspended in an aqueous hydrochloric acid solution at a concentration of 6 M. The reaction is carried out at room temperature over a period of 6 days. This hydrolysis selectively cleaves the boroxine rings while preserving the hydrazone linkages, thereby promoting the formation of nanotubes. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis confirmed the successful transformation with the resulting nanotubes exhibiting a mean diameter of approximately 5.5 ± 0.6 nm. This precise condition is crucial in achieving reproducible formation of nanotubes with the desired nanoscale channel structure.

#### 5.2.2 Covalent Cross-Linking and Stability Testing for COF-OAl-CR  
For COF-OAl, application testing extends to post-synthetic modification via covalent cross-linking. Initially synthesized at 120 °C for 3 days in a 1:1 mixture of n-butyl alcohol and mesitylene, COF-OAl undergoes a subsequent cross-linking step. In this step, the COF is stirred in dichloromethane with Grubbs catalyst 1st generation under reflux conditions at 45 °C for 36 hours. This reaction facilitates the formation of covalent bonds between stacked macrocycles, thereby locking the multilayer structure and significantly enhancing the material’s stability. The improvement in robustness is evident when comparing the cross-linked nanotubes to those obtained from COF-OEt, which are more sensitive under ultrasonic treatment.

#### 5.2.3 Ultrasonic Sensitivity Evaluation  
To assess the stability of the COF-derived nanotubes under operational conditions, ultrasonic treatment is applied and subsequent morphological analysis is conducted via TEM. This evaluation monitors potential disassembly or disruption of the nanotubular structures. Observations indicate that while COF-OEt-derived nanotubes are prone to disruption, the cross-linked COF-OAl-CR nanotubes exhibit enhanced structural integrity. These testing conditions, therefore, provide insight into the impact of controlled chemical modifications on the application performance of COF-based materials.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystallinity and phase purity of the COFs were assessed by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), a key technique for confirming framework order and stacking arrangements.
- **Findings:** For COF-OEt, the PXRD profile displayed an intense diffraction peak at 2.42°, with additional peaks at 4.05°, 6.59°, 8.85°, 11.50°, and 22.27°. These peaks closely matched simulated patterns for both eclipsed (AA) and staggered (AB) stacking models, with the experimental data and lower total energy supporting AA stacking as the predominant arrangement. COF-OAl exhibited PXRD patterns in excellent agreement with simulated models, with lattice parameters determined as a = b = 39.00 Å, c = 4.07 Å, α = β = 90°, γ = 120°, confirming the dual-pore crystalline framework.
- **Observations:** The sharp and well-defined peaks indicate high crystallinity and phase purity, essential for subsequent structural transformations.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Bond Formation
- **Description:** Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) and solid-state ^13C CP/MAS NMR were employed to verify the formation of key covalent linkages within the COFs.
- **Findings:** FTIR spectra of both COF-OEt and COF-OAl showed attenuation of –CHO, –OH, and –NHNH₂ bands, alongside the emergence of characteristic CN (hydrazone) and B₃O₃ (boroxine) bands, confirming successful condensation. In ^13C CP/MAS NMR, a resonance at 148.9 ppm was observed for both COFs, corresponding to the hydrazone carbon.
- **Observations:** These spectroscopic signatures confirm the formation of the intended framework linkages and the absence of unreacted monomers.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms (BET analysis) were used to determine surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution, which are critical for evaluating framework accessibility.
- **Findings:** COF-OEt exhibited a BET surface area of 465 m²/g and a total pore volume of 0.85 cm³/g, with a main pore size distribution around 23.5 Å. COF-OAl showed a lower BET surface area of 202 m²/g and a main pore size centered at 20.0 Å, consistent with the theoretical hexagonal pore diameter (19.5 Å). The reduced surface area in COF-OAl is attributed to gelation during synthesis, which limits solvent removal.
- **Observations:** Both COFs display dual-pore architectures, but only the larger hexagonal pores are accessible to N₂, as the rectangular pores are too narrow.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Structural Transformation
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were utilized to examine particle morphology and post-synthetic transformations.
- **Findings:** SEM revealed that as-synthesized COFs aggregate into irregular, lamellar particles. TEM showed that pristine COF-OEt and COF-OAl possess lamellar morphologies, which transform into hollow nanotubes (NT-OEt and NT-OAl-CR) after hydrolysis. The mean diameters of the nanotubes were 5.5 (±0.6) nm for NT-OEt and 5.6 (±0.5) nm for NT-OAl-CR, closely matching theoretical predictions.
- **Observations:** The transformation from 2D lamellar frameworks to 1D nanotubes demonstrates the structural tunability of these COFs. Intertwining of nanotubes, attributed to hydrogen bonding at the periphery, was frequently observed.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed to assess the thermal robustness of the COFs.
- **Findings:** TGA indicated that COF-OEt possesses good thermal stability, with no significant decomposition at moderate temperatures.
- **Observations:** The high thermal stability supports the suitability of these materials for further processing and application.




### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Fabrication and Structural Integrity of Organic Nanotubes

- **Description:** The primary application explored is the transformation of two-dimensional covalent organic frameworks (COFs) into one-dimensional organic nanotubes via selective hydrolysis. The performance metric centers on the successful formation, dimensional control, and stability of these nanotubes, which are relevant for applications requiring nanoscale channels and robust nanostructures.

- **Experimental Data:**
  - **COF-OEt:** Upon hydrolysis in 6 M HCl at room temperature for 6 days, COF-OEt disassembles into hollow organic nanotubes (NT-OEt) with a mean diameter of 5.5 ± 0.6 nm, closely matching theoretical predictions.
  - **COF-OAl-CR:** Similarly, COF-OAl, after covalent cross-linking via alkene metathesis (using Grubbs catalyst 1st generation), yields NT-OAl-CR nanotubes with a mean diameter of 5.6 ± 0.5 nm following hydrolysis.

- **Observations and Trends:** The dual-pore design of both COFs enables precise control over nanotube diameter, directly inherited from the COF precursor. TEM imaging confirms the successful top-down transformation from lamellar COF to discrete nanotubes, validating the structural tunability of the approach.

#### 6.2.2 Stability and Robustness under Mechanical Stress

- **Description:** Stability is assessed by the retention of nanotube morphology after exposure to ultrasonic treatment, a proxy for mechanical robustness. This metric is critical for practical applications where materials may encounter physical agitation.

- **Experimental Data:**
  - **NT-OEt:** Nanotubes derived from COF-OEt are labile; TEM images show complete disassembly after 10 minutes of ultrasonic treatment, with no regeneration observed after 12 hours at room temperature.
  - **NT-OAl-CR:** In contrast, nanotubes derived from covalently cross-linked COF-OAl (NT-OAl-CR) remain intact after identical ultrasonic treatment, demonstrating enhanced mechanical stability.

- **Observations and Trends:** The introduction of covalent cross-links via alkene metathesis significantly improves the robustness of the nanotubes, preventing disassembly under conditions that disrupt non-cross-linked analogues. This highlights the importance of post-synthetic modification for enhancing material performance.

#### 6.2.3 Structural and Thermal Stability

- **Description:** Structural and thermal stability are evaluated through PXRD, BET, and TGA analyses, which are essential for determining suitability in demanding environments.

- **Experimental Data:**
  - **COF-OEt:** BET surface area: 465 m²/g; main pore size: 23.5 Å. TGA confirms good thermal stability.
  - **COF-OAl:** BET surface area: 202 m²/g; pore size distribution centered at 20.0 Å. PXRD confirms high crystallinity and retention of structure post-cross-linking.

- **Observations and Trends:** Both COFs exhibit defined porosity and thermal stability, with COF-OAl showing lower surface area due to gelation. The retention of crystallinity and porosity after cross-linking and hydrolysis underscores the frameworks’ resilience and adaptability for further application-driven research.
