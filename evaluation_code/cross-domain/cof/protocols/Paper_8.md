## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of COF‑701 were assessed using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) with Pawley refinement, complemented by FT‑IR spectroscopy.
- **Findings:** PXRD patterns matched a staggered (AB) stacking model in space group P63/m, confirming a layered hcb topology. Pawley refinement yielded unit cell parameters of a = b = 29.1 Å, c = 6.9 Å, α = β = 90°, γ = 120°, with agreement factors Rwp = 2.92% and Rp = 1.73%. FT‑IR spectra displayed a distinct absorbance at 1627 cm⁻¹, attributed to the 12C–12C stretch of the olefin linkage, and attenuation of the aldehyde C=O stretch at 1689 cm⁻¹, confirming complete conversion of starting materials.
- **Observations:** The retention of sharp PXRD peaks and characteristic FT‑IR bands after exposure to strong acids, bases, and organolithium reagents demonstrates high crystallinity and phase purity, as well as exceptional chemical robustness.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Linkage Confirmation
- **Description:** Solid-state 13C CP‑MAS NMR and isotopic labeling were employed to confirm the formation of olefin linkages.
- **Findings:** In 13C-labeled COF‑701, the aldehyde carbon resonance at ~192 ppm (from BPDA) was largely attenuated, while a new peak at 144 ppm appeared, corresponding to the olefinic carbons. The FT‑IR C=C stretch shifted from 1627 cm⁻¹ (12C) to 1599 cm⁻¹ (13C), further validating the linkage assignment.
- **Observations:** These results confirm the successful and selective formation of unsubstituted olefin linkages throughout the framework.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** N₂ sorption measurements at 77 K were used to determine surface area and pore size distribution.
- **Findings:** The Langmuir surface area was 1715 m² g⁻¹, and the BET surface area was 1366 m² g⁻¹. Pore size distribution, modeled by QSDFT, was centered at 1.14 nm, closely matching the 1.1 nm channel width predicted by structural analysis.
- **Observations:** High surface area and uniform pore size confirm the successful formation of a highly porous, crystalline framework.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Elemental Distribution
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping were used to examine particle morphology and elemental distribution.
- **Findings:** SEM imaging revealed the particle morphology of COF‑701, while EDS mapping confirmed the presence of carbon and, in BF₃-loaded samples, fluorine localized within the framework. After washing and re-activation, fluorine was effectively removed, as shown by EDS.
- **Observations:** The ability to immobilize and subsequently remove catalytic species demonstrates the framework’s functional versatility.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal and Chemical Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and post-treatment PXRD/FT‑IR were used to assess stability.
- **Findings:** TGA showed no significant weight loss up to 400 °C under N₂, indicating high thermal stability. PXRD and FT‑IR spectra remained unchanged after exposure to strong acids, bases, and organolithium reagents.
- **Observations:** COF‑701 exhibits outstanding thermal and chemical stability, retaining crystallinity and composition under harsh conditions.



### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Catalytic Activity in Diels–Alder Reaction
- **Description:** Catalytic activity was evaluated by measuring the yield (%) of the Diels–Alder product after a fixed reaction time. The reaction was conducted at room temperature for 8 hours in CD₂Cl₂, comparing free BF₃·OEt₂, BF₃ immobilized in COF‑701 (BF₃⊂COF‑701), and a non-catalyzed control.
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - Free BF₃·OEt₂: 99% yield  
  - BF₃⊂COF‑701: 88% yield  
  - Non-catalyzed: 19% yield  
  Yields were quantified by ^1H NMR using toluene as an internal standard.
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  BF₃⊂COF‑701 retained 89% of the catalytic activity of free BF₃·OEt₂, demonstrating that immobilization within the COF framework preserves most of the Lewis acid function. The significant increase in yield compared to the non-catalyzed reaction confirms the effectiveness of the heterogenized catalyst. The use of a non-coordinating, aprotic solvent (CD₂Cl₂) ensured accurate assessment of Lewis acid activity.

#### 6.2.2 Stability and Recyclability
- **Description:** Stability was assessed by monitoring the retention of crystallinity, chemical composition, and catalytic performance after exposure to harsh chemical conditions and after catalytic cycles. Techniques included WAXS, FT-IR, and TGA.
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - COF‑701 retained crystallinity and chemical composition after exposure to 12.1 mol L⁻¹ HCl, saturated aqueous and methanolic KOH, and organolithium reagents (n-BuLi, MeLi, with/without TMEDA).
  - TGA showed no significant weight loss up to 400 °C.
  - After catalytic use and re-activation, WAXS and FT-IR spectra of COF‑701 were unchanged, and the framework could be regenerated for reuse.
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The robust olefin (–CH=CH–) linkages confer exceptional chemical and thermal stability, enabling the COF to withstand strong acids, bases, and organolithium reagents without loss of structure or function. The catalytic activity of BF₃⊂COF‑701 remained high after recovery, indicating stable performance over cycles.

#### 6.2.3 Structure–Performance Correlation
- **Description:** The relationship between the COF’s structural features and its catalytic performance was examined.
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - COF‑701 features 1.1 nm wide rhombic channels and a high surface area (BET: 1366 m²/g).
  - SEM EDS confirmed uniform distribution of immobilized BF₃.
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The high porosity and well-defined channels facilitate efficient immobilization and accessibility of catalytic sites, supporting the observed high catalytic activity and stability. The framework’s crystallinity and pore structure are maintained throughout catalytic cycles and harsh treatments, underscoring the material’s suitability as a durable catalytic platform.
