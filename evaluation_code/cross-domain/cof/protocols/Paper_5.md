## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The article reports the synthesis of COFs via a solvothermal route, employing controlled mixing, polymerization/condensation, crystallization, washing, and drying steps. The synthesis methods for TAH‐COF, BAH‐COF, and TPPA‐COF are detailed below.

#### • TAH‑COF Solvothermal Synthesis
1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
   - Reagents: 210 mg (1 mmol) 1,3,5‑Triformylphloroglucinol (Tp) and 270 mg (3 mmol) tartaric acid dihydrazide (TAH).  
   - Solvent System: 4.0 mL Dimethylacetamide, 1.0 mL 1,2-Dichlorobenzene, and 1.0 mL 6 M aqueous acetic acid.  
   - Equipment: Pyrex tube (10 × 8 mm²) ensures a closed and controlled environment.

2. Polymerization/Condensation  
   - Conditions: Heating at 120 °C for 120 hours; after sonication and ensuring a homogeneous mixture, the sealed tube is placed under static heating.
   - Observation: Precipitate formation signifies COF framework formation via irreversible β‑ketoenamine linkages.

3. Crystallization, Washing, and Drying  
   - Post-reaction: Cooling to room temperature, followed by filtration.  
   - Washing: Successive washes with DMF, acetone, and THF remove residual reactants.
   - Drying: Final product is dried under vacuum at 80 °C overnight.

#### • BAH‑COF Solvothermal Synthesis
1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
   - Reagents: 21 mg (0.1 mmol) Tp with 22 mg (0.15 mmol) butanedioic acid dihydrazide (BAH).  
   - Solvent System: 0.5 mL Dimethylacetamide, 0.5 mL 1,2-Dichlorobenzene, and 0.2 mL 6 M aqueous acetic acid.
   
2. Subsequent Steps  
   - The polymerization/condensation is conducted under similar conditions to TAH‑COF, followed by crystallization, washing, and drying to yield a highly crystalline framework.

#### • TPPA‑COF Solvothermal Synthesis
1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
   - Reagents: 1,3,5‑Triformylphloroglucinol and p‑Phenylenediamine (Pa) in a homogenous mixture.
   - Solvent System: A blend of mesitylene, dioxane, and 3 M aqueous acetic acid.

2. Polymerization/Condensation  
   - Conditions: Heating at 120 °C for 72 hours initiates rapid imine formation with observable color changes.

3. Crystallization, Washing, and Drying  
   - Washing: Filtration followed by washing with ethanol and THF.
   - Drying: The product is dried at 60 °C for 12 hours, ensuring reproducibility.

These detailed steps, including precise reagent amounts, reaction conditions, equipment specifications, and post-treatment protocols, ensure clarity and reproducibility, as required for constructing efficient photocatalytic COFs with tailored water affinity and crystallinity.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: TAH-COF
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: 1,3,5‑Triformylphloroglucinol (Tp, 21 mg, 0.1 mmol) and tartaric acid dihydrazide (TAH, 27 mg, 0.15 mmol).
- **Conditions**: Use 0.8 mL Dimethylacetamide, 0.2 mL 1,2-Dichlorobenzene, and 0.2 mL 6 M aqueous acetic acid.
- **Process**:
  - Charge a Pyrex tube with Tp and TAH in the solvent mixture.
  - Sonicate for 10 minutes to form a homogeneous suspension.
  - Flash freeze the mixture at liquid nitrogen temperature (77 K) and perform three freeze-pump-thaw cycles for degassing.
  - Seal the tube to ensure an inert atmosphere.
- **Key Steps**: Accurate reagent weighing and strict adherence to the degassing procedure are critical for reproducibility.

##### Step 2: COF Assembly (Polymerization/Condensation)
- **Conditions**: Heat the sealed tube at 120 °C for 120 hours under static conditions.
- **Process**:
  - Initiate the condensation reaction in which 6 M aqueous acetic acid modulates the pH, facilitating the formation of the irreversible β‑ketoenamine linkage.
- **Key Steps**: Proper temperature control and time monitoring are essential.

##### Step 3: Purification
- **Process**:
  - Cool the reaction to room temperature.
  - Filter to collect the red precipitate, then wash successively with THF, DMF, H₂O, and MeOH.
  - Further purify via Soxhlet extraction using THF and MeOH for 24 hours.
  
##### Step 4: Drying & Activation
- **Conditions**: Dry under vacuum at 80 °C overnight.
- **Process**:
  - Remove residual solvents and water to yield a red powder of TAH-COF.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The COF activation procedure is a critical post‐synthesis treatment designed to remove residual solvents and guest molecules, thereby enhancing porosity and framework stability. The process strictly follows the experimental steps detailed in the article and is applied exclusively to pre‐application treatments.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature is essential for promoting the evaporation of residual solvents and adsorbed water, thus ensuring a guest‐free framework.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The COF materials were subjected to vacuum drying at 80 °C overnight.
- **Observations:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) revealed a mass loss above 100 °C, attributed to the removal of adsorbed water, thereby justifying the use of 80 °C for effective thermal activation without risking framework collapse.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** Activation under reduced pressure facilitates gentle removal of solvents while preserving the structural integrity of the COF.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Although a precise numerical vacuum level was not specified, the activation was conducted using a vacuum oven.
- **Observations:** The absence of high pressure during drying enhanced the removal efficiency without compromising crystallinity.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Solvent
- **Definition:** Solvent exchange ensures that high-boiling-point solvents are removed and that the COF pores remain accessible.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Prior to thermal activation, the COF precipitate underwent extensive washing with THF, DMF, water, and methanol. This was followed by a Soxhlet extraction with THF and methanol for 24 hours to thoroughly eliminate residual guests and oligomers.
- **Observations:** This solvent treatment aided in obtaining highly porous structures, as evidenced by the high BET surface areas and maintained crystallinity observed in subsequent characterizations.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time
- **Definition:** The duration of activation is critical to ensure complete removal of guest molecules while avoiding damage to the framework.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The Soxhlet extraction was carried out for 24 hours, followed by an overnight vacuum drying.
- **Observations:** These combined time periods provided sufficient solvent removal and resulted in COF materials with optimal porosity and structural stability, as confirmed by matching PXRD patterns and gas adsorption measurements.

By integrating these activation parameters within the pre‐application treatment, the COF structures, particularly those based on hydrophilic aliphatic linkers, retain high crystallinity and pore integrity, ensuring their superior performance in photocatalytic applications.

### 5.2 Application/Testing

#### 5.2.1 Photocatalytic H₂O₂ Evolution  
The photocatalytic performance for H₂O₂ production was evaluated using TAH‑COF in a Pyrex top-irradiation reaction vessel. In these tests, 5 mg of COF powder was dispersed in 50 mL of deionized water and illuminated with a 300 W Xe-lamp (λ > 420 nm) at 25 °C. The measurements were carried out under AM 1.5G simulated solar irradiation, yielding an H₂O₂ evolution rate of 6003 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ with a solar-to-chemical conversion (SCC) efficiency of 0.66%. These controlled conditions ensure reproducibility and a clear assessment of COF performance without sacrificial agents.

#### 5.2.2 Photocatalytic H₂ Evolution  
In addition to H₂O₂ production, photocatalytic H₂ evolution was conducted by incorporating a 3 wt% Pt cocatalyst, using an aqueous solution of 0.1 M ascorbic acid as the electron donor. Under AM 1.5G irradiation in a glass gas-closed-circulation system and at a maintained temperature of 25 °C, TAH‑COF demonstrated a H₂ evolution rate of 31.4 mmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹. The setup also involved stirring (800 rpm) and ultrasonication to ensure uniform dispersion and reproducibility.

#### 5.2.3 Gas Adsorption Measurements  
Gas adsorption experiments were performed to assess surface properties and nanopore accessibility. N₂ adsorption isotherms were recorded at 77 K, while CO₂ adsorption measurements were conducted at 273 K. These tests confirmed the presence of persistent, uniform micropores (~0.6 nm) and provided insights into the framework’s capacity for selective molecular adsorption.

#### 5.2.4 Water Affinity Evaluation  
Water affinity was quantified via contact angle measurements and water vapor uptake tests. TAH‑COF exhibited a notably low water contact angle of 38.9°, which correlates with superior water uptake at low partial pressures (P/P⁰ < 0.2). This high hydrophilicity, enhanced by the presence of aliphatic linkers and hydrogen bonding networks, facilitates efficient photocatalytic and adsorption performance. Overall, the defined parameters, including temperature, controlled illumination, and precise catalyst loadings, support the reproducibility and accurate assessment of COF applications in water-based photocatalysis and gas adsorption.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
The crystal structure and phase purity of the synthesized COFs were evaluated by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD). TAH-COF and BAH-COF exhibited sharp and well-defined PXRD peaks, with prominent reflections at 2θ ≈ 7.1°, 12.1°, and 27°, corresponding to the 110, 300, and 10-2 planes, respectively. Pawley refinement confirmed that the experimental patterns matched the simulated ABC stacking model in the trigonal R3̅ space group (lattice parameters: a = b = 26.06 Å, c = 6.57 Å, α = β = 90°, γ = 120°), indicating high crystallinity and phase purity. The presence of persistent ~0.6 nm nanopores was corroborated by both PXRD and gas adsorption data. The retention of PXRD patterns after chemical treatments and photocatalysis further demonstrated the structural stability of these frameworks.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Linkage Formation
Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was used to confirm the formation of characteristic linkages. For TAH-COF and BAH-COF, FTIR spectra displayed bands at 1628 cm⁻¹ (C=O), 1594 cm⁻¹ (C=C), and 1281 cm⁻¹ (C–N), indicative of the irreversible β‑ketoenamine linkage. In contrast, TPPA-COF showed absorption bands characteristic of imine linkages, with shifts in C=O, C=C, and C–N stretching frequencies due to the absence of strong hydrogen bonding networks.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms at 77 K were measured to determine surface area and porosity. TAH-COF exhibited a BET surface area of 703 m²/g, while BAH-COF showed 530 m²/g. Pore size distributions, derived from N₂ and CO₂ adsorption, were centered at 5.8–5.9 Å (~0.6 nm), confirming ultramicroporosity and uniform nanopore formation. These features are consistent with the structural models and support efficient mass transport within the frameworks.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
Scanning and transmission electron microscopy (SEM/TEM) analyses revealed distinct morphologies. TAH-COF formed uniform belt-like crystallites with average dimensions of approximately 600 nm, reflecting controlled nucleation and growth. BAH-COF displayed aggregated spherical particles ranging from 200 to 400 nm in diameter. These morphological differences are attributed to the nature of the aliphatic linkers and their influence on framework assembly.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) under nitrogen atmosphere demonstrated that TAH-COF and BAH-COF are thermally stable, with mass loss observed above 100 °C due to desorption of adsorbed water. No significant decomposition occurred below this temperature, confirming the robustness of the frameworks for practical applications.

#### 6.1.6 Water Affinity and Hydrophilicity
Water contact angle measurements and water sorption isotherms were employed to assess hydrophilicity. TAH-COF exhibited a low water contact angle of 38.9°, and BAH-COF 47.5°, indicating high surface hydrophilicity. Water uptake at low partial pressures was also significantly higher for these COFs compared to TPPA-COF (contact angle 54.2°), highlighting the role of aliphatic linkers and hydrogen bonding in enhancing water affinity.

### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Photocatalytic H₂O₂ Evolution
- **Description:** Photocatalytic H₂O₂ evolution quantifies the rate at which hydrogen peroxide is produced under light irradiation, typically expressed as micromoles per hour per gram of catalyst (μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹). The solar-to-chemical conversion (SCC) efficiency and apparent quantum yield (AQY) are also used to assess performance.
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - TAH-COF achieved a record H₂O₂ evolution rate of 6003 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ in pure water without sacrificial agents.  
  - SCC efficiency: 0.66% under AM 1.5G simulated solar irradiation (5 mg catalyst, 50 mL water, λ > 420 nm).  
  - AQY at 500 nm: 7.72% for TAH-COF.
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  TAH-COF outperformed all previously reported COF-based photocatalysts, attributed to its hydrophilic aliphatic linker, high crystallinity, and efficient stabilization of the *OOH intermediate. BAH-COF also exhibited high H₂O₂ evolution, though specific rates were lower than TAH-COF. TPPA-COF, with aromatic linkers, showed significantly reduced activity.

#### 6.2.2 Photocatalytic H₂ Evolution
- **Description:** The rate of hydrogen gas generation under photocatalytic conditions, measured in μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ or mmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹, often with a cocatalyst and sacrificial donor.
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - TAH-COF (with 3 wt% Pt cocatalyst, 0.1 M ascorbic acid, AM 1.5G): 31,400 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ (31.4 mmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹).  
  - TPPA-COF (same conditions): 2,100 μmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹ (2.1 mmol h⁻¹ g⁻¹).
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  TAH-COF’s H₂ evolution rate is over 15 times higher than TPPA-COF, highlighting the impact of hydrophilic aliphatic linkers on catalytic efficiency. BAH-COF’s performance is intermediate but not explicitly quantified.

#### 6.2.3 Water Affinity and Uptake
- **Description:** Water affinity is assessed by contact angle measurements (lower angles indicate higher hydrophilicity), while water uptake is measured via vapor adsorption isotherms (cm³ g⁻¹ at 298 K).
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - TAH-COF: Contact angle 38.9°, water uptake 77.8 cm³ g⁻¹ (P/P₀ < 0.2).  
  - BAH-COF: Contact angle 47.5°, water uptake 86.2 cm³ g⁻¹.  
  - TPPA-COF: Contact angle 54.2°, water uptake 21.2 cm³ g⁻¹.
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  TAH-COF and BAH-COF demonstrate markedly enhanced hydrophilicity and water uptake compared to TPPA-COF, correlating with their superior photocatalytic performance.

#### 6.2.4 Stability and Recyclability
- **Description:** Stability is evaluated by monitoring catalytic activity and structural integrity over repeated cycles and after chemical treatments.
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - TAH-COF retained catalytic activity and crystallinity after 5 h of irradiation and after exposure to water, 1 M NaOH, and 1 M HCl.
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The robust framework and persistent porosity of TAH-COF ensure durable performance, with no significant loss in activity or structure after multiple uses.

#### 6.2.5 Mechanistic Insights: O₂ Adsorption and *OOH Stabilization
- **Description:** The efficiency of the two-electron oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) pathway is linked to O₂ adsorption energy and stabilization of the *OOH intermediate.
- **Experimental Data:**  
  - O₂ adsorption energy (DFT): TAH-COF –0.67 eV; BAH-COF –0.27 eV; TPPA-COF –0.30 eV.  
  - ΔG for *OOH formation: TAH-COF –1.62 eV; BAH-COF –1.12 eV; TPPA-COF –0.46 eV.
- **Observations and Trends:**  
  TAH-COF’s strong O₂ binding and effective *OOH stabilization, confirmed by in situ DRIFT and EPR, underpin its high selectivity and efficiency in H₂O₂ production.
