## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The article reports a solvothermal synthesis method for assembling COFs via a polycondensation reaction between ketone-based monomers and hydrazine. Two representative methods are detailed: one for TKB‐Ketazine‐COF and another for TKBPB‐Ketazine‐COF. Both follow a similar overall workflow while differing in catalyst selection and solvent composition.

#### TKB‐Ketazine‐COF

1. • Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
  – TKB (20 mg, 98 µmol) is combined with hydrazine (7.1 μL, 45 µmol) and acetophenone (34.3 μL, optimized at a 3:1 molar ratio to TKB) in a solvent mixture of anisole/n‐BuOH in a volumetric ratio of 3/1 (total 2.12 mL).  
  – Pure trifluoroacetic acid (TFA, 60 μL) is introduced as a catalyst.

2. • Polymerization/Condensation  
  – Following mixing, the solution is transferred into a 10 mL Pyrex tube where it is degassed via three freeze–pump–thaw cycles to remove air and moisture.  
  – The tube is then sealed to maintain an inert reaction environment.

3. • Crystallization / Reaction  
  – The sealed tube is heated at 120 °C for 5 days to allow polymerization and framework formation.

4. • Washing / Purification  
  – The precipitate is collected by suction filtration, then washed five times with THF to remove unreacted species and by-products.  
  – Soxhlet extraction with THF overnight further purifies the material.

5. • Drying  
  – The sample is dried under vacuum at 120 °C overnight, ensuring complete solvent removal and optimal crystallinity.

#### TKBPB‐Ketazine‐COF

A similar procedure is applied:
1. • Monomer solutions are prepared using TKBPB with hydrazine and a solvent system that may include an anisole/n‐BuOH/pure AcOH mixture (example ratio: 80/20/6 vol.).  
2. • The degassing, sealing, and heating steps (120 °C for 5 days) remain consistent, promoting proper condensation and framework crystallization.  
3. • Post-reaction washing with THF, centrifugation, and vacuum drying secure purified and well-ordered COF structures.

### Comparative Analysis

• The TKB‐system employs TFA as a catalyst, offering high crystallinity with a noted improvement in PXRD intensity, while the TKBPB‐variant uses either TFA or AcOH, influencing yield and microstructural features.  
• Both methods emphasize strict adherence to the order of operations to ensure reproducibility, with detailed reporting of reagent concentrations, reaction conditions (temperature, duration), and post-processing steps that are critical for scale-up and application in sensing and optoelectronics.

### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of the COFs were determined by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), with structural models refined using Pawley and Rietveld methods. PXRD is essential for confirming framework formation and long-range order in COFs.
- **Findings:**  
  - *TKB‐Ketazine‐COF* exhibited a strong PXRD peak at 6.77° (assigned to the (100) facet), with additional peaks at 11.74° (110), 13.63° (200), and 25.15° (001, corresponding to an interlayer interval of 3.539 Å). Pawley refinement yielded RWP = 5.01% and RP = 3.33%, confirming high crystallinity and phase purity. The structure fits a hexagonal AA-stacking model (space group P1; a = 14.9828 Å, b = 15.0122 Å, c = 3.7660 Å; α = 90.52°, β = 90.51°, γ = 120.08°).
  - *TKBPB‐Ketazine‐COF* displayed a strong peak at 2.36° (100), with additional peaks at 3.99° (110), 4.65° (200), and 18.25° (001), all absent in the monomer, confirming the formation of a new, highly ordered framework.
- **Observations:** The use of modulators (e.g., acetophenone, acid catalysts) significantly enhanced PXRD intensity and crystallinity.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Bonding
- **Description:** FTIR and solid-state ^13C CP/MAS NMR spectroscopy were used to confirm the formation of ketazine linkages and the presence of characteristic functional groups.
- **Findings:**  
  - FTIR spectra for both COFs showed a band at 1630 cm⁻¹ (C=N, ketazine linkage) and 1361 cm⁻¹ (C–H, methyl group).
  - Solid-state ^13C NMR for TKB‐Ketazine‐COF revealed signals at 122.9 and 122.4 ppm (C=N) and 9.31 ppm (methyl), confirming ketazine bond formation.
- **Observations:** These spectroscopic signatures verify the successful condensation and extended π-conjugation in the frameworks.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption experiments (BET and NLDFT analyses) assessed surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution, which are critical for COF performance.
- **Findings:**

- **Observations:** The larger pore size and volume in TKBPB‐Ketazine‐COF confirm its mesoporosity.
