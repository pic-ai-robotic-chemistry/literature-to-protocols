## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The COF synthesis methods are based on precise control of reagent quantities, reaction conditions, and subsequent post-synthetic modifications to ensure reproducibility and scalability.

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis of DhaTab-COF
1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
   - Reagents: 2,5-dihydroxyterephthalaldehyde (21.6 mg, 0.13 mmol) and 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl)benzene (30.2 mg, 0.086 mmol) are combined in a solvent system of n‑butyl alcohol (1 mL) and ortho‑dichlorobenzene (1 mL).  
   - Catalyst: 0.2 mL of 6 M aqueous acetic acid is added to modulate the reaction.
   
2. Polymerization/Condensation  
   - The mixture is subjected to three freeze-pump-thaw cycles for degassing, and then the reaction solution is flame-sealed under vacuum in a sealed tube.  
   - Reaction Conditions: The sealed tube is heated at 120 °C for 72 hours. A distinct orange-colored precipitate forms during polymerization, indicating effective imine bond formation.

3. Crystallization / Washing  
   - Post-reaction, the as-prepared COF is collected by centrifugation.  
   - Washing is performed repeatedly with DMF, THF, and methanol to remove unreacted monomers and residual salts.

4. Drying / Post-treatment  
   - The washed powder is dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C overnight.  
   - For further modification, DhaTab-COF is reduced by adding NaBH₃CN (66.0 mg, 1.05 mmol) in small portions over 10 minutes to a suspension in 25 mL absolute THF, stirred for 24 hours at room temperature to yield RDT-COF.
   
5. Additional Post-treatment for SDT-COF  
   - RDT-COF is functionalized by grafting propanesulfonic acid groups using 3‑bromopropanesulfonic acid sodium salt (2.078 g, 6.857 mmol), followed by acidification and ion-exchange to obtain SDT-COF.

### Comparative Analysis
- Advantages of the solvothermal method include the formation of crystalline, porous structures with high surface area.  
- Disadvantages include the requirement for precise temperature control and extended reaction times.  
- Equipment: Sealed reaction tubes or Teflon-lined autoclaves are used, ensuring consistent reaction conditions critical for reproducibility.

This structured approach, featuring meticulous reagent measurements, controlled degassing and heating steps, and strict post-synthetic treatments, enables researchers to reliably replicate the COF synthesis process while comparing variations in synthesis complexity, cost, and scalability.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: DhaTab-COF  
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation and COF Assembly  
- **Reagents**:  
  • 2,5-dihydroxyterephthalaldehyde (21.6 mg, 0.13 mmol)  
  • 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl)benzene (30.2 mg, 0.086 mmol)  
  • n‑butyl alcohol (1 mL)  
  • ortho‑dichlorobenzene (1 mL)  
  • Aqueous 6 M acetic acid (0.2 mL)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Reaction vessel: Sealed 100 mL round-bottom flask with magnetic stirring and condenser  
  • Degassing via three freeze-pump-thaw cycles followed by flame-sealing under vacuum  
  • Heating at 120 °C in a Teflon-lined autoclave for 72 hours  
- **Process**:  
  1. Dissolve the primary aldehyde and amine monomers in the mixed solvents.  
  2. Add aqueous 6 M acetic acid to catalyze imine condensation, ensuring uniform stirring for homogeneous mixing.  
  3. Transfer the mixture to a properly sealed reaction vessel and heat at 120 °C for 72 hours, enabling formation of DhaTab-COF with defined pore channels.  
- **Key Steps**: Emphasize accurate measurement of reagents, controlled temperature, and proper degassing to ensure reproducibility.

##### Step 2: Reduction to RDT-COF  
- **Reagents**:  
  • Glacial acetic acid (35 μL, 0.61 mmol)  
  • NaBH₃CN (66.0 mg, 1.05 mmol)  
  • Absolute THF (25 mL)  
  • DhaTab-COF (10.0 mg, based on imine content)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Room temperature stirring initially for 10 minutes, then continued for 24 hours  
- **Process**:  
  1. Disperse DhaTab-COF in THF and add glacial acetic acid.  
  2. Introduce NaBH₃CN slowly over 10 minutes under continuous stirring, followed by a 24-hour reduction period, converting imine linkages to secondary amines.

##### Step 3: Post-Synthetic Grafting for SDT-COF  
- **Reagents**:  
  • 3‑bromopropanesulphonic acid sodium salt (2.078 g, 6.857 mmol)  
  • KI (75.8 mg, 0.456 mmol)  
  • Na₂CO₃ (1.263 mg, 9.138 mmol)  
  • DMSO (40 mL)  
  • RDT-COF (100 mg)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Ultrasonic dispersion for 30 minutes  
  • Heating at 120 °C for 48 hours  
  • Subsequent acid exchange: Soak in 1 M HClO₄ for 12 hours  
- **Process**:  
  1. Mix RDT-COF with grafting reagents and disperse ultrasonically.  
  2. Heat the reaction mixture to facilitate grafting of –SO₃H groups onto the COF.  
  3. After reaction, wash sequentially with methanol and water, ensuring removal of impurities, then perform acidification to obtain SDT-COF.

##### Purification, Drying & Activation  
- **Process**:  
  • Collect COF products by centrifugation and wash with solvents (DMF, THF, MeOH) to eliminate unreacted species.  
  • Dry the final products in a vacuum oven at 60 °C overnight, ensuring complete solvent removal and optimal porosity.  

**Key Control Points**: Maintain precise temperature, pH, and reaction times. Uniform stirring and thorough washing are essential for reproducibility.

### 5.1 Activation

- **Definition:** The activation temperature is critical for the gentle removal of residual solvents without compromising the COF’s pore structure.
- **Experimental Conditions:** COF samples, such as RDT‑COF, were vacuum dried at 60 °C overnight.
- **Observations:** This mild thermal treatment efficiently removes retained solvents while preventing any thermal degradation, as the framework is stable up to temperatures near 200 °C.

- **Observations:** The vacuum-assisted drying ensured effective solvent removal, thereby contributing to the integrity of the porous framework.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
The crystal structure and phase purity of the synthesized COFs were evaluated using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD). PXRD patterns were recorded from 1.5° to 80° (2θ) with a step size of 0.02° and a scan rate of 10° min⁻¹, using Cu-Kα radiation (λ = 0.154056 nm) at 40 kV and 50 mA. Samples were mounted on silicon zero background holders and evacuated at 120 °C overnight to remove guest molecules. The experimental PXRD patterns exhibited sharp, intense peaks that closely matched simulated AA stacking models, confirming high crystallinity and the presence of eclipsed AA stacking. Notably, the (100) plane was identified at a characteristic 2θ angle, indicating long-range order and phase purity across DhaTab-COF, DbdTab-COF, DbdBtt-COF, and LZU-1.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Functional Group Analysis
Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) and solid-state ¹³C NMR were employed to confirm the formation and subsequent reduction of imine bonds. FTIR spectra displayed characteristic C=N stretching vibrations in DhaTab-COF, which diminished upon reduction to RDT-COF, accompanied by the emergence of bands corresponding to secondary amine linkages. Solid-state ¹³C NMR corroborated this transformation, with the disappearance of imine carbon resonances and the appearance of secondary amine signals in RDT-COF and SDT-COF. Successful grafting of propanesulfonic acid groups was further confirmed by the appearance of S 2p peaks in X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), with chemical state analysis indicating grafting on tertiary amines.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition
Elemental analysis (CHN) and XPS were used to verify the elemental composition and stoichiometry. For SDT-COF, CHN analysis yielded C 52.25%, H 3.77%, N 4.28%, S 5.93%, consistent with the expected structure after sulfonation. RDT-COF exhibited C 68.71%, H 6.28%, N 9.84%. XPS confirmed the presence and correct ratios of C, N, O, and S, with calibration referenced to the C 1s peak at 284.8 eV, ensuring accuracy.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Area and Porosity
Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms at 77 K were used to assess porosity via the BET method. DhaTab-COF exhibited a high BET surface area of 2255 m² g⁻¹ and a pore size of 3.4 nm, indicative of uniform mesoporous channels. Other COFs showed surface areas ranging from 1081 to 1308 m² g⁻¹ with pore sizes between 2.8 and 4.1 nm. These mesoporous structures facilitate rapid oxygen diffusion, as confirmed by gas sorption profiles.

#### 6.1.5 Morphology and Nanosheet Structure
Morphological analysis by SEM, TEM, and AFM revealed that SDT-COF and related COFs adopt a nanosheet morphology. AFM measurements showed an average nanosheet thickness of ~0.8 nm, while SEM and TEM images confirmed well-dispersed, thin sheets with uniform lateral dimensions. EDS mapping demonstrated homogeneous distribution of key elements, and the Tyndall effect indicated good dispersion in aqueous media.

#### 6.1.6 Thermal Stability
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed from 40 to 800 °C at a heating rate of 10 °C min⁻¹ under air. SDT-COF exhibited a decomposition temperature of 200 °C, indicating moderate thermal stability suitable for fuel cell applications.

### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Oxygen Permeability

- **Description:** Oxygen permeability quantifies the rate at which O₂ molecules traverse the membrane, a critical parameter for evaluating ionomer performance in proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs). It is typically measured in Barrer under controlled humidity and temperature conditions.
- **Experimental Data:** The SDT-COF/Nafion composite membrane (2:8 ratio) exhibited an O₂ permeability of 36 Barrer at 100% relative humidity (RH), whereas pure Nafion under identical conditions showed 0 Barrer. Under dry conditions (0% RH), SDT-COF/Nafion reached 115 Barrer, compared to 21 Barrer for Nafion.
- **Observations and Trends:** Incorporation of SDT-COF into Nafion dramatically enhanced oxygen transport, especially under humid conditions where Nafion alone becomes impermeable. This improvement is attributed to the intrinsic mesoporous channels of the COF, which facilitate rapid O₂ diffusion even in hydrated environments.

#### 6.2.2 Mass Transfer Resistance

- **Description:** Mass transfer resistance reflects the hindrance to O₂ transport through the catalyst layer and membrane, directly impacting fuel cell efficiency. It is measured as pressure-independent O₂ diffusion resistance (R_NP, in s m⁻¹) and total O₂ mass transfer resistance (Ω).
- **Experimental Data:** R_NP decreased from 45.8 s m⁻¹ (Pt/C@Nafion) to 27.5 s m⁻¹ (Pt/C@SDT-Nafion). Total O₂ mass transfer resistance in H₂–O₂ cells dropped from 0.027 Ω (Nafion) to 0.0068 Ω (SDT-COF/Nafion), and in H₂–air cells from 0.055 Ω to 0.032 Ω.
- **Observations and Trends:** The SDT-COF/Nafion composite significantly reduced O₂ mass transfer resistance, enabling more efficient oxygen delivery to catalytic sites and supporting higher current densities.

#### 6.2.3 Fuel Cell Performance Metrics

- **Description:** Key performance indicators include peak power density (W cm⁻²) and mass activity (MA, A mgPt⁻¹), measured under standardized PEMFC operating conditions.
- **Experimental Data:** In H₂–O₂ fuel cell tests (80°C, 100% RH, 150 kPa abs), the SDT-COF/Nafion composite achieved a peak power density of 2.21 W cm⁻² (1.4× higher than control) and an MA of 0.35 A mgPt⁻¹ (vs. 0.21 A mgPt⁻¹ for Nafion). Under H₂–air, peak power density reached 1.08 W cm⁻² (1.6× higher), and MA for commercial catalysts (PtCo/C, Pt/KB) exceeded the DOE 2025 target (0.50 and 0.65 A mgPt⁻¹, respectively).
- **Observations and Trends:** The COF-enhanced ionomer layers enabled superior catalyst utilization and power output, particularly at low Pt loadings, demonstrating their potential for cost-effective, high-performance fuel cells.

#### 6.2.4 Durability and Stability

- **Description:** Durability assesses the retention of electrochemical performance after prolonged operation or accelerated stress testing (AST).
- **Experimental Data:** After 30,000 AST cycles, the SDT-COF/Nafion MEA showed a 38% decrease in MA, meeting DOE durability requirements. In continuous operation at 0.7 V for 24 h, current decay was 41% for SDT-COF/Nafion, compared to 87% for Nafion.
- **Observations and Trends:** The SDT-COF/Nafion composite demonstrated robust operational stability and resistance to flooding, attributed to its mesoporous structure and chemical resilience.

#### 6.2.5 Structure–Performance Correlation

- **Description:** The relationship between COF pore size/morphology and functional performance.
- **Experimental Data:** Only COFs with mesoporous apertures (2.8–4.1 nm) significantly enhanced oxygen permeability and fuel cell output. Microporous or nonporous controls showed negligible improvement.
- **Observations and Trends:** The presence of interconnected mesopores and nanosheet morphology (thickness ~0.8 nm) was critical for maximizing oxygen transport and catalyst accessibility, underpinning the superior performance of SDT-COF-based systems.
