## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

This section outlines the solvothermal synthesis route used for preparing COFs such as PCOF‑34 and PCOF‑90. The procedure is reported to ensure reproducibility while balancing synthesis complexity, scalability, and cost.

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis Method

Step 1: Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
• Reagents: Por-8‑CHO (28.92 mg, 0.02 mmol) combined with Por-8‑NH₂ (26.85 mg, 0.02 mmol) are used for PCOF‑34, while PCOF‑90 incorporates additional pyrenyl nodes (e.g., Py‑8‑NH₂) to adjust porphyrin content.  
• Solvent System: Anisole (1.33 mL) and benzyl alcohol (0.67 mL) are employed.  
• Process: The monomers are sonicated for 5 minutes to form a cloudy solution followed by the addition of 0.2 mL of 6 M acetic acid as a catalyst.

Step 2: Polymerization/Condensation  
• The solution is flash frozen at 77 K in a liquid nitrogen bath.  
• Degassing is performed using three freeze–pump–thaw cycles to remove air and moisture, which is critical for forming imine linkages.

Step 3: Crystallization / Reaction  
• The degassed mixture is sealed in a glass ampoule and heated at 120 °C for 5 days. This step facilitates polymerization and crystallization, yielding a reddish-brown solid indicative of imine bond formation.

Step 4: Washing / Purification  
• The solid is isolated by centrifugation and washed sequentially with N,N‑dimethylacetamide and acetone.  
• A further Soxhlet extraction with tetrahydrofuran and acetone is performed for 48 hours to remove residual impurities.

Step 5: Drying  
• The purified material is transferred to a vacuum chamber and dried at 80 °C under 20 mTorr for 24 hours, ensuring the complete removal of solvents and proper activation.

### Comparative Analysis  
• Advantages: The solvothermal method provides strong covalent bonding and high crystallinity, essential for achieving a 2‑fold interpenetrated bcu topology.  
• Disadvantages: It requires specialized equipment (sealed glass ampoules), precise temperature control, and multi-step degassing, which may affect scalability and cost.

This detailed procedure, including exact reagent quantities, reaction conditions, and post-processing steps, allows another researcher to replicate the synthesis reliably while also highlighting the influence of monomer ratios and solvent composition on the final COF structure.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: PCOF-34

##### Step [1]: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  • Por-8-CHO (28.92 mg, 0.02 mmol)  
  • Por-8-NH₂ (26.85 mg, 0.02 mmol), synthesized via a Suzuki coupling using Por-8-Br (1.00 g, 0.81 mmol) and 4-aldehydophenylboronic acid pinacol ester (2.07 g, 8.91 mmol) with K₂CO₃ (2.80 g, 20.25 mmol) and Pd(PPh₃)₄ (0.23 g, 0.20 mmol) in toluene (80 mL), ethanol (40 mL), and water (40 mL).  
- **Conditions**: Reaction at 80 °C for 48 hours under nitrogen.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Combine reagents in the solvent mixture; heat and stir for 48 hours.  
  2. Cool, extract with ethyl acetate, wash with brine, dry with MgSO₄, and concentrate under reduced pressure.  
  3. Purify the product via column chromatography.  
- **Key Steps**: Maintain inert atmosphere and precise temperature control.

##### Step [2]: COF Assembly via Imine Condensation  
- **Reagents**:  
  • Pre-synthesized Por-8-CHO and Por-8-NH₂  
  • Anisole (1.33 mL) and benzyl alcohol (0.67 mL)  
  • 6 M acetic acid (0.2 mL) as catalyst  
- **Conditions**: Sonicate for 5 minutes until a cloudy solution forms; flash freeze at 77 K using liquid nitrogen; perform three freeze–pump–thaw cycles; seal in a glass ampoule and heat at 120 °C for 5 days.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Mix all reagents in a glass ampoule and sonicate.  
  2. Add 6 M acetic acid to promote imine bond formation.  
  3. Immediately flash freeze the mixture; degas by three freeze–pump–thaw cycles.  
  4. Seal and heat the ampoule at 120 °C for 5 days to achieve condensation.  
- **Key Steps**: Ensure complete degassing and meticulous temperature control during polymerization.

##### Step [3]: Purification  
- **Process**:  
  1. Recover the reddish-brown COF solid via centrifugation at appropriate speed.  
  2. Wash the precipitate three times with N,N-dimethylacetamide and acetone (10 mL each).  
  3. Subject the sample to Soxhlet extraction using tetrahydrofuran and acetone for 48 hours to remove impurities.  
- **Key Steps**: Effective removal of unreacted precursors by thorough solvent exchanges.

##### Step [4]: Drying and Activation  
- **Conditions**: Dry the purified COF in a vacuum chamber at 20 mTorr and 80 °C for 24 hours.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Transfer the washed sample to a drying apparatus.  
  2. Evacuate and maintain prescribed conditions to remove residual solvents.

#### COF Preparation [2]: PCOF-90

Follow an analogous workflow using Por-8-CHO combined with Py-8-NH₂ (prepared via a Suzuki reaction from 1,3,6,8-tetrabromopyrene and diphenylaminophenylboronic acid pinacol ester) to yield a framework with lower porphyrin content.

This detailed protocol—from precursor synthesis to final activation—ensures reproducibility and high crystallinity, providing a robust platform for subsequent electrocatalytic performance evaluations.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation process for the COF materials is designed to remove residual synthesis solvents while preserving the framework’s delicate imine-linked structure. This process involves a carefully controlled sequence of solvent exchange and thermal vacuum drying steps.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
• Definition: Activation temperature is essential for facilitating the removal of residual solvents from the COF pore network and enhancing porosity without compromising structural integrity.  
• Experimental Conditions: The COF materials were thermally activated at 80 °C during the vacuum drying step.  
• Observations: Operating at 80 °C enabled efficient solvent removal while maintaining the crystalline framework, as the moderate temperature was sufficient to open the pore network without inducing collapse.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
• Definition: Maintaining a low-pressure environment during activation helps in the swift evaporation of guest molecules and minimizes capillary forces that could lead to pore collapse.  
• Experimental Conditions: The activation was performed under a vacuum level of 20 mTorr.  
• Observations: The low-pressure (20 mTorr) condition was critical in promoting rapid solvent removal, thereby preserving the framework’s consistency and ensuring reproducibility in porosity.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Solvent
• Definition: Prior to thermal activation, solvent exchange is used to displace the high-boiling-point synthesis solvents that may be trapped within the COF pores. This gradual exchange is vital for preventing pore collapse and ensuring that activation proceeds uniformly.  
• Experimental Conditions: The protocol involved sequential washing starting with water for initial cleaning, followed by immersion in heated N,N‑dimethylformamide (or N,N‑dimethylacetamide) with repeated washes (typically 3×10 mL at 80 °C). Additional rinsing with acetone was conducted, and in some cases, a Soxhlet extraction using tetrahydrofuran for 48 hours was employed.  
• Observations: These combined solvent exchange steps effectively replaced residual synthesis solvents, thereby optimizing the pore accessibility.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time
• Definition: The duration of the activation process is a key factor in ensuring complete removal of residual solvents without damaging the COF framework.  
• Experimental Conditions: The activation was maintained for a total of 24 hours under the specified conditions.  
• Observations: A 24-hour activation period ensured thorough evaporation of guest molecules, resulting in open and accessible pores conducive to improved catalytic performance.

### 5.2 Application/Testing

#### 5.2.1 Test Temperature  
The test temperature is a critical parameter that directly influences the reaction kinetics and adsorption behavior of the COF materials. Two distinct temperatures are reported: for flash freezing during gas adsorption measurements, the COF samples are exposed to 77 K, ensuring rapid solvent immobilization and preservation of the pore structure; for electrocatalytic urea synthesis, the reaction is conducted at 90 °C. The elevated temperature in the electrochemical tests facilitates enhanced reactant activation and improves the overall urea yield and Faradaic efficiency.

#### 5.2.2 Test Pressure  
Test pressure plays a vital role in both sample preparation and application. During the evacuation of COF samples, a vacuum of 20 mTorr is applied to effectively remove impurities and solvents, thereby optimizing the adsorption capacity. In contrast, the application tests themselves are carried out under ambient pressure conditions. This ambient setting is essential for maintaining stable electrochemical performance throughout the reaction, as the operational conditions are designed to mimic realistic environments.

#### 5.2.3 Analyte Concentration/Feed Composition  
The electrocatalytic urea synthesis experiments utilize a precisely defined cathode electrolyte, comprising a mixture of 0.1 M KHCO₃ and 0.1 M KNO₃. This feed composition is critical for ensuring sufficient availability of both CO₂ and NO₃⁻, which are key in promoting the targeted carbon–nitrogen coupling reaction. The balanced concentration of these reactants is pivotal in obtaining high nitrogen selectivity (~92.4%) and nearly 100% carbon selectivity, as reported in the evaluation.

#### 5.2.4 Flow Rate/Space-Time Velocity  
A controlled flow rate is maintained to ensure reproducibility and effective mass transfer in the testing setup. In the electrocatalytic cell, the flow rates are rigorously set at 60 mL/min for both the cathode and anode electrolyte streams, while CO₂ is continuously introduced at 20 mL/min. These flow parameters are instrumental in providing a consistent supply of reactants and in minimizing concentration gradients, ultimately contributing to the observed high urea yield rate of 135.6 mmol g_cat⁻¹ h⁻¹ and sustained catalyst durability during over 100 hours of continuous operation.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
The crystal structure and phase purity of the synthesized COFs were primarily determined by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) and wide-angle X-ray scattering (WAXS). PXRD patterns of PCOF-34 and PCOF-90 exhibited multiple sharp, well-resolved peaks in the low-angle region, indicative of high crystallinity. Indexing of these peaks, supported by the Dicvol procedure, yielded unit cell parameters consistent with a two-fold interpenetrated bcu topology. Structural models based on this topology produced simulated PXRD patterns that closely matched experimental data. Pawley and Rietveld refinements further validated the structure, with low reliability factors (e.g., Rp = 0.58%, Rwp = 0.99%) confirming excellent agreement between observed and calculated patterns. These results establish the frameworks’ high crystallinity and phase purity.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Bonding and Framework Formation
Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and solid-state ^13C CP/MAS NMR were employed to confirm the formation of imine linkages. FTIR spectra showed the disappearance of the aldehyde (C=O, ~1700 cm⁻¹) and amine (N–H, ~3430–3360 cm⁻¹) vibrations from the starting monomers, alongside the emergence of a prominent C=N stretching peak at 1623 cm⁻¹, characteristic of imine bonds. Solid-state ^13C NMR revealed imine carbon resonances at 157.63 ppm (PCOF-34) and 158.87 ppm (PCOF-90), further confirming successful Schiff-base condensation and framework integrity.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition and Metal Incorporation
Elemental analysis (CHN), inductively coupled plasma–mass spectrometry (ICP-MS), and energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) mapping were used to verify composition. For PCOF-34, CHN analysis yielded C 87.25%, H 4.33%, N 8.42%, closely matching theoretical values. ICP-MS quantified Fe content in PCOF-34-Fe at 3.31 wt%, consistent with the expected 3.90 wt%. EDX mapping confirmed uniform distribution of C, N, and Fe throughout the COF skeleton, demonstrating successful and homogeneous metal incorporation.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Area and Porosity
Nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms at 77 K were analyzed using the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) method and density functional theory (DFT) for pore size distribution. PCOF-34-SC exhibited a BET surface area of 826 m²·g⁻¹, while PCOF-90-SC reached 1017 m²·g⁻¹. DFT analysis revealed primary pore sizes of 6, 12, and 15 Å for PCOF-34, and 6, 12, and 16 Å for PCOF-90, confirming the presence of permanent, well-defined porosity essential for catalytic applications.

#### 6.1.5 Morphology and Microstructure
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and cryogenic TEM (cryo-TEM) were utilized to assess morphology and microstructure. SEM images showed spindle-shaped microcrystals (~200 nm) for PCOF-34 and aggregated square microcrystals (~50 nm) for PCOF-90. Controlled crystallization yielded larger, well-dispersed crystals (up to ~2 μm for PCOF-34-SC). Cryo-TEM and AC-HAADF-STEM provided direct visualization of the 2-fold interpenetrated networks and twin iron sites, with measured intersite distances of 8.8 Å (PCOF-34-Fe) and 9.3 Å (PCOF-90-Fe), confirming precise spatial arrangement at the atomic level.

#### 6.1.6 Thermal Stability
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) assessed thermal stability under both N₂ and O₂ atmospheres. PCOF-34 and PCOF-34-Fe demonstrated high stability, with decomposition temperatures up to 550 °C under N₂ and 370 °C under O₂. These results indicate robust frameworks suitable for demanding catalytic environments.

### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Electrocatalytic Urea Synthesis Performance

- **Description:**  
  The electrocatalytic performance of COF-based catalysts was evaluated for the synthesis of urea from CO₂ and NO₃⁻ in an H-type cell. Key performance metrics include Faradaic efficiency (FE), urea yield rate, nitrogen selectivity, carbon selectivity, and turnover frequency (TOF).  
  - *Faradaic efficiency (FE)* is defined as the ratio of the charge used for urea formation to the total charge passed during electrolysis:  
    \[
    \text{FE} = \frac{n \times F \times \text{amount of urea produced}}{\text{total charge passed}} \times 100\%
    \]
    where \( n \) is the number of electrons transferred and \( F \) is the Faraday constant.
  - *Urea yield rate* is reported as millimoles of urea produced per gram of catalyst per hour (mmol g⁻¹_cat_ h⁻¹).
  - *Selectivity* metrics quantify the fraction of nitrogen or carbon from the reactants incorporated into urea.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - **PCOF-34-Fe** (twin iron site distance: 8.8 Å):  
    - Faradaic efficiency: 90.0%  
    - Urea yield rate: 135.6 mmol g⁻¹_cat_ h⁻¹ (at –0.6 V vs. RHE)  
    - Nitrogen selectivity: 92.4% (at –0.5 V), 90.8% (at –0.6 V)  
    - Carbon selectivity: nearly 100% (no CO or CH₄ detected)  
    - TOF: 215.9 h⁻¹ (at –0.6 V)  
  - **PCOF-90-Fe** (twin iron site distance: 9.3 Å):  
    - Faradaic efficiency: 44.7%  
    - Urea yield rate: 42.2 mmol g⁻¹_cat_ h⁻¹  
  - **NUST-5-Fe** (twin iron site distance: 21.7 Å):  
    - Faradaic efficiency: 12.6%  
    - Urea yield rate: 9.7 mmol g⁻¹_cat_ h⁻¹  

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The spatial proximity of twin iron sites in PCOF-34-Fe (8.8 Å) is critical for efficient carbon–nitrogen coupling, enabling rapid transfer of *CO and *NH₂ intermediates and suppressing side reactions. This results in superior urea yield, selectivity, and FE compared to COFs with more distant or isolated iron sites. Comparative studies confirm that increasing the distance between active sites (e.g., in NUST-5-Fe) leads to lower catalytic performance.

#### 6.2.2 Stability and Durability

- **Description:**  
  Stability and durability were assessed by monitoring catalytic activity and structural integrity during prolonged electrocatalytic operation. Key parameters include retention of urea yield rate, FE, and framework structure over extended testing.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - PCOF-34-Fe maintained stable urea yield rate and FE over 100 hours of continuous operation.
  - No significant current decay was observed during the durability test.
  - Post-test analyses (TEM, EDX mapping, PXRD) confirmed preservation of crystal structure and elemental distribution.

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The robust framework and entangled twin iron sites of PCOF-34-Fe confer excellent operational stability, with no loss of activity or structural degradation after extended use. This durability is essential for practical electrocatalytic applications.

#### 6.2.3 Structure–Performance Relationship

- **Description:**  
  The relationship between catalyst structure—specifically, the spatial arrangement of twin iron sites—and electrocatalytic performance was systematically investigated.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - PCOF-34-Fe (8.8 Å site distance) outperformed PCOF-90-Fe (9.3 Å) and NUST-5-Fe (21.7 Å) in all key metrics.
  - DFT calculations and in situ spectroscopic studies corroborated that closer site proximity lowers the reaction free energy for C–N coupling and accelerates intermediate transfer.

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The entangled, subnanoscale arrangement of twin iron sites in PCOF-34-Fe is directly responsible for its high efficiency and selectivity in urea synthesis. This structure–function correlation underscores the importance of precise active site engineering in COF catalyst design.
