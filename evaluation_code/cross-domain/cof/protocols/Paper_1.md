## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

This section outlines the solvothermal synthesis methods employed to produce COFs, specifically highlighting the procedures for Py-ttTII COF and Py-nTII COF. Both methods rely on a similar stepwise protocol, ensuring reproducibility through controlled reaction conditions and standardized reagent amounts.

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis for Py-ttTII COF

1. **Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation**  
   - Mix tetradentate Py(NH₂)₄(dioxane)₁.₅ (e.g., 20 µmol, 14.0 mg) with the linear dialdehyde ttTII(CHO)₂ (e.g., 40 µmol, 31.0 mg) in a reaction vessel.  
   - Add solvents such as mesitylene and benzyl alcohol.

2. **Polymerization/Condensation**  
   - Introduce 6 M acetic acid (typically 100 µL) as a modulator.  
   - Seal the PTFE-lined glass tube or autoclave under an inert argon atmosphere.  
   - Initiate the reaction by heating to 120 °C.

3. **Crystallization / Reaction**  
   - Maintain the mixture at 120 °C for 6 days for bulk powder synthesis (or 3 days for thin film formation) to allow for controlled imine condensation.

4. **Washing / Purification**  
   - After cooling, filter the precipitate and wash with solvents such as MeCN to remove unreacted materials.

5. **Drying**  
   - Dry the product under high vacuum or employ supercritical CO₂ extraction to enhance porosity and remove residual solvents.

6. **Additional Post-treatments**  
   - Further degassing at elevated temperatures (e.g., 100 °C) can be applied to ensure optimal crystallinity and porosity.

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis for Py-nTII COF

1. **Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation**  
   - Combine Py(NH₂)₄(dioxane)₁.₅ with nTII(CHO)₂ in the presence of a solvent mixture that includes mesitylene, benzyl alcohol, and 1,4-dioxane.  

2. **Polymerization/Condensation and Reaction**  
   - Add 6 M acetic acid as a catalyst.  
   - Seal the reaction vessel (PTFE-sealed microtube for powders or an autoclave with a glass liner for thin films) and heat at 120 °C for 3 days (thin films) or 6 days (bulk powder).

3. **Washing and Drying**  
   - Proceed with a similar washing process and drying protocol as described above.

### Comparative Analysis

Both methods offer the advantage of producing highly crystalline, porous COFs with well-defined imine linkages (confirmed by characteristic C=N resonances). However, the need for specialized equipment (autoclaves, sealed reaction tubes) and extended reaction times increases complexity and costs. The controlled inert environment is critical to prevent side reactions, ensuring reproducibility and optimal framework formation.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: Py-ttTII COF

##### Step [1]: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**:  
  • Py(NH₂)₄(dioxane)₁.₅ (14.0 mg, 20 µmol, 1.0 eq.)  
  • ttTII(CHO)₂ (31.0 mg, 40 µmol, 2.0 eq.)

- **Conditions**:  
  • Anhydrous solvents under argon atmosphere in oven-dried PTFE-lined glass reaction tubes  
  • Maintain stirring at room temperature for initial mixing  
  • Reaction environment controlled at 120 °C; duration is 6 days for bulk powder synthesis or 3 days if thin films are targeted
- **Process**:  
  1. Dissolve the primary monomers in the solvent mixture.  
  2. Add 6 M acetic acid gradually to promote condensation.  
  3. Seal the tube, ensuring an inert argon environment is maintained, then transfer into a Teflon-lined autoclave if required.  
  4. Heat at 120 °C for the prescribed duration.  
  5. Once complete, cool to room temperature and collect the precipitate by filtration; wash thoroughly with appropriate solvents (e.g., MeCN) to remove residual reagents.
- **Key Steps**: Precise reagent measurements, using degassed solvents and inert atmosphere, and stringent temperature control are crucial for achieving high crystallinity.

##### Step [2]: Purification, Drying & Activation
- **Process**:  
  1. Purify the product by repetitive washing and optionally perform supercritical CO₂ extraction (approx. 1 hour) to remove occluded solvents.  
  2. Dry under high vacuum at 70–100 °C until complete solvent removal is confirmed.  
- **Key Considerations**: Ensure minimal exposure to moisture throughout the purification process, as maintained reaction conditions are critical for reproducibility.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The COF activation process is integrated into the standard post‐synthesis purification and pre‐application treatment. It is designed to remove residual high-boiling-point solvents and impurities while preserving the structural integrity and porosity of the COF frameworks. The following subsections detail the key activation steps as extracted from the article.

#### 5.1.1 Solvent Exchange and Pre-Activation Washing
After COF synthesis, the materials undergo a brief solvent exchange. For example, COF films are immersed in dry acetonitrile for approximately 2 minutes before being dried with a stream of nitrogen. This exchange step is crucial for displacing higher boiling point solvents acquired during synthesis, ensuring that interfering residues are minimized prior to further drying. Although the procedure is not cycled or extended, the rapid immersion effectively prepares the material for subsequent activation treatments.

#### 5.1.2 High Vacuum Drying and Thermal Degassing
Following solvent exchange, COF samples are subjected to drying under high vacuum conditions. This high-vacuum treatment, while not quantified by exact pressure values, plays a vital role in the removal of residual solvents. In addition, a degassing phase is implemented whereby the COF is heated at 100 °C under high vacuum for 1 hour. This thermal degassing is essential to achieve efficient solvent evacuation from the pores without inducing thermal degradation or framework collapse, thereby ensuring that the intrinsic porosity is maintained.

#### 5.1.3 Post-Synthetic Supercritical CO₂ Extraction
For certain samples, a post-synthetic supercritical CO₂ extraction is employed for about 1 hour. This method is particularly effective in extracting any remaining high-boiling-point solvents due to the unique properties of supercritical CO₂. The controlled duration and ambient conditions during extraction contribute to a thorough cleaning of the framework, optimizing the COF’s pore accessibility and enhancing its performance in subsequent electrochemical applications.

Collectively, these activation steps—solvent exchange, vacuum drying with thermal degassing, and supercritical CO₂ extraction—are critical in delivering high-quality COFs with robust porosity and electrochemical responsiveness.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
The crystal structure and phase purity of the COF materials were primarily assessed using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) and grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS). PXRD patterns of the Py-ttTII COF bulk material displayed sharp reflections with minimal background, indicative of high crystallinity. Rietveld refinement in the monoclinic space group C2/m yielded refined lattice parameters (a = 6.33 ± 0.02 nm, b = 5.89 ± 0.02 nm, c = 0.39 ± 0.02 nm, β = 60 ± 5°), closely matching the simulated structure. GIWAXS analysis of thin films confirmed a strong texture, with the a–b planes predominantly aligned parallel to the substrate, as evidenced by the concentration of hk0 reflections above the sample horizon. This orientation ensures that the pores are accessible at the film surface.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Bond Formation
Solid-state ^13C CP-MAS NMR spectroscopy was employed to confirm the formation of imine linkages. The spectra exhibited a characteristic imine (C=N) resonance at approximately 170 ppm, with the absence of aldehyde signals near 190 ppm, verifying complete condensation and the absence of unreacted aldehyde groups. This result was consistent across all COFs studied, confirming successful framework formation.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition
Elemental (CHN) analysis validated the expected stoichiometry of the COFs. For Py-ttTII COF, the measured values (C: 67.48%, H: 4.53%, N: 5.23%, S: 18.37%) closely matched the calculated composition, confirming the successful incorporation of the designed building blocks. Similar agreement was observed for other COFs in the series.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Area and Porosity
Nitrogen sorption isotherms at 77 K, analyzed using the BET method and QSDFT, revealed high surface areas and porosity. Py-ttTII and Py-nTII COFs exhibited BET surface areas of 1370 m²/g and 1390 m²/g, respectively, with total pore volumes of 0.85–0.87 cm³/g. The pore size distribution was bimodal, with maxima at approximately 2.3 and 3.4 nm, consistent with a shamrock-shaped pore cross-section as predicted by structural models. These results confirm the permanent and accessible porosity of the frameworks.

#### 6.1.5 Morphology and Film Structure
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) provided insights into morphology. SEM cross-sections of Py-ttTII COF films revealed a unique pillar-like, open morphology with film thicknesses of 280 ± 30 nm. TEM of Py-nTII COF powders showed nanocrystalline domains of 50–100 nm and confirmed periodicity along the pore direction (4.0–4.2 nm). These morphological features support efficient mass transport and accessibility of the pore network.

#### 6.1.6 Optical Properties
UV–Vis–NIR spectroscopy demonstrated strong absorption features attributed to π–π* transitions (350–500 nm) and intramolecular charge transfer, with absorption onsets extending into the near-infrared. The red-shifted and broadened bands relative to monomers reflect the extended π-conjugation and donor–acceptor architecture of the COFs, which are critical for their electrochromic performance.

### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Electrochromic Switching Performance
- **Description:** Electrochromic switching refers to the reversible change in optical properties (absorbance) of the COF films upon application of an external potential. Key metrics include switching speed, coloration efficiency, and cycling stability, typically measured using cyclic voltammetry (CV) and spectroelectrochemistry.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **Switching Window:** Potentials cycled between –700 mV and +500 mV (vs. fc/fc⁺) with 2 s dwell time per step.
  - **Absorbance Change:** At 550 nm, ΔA = 1.2 OD per cycle.
  - **Switching Speed:** 0.38 s for oxidation (coloration), 0.20 s for reduction (bleaching) at 550 nm (10–90% ΔA).
  - **Coloration Efficiency:** Up to 858 cm² C⁻¹ at 880 nm (NIR region).
  - **Cycling Stability:** >95% of initial response retained after 100 cycles; >85% after 200 cycles; minimal drift (<5%) over 100 cycles.
- **Observations and Trends:** The Py-ttTII COF films exhibit exceptionally fast and stable electrochromic switching, with high coloration efficiency in both visible and NIR regions. The oriented, pillarlike morphology and accessible pores facilitate rapid ion transport and electron transfer, as evidenced by minimal peak separation (~20 mV) in CV and highly reversible redox profiles.

#### 6.2.2 Redox Activity and Electron Transfer Kinetics
- **Description:** Redox activity is assessed by the reversibility and kinetics of electron transfer during electrochemical cycling, typically quantified by CV peak separation and charge density.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **Oxidation Waves:** Two distinct oxidation waves observed in CV at ca. –100 mV and +300 mV (vs. fc/fc⁺).
  - **Peak Separation:** ~20 mV, indicating fast electron transfer.
  - **Charge Density:** Extracted value of 0.0032 C cm⁻², closely matching the theoretical value (0.0030 C cm⁻²) for two-electron oxidation per ttTII unit.
- **Observations and Trends:** The small peak separation and close agreement between experimental and theoretical charge densities confirm rapid and efficient electron transfer. The redox processes are highly reversible, with no significant drift or loss of performance over repeated cycles.

#### 6.2.3 Porosity and Gas Uptake Capacity
- **Description:** Porosity and gas uptake are evaluated by nitrogen sorption isotherms, BET surface area, and pore size distribution, which are critical for applications involving mass transport and adsorption.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **BET Surface Area:** 1370 m² g⁻¹ (Py-ttTII COF).
  - **Total Pore Volume:** 0.85 cm³ g⁻¹.
  - **Pore Size Distribution:** Bimodal, with maxima at 2.3 nm and 3.4 nm.
- **Observations and Trends:** The high surface area and accessible mesopores, confirmed by type IVb isotherms and QSDFT analysis, support efficient gas adsorption and rapid ion diffusion, which are essential for both electrochromic and potential separation applications.

#### 6.2.4 Stability and Recyclability
- **Description:** Stability and recyclability are determined by the retention of electrochromic and structural properties after repeated cycling.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **Electrochromic Retention:** >95% of initial absorbance change after 100 cycles; >85% after 200 cycles.
  - **Structural Integrity:** GIWAXS patterns before and after cycling show no loss of crystallinity or orientation.
- **Observations and Trends:** The COF films maintain both their electrochemical performance and crystalline structure over extended cycling, demonstrating robust stability suitable for practical device applications.

#### 6.2.5 Morphology and Film Thickness
- **Description:** Film morphology and thickness, determined by SEM, influence optical contrast, switching speed, and mechanical durability.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **Film Thickness:** 280 ± 30 nm (Py-ttTII COF), up to 430 nm.
  - **Morphology:** Pillarlike, open structure with continuous nanocrystals.
- **Observations and Trends:** The unique morphology ensures optimal electronic coupling and unimpeded ion transport, directly contributing to the rapid and efficient electrochromic response observed in these COF films.
