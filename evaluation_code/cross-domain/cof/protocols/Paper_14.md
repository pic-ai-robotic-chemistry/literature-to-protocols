### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

For COF-701, a solvothermal synthesis technique is employed that emphasizes reproducibility and controlled crystallization. The following block details the sequential procedure as reported:

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis of COF-701
1. **Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation**
   - Reagents: 2,4,6‑trimethyl‑1,3,5‑triazine (TMT) and 4,4′‑biphenyldicarbaldehyde (BPDA) are combined.
   - Solvent Mixture: The monomers are dissolved in a blend of mesitylene, 1,4‑dioxane, and acetonitrile.
   - Catalyst: Trifluoroacetic acid is added to catalyze the aldol condensation, ensuring proper chemical activation.
   - Equipment: Initial mixing is done in a standard laboratory vessel before transfer to reaction equipment.

2. **Polymerization/Condensation**
   - The acid‑catalyzed aldol condensation reaction is initiated in the Teflon‑lined autoclave.
   - The controlled solvothermal conditions promote the formation of olefin linkages (–CH=CH–) between the monomers.
   - The sequence prioritizes polymerization immediately following monomer mixing to ensure bond formation precedes crystallization.

3. **Crystallization / Reaction**
   - The reaction mixture is maintained in the sealed autoclave under controlled temperature and pressure to achieve crystallization.
   - This step is critical to avoid the formation of amorphous structures that typically arise under conventional reflux conditions.

4. **Washing / Purification**
   - Post-reaction, the product undergoes sequential washes with methanol and an aqueous NH₄OH solution to remove unreacted species and byproducts.
   - Observations during this stage include the effective removal of impurities, facilitating a cleaner framework.

5. **Drying**
   - The purified COF powder is dried under dynamic vacuum.
   - Although specific drying conditions (e.g., temperature, duration) are not provided, the process is optimized to prepare the COF for further post‑synthesis modifications.

6. **Additional Post‑Treatments**
   - For subsequent catalytic applications, BF₃·OEt₂ is immobilized into the COF matrix. This involves mixing the COF with the catalyst followed by an additional drying step, ensuring the Lewis acid remains active.

In summary, the solvothermal approach—compared to room-temperature or microwave methods—provides a superior route for obtaining crystalline and highly stable COF‑701 while allowing for fine control over reaction variables such as reagent ratios, controlled condensation, and effective purification steps.

In this preparation sequence, precise control over reagent weighing, liquid transfer, and mixing is critical to the reproducibility of the COF-701 synthesis. Liquid reagents—mesitylene, 1,4-dioxane, and acetonitrile—are introduced using appropriate volumetric devices to guarantee exact volumes, while the combination of magnetic stirring and ultrasonic treatment ensures a critically homogeneous solution. ## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of COF-701 were determined using wide-angle X-ray scattering (WAXS) with Pawley refinement. This technique assesses the long-range order and stacking mode of the framework, which are critical for confirming the intended topology and crystallinity of covalent organic frameworks (COFs).
- **Findings:** WAXS analysis, supported by Pawley refinement, revealed that COF-701 adopts a staggered (AB) stacking mode in space group P6₃/m. The refined unit cell parameters are a = b = 29.1 Å, c = 6.9 Å, α = β = 90°, γ = 120°, with agreement factors Rwp = 2.92% and Rp = 1.73%. The structure features 1.1 nm wide rhombic channels along the c-axis, consistent with a layered hcb topology.
- **Observations:** The presence of sharp diffraction peaks and successful refinement to a specific stacking model confirm high crystallinity and phase purity.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Surface area and porosity were evaluated by nitrogen sorption isotherms at 77 K, using both Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) and Langmuir models, with pore size distribution analyzed via quenched solid density functional theory (QSDFT).
- **Findings:** COF-701 exhibits a BET surface area of 1366 m² g⁻¹ and a Langmuir surface area of 1715 m² g⁻¹. The pore size distribution is centered at 1.14 nm, closely matching the structural model.
- **Observations:** The high surface area and narrow pore size distribution confirm the successful formation of a porous architecture.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Particle Structure
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was employed to examine particle morphology and size distribution, providing insight into the framework’s physical form and uniformity.
- **Findings:** SEM images show well-defined COF-701 particles, indicating uniform morphology. The particles were imaged after dry deposition on a Si wafer.
- **Observations:** The observed morphology supports controlled nucleation and growth during solvothermal synthesis.

#### 6.1.4 Chemical Structure and Elemental Composition
- **Description:** The chemical structure was probed by FT-IR spectroscopy and solid-state ^13C CP-MAS NMR, while elemental composition was assessed by SEM EDS mapping and ICP-OES (for boron in post-synthetic modifications).
- **Findings:** FT-IR spectra display a new absorbance at 1627 cm⁻¹, attributed to the 12C–12C olefin stretch, and attenuation of the 12C–16O stretch at 1689 cm⁻¹, confirming olefin linkage formation. ^13C NMR shows disappearance of the aldehyde carbon peak (~192 ppm) and emergence of a new peak at 144 ppm, assigned to alkene carbons. SEM EDS mapping confirms the presence of carbon and the removal of fluorine after BF₃ removal; boron content was quantified by ICP-OES.
- **Observations:** These results confirm the successful conversion of aldehyde groups to olefin linkages and validate the expected framework composition.

#### 6.1.5 Optical Properties
- **Description:** Solid-state UV/Vis spectroscopy was used to assess the optical absorbance properties and guest–host interactions.
- **Findings:** The absorbance band of COF-701 undergoes a significant redshift upon BF₃ immobilization, which is fully reversed after guest removal.

#### 6.1.6 Thermal Stability

#### 6.1.7 Structural Integrity Post-Modification
