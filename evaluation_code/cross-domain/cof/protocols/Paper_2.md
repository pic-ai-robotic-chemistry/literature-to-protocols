## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

#### Solvothermal Synthesis for MeDBP‑TFB COF

1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
 • Organic monomers—namely, 5,10‐dimethylindeno[2,1‑a]indene bis(benzophenone imine) and 1,3,5‑triformylbenzene—are suspended in a solvent mixture of mesitylene and dioxane at a 1:1 volumetric ratio.  
 • The mixture is heated at 70 °C for 20 minutes to promote dissolution and ensure a uniform solution.

2. Polymerization / Condensation  
 • After the initial heating, the solution is cooled and sonicated.  
 • Aqueous trifluoroacetic acid (TFA, 6 M, ca. 18 equivalents) is added to catalyze the imine formation via condensation.  
 • This step is critical in initiating the polymerization sequence.

3. Crystallization / Reaction  
 • The reaction mixture undergoes three freeze–pump–thaw cycles to degas the vessel before backfilling with argon, ensuring an inert atmosphere.  
 • The sealed mixture is then heated at 120 °C for 3 days in an oven‑dried pressure flask, leading to the crystallization of the COF framework.

4. Washing / Purification  
 • Post-reaction, the solid product is collected using a PTFE membrane filter.  
 • It is extensively washed with acetone and methanol to remove residual monomers and byproducts, ensuring purity.

5. Drying  
 • The purified COF is subjected to supercritical CO₂ drying following a 24-hour methanol soak, preserving the porosity and framework integrity.

6. Additional Post-treatments  
 • Techniques like enclosing the material in a tea bag for uniform treatment during drying further enhance the quality and reproducibility of the final product.

#### Solvothermal Synthesis for PhDBP‑TFP COF

• The procedure mirrors that of MeDBP‑TFB COF, with the primary difference being the use of a DBP‑based linker and 1,3,5‑triformylphloroglucinol.  
• In this case, 6 equivalents of trifluoromethanesulfonic acid initiate polymerization, followed by identical degassing, heating (120 °C, 3 days), washing, and drying steps.

### Comparative Analysis

• Both methods yield crystalline frameworks, as evidenced by narrow diffraction peaks in PXRD.  
• While similar in basic procedures, differences in acid type and concentration affect the bond formation (imine versus keto–enamine) and may influence scalability and cost.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation of the COF materials was achieved through a two‐stage process that combines meticulous solvent exchange with thermal and vacuum treatments to ensure complete removal of residual synthesis solvents while maintaining the integrity of the porous network.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature  
Activation temperature plays a critical role in desorbing entrapped solvents from the COF pores. In this study, a controlled thermal treatment at 120 °C under vacuum conditions was applied during the final activation step. This moderate heating facilitates the removal of high‐boiling solvents without compromising framework stability, as indicated by retained crystallinity and improved BET surface areas.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure  
For effective solvent evaporation, the COF samples were subjected to high‐vacuum conditions maintained at approximately 1 mbar. This low pressure lowers the boiling point of residual solvents, ensuring efficient removal while protecting the delicate pore structure.

#### 5.1.3 Solvent Exchange Procedure  
Prior to thermal activation, a stringent solvent exchange protocol was implemented. The as‐synthesized filter cake was washed thoroughly with large quantities of acetone followed by methanol. The material was then encapsulated in a tea bag to prevent premature drying and submerged in methanol for 24 hours. This step is crucial as it replaces the synthesis solvents (such as mesitylene and dioxane) with more volatile media, thereby facilitating complete removal during subsequent drying processes.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Duration and Supercritical Drying  
Following the solvent exchange, the samples underwent vacuum drying for 18 hours at 120 °C. In some experiments, to further ensure the preservation of the framework and porosity, supercritical CO₂ drying was employed using a samdri-PVT-3B dryer. In this mode, the chamber was brought to pressures of 58–62 bar before slowly venting above the critical point, thereby preventing pore collapse and ensuring an effective post-synthetic activation.

Collectively, these carefully tuned activation parameters—temperature, pressure, solvent exchange, and time—contributed to the enhanced porosity, crystallinity, and overall performance of the COF materials in their intended applications.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
The crystal structure and phase purity of the synthesized COFs were primarily assessed by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD). PXRD patterns for MeDBP‑TFB COF displayed sharp, well-defined peaks, including a prominent reflex at 2θ = 3.3° corresponding to the (100) plane, with additional peaks at 5.7° ((110)), 6.5° ((200)), 8.7° ((120)), and a broad peak at 25.8° ((001)). PhDBP‑TFP COF exhibited its most intense (100) reflection at 3.3°, with further peaks at 6.7° ((200)) and 25.3° ((001)). These patterns closely matched simulated AA-stacked models, as confirmed by Pawley refinement in the P6/m space group, yielding lattice parameters of a = b = 32.5–38.6 Å and c = 3.4–3.8 Å, depending on the COF. The agreement between experimental and simulated data, along with the sharpness of the peaks, confirms high crystallinity and phase purity, supporting the formation of well-ordered, layered structures.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
Porosity and surface area were evaluated using nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherms at 77 K, with BET analysis and NL-DFT pore size distribution. MeDBP‑TFB COF exhibited a BET surface area of 1024 m²/g and a narrow pore size distribution centered at 20–25 Å, indicative of a microporous framework. PhDBP‑TFP COF showed a BET surface area of 790 m²/g with a pore size distribution around 25 Å, while DBP‑Ph‑TFB COF reached 1691 m²/g with pores centered at 38 Å. These high surface areas and uniform pore sizes confirm the successful formation of extended, porous frameworks.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Crystallinity (SEM and HR-TEM)
Morphological analysis by SEM revealed that MeDBP‑TFB COF forms highly uniform aggregates of spherical particles with diameters around 1 μm. HR-TEM imaging of PhDBP‑TFP COF showed uniform rod-shaped crystallites and clear lattice fringes with a spacing of 1.24 nm, corresponding to the (200) plane observed in PXRD. These direct visualizations corroborate the high crystallinity and structural order inferred from diffraction data.

#### 6.1.4 Chemical Structure Verification (FT-IR and Solid-State NMR)
FT-IR spectroscopy confirmed complete imine bond formation in TFB-linked COFs by the disappearance of the aldehyde C=O stretch (1695 cm⁻¹) and the appearance of a strong C=N stretch at 1595–1600 cm⁻¹. For TFP-linked (keto-enamine) COFs, the absence of the aldehyde C=O stretch (1638 cm⁻¹) and the presence of a new C=O stretch at 1570–1578 cm⁻¹, along with a C–N stretch near 1270 cm⁻¹, verified the expected linkage. Solid-state CP-MAS NMR spectra showed sharp signals for aromatic, imine, and carbonyl carbons, with the absence of aldehyde signals, further validating the proposed framework structures.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
Thermal stability was assessed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) under inert atmosphere. The COFs demonstrated high thermal stability, with onset decomposition temperatures ranging from 325 °C (MeDBP‑TFB COF) to 410 °C (DBP‑Ph‑TFP POP). These results indicate robust frameworks suitable for practical applications requiring thermal resilience.



### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Gas Sorption and Porosity

- **Observations and Trends:** The crystalline COFs, particularly those linked with TFB, exhibited higher surface areas and more defined pore structures compared to amorphous POPs. These features are directly linked to enhanced gas adsorption capacity and selectivity.

#### 6.2.2 Chemical and Thermal Stability

- **Description:** Stability was assessed by exposing COFs to water, acidic, and basic media, as well as by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). Retention of structural and chemical integrity under these conditions is essential for practical applications.
- **Experimental Data:** 
  - TGA: Onset of thermal decomposition above 320 °C for MeDBP‑TFB COF and ~335 °C for PhDBP‑TFP COF.

- **Observations and Trends:** Both MeDBP‑TFB COF and PhDBP‑TFP COF demonstrated excellent stability, maintaining crystallinity and porosity after harsh treatments, which is advantageous for long-term application in gas adsorption and electrochemical devices.

#### 6.2.3 Electrochemical Performance in Li-Organic Batteries

- **Description:** The electrochemical performance of PhDBP‑TFP COF was evaluated as a positive electrode material in Li-organic half-cells. Key metrics include specific discharge capacity, redox potential, and cycling stability.
- **Experimental Data:** 
  - Specific discharge capacity: 26 mA h g⁻¹ at 100 mA g⁻¹ (second cycle).
  - Redox potential: Plateau at ~3.95 V vs. Li/Li⁺.
  - Capacity retention: High stability over multiple cycles; capacity restored after rate changes.
  - Comparative performance: Amorphous DBP‑Ph‑TFP POP exhibited a lower capacity (14 mA h g⁻¹) under identical conditions.
- **Observations and Trends:** The crystalline PhDBP‑TFP COF outperformed its amorphous counterpart, demonstrating superior redox activity, higher capacity, and robust cycling stability. These results are attributed to its high crystallinity, uniform porosity, and efficient charge transport pathways.

#### 6.2.4 Reproducibility and Validation

- **Description:** Reproducibility of performance metrics was ensured by repeated syntheses and measurements, with consistent BET surface areas and electrochemical results.
