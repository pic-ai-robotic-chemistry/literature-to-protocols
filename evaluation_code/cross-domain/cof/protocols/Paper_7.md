## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

For both COF systems, the reported synthesis follows a reproducible solvothermal approach with a clear sequence of experimental steps:

#### • Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation
1. (R)-BINOL-DA is combined with the appropriate amine node:
   - For CCOF 7: TPE-TAM is used.
   - For CCOF 8: iPr-TAM is used.
2. The monomers are dissolved in a solvent system consisting of ethanol and mesitylene. Notably, CCOF 7 typically involves 1.5 mL ethanol and 0.5 mL mesitylene, while CCOF 8 is prepared with 1.5 mL ethanol and 0.3 mL mesitylene.
3. The mixture is sonicated for five minutes to ensure thorough mixing.

#### • Polymerization/Condensation
4. Immediately following mixing, 9 M acetic acid is added as a catalyst/modulator.
5. A distinct step involving flash freezing at 77 K using a liquid nitrogen bath is performed. The sealed Schlenk tube is evacuated and maintained under an inert atmosphere, ensuring moisture-free conditions before the condensation reaction proceeds.
6. During the condensation, the disappearance of aldehyde C=O stretches (∼1700 cm⁻¹) and the emergence of imine (CN) bonds at ∼1620–1621 cm⁻¹ are observed by FT-IR.

#### • Crystallization / Reaction
7. The sealed reaction vessel is subsequently heated at 120 °C for 3 days, facilitating crystallization of the COF network.

#### • Washing / Purification
8. Upon completion, the yellow solid formed (indicative of successful imine condensation) is isolated by hot filtration.
9. The material is washed with 1,4-dioxane (or in some steps, with DMF/acetone) to remove unreacted monomers and byproducts.

#### • Drying and Additional Post-treatments
10. The purified solid is dried in a vacuum chamber, typically at 100 °C under 20 mTorr for 12 hours. This activation step enhances porosity and is crucial for applications.
11. It is noted that while CCOF 7 undergoes successful exfoliation into ultrathin nanosheets (benefiting enantioselective fluorescence sensing), CCOF 8, due to its bulky isopropyl substituents, exhibits improved chemical resistance but resists exfoliation.

This systematic approach, balancing precise solvent ratios, reaction conditions, and post-treatments, ensures reproducibility and allows direct comparison of the two structurally and functionally distinct COFs for enantioselective sensing applications.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: CCOF 7

**Step 1: Precursor Preparation**  
- **Reagents**: (R)-BINOL-DA (46.7 mg, 0.1 mmol) and TPE-TAM (19.6 mg, 0.05 mmol) are used with ethanol (1.5 mL) and mesitylene (0.5 mL) as the solvent system.  
- **Conditions**: The mixture is sonicated for 5 minutes to ensure complete dissolution.  
- **Process**:  
  - Dissolve the primary precursors in the solvent mixture with thorough sonication, ensuring a homogeneous solution.  
  - Add 9 M acetic acid as a modulator.  
  - Transfer the mixture into a Schlenk tube and flash-freeze at 77 K using a liquid nitrogen bath, then evacuate and seal the tube to maintain inert conditions.  
- **Key Steps**: Rapid flash freezing and controlled addition of acetic acid are essential for reproducibility.

**Step 2: COF Assembly (Polymerization/Condensation)**  
- **Conditions**: The sealed reaction vessel is heated at 120 °C for 3 days.  
- **Process**:  
  - After flash freezing, allow the reaction mixture to warm to room temperature and then heat steadily.  
  - Monitor the progress via FT-IR spectroscopy, noting the disappearance of the aldehyde C=O stretch (∼1700 cm⁻¹) and the emergence of the imine (CN) vibration near 1621 cm⁻¹.  
- **Key Steps**: Maintaining precise temperature and timing ensures robust imine bond formation.

**Step 3: Purification, Drying & Activation**  
- **Process**:  
  - Upon reaction completion, cool the vessel to room temperature.  
  - Isolate the yellow solid via hot filtration, washing repeatedly with 1,4-dioxane.  
  - For nanosheet formation, suspend 5 mg of CCOF 7 bulk powder in 50 mL methanol, sonicate at 40 kHz for 4 hours, then centrifuge at 7000 rpm for 10 minutes and further at 10000 rpm for 10 minutes to remove non-exfoliated material.  
- **Drying & Activation**: Transfer the washed product to a vacuum chamber and evacuate at 20 mTorr at 100 °C for 12 hours.

#### COF Preparation [2]: CCOF 8

**Step 1: Precursor Preparation & Assembly**  
- **Reagents**: (R)-BINOL-DA (46.7 mg, 0.1 mmol) and iPr-TAM (40.4 mg, 0.067 mmol) with a solvent system comprising ethanol (1.5 mL) and mesitylene (0.3 mL).  
- **Process**: Employ the same sonication, acetic acid addition, and flash-freezing steps as for CCOF 7 to ensure compatibility and reproducibility.

**Step 2: COF Assembly and Post-Processing**  
- **Conditions & Process**: The sealed mixture undergoes solvothermal treatment at 120 °C for 3 days. Purification via hot filtration and washing with 1,4-dioxane, followed by activation using vacuum drying (20 mTorr at 100 °C for 12 hours), is performed. Note that the bulky isopropyl groups in iPr-TAM impart enhanced chemical stability though they inhibit effective exfoliation.

Both protocols emphasize critical operational details—such as reagent quantities, precise pH regulation using 9 M acetic acid, and strict timing—ensuring that the entire workflow is reproducible for researchers seeking to replicate these chiral COF syntheses.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation process for the COF materials is carried out through a two-step approach involving solvent exchange and subsequent thermal vacuum treatment, both critical for removing residual solvents and guest molecules from within the porous framework.

#### 5.1.1 Solvent Exchange with 1,4-Dioxane

Following hot filtration, the as‐formed COF solid is thoroughly washed with 1,4‑dioxane. This step is designed to replace high‑boiling‑point solvents and facilitate the removal of impurities that might otherwise compromise pore accessibility. Although specific parameters such as soaking time or refresh cycles are not detailed, this washing stage is essential for minimizing trapped solvents before proceeding to the vacuum drying step. The solvent exchange helps in enhancing the overall crystallinity of the material, contributing indirectly to improved porosity.

#### 5.1.2 Thermal and Vacuum Activation

Post solvent exchange, the COF is subjected to thermal activation combined with vacuum drying. In this step, the washed COF material is transferred into a vacuum chamber where it undergoes heating at 100 °C for 12 hours under an evacuation pressure of 20 mTorr. The activation temperature is critical, as it aids in the complete removal of residual guest molecules and solvents, while enhancing the inherent porosity of the framework. For samples prepared ahead of nitrogen adsorption measurements, an alternative condition is employed where the material is heated at 120 °C under a similar vacuum environment (below 10⁻³ torr) for 12 hours. This process ensures that the COF’s pores are fully accessible, which is vital for applications such as gas storage, separation, and enantioselective sensing.

The combined effect of the solvent exchange with 1,4‑dioxane and the subsequent thermal vacuum drying guarantees that the COF frameworks remain clean and highly porous. This dual activation process thus not only prevents potential framework collapse by carefully balancing the removal of residual solvents but also preserves the material's structural integrity, which is paramount for its performance in enantioselective fluorescence sensing and other practical applications.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) was employed to assess the crystallinity and phase purity of both CCOF 7 and CCOF 8. For CCOF 7, the PXRD pattern displayed a prominent peak at 2θ = 4.5°, corresponding to the (110) reflection, with additional peaks at 5.9°, 7.6°, 7.9°, 8.8°, 11.6°, 21.6°, and 23.3°, assigned to the (001), (020), (111), (220), (002), (550), and (043) planes, respectively. Pawley refinement yielded unit cell parameters (a = 40.2 Å, b = 24.4 Å, c = 15.3 Å, β = 104.5°) in the monoclinic C2 space group, with excellent agreement factors (Rwp = 1.92%, Rp = 1.03%). CCOF 8 exhibited its most intense peak at 4.1° (2θ) for the (110) plane, with further peaks at 7.0°, 8.1°, 10.8°, 12.9°, 14.1°, and 16.2°, corresponding to a staggered stacking (ABC) model in the trigonal R3 space group. The experimental patterns for both COFs matched well with simulated models, confirming high crystallinity and phase purity.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure Verification (FT-IR)
Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy confirmed the successful formation of imine-linked frameworks. In both COFs, the characteristic aldehyde C=O stretching bands (~1700 cm⁻¹) nearly disappeared, while strong C=N stretching vibrations emerged at ~1621 cm⁻¹ for CCOF 7 and ~1620 cm⁻¹ for CCOF 8. These spectral changes indicate complete consumption of aldehyde groups and formation of imine bonds.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
Nitrogen adsorption–desorption measurements at 77 K were used to determine surface area and porosity. CCOF 8 exhibited a BET surface area of 346 m²/g, a total pore volume of 0.53 cm³/g (P/P₀ = 0.99), and average pore widths of 0.80 and 1.0 nm (NLDFT). CCOF 7 showed a BET surface area of 281 m²/g, a pore volume of 0.37 cm³/g, and average pore widths of 0.81 and 1.1 nm. These results demonstrate that minor modifications in the amine monomer can significantly influence porosity.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Exfoliation
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed that CCOF 7 possesses a sphere-like bulk morphology. Upon solvent-assisted liquid sonication, TEM images showed transformation into ultrathin, sheet-like nanosheets (7-NS). Atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements determined the nanosheet thickness to be 3.5–4.0 nm, corresponding to approximately 5–6 COF layers. In contrast, CCOF 8 could not be exfoliated into nanosheets, likely due to the steric hindrance from bulky isopropyl groups.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal and Chemical Stability
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) under N₂ (10 °C/min) indicated that both COFs are thermally stable up to 380 °C, with no significant weight loss observed below this temperature. CCOF 8 demonstrated superior chemical resistance, retaining its PXRD pattern and BET surface area after exposure to harsh conditions (0.1 M HCl, 20 M NaOH, boiling water), attributed to the kinetic protection offered by isopropyl substituents.

#### 6.1.6 Chiral and Optical Properties
Circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy confirmed the chiral nature of CCOF 7, with frameworks derived from R- and S-BINOL monomers displaying mirror-image CD spectra. Molecular simulations and fluorescence studies further supported the chiral environment, with binding energies for (–)- and (+)-α-pinene calculated as –21.92 kcal/mol and –18.63 kcal/mol, respectively. Fluorescence quenching experiments demonstrated enantioselective sensing, with a higher quenching rate for the (–)-enantiomer, underscoring the functional significance of the chiral framework.



### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Enantioselective Fluorescence Sensing

- **Description:**  
  Enantioselective fluorescence sensing evaluates the ability of the COF to distinguish between enantiomers of chiral analytes via differential fluorescence quenching. The performance is quantified using the Stern–Völmer constant (Ksv), quenching ratio (QR), and enantioselective fluorescence decrease ratio (EF). The Stern–Völmer equation is applied:  
  \[
  \frac{I_0}{I} = 1 + K_{sv}[Q]
  \]  
  where \(I_0\) and \(I\) are the fluorescence intensities before and after analyte addition, and \([Q]\) is the analyte concentration. EF is defined as:  
  \[
  EF = \frac{(I_0/I^R - 1)}{(I_0/I^S - 1)}
  \]  
  where \(I^R\) and \(I^S\) are the intensities after exposure to the R- and S-enantiomers, respectively.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - CCOF 7 nanosheets (7-NS) exhibited an emission maximum at 380 nm.  
  - For α-pinene in solution:  
    - Ksv for (–)-enantiomer: 1348 M⁻¹  
    - Ksv for (+)-enantiomer: 395 M⁻¹  
    - Quenching ratio (QR): 3.41  
  - In membrane format (7@PVDF), after 480 s exposure to α-pinene vapor:  
    - Fluorescence decrease: 45% for (–)-enantiomer, 8% for (+)-enantiomer  
    - EF for α-pinene: 9.5  
    - EF values for other chiral vapors (limonene, fenchone, terpinen-4-ol, carvone): 1.7–9.5

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The 7-NS and 7@PVDF systems demonstrated pronounced enantioselectivity, with much higher quenching efficiency and faster response for the (–)-enantiomer of α-pinene. Control experiments with bulk CCOF 7 and BINOL-DA monomer showed negligible enantioselectivity, underscoring the critical role of nanosheet morphology and the chiral microenvironment provided by interlayer BINOL moieties.

#### 6.2.2 Morphology-Dependent Sensing Enhancement

- **Description:**  
  The accessible active sites and microenvironment within the COF structure, particularly after exfoliation into nanosheets, are directly linked to sensing performance. Exfoliation increases the external surface area and exposes more binding sites for analyte interaction.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - AFM-measured nanosheet thickness: 3.5–4.0 nm (5–6 layers)  
  - Only CCOF 7 could be exfoliated into nanosheets; CCOF 8 remained bulk due to steric hindrance from isopropyl groups.

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The enhanced enantioselective sensing of 7-NS and 7@PVDF is attributed to increased surface area and the rigid, chiral channels formed by BINOL units. CCOF 8’s inability to exfoliate limited its application in this context.

#### 6.2.3 Stability and Recyclability

- **Description:**  
  Stability and recyclability assess the retention of sensing performance after repeated use and under regeneration conditions.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - 7@PVDF membrane retained high EF values for α-pinene over three cycles (9.5, 9.4, 9.2).  
  - Membrane structure and quantum yield showed minimal change after regeneration (heating at 100 °C in vacuum for 1 h).

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The COF-based membrane demonstrated robust stability and could be reused for multiple sensing cycles without significant loss of enantioselectivity or fluorescence intensity.

#### 6.2.4 Chemical Resistance

- **Description:**  
  Chemical resistance evaluates the COF’s structural and functional integrity under harsh chemical conditions.

- **Experimental Data:**  
  - CCOF 8 exhibited superior resistance to alkaline and boiling water conditions, attributed to the protective isopropyl groups on the iPr-TAM node.

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  While CCOF 8’s chemical stability is enhanced, its application in enantioselective sensing is limited by its inability to form nanosheets.
