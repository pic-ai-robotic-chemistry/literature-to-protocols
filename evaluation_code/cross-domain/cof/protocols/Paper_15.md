## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

This section details the reproducible and scalable synthesis methods employed for constructing covalent organic frameworks (COFs) as described in the article. The primary technique utilized is solvothermal synthesis, which is implemented for both powder and thin‐film formats. The following procedures are systematically reported to ensure accuracy and replicability in COF fabrication.

#### • Py-TT COF Synthesis
1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
 • Reagents: 4PE(NH₂)₄ (5.9 mg, 15 µmol, 1.0 eq.) and TT(CHO)₂ (5.9 mg, 30 µmol, 2.0 eq.)  
 • Solvent System: Mesitylene (200 µL) and benzyl alcohol (1800 µL)  
2. Polymerization/Condensation  
 • Catalyst: 6 M acetic acid (200 µL) is added to facilitate imine bond formation.  
3. Crystallization / Reaction  
 • Equipment: Teflon-lined autoclave  
 • Conditions: Heating at 120 °C for 3 days under an inert atmosphere (N₂ or argon)  
4. Washing / Purification  
 • The precipitate is collected by filtration and washed with solvents such as DMF, acetone, or MeCN until the washings are clear.  
5. Drying  
 • Conditions: Drying is performed at 80 °C under vacuum.  
6. Post-Treatment  
 • Some samples are further exposed to acid vapors (e.g., TFA) for protonation, evidenced by a color change from yellow to dark brown, confirming imine bridge formation.

#### • Py-1P and Py-1POH COF Synthesis
1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
 • For Py-1P, Per(NH₂)₄ (e.g., 12.3 mg, 20 µmol, 1.0 eq.) is combined with terephthalaldehyde (5.4 mg, 40 µmol, 2.0 eq.) in a mixture of 1,4-dioxane (167–333 µL) and mesitylene, supplemented by 6 M acetic acid (100 µL).  
2. Subsequent steps mirror those for Py-TT COF, with reaction conditions maintained at 120 °C for 3 days using sealed glass reaction tubes or autoclaves.  
3. In Py-1POH COF synthesis, the incorporation of –OH groups slightly delays protonation, due to intramolecular hydrogen bonding stabilizing the free-base form.

These structured methods emphasize a clear order—monomer mixing, polymerization/condensation, crystallization, washing, drying, and optional post-treatments—to ensure reproducibility and clarity. The reported numerical values and precise conditions allow researchers to replicate the COF synthesis process confidently, supporting application testing in acid vapor sensing.

This section details the step‐by‐step preparation of reagents for each covalent organic framework (COF) to ensure complete reproducibility in synthesis.

##### 3.1.1.3 Py-1POH COF
- **Hydroxyl‐functionalized pyrene tetraamine**: Weighed 0.19 g under controlled conditions.  
- **Terephthalaldehyde**: Weighed 0.22 g, with careful confirmation of measurement accuracy.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

##### 3.1.2.1 Py-TT COF
- **Mesitylene**: Poured 200 µL into the reaction vessel using a micropipette to ensure precise volume control.  
- **1,4‐Dioxane**: Transferred 167 µL by pipetting directly into the vessel containing the solid reagents.

##### 3.1.2.2 Py-1P COF
- **1,4‐Dioxane**: Delivered 250 µL using a calibrated syringe into the pre-weighed monomer assembly.

##### 3.1.2.3 Py-1POH COF
- **Benzyl alcohol**: Measured and poured 1800 µL into the reaction tube with the aid of a graduated cylinder.  
- **6 M Acetic Acid**: Added 200 µL via pipetting to catalyze the imine condensation effectively.

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

##### 3.1.3.1 Py-TT COF
- **Reaction mixture**: Initially stirred at 600 rpm for 20 minutes at room temperature to promote homogenization, then the vessel was sealed and heated at 120 °C for 2–3 days under nitrogen to drive the condensation reaction.

##### 3.1.3.2 Py-1P COF
- **Precursor solution**: Mixed at 400 rpm for 30 minutes under an argon atmosphere, ensuring an inert environment that minimizes moisture and oxygen interference.

##### 3.1.3.3 Py-1POH COF
- **Combined reagents**: Gently stirred at 500 rpm for 30 minutes to achieve uniform dispersion. Subsequently, the sealed reaction mixture was maintained at 120 °C for an extended period to accommodate the slightly delayed protonation behavior associated with the hydroxyl groups.

Each set of procedures—from solid weighing through liquid addition and final mixing—is designed to align with the overall synthesis strategy, ensuring precise control over reagent ratios, thorough mixing, and stable reaction conditions that ultimately contribute to the COFs’ well-defined stacking and reproducible performance.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: Py-TT COF

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: Py(NH₂)₄(dioxane)₁.₅ (7.0 mg, 10 µmol), TT(CHO)₂ (3.9 mg, 20 µmol), mesitylene (1333 µL), benzyl alcohol (667 µL), and 6 M acetic acid (200 µL).
- **Conditions**: Use oven-dried glassware; dissolve and degas reagents under an argon atmosphere to eliminate moisture.
- **Process**:
  - Dissolve Py(NH₂)₄(dioxane)₁.₅ and TT(CHO)₂ in mesitylene and benzyl alcohol, ensuring homogeneity.
  - Add 6 M acetic acid to the mix, adjusting the pH to promote imine bond formation.
  - Stir at ambient temperature until a uniform solution is achieved.
- **Key Steps**: Emphasize the purity and degassing of reagents, as these initial measures are critical for reproducibility.

##### Step 2: COF Assembly
- **Reagents**: Use the prepared precursor solution without additional chemical adjustments.
- **Conditions**: Transfer the solution into a PTFE-lined autoclave; maintain an argon atmosphere; heat at 120 °C for 3 days.
- **Process**:
  - Seal the loaded autoclave to create a closed system.
  - Heat steadily, ensuring that the reaction mixture undergoes complete polymerization and condensation.
  - After the allotted time, slowly cool to room temperature.
- **Key Steps**: Consistent temperature control and an inert atmosphere are vital for effective imine condensation.

##### Step 3: Purification, Drying & Activation
- **Process**:
  - Transfer the cooled reaction mixture; collect the precipitate by filtration.
  - Rinse thoroughly with dry acetonitrile and acetone to remove residual solvents and reagents.
  - Dry the COF under argon. If activation is required, expose the material to TFA vapor at room temperature.
- **Key Steps**: Effective washing and controlled drying secure the framework’s crystallinity and porosity.

#### Additional Workflows for Py-1P and Py-1POH COFs
For Py-1P, similar steps are followed with a higher volume of acetic acid (400 µL) and longer reaction times if needed. Py-1POH synthesis mirrors these procedures but incorporates the functionalization effects of hydroxyl groups, resulting in a tunable protonation response. Each step strictly follows the order: Precursor Preparation → COF Assembly → Purification → Drying → Activation, ensuring the reproducibility of the COF synthesis workflow.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation process for the COF materials is integrated into the overall synthesis and purification workflow, focusing on the removal of residual solvents and ensuring the framework’s porosity and integrity prior to acid vapor sensing applications.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Process Overview
Activation is achieved through a combination of solvent exchange and drying procedures carried out immediately after COF synthesis. This approach serves to replace high-boiling synthesis solvents with lower-boiling solvents and eliminate any remaining guest molecules. The use of an inert atmosphere during drying further minimizes any risk of moisture-induced degradation.

#### 5.1.2 Thermal Activation Conditions
During the synthesis of the COFs, the reaction is conducted in sealed vessels at 120 °C for several days. This elevated temperature drives the imine condensation reaction to completion and assists with the expulsion of solvents from the pores. Although these conditions occur within the synthesis phase, they also contribute to the initial activation by reducing solvent content within the framework.

#### 5.1.3 Vacuum Drying and Inert Atmosphere
Following the solvent exchange, the COF powders (or films) undergo a drying step. For films, a brief immersion in dry acetonitrile (MeCN) for approximately 5 minutes is performed prior to drying. The drying is executed under high vacuum or with a flow of inert gas (argon or nitrogen), which helps in efficiently removing the exchanged solvent while preserving the structural integrity. Despite the lack of specific numerical values for vacuum pressure, the high-vacuum environment is critical for complete solvent removal.

#### 5.1.4 Role of Solvent Exchange
The solvent exchange step is essential for replacing residual high-boiling synthesis solvents with a volatile solvent like MeCN. This substitution minimizes the potential for pore collapse and ensures that subsequent drying steps do not compromise the framework’s porosity. Overall, the combined activation strategy yields COF materials that retain crystallinity and high porosity, prerequisites for effective acid vapor sensing.

### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of the COFs were assessed using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) and grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS). PXRD provides information on long-range order and stacking, while GIWAXS is particularly suited for thin films, revealing in-plane orientation and crystallinity.
- **Findings:** PXRD patterns, measured on a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation, displayed sharp reflections and well-resolved higher-order peaks, indicative of high crystallinity and long-range order. The patterns were indexed in the hexagonal space group P6 and refined using Pawley or Rietveld methods, confirming eclipsed (AA) stacking. GIWAXS data, collected with an Anton Paar SAXSpace system, further corroborated the in-plane orientation of imine-linked layers parallel to the substrate, consistent with AA stacking.
- **Observations:** The retention of sharp, intense peaks after repeated protonation/deprotonation cycles demonstrates robust crystallinity and phase purity.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Imine Bond Formation
- **Description:** The formation of imine linkages was verified by FTIR and ^1H NMR spectroscopy. FTIR identifies characteristic vibrational modes, while ^1H NMR confirms the disappearance of precursor signals and the emergence of imine protons.
- **Findings:** FTIR spectra showed the disappearance of aldehyde (1680 cm⁻¹) and amine (1280 cm⁻¹) bands, with the appearance of the imine C=N stretch at ~1610 cm⁻¹. Upon protonation, a new C=NH⁺ stretching mode emerged at ~1650 cm⁻¹. ^1H NMR (Bruker Avance III HD) of soluble intermediates confirmed imine formation by the loss of starting material signals and the appearance of new singlets consistent with the aromatic environment.
- **Observations:** Cross-validation between FTIR and ^1H NMR supports complete conversion to the imine-linked framework.

#### 6.1.3 Optical Properties and Protonation Response
- **Description:** UV-Vis-NIR spectroscopy was used to monitor optical changes upon protonation, which are critical for acid vapor sensing applications.
- **Findings:** For Py-TT COF, the main absorption band shifted from 578 nm (deprotonated) to longer wavelengths upon protonation, with a new band appearing between 550–750 nm. Py-1P COF exhibited a similar shift at 544 nm. The intensity of these bands followed a logarithmic dependence on acid concentration. Py-1POH COF showed delayed and weaker protonation-induced absorption due to intramolecular hydrogen bonding.
- **Observations:** The spectral changes were fully reversible with base treatment, and the response range spanned at least four orders of magnitude in acid concentration, confirming robust and tunable protonation behavior.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption isotherms at 77 K were used to evaluate surface area and pore size distribution, essential for understanding framework accessibility.
- **Findings:** The Per-N COF displayed a reversible type IV(b) isotherm, typical for small mesopores, while Per-Py COF showed a distinct hysteresis, indicating larger mesopores. Pore size distributions revealed two maxima corresponding to trigonal and hexagonal pores. Samples were outgassed at 120 °C for 24 h prior to measurement.
- **Observations:** The COFs maintained porosity and surface area after repeated cycling, supporting their structural robustness.

### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Acid Vapor Sensing Sensitivity and Response Range
- **Description:** Sensitivity and dynamic range are critical metrics for evaluating COF-based acid vapor sensors. Sensitivity is defined by the lowest acid vapor concentration that induces a measurable optical response, while the response range denotes the span of concentrations over which the sensor provides a quantifiable signal.
- **Experimental Data:** Py-TT COF thin films demonstrated a detection limit for HCl vapor of approximately 100 μg L⁻¹, with a linear (logarithmic) response over at least three orders of magnitude in acid concentration. For Py-1P COF, the detection limit reached as low as 35 μg L⁻¹, with a response range spanning at least four orders of magnitude. These values were determined by exposing COF films in sealed cuvettes to defined acid vapor concentrations and recording UV-Vis-NIR absorption spectra.
- **Observations and Trends:** Both Py-TT and Py-1P COFs exhibited high sensitivity and broad dynamic range, with no saturation observed within the tested concentration window. The response was immediate upon exposure, indicating rapid protonation kinetics suitable for practical sensing applications.

#### 6.2.2 Optical Response and Reversibility
- **Description:** The optical response is characterized by color changes and the emergence of new absorption bands upon protonation. Reversibility assesses the ability of the COF to return to its original state after deprotonation, a key factor for sensor reusability.
- **Experimental Data:** Upon exposure to TFA vapor, Py-TT COF films changed from orange to brown (mono-protonated) and then to black (bis-protonated) as acid strength or concentration increased. UV-Vis-NIR spectra revealed new absorption bands between 500–750 nm and a bleach band at ~370 nm. These changes were fully reversible: treatment with triethylamine vapor restored the original color and absorption profile. Py-1P COF showed analogous reversible spectral shifts, with a prominent absorption at 544 nm in the mono-protonated state.
- **Observations and Trends:** The COFs maintained consistent optical properties and reversibility over multiple acid/base cycles, confirming their robustness for repeated sensing.

#### 6.2.3 Tunability and Selectivity via Chemical Structure
- **Description:** The chemical composition of the COF framework modulates its protonation behavior, enabling tunable sensing performance and selectivity.
- **Experimental Data:** Py-1POH COF, containing hydroxyl groups, required significantly higher acid concentrations to achieve protonation, as evidenced by lower intensity and delayed appearance of protonation-induced absorption bands compared to Py-TT and Py-1P COFs. This effect is attributed to intramolecular –OH···N hydrogen bonding, which stabilizes the nonprotonated state.
- **Observations and Trends:** The distinct protonation thresholds and spectral responses among the COFs demonstrate that sensor performance can be tailored through rational design of the framework’s functional groups.

#### 6.2.4 Structural Stability and Cycling Performance
- **Description:** Structural stability is assessed by retention of crystallinity and porosity after repeated sensing cycles, ensuring long-term operational reliability.
- **Experimental Data:** GIWAXS and PXRD measurements confirmed that Py-TT and Py-1P COFs retained their crystallinity and porosity after at least five protonation/deprotonation cycles. Nitrogen sorption isotherms showed no significant loss in surface area or pore structure.
- **Observations and Trends:** The COFs’ robust frameworks enable stable performance and reusability, with no observable degradation after multiple sensing cycles.
