## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

This section delineates the solvothermal synthesis techniques used to construct the interlocked COF networks, offering a comparative analysis in terms of synthesis complexity, scalability, cost, and equipment requirements. All methods follow a consistent experimental workflow comprising monomer mixing, polymerization/condensation, crystallization, washing, drying, and additional post-treatments as applicable.

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis: catena-COF-805
1. **Monomer Mixing/Solution Preparation**
   - Charge a sealed pyrex pressure tube with [Cu(PDB)₂]BF₄ (8.0 mg, 0.008 mmol), tris(4-aminophenyl)amine (TAPA, 3.3 mg, 0.011 mmol), and p‑Br‑aniline (27.5 mg, 20 equiv.).
   - Add 0.25 mL 1,4‑dioxane, 0.25 mL mesitylene, and 0.05 mL 6 M aqueous acetic acid.
2. **Polymerization/Condensation**
   - Seal the tube and heat at 150°C for 72 h.
3. **Crystallization/Reaction**
   - Formation of a reddish-brown solid indicates successful crystallization.
4. **Washing/Purification**
   - Isolate the solid by centrifugation and wash sequentially with DMF and THF.
5. **Drying**
   - Dry the material at room temperature for 12 h followed by 120°C for 12 h.
6. **Post-Treatment**
   - Verify insolubility in water and common organic solvents.

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis: catena-COF-806
1. **Monomer Mixing/Solution Preparation**
   - Combine [Cu(PDB)₂]BF₄ (8.0 mg, 0.008 mmol) with tris(4‑aminophenyl)methane (TAPM, 5.0 mg, 0.017 mmol) in 0.5 mL 1,4‑dioxane, adding 20 μL aniline (24.4 equiv.) and 0.1 mL 6 M aqueous acetic acid.
2. **Polymerization/Condensation**
   - Heat the sealed tube at 120°C for 72 h.
3. **Crystallization/Reaction**
   - Observe the formation of a solid with interlocked polyhedra.
4. **Washing/Purification**
   - Isolate by centrifugation and wash as described.
5. **Drying**
   - Follow the same drying protocol as COF‑805.
6. **Post-Treatment**
   - For enhanced framework flexibility, a demetalation step using KCN at 75°C is performed, removing approximately 90% of Cu.

#### • Solvothermal Synthesis: catena-COF-807
1. **Monomer Mixing/Solution Preparation**
   - Similar procedure with [Cu(PDB)₂]BF₄ and tris(4‑aminophenyl)methanol (TAPMol) in a solvent mixture of 1,4‑dioxane and mesitylene, catalyzed by 6 M aqueous acetic acid.
2. **Polymerization/Condensation**
   - Heat at a lower temperature of 85°C for 72 h.
3. **Crystallization, Washing, and Drying**
   - Conduct these steps following the procedure established for COF‑805 to yield interlocked, high‐crystallinity structures.

This detailed protocol, with explicit reagent concentrations, reaction temperatures, durations, and equipment selections, ensures reproducibility and facilitates comparison in synthesis complexity and cost among the three COF systems.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** Crystal structure and phase purity were determined primarily by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), with further validation via Pawley and Rietveld refinements, and supported by electron diffraction.
- **Findings:**  
  - *catena-COF-805*: PXRD patterns revealed a highly crystalline phase distinct from starting materials. Pawley refinement in space group F4132 (a = 54.858(8) Å; Rp = 0.63%, wRp = 1.62%) confirmed excellent agreement with simulated patterns for a doubly interpenetrated bor-y topology (AA stacking), indicating high phase purity.
  - *catena-COF-806*: PXRD (laboratory and synchrotron) and Rietveld refinement confirmed a doubly interpenetrated bor-y topology in space group P4232 (a = 27.854(4) Å; Rp = 0.69%, wRp = 1.24%). 
  - *catena-COF-807*: PXRD patterns matched a cubic space group P4232 (a = 26.1114 Å), with reflection rules (00l: l = 2n) confirming the interlocked 3D catenane structure.
- **Observations:** The close match between experimental and simulated PXRD patterns, low refinement residuals, and distinct peak positions confirm the intended topologies and high crystallinity for all COFs.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition
- **Description:** Elemental composition was assessed by CHN analysis to verify framework stoichiometry.
- **Findings:**  
  - *catena-COF-806*: Calculated (C 68.13%, H 4.50%, N 7.91%); found (C 68.93%, H 4.47%, N 8.09%).
  - *catena-COF-807*: Calculated (C 69.17%, H 4.74%, N 8.01%); found (C 69.58%, H 4.71%, N 7.93%).
- **Observations:** The measured values closely match theoretical predictions, confirming successful synthesis and expected stoichiometry.

#### 6.1.3 Chemical Bonding and Imine Formation
- **Description:** FTIR and solid-state NMR (SSNMR) were used to confirm imine bond formation and the consumption of aldehyde and amine groups.
- **Findings:** FTIR spectra showed disappearance of the –CHO (~1693 cm⁻¹) and –NH₂ (3330–3427 cm⁻¹) bands, while SSNMR revealed loss of aldehyde carbon signals (~190–193 ppm) and emergence of imine carbon signals (154–156 ppm). 2D ^1H–^13C HETCOR confirmed direct imine bond formation.
- **Observations:** Complete imine condensation is evidenced by the absence of residual aldehyde signals and the presence of characteristic imine features.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Morphology and particle size were characterized by SEM and TEM, including high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) and selected area electron diffraction (SAED).
- **Findings:**  
  - *catena-COF-805*: Polyhedron-shaped particles, ~500 nm average size.

  - *catena-COF-807*: Polyhedral crystals, ~3 μm.
- **Observations:** All COFs exhibit well-defined polyhedral morphologies, with size differences reflecting linker and synthesis variations. TEM and HRTEM confirmed the interlocked polyhedral topology.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermal stability was evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA).
- **Findings:** All COFs exhibited decomposition temperatures in the range of 450–500°C.
- **Observations:** The high decomposition temperatures indicate excellent thermal stability, supporting their suitability for practical applications.

#### 6.1.6 Mechanical Properties and Framework Flexibility
- **Description:** Nanoindentation and THF vapor sorption were used to assess mechanical properties and framework flexibility, particularly before and after demetalation.
- **Findings:**  
  - *catena-COF-806*: Elastic modulus of 3.81±0.60 GPa and hardness of 154.7±27.7 MPa (metalated); after demetalation, modulus decreased to 1.41±0.37 GPa and hardness to 55.8±8.7 MPa. Creep depth increased from ~10 nm to ~80 nm.
  - THF vapor sorption: Metalated COF showed Type I isotherm; demetalated COF exhibited a linear isotherm with doubled saturated uptake, indicating increased flexibility.
- **Observations:** Demetalation significantly enhances framework flexibility and guest accommodation, as reflected in both mechanical and sorption measurements.



### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 THF Vapor Adsorption Capacity and Isotherm Behavior

- **Description:** THF vapor adsorption capacity quantifies the amount of tetrahydrofuran (THF) vapor a COF can uptake, typically measured in cm³ g⁻¹ at specified relative pressures (P/P₀). The isotherm profile (e.g., Type I, linear) reflects the framework’s porosity and flexibility.

- **Experimental Data:**
  - **catena-COF-806 (metalated):**
    - At P/P₀ = 0.1: ~27 cm³ g⁻¹ (~13 × 10⁻²² cm³ per unit cell)
    - At P/P₀ = 0.98 (saturated): ~67 cm³ g⁻¹ (~33 × 10⁻²² cm³ per unit cell)
    - Isotherm type: Type I (microporous filling)
  - **catena-COF-806 (demetalated, ~90% Cu removed):**
    - At P/P₀ = 0.98 (saturated): ~132 cm³ g⁻¹ (~58 × 10⁻²² cm³ per unit cell)
    - Isotherm type: Linear (indicative of framework expansion and flexibility)

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The demetalated catena-COF-806 exhibits a saturated THF uptake approximately double that of the metalated form, directly correlating copper removal with enhanced adsorption capacity. The transition from a Type I to a linear isotherm upon demetalation demonstrates increased framework flexibility and guest accommodation. This behavior is attributed to the loss of rigidifying copper(I) ions, allowing the mechanically interlocked polyhedra to expand more freely.

#### 6.2.2 Framework Flexibility and Structural Response

- **Description:** Framework flexibility is assessed by monitoring structural changes (e.g., unit cell expansion, peak shifts in PXRD) upon guest molecule uptake. This property is critical for dynamic adsorption and responsive material behavior.

- **Experimental Data:**
  - **catena-COF-805:** PXRD analysis reveals peak shifts to lower Bragg angles and increased peak splitting as THF content increases, indicating unit cell expansion and framework distortion.
  - **catena-COF-806:** Demetalation leads to decreased crystallinity (PXRD), reduced elastic modulus and hardness (nanoindentation), and a more flexible, expandable framework as evidenced by sorption data.

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  The interlocked polyhedral architecture, templated by copper(I), imparts rigidity and crystallinity. Upon demetalation, the frameworks become highly flexible, enabling significant expansion and increased guest uptake. This dynamic response is most pronounced in catena-COF-806, but similar qualitative behavior is observed in catena-COF-805 and catena-COF-807.

#### 6.2.3 Thermal and Chemical Stability

- **Description:** Stability metrics include decomposition temperature (TGA), insolubility in common solvents, and retention of structural integrity after chemical modification (e.g., demetalation).

- **Experimental Data:**
  - All catena-COFs exhibit decomposition temperatures of ~450–500 °C (TGA).
  - Frameworks remain intact and crystalline after demetalation, as confirmed by TGA, FT-IR, and SSNMR.
  - Insoluble in water and common organic solvents; air-stable.

- **Observations and Trends:**  
  High thermal and chemical stability supports the practical application of these COFs in adsorption and separation processes, ensuring durability under harsh conditions and after postsynthetic modifications.
