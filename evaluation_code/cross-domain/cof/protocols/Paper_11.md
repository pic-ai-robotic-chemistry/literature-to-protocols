## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The synthesis of the amine-linked COFs reported in the article follows a systematic workflow to ensure reproducibility and high crystallinity. Below are the extracted procedures for each COF synthesis method.

#### • Solvothermal Method (e.g., for PI‐3‐COF / rPI‐3‐COF)

1. Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
   - Monomers: 1,3,5‑triformylbenzene and 4,4′,4″‑(1,3,5‑triazine‑2,4,6‑triyl)trianiline.  
   - Solvent System: A 2:1 mixture of mesitylene and 1,4‑dioxane.  
   - Modulator: Aqueous 6 M AcOH is added to the mixture in a round‑bottom flask.

2. Polymerization/Condensation  
   - The modulator (6 M AcOH) initiates the condensation reaction at 120°C, facilitating imine bond formation.  
   - This step is performed immediately after monomer mixing.

3. Crystallization / Reaction  
   - The reaction mixture is heated at 120°C for 72 hours under an inert (Ar) atmosphere to achieve crystallization.  
   - Observations include a change in solution appearance indicating framework formation.

4. Washing / Purification  
   - Post‑reaction, the precipitate is collected by suction filtration and washed with solvents such as DMF, THF, and optionally DCM to remove any unreacted species.

5. Drying  
   - The cleaned material is dried under high vacuum; in some cases, drying is carried out at 80°C (or 60°C for alternative protocols) overnight.

6. Additional Post‑treatments  
   - For conversion to the reduced form (rPI‐3‐COF), 21 equiv of formic acid are added either sequentially or in a one‑pot approach.  
   - The reduction is performed at 120°C for 24–48 hours, enabling the Leuckart–Wallach conversion of imine links into secondary amines.

#### • Variations for TTI‐COF and Py1P‐COF

• The basic steps remain consistent:  
 1. Mixing of the respective aldehyde and amine monomers in a mesitylene/1,4‑dioxane (2:1) system with 6 M AcOH,  
 2. Heating at 120°C for 72 hours to effect condensation and crystallization, and  
 3. Subsequent washing and vacuum drying.  

• For Py1P‐COF, special purification (using metal scavengers) is implemented prior to reduction to prevent palladium contamination.  

This structured approach, including clearly defined steps and stringent reaction conditions, allows for reproducible synthesis of COF materials with high crystallinity and porosity while accommodating both sequential and one‑pot methodologies.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: PI‐3‐COF / rPI‐3‐COF  
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**: Dissolve 22.1 mg of 1,3,5‑triformyl benzene (TFB) and 46.8 mg of 4,4′,4″‑(1,3,5‑triazine‑2,4,6‑triyl)trianiline (TTA) in 10 mL of a 2:1 mixture of mesitylene and 1,4‑dioxane.  
- **Conditions**: Use solvents pre-distilled, degassed by purging with argon for at least 10 minutes. Add 0.5 mL of aqueous 6 M AcOH to maintain the acidic environment.  
- **Process**:  
  - Stir the mixture at room temperature until complete dissolution is achieved.  
  - Transfer the solution into a Teflon-lined autoclave.  
- **Key Steps**: Precise measurement of reagents and stringent solvent degassing are essential for reproducibility.

##### Step 2: COF Assembly and Polymerization  
- **Reagents**: Utilize the precursor solution from Step 1 and add formic acid (approximately 21 equivalents) to initiate the reduction of imine linkages.  
- **Conditions**: Seal the autoclave and heat at 120 °C for 72 hours under an inert (argon) atmosphere to ensure controlled polymerization and crystallization.  
- **Process**:  
  - Under continuous stirring, allow the imine-linked framework to form.  
  - Introduce formic acid and maintain the temperature at 120 °C for an additional 24–48 hours to achieve a complete transformation into secondary amine bonds.  
- **Key Steps**: Careful control of the reaction temperature, acid equivalents, and reaction time is critical to avoid over-reduction.

##### Step 3: Purification, Drying & Activation  
- **Purification Process**:  
  - Collect the precipitate using suction filtration.  
  - Sequentially wash the solid with DMF, THF, and dichloromethane to remove impurities.  
- **Drying & Activation**:  
  - Dry the purified COF under high vacuum.  
  - Optionally, perform supercritical CO₂ drying to enhance porosity.  
- **Key Considerations**: Maintaining consistent stirring during reagent addition and ensuring thorough washing are vital for reproducibility.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation of the COF materials is a critical step to remove residual solvents and guest molecules from the pore system, thereby enhancing the accessible porosity and ensuring framework stability. The article details a multi‐step activation procedure, combining solvent exchange, thermal treatment, and vacuum drying.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature  
• Definition: The activation temperature is essential for driving off entrapped, high‑boiling solvents and “locking in” the porous structure.  
• Experimental Conditions: Two temperature regimes were utilized. In one protocol, the COF sample was heated under autogenous pressure at 170 °C for 3 hours, while another commonly reported condition involved heating the sample at 120 °C for durations between 48 and 72 hours. In addition, a subsequent degassing step was carried out at 120 °C for 12 hours under high vacuum.  
• Observations: Elevated activation temperatures were effective in both consolidating the framework and enhancing porosity; however, the choice of temperature had to be balanced to avoid any structural degradation.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure  
• Definition: The pressure conditions, particularly during vacuum drying, facilitate the efficient removal of residual solvent molecules.  
• Experimental Conditions: Samples were dried under “high vacuum” conditions. Although exact numerical values are not provided, the implementation of vacuum drying is emphasized as key to eliminating solvent residues from the pore network.  
• Observations: The high vacuum drying step was directly linked to improvements in BET surface areas, indicating an effective removal of guest molecules without inducing framework collapse.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Solvent and Solvent Exchange  
• Definition: Sequential solvent exchange is performed to replace high‑boiling original solvents with lower‑boiling ones, thereby reducing the risk of pore collapse during subsequent drying steps.  
• Experimental Conditions: The procedure involved successive washing of the filtered COF precipitate with water, DMF, THF, and DCM (typically 4 × 2 mL each). • Observations: The thorough solvent exchange resulted in retained crystallinity and higher porosity, as confirmed by increased BET surface area values.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time  
• Definition: Adequate activation time ensures complete removal of solvent residues, which is necessary to achieve the desired porous network.  
• Experimental Conditions: Activation durations varied from a short 3‑hour heating period to prolonged treatments (up to 72 hours at 120 °C) and an additional 12‑hour vacuum degassing step.  
• Observations: Extended activation times verified that solvent removal was complete, providing a stable and accessible pore structure for further post‐synthetic modification.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** Crystal structure and phase purity were assessed using X-ray powder diffraction (PXRD/XRPD), with Rietveld refinement for precise cell parameters.
- **Findings:** PI-3-COF exhibited four narrow reflections at 2θ = 5.6°, 9.7°, 11.2°, and 14.9° (indexed as 100, 110, 200, 210; space group P6̅), and a broad stacking reflection at 2θ = 25.6°. Upon reduction to rPI-3-COF, these reflections were retained, but the stacking reflection shifted to 2θ = 25.3°, indicating increased stacking distance. Rietveld analysis revealed an increase in the in-plane unit cell parameter (a = 18.090(7) Å for rPI-3-COF vs. 18.034(7) Å for PI-3-COF) and stacking distance (c = 3.5425(12) Å vs. 3.5058(12) Å). TTI-COF showed a transition from antiparallel slip-stacked (P1) to more eclipsed stacking (P63/m) in rTTI-COF, with in-plane contraction (a = 25.147(9) Å) and interlayer contraction (c = 3.504(2) Å). Py1P-COF displayed sharp reflections at 2θ = 3.7°, 5.4°, 7.5°, 8.5°, and 11.3°, confirming high crystallinity.
- **Observations:** The retention and shifts of characteristic peaks confirm successful conversion from imine to secondary amine linkages, with framework symmetry and stacking modulated by linker flexibility.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Linkage Transformation (FTIR and Solid-State NMR)
- **Description:** FTIR and solid-state NMR (ssNMR) were used to monitor chemical transformations from imine to secondary amine linkages.
- **Findings:** FTIR spectra showed disappearance of the imine (C=N) stretch at ~1630 cm⁻¹ and emergence of secondary amine (N–H) stretches at 3405 cm⁻¹ (rPI-3-COF), 3403 cm⁻¹ (rTTI-COF), and 3398 cm⁻¹ (rPy1P-COF). In ¹³C ssNMR, the imine carbon signal at 155.3 ppm vanished after reduction, replaced by an aliphatic carbon at 45.4 ppm. ¹⁵N ssNMR revealed loss of the imine nitrogen signal (−59.0 ppm) and appearance of secondary amine nitrogen at −313 to −318 ppm.
- **Observations:** These spectral changes provide direct evidence for quantitative conversion to secondary amine linkages.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption isotherms at 77 K and BET analysis were used to determine surface area and porosity.
- **Findings:** rPI-3-COF retained a high BET surface area (1395 m²/g), nearly identical to PI-3-COF (1404 m²/g). rTTI-COF also maintained high surface area (1419 m²/g). In contrast, rPy1P-COF showed a decrease from 1883 m²/g (Py1P-COF) to 1042 m²/g, attributed to increased framework flexibility and local distortions. Pore size distributions remained consistent for rigid frameworks, while functionalization (e.g., with BzCl or TDI) led to reduced N₂ uptake due to partial pore clogging.
- **Observations:** Rigid [3+3] linker frameworks preserve porosity after reduction, while more flexible [4+2] systems show decreased surface area.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Structure
- **Description:** Scanning and transmission electron microscopy (SEM/TEM) were employed to examine particle morphology and nanostructure.
- **Findings:** PI-3-COF and rPI-3-COF exhibited intergrown, coral-shaped particles (600–1000 nm) with stings (~200 nm long, 60 nm wide). TEM confirmed extended porous channels with hexagonal symmetry. rTTI-COF and rPy1P-COF maintained porous, crystalline morphologies post-reduction.
- **Observations:** Uniform particle shapes and visible pore structures corroborate high crystallinity and structural integrity.

#### 6.1.5 Elemental and Purity Analysis
- **Description:** Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) and TEM were used to detect elemental composition and contaminants.
- **Findings:** Palladium contamination was identified in Py1P-COF via TEM-EDX, originating from linker synthesis. Purification with a metal scavenger eliminated Pd, as confirmed by subsequent analyses.
- **Observations:** Effective purification is critical for framework integrity and reproducibility.

#### 6.1.6 Structural Stability and Order
- **Description:** Pair distribution function (PDF) analysis and post-synthetic stability assessments were conducted.
- **Findings:** PDF analysis confirmed medium- and long-range order in rPI-3-COF, consistent with stacked layers and preserved pore channels. Amine-linked COFs demonstrated enhanced hydrolytic stability compared to imine-linked analogues.
- **Observations:** The frameworks retain crystallinity and porosity after reduction and functionalization, supporting their suitability for further applications.



### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Gas Uptake Capacity and Surface Area

- **Description:** Gas uptake capacity and BET surface area are critical indicators of a COF’s porosity and suitability for applications such as gas storage and separation. Nitrogen adsorption isotherms at 77 K were used to determine these metrics, with BET surface area (in m²/g) calculated using multipoint analysis.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **rPI-3-COF:** BET surface area of 1395 m²/g (activated sample); one-pot synthesized rPI-3-COF showed a lower BET surface area of 174 m²/g due to increased disorder and pore blockage.
  - **PI-3-COF:** BET surface area of 1404 m²/g.
  - **rTTI-COF:** BET surface area of 1419 m²/g, indicating full retention of porosity after reduction.
  - **Py1P-COF:** BET surface area of 1883 m²/g; **rPy1P-COF:** reduced to 1042 m²/g post-reduction, attributed to increased linker flexibility and framework distortion.
- **Observations and Trends:** High BET surface areas and nitrogen uptake volumes were retained in COFs with rigid linkers (e.g., rTTI-COF), while frameworks with more flexible linkers (e.g., rPy1P-COF) exhibited moderate decreases in surface area. Proper activation (e.g., scCO₂ extraction) was essential for maximizing accessible porosity.

#### 6.2.2 Pore Size Distribution and Channel Preservation

- **Description:** Pore size distribution, determined from nitrogen adsorption isotherms using the QSDFT model, reflects the uniformity and accessibility of the COF’s internal channels, which are vital for molecular transport and separation.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **rPI-3-COF and PI-3-COF:** Both exhibited well-defined pore size distributions and extended porous channels of hexagonal symmetry, as confirmed by TEM and PDF analysis.
  - **rTTI-COF:** Maintained a defined pore size distribution post-reduction and after postsynthetic functionalization, though functionalization (e.g., with benzoyl chloride or toluene diisocyanate) led to decreased nitrogen uptake due to partial pore clogging.
- **Observations and Trends:** The preservation of pore channels and uniform pore size distribution was directly correlated with high crystallinity and structural integrity, supporting the COFs’ suitability for separation and storage applications.

#### 6.2.3 Hydrolytic Stability

- **Description:** Hydrolytic stability assesses the COF’s resistance to decomposition under acidic or aqueous conditions, a key requirement for practical deployment.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **rPI-3-COF:** Retained crystallinity and porosity after exposure to acidic conditions, as evidenced by unchanged or only slightly broadened XRPD reflections.
- **Observations and Trends:** The conversion to secondary amine linkages significantly enhanced hydrolytic stability, with no observable decomposition under harsh conditions, making these COFs robust for demanding environments.

#### 6.2.4 Structural Integrity and Functionalization Potential

- **Description:** Structural integrity post-reduction and after postsynthetic modification is crucial for maintaining performance. The presence of reactive secondary amine sites enables further functionalization.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **rTTI-COF:** Retained high crystallinity and large surface area after functionalization, as shown by sharp XRPD peaks and minimal loss in porosity.
  - **rPy1P-COF:** Maintained crystalline morphology and accessible amine sites, with careful purification required to avoid over-reduction.
- **Observations and Trends:** The frameworks’ ability to undergo postsynthetic modification without significant loss of porosity or crystallinity highlights their versatility for tailored applications.

The materials’ robust structural integrity and functionalization potential position them as promising candidates for gas storage, separation, and further application-specific modifications.
