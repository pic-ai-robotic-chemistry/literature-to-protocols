## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

#### Mechanochemical Synthesis (MC) of COFs
• Step 1: Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
 – Combine 1,3,5‐Triformylphloroglucinol (Tp) with the selected aromatic diamine (e.g., p‐phenylenediamine for TpPa-1 or its methylated derivative for TpPa-2) in a mortar or ball-milling vessel.  
 – Add a controlled amount of a mesitylene/dioxane mixture (1:1 ratio) to facilitate liquid-assisted grinding.

• Step 2: Polymerization/Condensation  
 – Initiate the Schiff base condensation reaction by grinding the mixture.  
 – Using a Retsch MM400 ball mill operating at 25 Hz for 45 minutes or manual grinding, the reaction is monitored via color changes—from a light yellow powder at 5 minutes to an orange hue at 15 minutes, and finally to a dark red powder at 40 minutes, indicating complete conversion.

• Step 3: Crystallization / Reaction  
 – Crystallization occurs concurrently with the condensation process during prolonged grinding periods.

• Step 4: Washing / Purification  
 – Wash the obtained powder repeatedly (typically 5–6 times) with anhydrous acetone and dichloromethane to remove unreacted monomers and oligomeric impurities.  
 – In some cases, additional solvent exchange (e.g., with DMF) may be employed to further purify the sample.

• Step 5: Drying  
 – Dry the COF product under vacuum at 180 °C for 24 hours to ensure removal of residual solvents and stabilization of the framework.

• Step 6: Post-treatment  
 – Final activation or functionalization steps are applied if necessary to enhance porosity or chemical stability.

#### Solvothermal Synthesis (ST) of COFs  
• Step 1: Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation  
 – Dissolve Tp and the aromatic diamine in a suitable solvent medium.  
• Step 2: Polymerization/Condensation  
 – Initiate the condensation reaction, monitored by a change in solution color.  
• Step 3: Crystallization / Reaction  
 – Transfer the mixture to a Pyrex tube and heat at 120 °C for 72 hours using 3 M aqueous acetic acid as the catalyst/modulator.  
• Steps 4–6: Washing, Drying, and Post-treatment  
 – Wash extensively with DMF and acetone, then dry under conditions that preserve crystallinity, ensuring reproducible and high-quality COF formation.

Both routes are detailed with precise operational parameters, enabling direct replication and comparative analysis of synthesis complexity, scalability, and equipment requirements.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: TpPa-1

##### Step [1]: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**: 1,3,5‑triformylphloroglucinol (0.30 mmol) and p‑phenylenediamine (0.45 mmol) are accurately measured. Use 1–2 drops of a mesitylene/dioxane (1:1) mixture as a liquid additive during the mixing.  
- **Conditions**: Manual grinding at ambient temperature with a mortar and pestle, or using a Retsch MM400 ball mill operating at 25 Hz for 45 minutes.  
- **Process**:  
  - Grind the Tp and Pa‑1 together. The initial 5-minute grinding forms a light yellow powder, which transitions to orange by 15 minutes and becomes dark red by 40 minutes, indicating full precursor integration and onset of polymerization.  
- **Key Steps**: Monitor the color change as an in situ indicator of reaction progress.

##### Step [2]: COF Assembly  
- **Reagents**: The pre-mixed Tp and Pa‑1 are used directly. In the solvothermal route, the addition of 3 M aqueous acetic acid (serving as a catalyst/modulator) is included.  
- **Conditions**:  
  - For mechanochemical synthesis, continue with room-temperature conditions.  
  - For solvothermal synthesis, sonicate the mixture for 10 minutes, perform flash freezing at 77 K and degassing by three freeze–pump–thaw cycles, then seal in a Pyrex tube and heat at 120 °C for 72 hours.  
- **Process**: Follow a strict chronological order to ensure complete condensation of precursors into the TpPa‑1 framework.

##### Step [3]: Purification  
- **Process**: Recover the precipitate via filtration or centrifugation; wash multiple times (5–6 cycles) with anhydrous acetone and dichloromethane to remove unreacted materials.

##### Step [4]: Drying & Activation  
- **Conditions**: Dry the final product at 180 °C under vacuum for 24 hours.  
- **Activation**: Engage in solvent exchange activation at 170 °C for 12 hours under high vacuum to render the pores guest-free.

Grind for 45 minutes (or use a ball mill at 25 Hz), then purify, dry, and activate as detailed above. ## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation procedure for the COFs was designed to remove residual solvents and guest molecules efficiently, thereby enhancing the material’s porosity and framework stability before application testing.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature  
Activation temperature is a critical parameter for evacuating entrapped solvent molecules from the COF pores. In this procedure, the COFs were thermally activated by drying at 180 °C under vacuum for 24 hours. This elevated temperature not only promotes thorough solvent evaporation but also preserves the intrinsic stability of the framework, ensuring that the material remains chemically and thermally robust.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure  
Vacuum drying is employed to minimize solvent re-adsorption during the high-temperature treatment. Although specific pressure values were not explicitly reported, the use of “high vacuum” conditions was integral to the process. These conditions enhanced the efficiency of solvent removal, facilitating the creation of guest-free pores in the COF structure.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Solvent  
Prior to thermal activation, solvent exchange is performed to replace high-boiling and less volatile solvents that could compromise the porosity during drying. The COF powders were washed with anhydrous acetone, and in some instances dichloromethane was also employed, through 5–6 repetitive cycles. This systematic washing helps ensure that any unreacted material or impurities are adequately removed, thereby preventing potential pore collapse during subsequent heating.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time  
The activation time of 24 hours under vacuum conditions was chosen to guarantee complete removal of residual guest molecules. This extended period allows enough time for all solvents to be driven off, resulting in well-defined, open pore channels without degrading the overall framework structure.

In summary, these activation steps—combining solvent exchange, high-vacuum drying, and precise thermal treatment—were instrumental in achieving COF materials with clean, guest-free pores and stable, porous frameworks suitable for reliable gas adsorption performance.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** The crystal structure and phase purity of the COFs were assessed using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), with structural models validated by comparison to simulated patterns.
- **Findings:** For TpPa-1 and TpPa-2, PXRD patterns display a prominent first 2θ peak at 4.7°, corresponding to the (100) reflection plane, and a broad peak at ~27° attributed to π–π stacking between layers ((001) plane). TpBD exhibits a first 2θ peak at 3.3°, also indicative of the (100) plane. All experimental PXRD patterns closely match simulated eclipsed stacking models, confirming high crystallinity and phase purity. The d-spacing for the (001) plane is ~3.4 Å for TpPa-1 and TpPa-2, and 3.25 Å for TpBD.
- **Observations:** The retention of peak positions and intensities after exposure to boiling water, strong acid (9 N HCl), and base (3 N NaOH) demonstrates exceptional structural robustness.

#### 6.1.2 Spectroscopic Confirmation of Framework Formation
- **Description:** FTIR, Raman, and solid-state ^13C NMR spectroscopy were employed to confirm the formation of the COF bonding network and the chemical environment of key functional groups.
- **Findings:** FTIR spectra for all COFs show the disappearance of the diamine N–H stretch (3100–3300 cm⁻¹) and the emergence of a keto-form C=C bond at 1582 cm⁻¹. TpPa-2 displays an additional methyl C–H stretch at 2885 cm⁻¹. TpBD exhibits characteristic bands at 1594, 1579, and 1464 cm⁻¹, confirming bond formation. Raman spectra (514.5 nm excitation) reveal consistent peak positions between mechanochemical and solvothermal samples, with no peaks for monomer references. ^13C NMR shows a carbonyl signal at ~180 ppm, confirming the keto form and complete conversion.
- **Observations:** The combined spectroscopic data confirm successful network formation and identical local structures across synthesis methods.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition
- **Description:** Elemental analysis (CHN) was conducted to verify the incorporation of carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen into the COF frameworks.
- **Findings:** The measured elemental ratios align with calculated values for the expected COF formulas, confirming the intended composition.
- **Observations:** The data support the successful integration of monomer units into the extended frameworks.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen gas adsorption measurements were used to determine BET surface area and pore characteristics.
- **Findings:** Mechanochemically synthesized COFs exhibit lower BET surface areas compared to solvothermal analogues: TpPa-1 (MC) 61 m²/g vs. (ST) 535 m²/g; TpPa-2 (MC) 56 m²/g vs. (ST) 339 m²/g; TpBD (MC) 35 m²/g vs. (ST) 537 m²/g. TpBD (MC) displays a mesoporous character with a pore size distribution of 1.0–1.7 nm.
- **Observations:** The reduced surface area in MC samples is attributed to partial exfoliation and morphological differences.

#### 6.1.5 Morphology and Microstructure
- **Description:** Scanning and transmission electron microscopy (SEM/TEM) were used to examine particle morphology and microstructure.
- **Findings:** TpPa-1 and TpPa-2 (MC) show agglomerated, spherical particles (5–7 μm) composed of layered sheets. TpBD (MC) exhibits a unique flower-like morphology with well-dispersed, exfoliated graphene-sheet–like layers.
- **Observations:** Mechanochemical synthesis induces pronounced exfoliation, resulting in distinct layered morphologies not observed in solvothermal samples.

#### 6.1.6 Thermal and Chemical Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and chemical stability tests were performed to assess framework robustness.
- **Findings:** All COFs are thermally stable up to ~350 °C, with TpBD (MC) showing only 28% weight loss up to 800 °C. PXRD and FTIR analyses after boiling water, acid, and base treatments confirm retention of crystallinity and framework integrity.
- **Observations:** The COFs, particularly TpBD, demonstrate outstanding thermal and chemical stability, attributed to irreversible enol-to-keto tautomerism within the frameworks.



### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 Gas Adsorption Capacity

- **Description:** Gas adsorption capacity quantifies the amount of gas a COF can adsorb per unit mass, typically measured in cm³/g or wt%. This metric is critical for evaluating the suitability of COFs for storage and separation applications. Measurements were performed using volumetric adsorption analyzers under controlled temperature and pressure, following activation to ensure guest-free pores.

- **Experimental Data:**
  - **Hydrogen (H₂) Uptake at 77 K:**
    - TpPa-1 (solvothermal): 1.1 wt%
    - TpPa-2 (solvothermal): 0.89 wt%
    - TpBD (solvothermal): 0.7 wt%
  - **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) Uptake at 273 K:**
    - TpPa-1: 78 cm³/g

- **Observations and Trends:** Solvothermally synthesized COFs consistently exhibited higher gas uptake capacities than their mechanochemically synthesized counterparts, correlating with their higher BET surface areas (TpBD: 537 m²/g, TpPa-1: 535 m²/g, TpPa-2: 339 m²/g). Among all samples, TpBD demonstrated the highest water vapor uptake, while TpPa-1 showed the highest H₂ and CO₂ uptakes.

#### 6.2.2 Surface Area and Porosity

- **Description:** The Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) surface area, measured in m²/g via N₂ adsorption at 77 K, reflects the accessible internal surface of the COF, directly impacting adsorption performance.

- **Experimental Data:**
  - TpPa-1 (MC): 61 m²/g
  - TpPa-2 (MC): 56 m²/g
  - TpBD (MC): 35 m²/g
  - TpPa-1 (solvothermal): 535 m²/g
  - TpPa-2 (solvothermal): 339 m²/g
  - TpBD (solvothermal): 537 m²/g

- **Observations and Trends:** Mechanochemically synthesized COFs exhibited lower BET surface areas due to their graphene-like layered morphology and possible pore blockage, resulting in reduced gas uptake. Solvothermal samples, with higher surface areas, demonstrated superior adsorption capacities.

#### 6.2.3 Thermal and Chemical Stability

- **Description:** Stability was assessed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) under N₂ atmosphere (30–800 °C, 5 °C/min) and by exposure to harsh chemical environments (boiling water, strong acid/base).

- **Experimental Data:**
  - All COFs stable up to ~350 °C.
  - Weight loss up to 800 °C: 45–60% for most COFs; TpBD (MC) showed only 28% loss.
  - Retained crystallinity after 7 days in boiling water and 9 N HCl; TpBD and TpBD (MC) stable in 3 N NaOH for ~3 days.

- **Observations and Trends:** All COFs demonstrated excellent thermal and chemical stability, with TpBD (MC) exhibiting the highest resistance to thermal decomposition and chemical attack, attributed to robust framework formation and irreversible enol-to-keto tautomerism.

#### 6.2.4 Morphology–Performance Correlation

- **Description:** Morphological features, as revealed by SEM/TEM, influence adsorption behavior by affecting layer accessibility and pore structure.

- **Experimental Data:** Mechanochemically synthesized COFs displayed exfoliated, graphene-like layered morphologies, while solvothermal samples showed more ordered stacking.

- **Observations and Trends:** The unique morphology of MC COFs, particularly TpBD (MC), may facilitate water vapor adsorption despite lower overall surface area, highlighting the interplay between structure and function in COF performance.
