   - The product is collected by suction filtration and washed multiple times with tetrahydrofuran (THF).
5. **Drying**  
   - Final drying is carried out under vacuum at 120 °C to ensure complete solvent removal.
6. **Post-Treatment**  
   - Soxhlet extraction with THF may be employed for additional purification.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
The crystal structure and phase purity of the COFs were determined by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) using a Bruker D8 Focus diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (40 kV, 40 mA) at room temperature. Samples were deposited on PMMA holders and scanned from 2θ = 1.5° to 30° with a 0.01° step size. For Hz-TP-BT-COF, distinct peaks at 2.64°, 4.56°, 5.28°, 7.00°, 7.94°, 10.64°, and 25.08° correspond to the (100), (110), (200), (210), (300), (310), and (001) facets, respectively. These patterns matched simulated sync AA stacking models, confirming high crystallinity, phase purity, and the presence of dual pore sizes. Similar PXRD analyses for Im-TP-BT-COF and sp² c-TP-BT-COF also confirmed their respective crystalline frameworks and stacking modes.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Structure and Linkage Verification
Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and solid-state ^13C CP/MAS NMR were employed to confirm the formation of specific linkages. In Hz-TP-BT-COF, FTIR spectra displayed characteristic N–H, C=O, and C=N stretching vibrations, indicative of hydrazone linkages. For Im-TP-BT-COF, the disappearance of the aldehyde C=O band and the emergence of a C=N stretch confirmed imine bond formation. Solid-state NMR further corroborated the presence of hydrazone, imine, and vinylene linkages in the respective COFs, with distinct carbon signals assigned to knots and linkers.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
Nitrogen sorption isotherms at 77 K were measured using a Micromeritics 3Flex analyser. Samples were pretreated under vacuum at 120 °C for 2 hours. The Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) method and non-local density functional theory (NLDFT) were used for data analysis. Hz-TP-BT-COF exhibited a high BET surface area of 851 m²/g, a pore volume of 0.33 cm³/g, and dual micropore sizes (1.2–1.9 nm). Im-TP-BT-COF and sp² c-TP-BT-COF showed lower surface areas (360 m²/g and 330 m²/g, respectively) and smaller pore volumes (0.17 cm³/g and 0.15 cm³/g). These results confirm the successful formation of microporous frameworks with accessible channels.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology
Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) was performed using a JEOL JSM-6701F at 5.0 kV. Samples were dispersed in isopropanol, drop-cast onto silica wafers, and gold-coated. Hz-TP-BT-COF displayed uniform micrometre-scale rod-like morphologies, while Im-TP-BT-COF and sp² c-TP-BT-COF exhibited rod-like objects assembled from flakes. These morphological features reflect controlled nucleation and growth during synthesis.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was conducted on a TA Instruments Q500, heating samples from 40 °C to 800 °C at 10 °C/min under nitrogen. All COFs demonstrated high thermal stability, with negligible weight loss below 350 °C. The onset of significant decomposition occurred above this temperature, validating their robustness for practical applications.

#### 6.1.6 Elemental Composition
Elemental (CHN) analysis was performed using a Thermo Fisher Scientific FlashSmart CHNS Elemental Analyser. The measured carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen contents were consistent with the calculated values for the proposed COF structures, confirming the expected stoichiometry and purity.
