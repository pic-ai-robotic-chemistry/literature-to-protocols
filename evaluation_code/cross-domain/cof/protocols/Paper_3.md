## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

This section details the synthesis of COF-A, COF-B, and COF-AB-c via a room‑temperature condensation “General Procedure A” carried out in an aqueous medium. The method is designed for reproducibility and high crystallinity, though it requires precise control of reagent ratios and pH.

#### • Monomer Mixing / Solution Preparation
1. Dissolve the aldehyde linkers and tetrakis(4‑aminophenyl)methane in water:
   - COF-A: Mix 1,4‑phthalaldehyde (0.2 mmol, 26.8 mg) with tetrakis(4‑aminophenyl)methane.
   - COF-B: Mix 2,5‑dihydroxyterephthalaldehyde (0.1 mmol, 16.6 mg) with tetrakis(4‑aminophenyl)methane.
   - COF‑AB‑c: Combine 1,4‑phthalaldehyde (0.1 mmol, 13.4 mg) with 2,5‑dihydroxyterephthalaldehyde (0.1 mmol, 16.6 mg) and tetrakis(4‑aminophenyl)methane.
   - Initially, sodium hydroxide is added to adjust the pH, followed by hydrochloric acid for neutralization.

#### • Polymerization/Condensation
2. Introduce p‑toluenesulfonic acid (PTSA; 2 mol l⁻¹) to protonate the amine groups, promoting imine bond formation.  
   - This step immediately follows monomer mixing and is essential for initiating the condensation reaction.

#### • Crystallization / Reaction
3. Transfer the reaction mixture into a Petri dish and maintain at 50 °C for 7 days.
   - Under these controlled conditions, in situ crystallization occurs as the framework forms, ensuring uniformity and single‑crystal growth.
   - Observations during this phase include gradual precipitate formation, indicating the progress of the reaction.

#### • Washing / Purification
4. Post‑reaction, collect the COF products via filtration and wash them thoroughly:
   - Perform three successive washes using water and tetrahydrofuran to remove unreacted components and impurities.

#### • Drying and Post‑Treatment
5. Dry the washed products in two stages:
   - Initially at ambient temperature for 12 hours, then at 100 °C for an additional 12 hours.
   - These drying steps consolidate the framework structure and remove residual solvents.

• Comparative Analysis:  
The room‑temperature condensation method offers straightforward equipment requirements (e.g., a simple Petri dish) and mild reaction conditions, which enhance cost-effectiveness and scalability. However, the necessity for precise pH control and accurate reagent concentrations poses challenges in reproducibility, making rigorous adherence to the detailed protocol essential for consistent crystal quality.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### COF Preparation [1]: COF-A

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  • 1,4‑phthalaldehyde (53.65 mg, 0.4 mmol)  
  • Tetrakis(4‑aminophenyl)methane (76 mg, 0.2 mmol)  
  • C16-GlyA (251 mg, 0.8 mmol)  
  • Sodium hydroxide (32 mg, 0.8 mmol)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Dissolve C16-GlyA and sodium hydroxide in 80 mL of water  
  • Adjust the pH with 4.0 mL of 0.2 mol L⁻¹ hydrochloric acid  
  • Stir for over 10 minutes at ambient conditions  
- **Process**:  
  • Dissolution of the primary reagent mixture ensures complete solvation and uniform pH adjustment.  
  • Next, add a solution of tetrakis(4‑aminophenyl)methane prepared in 8 mL of 0.1 mol L⁻¹ PTSA to protonate the amine groups effectively.  
  • Finally, introduce 1,4‑phthalaldehyde and stir the complete mixture at 50 °C for 5 minutes, ensuring thorough chemical integration.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  • Careful pH control and sequential addition are critical to achieve full precursor reaction.

##### Step 2: COF Assembly and Polymerization  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Transfer the reaction mixture to a Teflon-lined autoclave  
  • Maintain at 50 °C for 7 days
- **Process**:  
  • Monitor imine bond formation using periodic PXRD and in-process checks to validate crystallinity and conversion.

##### Step 3: Purification, Drying & Activation  
- **Purification**:  
  • Recover the product by filtration and wash three times using water followed by tetrahydrofuran to remove unreacted species and by-products.
- **Drying**:  
  • Dry first at ambient temperature for 12 hours, then at 100 °C for another 12 hours.
- **Activation**:  
  • Pre-activate by heating the dry product at 373 K under vacuum for 10 hours to enhance pore development.

#### COF Preparation [2]: COF‑B and COF‑AB‑c

For COF‑B, substitute 1,4‑phthalaldehyde with 2,5‑dihydroxyterephthalaldehyde, maintaining identical stirring, temperature, and reaction durations. For the bicomponent COF‑AB‑c, blend linker‑A and linker‑B at a molar ratio close to 1:0.83 and incorporate palmitoylglycine-regulated micelles to guide crystallization and single-crystal growth. The purification, drying, and activation protocols remain consistent to ensure reproducibility across COF systems.

This detailed workflow—from precise precursor mixing through controlled COF assembly and rigorous post-processing—ensures reproducible, high-quality imine-linked frameworks suitable for advanced applications.

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation process for the COF materials is a critical step in removing residual solvents and guest molecules, thereby “opening up” the pore structure while preserving framework integrity. The procedure integrates solvent exchange, controlled thermal treatment, and vacuum drying under predefined durations.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature is essential for driving off residual solvents, ensuring that the pore channels are unobstructed and the framework is stable.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Following synthesis, the COF powders are initially dried at ambient temperature for 12 hours. This is followed by a thermal treatment at 100 °C for an additional 12 hours. Additionally, for pre‑application preparation (e.g., prior to breakthrough experiments), the samples undergo vacuum activation at 373 K (≈100 °C) for 10 hours.
- **Observations:** These temperature conditions effectively enhance porosity while preserving the crystallinity of the COFs, as confirmed by sharp PXRD reflections.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** Applying a vacuum during activation facilitates the removal of residual solvents that may otherwise be retained under atmospheric conditions.
- **Experimental Conditions:** In the pre‑application activation phase, the COF samples are subjected to vacuum conditions during the 10‑hour activation at 373 K. Although the exact vacuum pressure is not specified, this setup is sufficient to evacuate solvent molecules.
- **Observations:** The approach yields efficient solvent removal without imposing excessive stress on the framework.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Solvent
- **Definition:** Solvent exchange is performed to replace high‑boiling synthesis solvents with more volatile ones, thus minimizing the risk of pore collapse during drying.
- **Experimental Conditions:** After synthesis, the COFs are thoroughly washed three times with water and tetrahydrofuran. This washing step ensures that residual, potentially occluding, high‑boiling-point solvents are replaced.
- **Observations:** The solvent exchange procedure has been observed to improve framework crystallinity and maximize accessible porosity.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time
- **Definition:** The duration of each activation stage is critical to ensure complete guest removal while preventing framework degradation.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The drying protocol includes a 12‑hour period at room temperature, followed by 12 hours at 100 °C. For samples prepared for gas separation testing, a further activation stage consists of 10 hours under vacuum at 373 K, with a subsequent 40‑minute helium purge during regeneration.
- **Observations:** These carefully timed intervals are instrumental in maintaining consistent performance and structural stability of the COF materials.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
The crystal structures and phase purities of COF-A, COF-B, and COF-AB-c were rigorously evaluated using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) and continuous rotation electron diffraction (cRED). PXRD measurements, performed on a PANalytical Empyrean diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å, 45 kV, 40 mA), revealed that COF-A and COF-B exhibit pronounced Bragg peaks matching simulated patterns, confirming their high crystallinity and phase purity. For COF-AB-c, PXRD patterns displayed multiple sharp peaks distinct from those of COF-A and COF-B, which could be indexed to the tetragonal space group I41/a. Le Bail refinement yielded unit cell parameters (a = 20.05 Å, c = 8.92 Å), and cRED via TEM further confirmed a single-phase structure with a = b = 19.92 Å, c = 8.64 Å, and cell volume of 3428.4 Å³. The absence of peak overlap with parent COFs and the excellent fit to simulated patterns substantiate the formation of a new, homogeneous crystalline phase.

#### 6.1.2 Chemical Connectivity and Imine Formation
Solid-state ^13C CP/MAS NMR spectroscopy was employed to verify the chemical connectivity and imine bond formation in all COFs. The spectra for COF-A, COF-B, and COF-AB-c displayed characteristic resonances for the node, linkers, and imine carbons (e.g., 65.0, 134.0, 153.0, and 158.0 ppm for COF-AB-c). Notably, signals corresponding to free aldehyde (201, 196 ppm) and amine (129 ppm) groups were absent, indicating complete conversion to imine linkages and the absence of unreacted starting materials.

#### 6.1.3 Homogeneity and Component Distribution
The compositional homogeneity of COF-AB-c was confirmed by confocal Raman spectroscopy and ^1H NMR analysis of DCl-digested crystals. Raman spectra collected from multiple regions of single crystals showed no significant variation in band positions or intensities, demonstrating a uniform distribution of both linker-A and linker-B throughout the framework. ^1H NMR further established the linker ratio at approximately 1:0.83, consistent with the intended stoichiometry.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
Morphological analysis using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed that COF-AB-c forms uniform cuboidal single crystals with typical dimensions of ~6.5 μm in length and ~0.5 μm in width and height. Similar cuboid morphologies were observed for related multicomponent COFs. While explicit morphology data for COF-A and COF-B were not detailed, COF-AB-c’s well-defined single-crystal nature was emphasized.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) demonstrated robust thermal stability for all COFs. COF-A exhibited decomposition at approximately 539 °C, COF-B at 480 °C, and COF-AB-c at 490 °C, as determined by the peak decomposition rate in differential thermogravimetric curves. The single decomposition event for COF-AB-c, intermediate between the parent COFs, further supports its homogeneous composition.

#### 6.1.6 Porosity and Surface Area
Porosity and surface area were assessed by N₂ adsorption isotherms at 77 K using a Quantachrome autosorb analyzer, with BET calculations applied. COF-AB-c displayed a high surface area and uniform pore size distribution, as determined by both N₂ and CO₂ adsorption measurements, supporting the successful formation of an open, accessible framework suitable for gas adsorption applications.

### 6.2 Application/Performance

#### 6.2.1 SO₂ Gas Uptake Capacity
- **Description:** SO₂ uptake capacity quantifies the volume of sulfur dioxide adsorbed per gram of COF material under specified conditions, serving as a primary indicator of adsorption performance. Uptake is measured volumetrically at 298 K and 0.002 bar using a corrosive gas adsorption analyzer.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - COF-A: 3.0 cm³ g⁻¹
  - COF-B: 1.0 cm³ g⁻¹
  - COF-AB-c: 22 cm³ g⁻¹
- **Observations and Trends:** COF-AB-c demonstrates a dramatic enhancement in SO₂ uptake—733% higher than COF-A and 2200% higher than COF-B. This improvement is attributed to the synergistic effect of mixed linkers and the resulting optimized pore structure.

#### 6.2.2 SO₂/CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** Selectivity is defined as the preferential adsorption of SO₂ over CO₂, calculated using the ideal adsorbed solution theory (IAST) based on single-component isotherms. It is a critical metric for evaluating separation efficiency in mixed-gas environments.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - COF-AB-c: SO₂/CO₂ selectivity ranges from 1230 (10:90 SO₂/CO₂ mixture) to 4247 (1:99 mixture)
- **Observations and Trends:** The selectivity values for COF-AB-c far exceed previous benchmarks (e.g., Mg-gallate at 325), indicating exceptional separation capability. This is ascribed to the tailored pore environment and uniform distribution of linkers.

#### 6.2.3 Dynamic Breakthrough Performance
- **Description:** Dynamic breakthrough experiments assess the real-time retention of SO₂ in a fixed-bed column under simulated flue gas conditions. Retention time (s g⁻¹) reflects the duration before SO₂ appears in the effluent, normalized by adsorbent mass.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - COF-AB-c: 14,037 s g⁻¹ (233.95 min g⁻¹)
  - COF-A: 1,020 s g⁻¹ (17 min g⁻¹)
  - COF-B: 1,080 s g⁻¹ (18 min g⁻¹)
- **Observations and Trends:** COF-AB-c achieves a 1300% increase in retention time over the pure COFs, confirming its superior performance in practical separation scenarios.

#### 6.2.4 Regenerability and Stability
- **Description:** Regenerability evaluates the ability of the COF to maintain adsorption performance after repeated cycles of SO₂ capture and desorption. Stability is assessed via thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and multi-cycle breakthrough tests.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - COF-AB-c: Complete SO₂ and CO₂ desorption achieved by heating at 373 K under helium flow (40 mL min⁻¹) for 40 min; SO₂ capture remains unchanged over six cycles; TGA confirms robust thermal stability.
- **Observations and Trends:** The COF-AB-c framework retains structural integrity and adsorption capacity after multiple cycles, with SO₂ purity upon recovery reaching 98%. This demonstrates its suitability for industrial desulfurization applications.

#### 6.2.5 Isosteric Heat of Adsorption (Qₛₜ)
- **Description:** The isosteric heat of adsorption (Qₛₜ) reflects the strength of interaction between SO₂ molecules and the COF, calculated from adsorption isotherms.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - COF-AB-c: 191.98 kJ mol⁻¹
  - COF-A: 143.75 kJ mol⁻¹
  - COF-B: 152.55 kJ mol⁻¹
- **Observations and Trends:** The higher Qₛₜ for COF-AB-c indicates stronger binding affinity for SO₂, correlating with its superior uptake and selectivity.
