## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

- **Overview of Catalyst Synthesis Techniques**  
  The article describes a multi-step synthesis approach for iron-based Fischer–Tropsch catalysts, including hydrothermal synthesis of Fe₂O₃ nanoparticles, deposition-precipitation of Mn promoter, SiO₂ shell coating via a modified Stöber method, and hydrophobic surface modification using chlorotrimethylsilane. The methods allow precise control over shell thickness and surface properties.  
  - **Advantages:**  
    - Hydrothermal synthesis yields uniform Fe₂O₃ nanoparticles with controlled morphology.
    - Deposition-precipitation enables homogeneous Mn promoter distribution.
    - Stöber method allows tunable SiO₂ shell thickness for core-shell structures.
    - Surface hydrophobization improves catalyst stability and selectivity.
  - **Disadvantages:**  
    - Multi-step process increases synthesis complexity and time.
    - Use of organic solvents and surfactants may increase cost and require additional purification.
    - Precise control of shell thickness and hydrophobicity requires careful optimization.
    - Potential for residual chlorine contamination during hydrophobic modification.

---

#### Hydrothermal Synthesis of Fe₂O₃ Nanoparticles

1. **Precursor Mixing:**
   - Dissolve 7.5 g FeCl₃·6H₂O in 110 mL deionized water.
   - Add 12.4 g sodium acetate trihydrate and 3.8 g hexadecyl trimethyl ammonium bromide.
   - Stir for 0.5 h.
   - Add 26 mL ethylenediamine.
   - Stir for 1 h.

2. **Hydrothermal Treatment:**
   - Transfer mixture to Teflon-lined autoclave.
   - Heat at 200 °C for 10 h.

3. **Purification:**
   - Wash product with water and ethanol.
   - Dry at 100 °C for 12 h.

4. **Calcination:**
   - Calcine at 500 °C for 5 h.

---

#### Deposition-Precipitation of Mn Promoter on Fe₂O₃

1. **Precursor Mixing:**
   - Disperse 0.5 g Fe₂O₃ in 300 mL ethanol by ultrasonication.

2. **Promoter Addition:**
   - Add 5 mL aqueous solution containing 0.078 g KMnO₄ dropwise using a peristaltic pump.

3. **Aging:**
   - Stir at 30 °C for 2 h.

4. **Purification:**
   - Collect product by centrifugation.
   - Wash with water.
   - Dry at 100 °C for 12 h.

---

#### SiO₂ Shell Coating via Modified Stöber Method

1. **Precursor Mixing:**
   - Disperse 0.5 g Fe@Mn in 150 mL ethanol by ultrasonication.

2. **TEOS Addition:**
   - Add x mL (x = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.8) TEOS.

3. **Aging:**
   - Stir at 30 °C for 3 h.

4. **pH Adjustment:**
   - Add 2.5 mL ammonia solution (25–28%) and 10 mL water.

5. **Further Stirring:**
   - Stir at 30 °C for 4 h.

6. **Purification:**
   - Collect by centrifugation.
   - Wash with ethanol.
   - Dry at 100 °C for 12 h.

---

#### Hydrophobic Surface Modification

1. **Pre-treatment:**
   - Dry 0.5 g Fe@Mn@xSi in vacuum oven at 120 °C for 10 h.

2. **Surface Modification:**
   - Cool in vacuum oven.
   - Add 70 mL n-hexane.
   - Add 0.5 mL chlorotrimethylsilane.
   - Ultrasonically treat at room temperature for 1 h.

3. **Purification:**
   - Centrifuge.
   - Wash with n-hexane.
   - Dry in vacuum oven at 80 °C for 6 h.

---

#### Hydrophobic Modification of Fe@Mn (without SiO₂ shell)

1. **Pre-treatment:**
   - Dry 0.5 g Fe@Mn in vacuum oven at 120 °C for 10 h.

2. **Surface Modification:**
   - Cool in vacuum oven.
   - Add 70 mL n-hexane.
   - Add 0.5 mL chlorotrimethylsilane.
   - Ultrasonically treat at room temperature for 1 h.

3. **Purification:**
   - Centrifuge.
   - Wash with n-hexane.
   - Dry in vacuum oven at 80 °C for 6 h.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Fe₂O₃ Nanoparticles

##### Step 1: Synthesis of Fe₂O₃ Nanoparticles
- **Reagents**: 7.5 g FeCl₃·6H₂O, 110 mL deionized water, 12.4 g sodium acetate trihydrate, 3.8 g hexadecyl trimethyl ammonium bromide, 26 mL ethylenediamine.
- **Conditions**: Stir for 0.5 h after initial mixing, add ethylenediamine, stir for 1 h, hydrothermal treatment at 200 °C for 10 h in Teflon-lined autoclave.
- **Process**:
  - Dissolve FeCl₃·6H₂O in deionized water.
  - Add sodium acetate trihydrate and hexadecyl trimethyl ammonium bromide, stir for 0.5 h.
  - Add ethylenediamine, stir for 1 h.
  - Transfer mixture to Teflon-lined autoclave.
  - Heat at 200 °C for 10 h.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure complete dissolution and homogeneous mixing before hydrothermal treatment.

##### Step 2: Purification and Calcination
- **Process**:
  - Wash product with water and ethanol.
  - Dry at 100 °C for 12 h.
  - Calcine at 500 °C for 5 h.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure thorough washing to remove surfactant and unreacted precursors.

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [2]: Mn-Promoted Fe₂O₃ (Fe@Mn)

##### Step 1: Deposition-Precipitation of Mn Promoter
- **Reagents**: 0.5 g Fe₂O₃, 300 mL ethanol, 0.078 g KMnO₄ in 5 mL aqueous solution.
- **Conditions**: Disperse Fe₂O₃ in ethanol by ultrasonication, add KMnO₄ solution dropwise, stir at 30 °C for 2 h.
- **Process**:
  - Disperse Fe₂O₃ in ethanol by ultrasonication.
  - Add KMnO₄ solution dropwise using a peristaltic pump.
  - Stir at 30 °C for 2 h.
- **Key Steps**: Dropwise addition ensures uniform promoter deposition.

##### Step 2: Purification and Drying
- **Process**:
  - Collect product by centrifugation.
  - Wash with water.
  - Dry at 100 °C for 12 h.
- **Key Steps**: Remove excess KMnO₄ and byproducts.

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [3]: SiO₂-Coated Catalysts (Fe@Mn@xSi)

##### Step 1: SiO₂ Shell Coating via Modified Stöber Method
- **Reagents**: 0.5 g Fe@Mn, 150 mL ethanol, x mL TEOS (x = 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.8), 2.5 mL ammonia solution (25–28%), 10 mL water.
- **Conditions**: Disperse Fe@Mn in ethanol by ultrasonication, add TEOS, stir at 30 °C for 3 h, add ammonia and water, stir at 30 °C for 4 h.
- **Process**:
  - Disperse Fe@Mn in ethanol by ultrasonication.
  - Add TEOS, stir at 30 °C for 3 h.
  - Add ammonia solution and water, stir at 30 °C for 4 h.
- **Key Steps**: Control TEOS dosage to adjust SiO₂ shell thickness.

##### Step 2: Purification and Drying
- **Process**:
  - Collect by centrifugation.
  - Wash with ethanol.
  - Dry at 100 °C for 12 h.
- **Key Steps**: Remove unreacted TEOS and byproducts.

---

#### Catalyst Preparation [4]: Hydrophobic Surface Modification (Fe@Mn@xSi-c)

##### Step 1: Pre-treatment
- **Reagents**: 0.5 g Fe@Mn@xSi.
- **Conditions**: Dry in vacuum oven at 120 °C for 10 h.
- **Process**:
  - Place Fe@Mn@xSi in vacuum oven at 120 °C for 10 h.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure complete removal of moisture.

##### Step 2: Surface Hydrophobization
- **Reagents**: 70 mL n-hexane, 0.5 mL chlorotrimethylsilane.
- **Conditions**: Cool in vacuum oven, add n-hexane and chlorotrimethylsilane, ultrasonically treat at room temperature for 1 h.
- **Process**:
  - Add n-hexane to dried catalyst.
  - Add chlorotrimethylsilane.
  - Ultrasonically treat at room temperature for 1 h.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure uniform hydrophobic modification.

##### Step 3: Purification and Drying
- **Process**:
  - Centrifuge.
  - Wash with n-hexane.
  - Dry in vacuum oven at 80 °C for 6 h.
- **Key Steps**: Remove excess surface modifier and solvent.

#### Catalyst Preparation [5]: Hydrophobic Modification of Fe@Mn (Fe@Mn-c)

## 6 Results

### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** Crystal phase composition and evolution determined by XRD and Mössbauer spectroscopy.
- **Findings:**
  - Hydrophilic Fe@Mn@0.2Si catalyst after reaction: 59.7% χ-Fe₅C₂, 34.3% Fe₃O₄, 6.0% Fe³⁺(spm).
  - Hydrophobic Fe@Mn@0.2Si-c catalyst after reaction: 74.1% χ-Fe₅C₂, 25.9% θ-Fe₃C; no Fe₃O₄ detected.
  - In situ XRD: Hydrophilic catalyst showed rapid oxidation of iron carbide to Fe₃O₄ upon H₂O exposure; hydrophobic catalyst maintained stable iron carbide phase.
- **Observations:** Surface hydrophobization effectively inhibited Fe₃O₄ formation and stabilized iron carbide phases.

#### 6.1.2 Elemental Composition and Surface Chemistry
- **Description:** Elemental composition and surface chemical states analyzed by ICP and XPS.
- **Findings:**
  - ICP confirmed Fe and Mn content in catalysts.
  - XPS detected chlorine on Fe@Mn@0.05Si-c catalyst, attributed to incomplete removal of chlorotrimethylsilane byproducts.
  - No chlorine detected on Fe@Mn@0.2Si-c catalyst.
- **Observations:** Chlorine contamination correlated with catalyst deactivation and inhibition of iron carbide formation.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Core-Shell Structure
- **Description:** Morphology and core-shell structure observed by TEM and EDS.
- **Findings:**
  - TEM and EDS mapping confirmed homogeneous SiO₂ shell coating and encapsulation of FeMn core.
  - Core shrinkage observed in hydrophobic catalysts after reaction, indicating phase evolution.
- **Observations:** SiO₂ shell thickness could be tuned by TEOS dosage; appropriate shell thickness critical for performance.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Hydrophobicity
- **Description:** Surface hydrophobicity assessed by water-droplet contact angle measurements and FTIR.
- **Findings:**
  - Hydrophobic modification increased contact angle, confirming successful surface silanization.
  - FTIR spectra showed characteristic Si–CH₃ stretching vibrations after hydrophobic treatment.
- **Observations:** Enhanced hydrophobicity correlated with improved catalyst stability and selectivity.

#### 6.1.5 Reduction and Carburization Behavior
- **Description:** Reduction and carburization behavior studied by CO-TPR and H₂-TPR.
- **Findings:**
  - Increasing SiO₂ shell thickness shifted reduction peaks to higher temperature and decreased peak area, indicating reduced accessibility.
  - H₂-TPR showed hydrogen could access internal iron species even with thicker shells.
- **Observations:** Excessive shell thickness inhibited CO diffusion and carburization, reducing active phase content.

#### 6.1.6 CO Adsorption Behavior
- **Description:** CO adsorption studied by in situ DRIFTS.
- **Findings:**
  - Fe@Mn@0.05Si catalyst showed strong CO adsorption and carbonate species formation.
  - Fe@Mn@0.05Si-c catalyst (with chlorine) showed only gaseous CO, indicating inhibited adsorption.
- **Observations:** Chlorine poisoning suppressed CO adsorption and iron carbide formation.

### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** The ratio of CO molecules converted by the catalyst to the CO molecules in the reactant feed.
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si: [CO conversion not numerically specified for this catalyst in main text; referenced as higher than hydrophobic analog in some cases.]
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si-c: [CO conversion not numerically specified in main text.]
  - Fe@Mn@0.05Si: 53.8%
  - Fe@Mn@2.8Si: 24.3%
  - Fe@Mn@0.05Si-c: <2%
- **Observations and Trends:** CO conversion decreased with increasing SiO₂ shell thickness; hydrophobic modification with appropriate shell thickness maintained high conversion, while excessive shell thickness or chlorine contamination led to low conversion.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** The proportion of converted CO molecules that form CO₂.
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si: 28.9%
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si-c: 7.9%
  - Hydrophilic Fe@Mn@xSi catalysts: ~40% (for all x)
- **Observations and Trends:** Hydrophobic surface modification significantly reduced CO₂ selectivity by inhibiting the water-gas shift side reaction.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** The proportion of converted CO molecules that form CH₄.
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si: 21.5%
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si-c: 14.1%
- **Observations and Trends:** Hydrophobic modification decreased methane selectivity, favoring longer-chain hydrocarbon formation.

#### 6.2.4 Olefins Selectivity
- **Description:** The proportion of hydrocarbons produced as olefins.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si: 49.8%
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si-c: 64.3%
  - Fe@Mn@0.05Si: 66.8%
  - Fe@Mn@2.8Si: 27.1%
- **Observations and Trends:** Hydrophobic catalysts with optimal shell thickness showed enhanced olefin selectivity; excessive shell thickness reduced olefin selectivity.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability
- **Description:** Catalyst performance over extended reaction time.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe@Mn@0.2Si-c: Maintained good catalytic stability during 110 h of continuous reaction.
- **Observations and Trends:** Hydrophobic surface modification improved catalyst stability by inhibiting oxidation and deactivation.

#### 6.2.6 Effect of Chlorine Poisoning
- **Description:** Impact of residual chlorine on catalyst activity.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - Fe@Mn@0.05Si-c: <2% CO conversion; only Fe₃O₄ phase detected after reaction.
- **Observations and Trends:** Chlorine contamination from hydrophobic modification severely inhibited CO adsorption, iron carbide formation, and catalytic activity.
