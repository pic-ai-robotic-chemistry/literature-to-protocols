## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The CoMnC catalyst is synthesized using a coprecipitation method followed by a carbonization process and physical mixing with PDVB. The procedure is organized into clear, reproducible steps:

#### Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation
1. Dissolve 29.4 g of Co(NO₃)₂⋅6H₂O and 17.9 g of 50 wt% Mn(NO₃)₂ in 75 mL of deionized water to form solution A.
2. Prepare solution B by dissolving 24.0 g of Na₂CO₃ in 75 mL of deionized water.
3. Under vigorous stirring at 30 °C, simultaneously dropwise add solutions A and B into 200 mL of deionized water. The pH is maintained at approximately 8 throughout the process.
4. Continue stirring for 1 hour and allow the mixture to age under stirring for an additional 2 hours to ensure uniform precursor formation.

#### Precipitation–Deposition
• This step is integrated with the co‑precipitation stage by the controlled dropwise addition of the precursor solutions, which promotes the deposition of active components onto forming particles before the subsequent separation.

#### Drying
• The resulting precipitate is collected, washed, and then dried at 100 °C for 10 hours to remove residual moisture.

#### Calcination
• The dried material is calcined at 330 °C for 3 hours in order to convert the precursor into a stable CoMn oxide phase, setting the stage for carbide formation.

#### Post‑treatment and Carbonization
• A pre‑reduction treatment is carried out in a pure H₂ flow at 300 °C for 5 hours. This step facilitates the transformation of the CoMn oxide into the desired CoMn carbide (CoMnC).

#### Physical Mixing with PDVB
• Post‑synthesis, the CoMnC catalyst is physically blended with poly(divinylbenzene) (PDVB). The hydrophobic PDVB, chosen for its thermal stability (up to 250 °C) and inert surface, is mixed at a controlled weight ratio to optimize water removal during Fischer–Tropsch conversion.

• Comparative analysis indicates that while the coprecipitation method offers excellent control and reproducibility through precise reagent concentrations and strict temperature and pH regulation, its scalability and complexity require careful consideration against alternative synthesis techniques.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: CoMnC Catalyst

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  • 29.4 g Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O  
  • 17.9 g of 50 wt% Mn(NO₃)₂  
  • 24.0 g Na₂CO₃  
  • 75 mL deionized water (for Co and Mn nitrates – forming solution A)  
  • 75 mL deionized water (for Na₂CO₃ – forming solution B)  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Temperature: 30 °C  
  • pH maintained at ~8 via dropwise addition of solution B  
  • Stirring: Vigorous stirring for 1 hour followed by a 2-hour aging period  
- **Process**:  
  1. Dissolve the cobalt and manganese nitrates in 75 mL deionized water to form solution A.  
  2. Separately, dissolve Na₂CO₃ in 75 mL deionized water to obtain solution B.  
  3. Under vigorous stirring at 30 °C, simultaneously add both solutions dropwise to a reaction vessel, ensuring the pH remains around 8.  
  4. Continue stirring the resulting mixture for 1 hour and then allow the suspension to age for an additional 2 hours to ensure complete coprecipitation.

- **Key Steps**:  
  • Monitor pH carefully using a pH meter; adjust only with Na₂CO₃ solution.  
  • Ensure uniform dropwise addition to promote reproducibility.

##### Step 2: Purification, Drying & Calcination  
- **Purification**:  
  • Centrifuge the precipitate and wash thoroughly with deionized water to remove soluble impurities.  
- **Drying**:  
  • Dry the washed precipitate at 100 °C for 10 hours using a laboratory oven.  
- **Calcination/Carbonization**:  
  • Heat the dried material in a pure H₂ atmosphere at 300 °C for 5 hours (in-situ reduction), then cool to 250 °C.  
  • Follow with a calcination step at 330 °C for 3 hours to stabilize the structure.

##### Step 3: Catalyst Assembly with PDVB  
- **Reagents**:  
  • Post-carbonization CoMnC  
  • PDVB (physical mixing at a 1:1 weight ratio)  
- **Process**:  
  1. Pre-dry the CoMnC catalyst and disperse it in n-hexane under gentle stirring.  
  2. Gradually add PDVB to achieve uniform hydrophobic coating, targeting granules within a 40–60 mesh size.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  • Control stirring speed and duration to ensure close intergranular packing, facilitating rapid water desorption during catalytic testing.

This structured workflow, from raw precursor preparation through purification, drying, calcination, and final assembly, ensures reproducibility and clear delineation of each critical step in the CoMnC/PDVB catalyst synthesis.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation protocol for the CoMnC/PDVB catalyst is a critical preparative step designed to expose the active CoMnC phase prior to its use in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. This step ensures that the catalyst is in its optimal state for efficient syngas conversion and olefin selectivity.

#### Activation Temperature
Activation temperature is key to promoting complete reduction of the CoMn oxide precursor. In this study, the catalyst is subjected to an in situ reduction at approximately 300 °C. This moderate temperature is chosen to effectively convert the precursor into the active carbide phase without causing sintering or undesired structural changes. The precise control of temperature is crucial, as it allows the catalyst's surface to develop a balanced reactive state, ensuring rapid water desorption during reaction.

#### Activation Pressure
The in situ reduction is performed under atmospheric pressure (approximately 1 bar). Operating at this pressure simplifies the reactor setup and maintains stable reaction conditions, which supports uniform gas-catalyst interaction throughout the activation process.

#### Activation Gas
Hydrogen (H₂) is employed as the sole reducing agent during the activation step. The use of a pure H₂ flow is instrumental in creating reductive conditions, which facilitate the full reduction of the oxide precursor. This step is essential not only to form the active CoMn carbide phase but also to clear any residual surface species that might impede catalytic performance.

#### Activation Time
Completing the activation process within a 5-hour period ensures that the catalyst attains a fully reduced state before being integrated with PDVB. This duration is optimized to allow complete reduction while avoiding any overexposure that might lead to adverse sintering effects. The outcome is a catalyst that consistently displays high CO conversion rates and favorable product selectivity in subsequent Fischer–Tropsch reaction tests.

This in situ reduction, executed at 300 °C under an atmospheric H₂ flow for 5 hours, is critical for establishing a stable active phase, thereby ensuring reproducibility and sustained catalytic performance in long-term operation.

### 5.2 Reaction

#### 5.2.1 Reaction Temperature
• **Definition:** The reaction temperature is a critical parameter in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis, governing the activation energy, kinetics, and product distribution by influencing the adsorption and reaction rates of CO and H₂ on the catalyst surface.  
• **Experimental Conditions:** The reaction was conducted at a constant temperature of 250 °C.  
• **Observations:** At 250 °C, the CoMnC/PDVB catalyst not only achieved a CO conversion of approximately 63.5% but also demonstrated robust stability. Notably, after 120 hours of continuous operation, the conversion rate remained steady at around 62.8%, highlighting resistance to deactivation.

• **Experimental Conditions:** The Fischer–Tropsch reaction was carried out at a pressure of 0.1 MPa.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structural Stability
- **Description:** The crystal phase composition and stability of the CoMnC/PDVB catalyst were assessed using powder X-ray diffraction (XRD). This technique is critical for identifying active carbide phases and detecting any phase transformations, particularly the formation of unwanted oxides that can diminish Fischer–Tropsch activity.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns of the CoMnC/PDVB composite are nearly identical to those of bare CoMnC, with clear peaks corresponding to the Co₂C phase and no detectable signals for oxide phases such as CoO, even after prolonged reaction or water treatment at 250 °C. In contrast, bare CoMnC exposed to water shows diminished Co₂C peaks and the emergence of CoO peaks, indicating oxidation.
- **Observations:** The preservation of the carbide phase in CoMnC/PDVB, despite exposure to water and reaction conditions, demonstrates that PDVB effectively hinders water-induced oxidation and maintains the structural integrity of the active phase.

#### 6.1.2 Morphology and Physical Mixing
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were employed to examine the morphology, particle distribution, and physical mixing of CoMnC and PDVB within the catalyst granules. These analyses are essential for understanding the spatial arrangement and potential mass transfer pathways.
- **Findings:** SEM images reveal that CoMnC and PDVB are tightly packed and randomly distributed within the granules, with intergranular distances ranging from nanometers to micrometers. TEM images of CoMnC separated from the composite after reaction show no significant morphological changes compared to the as-prepared catalyst.
- **Observations:** The homogeneous physical mixing and stable morphology ensure efficient contact between hydrophilic and hydrophobic domains, facilitating rapid water removal and supporting consistent catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Hydrophobicity
- **Description:** Water-droplet contact angle measurements were conducted to evaluate the hydrophobicity imparted by PDVB. Enhanced hydrophobicity is crucial for minimizing water adsorption on the catalyst surface, thereby reducing competitive inhibition of CO adsorption.
- **Findings:** The CoMnC/PDVB composite exhibits a significantly higher water-droplet contact angle than bare CoMnC, confirming the effective hydrophobic modification by PDVB.
- **Observations:** Increased hydrophobicity directly correlates with improved water desorption kinetics, as further supported by water adsorption tests, and is linked to enhanced catalytic efficiency.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Chemistry and Water Removal (FTIR Analysis)
- **Description:** In situ Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was used to monitor CO adsorption and hydrogenation behavior, as well as water desorption dynamics under reaction conditions.
- **Findings:** On CoMnC/PDVB, strong olefin signals and extremely weak water signals are observed, indicating rapid water desorption. In contrast, bare CoMnC shows increasing water signals over time, signifying water accumulation. Additional FTIR and pulse experiments demonstrate that, while water hinders CO adsorption on bare CoMnC, the presence of PDVB maintains CO adsorption even under high water content.
- **Observations:** These results confirm that PDVB accelerates water removal, preserves active sites for CO hydrogenation, and mitigates the negative effects of water on catalyst performance.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability of PDVB
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric-mass spectrometry (TG-MS) and thermogravimetric-differential scanning calorimetry (TG-DSC) were utilized to assess the thermal stability of PDVB within the composite under reaction conditions.
- **Findings:** PDVB remains stable at 250 °C, with no detectable decomposition or volatile emission. Weight loss and hydrocarbon formation are only observed above ~370 °C, and no morphological changes occur after prolonged heating at reaction temperature.
- **Observations:** The thermal robustness of PDVB ensures its inertness and sustained hydrophobic function during Fischer–Tropsch synthesis.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed into products, calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
where \( F_{\text{CO,in}} \) and \( F_{\text{CO,out}} \) are the molar flow rates of CO in the feed and product streams, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** The CoMnC/PDVB catalyst achieved a CO conversion of 63.5% at 250°C and 0.1 MPa, nearly double that of the bare CoMnC catalyst (32.2%) under identical conditions. Comparative catalysts, such as CoMnC/SiO₂-Me, reached 74.7%, while CoMn@Si and CoMn@Si-c exhibited lower conversions (23.7% and 22.2%, respectively).
- **Observations and Trends:** The physical mixing of CoMnC with hydrophobic PDVB significantly enhances CO conversion, attributed to improved water removal and sustained active site availability.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CO₂, indicating the extent of oxidation and water-gas shift reactions:
\[
\text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions, CO₂ selectivity ranged from 46.0% to 48.5%. When the H₂/CO ratio was increased to 3.6 and CO₂ was co-fed, selectivity decreased to 23.7%.
- **Observations and Trends:** High CO₂ selectivity is typical for Fischer–Tropsch to olefins processes, with values modulated by feed composition and reaction conditions.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity measures the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
\[
\text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity for CoMnC/PDVB remained below 5.0% throughout continuous operation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Suppressed methane formation is a key advantage, maximizing the yield of more valuable olefins.

#### 6.2.4 Light Olefin Selectivity (C₂–C₄)
- **Description:** Selectivity to light olefins (C₂–C₄) is defined as the percentage of converted carbon appearing as C₂–C₄ olefins:
\[
\text{Light Olefin Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{\text{C in C}_2\text{–C}_4 \text{ olefins}}{\text{Total C in products except CO}_2} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The CoMnC/PDVB catalyst achieved a light olefin selectivity of 71.4%. For comparison, CoMnC/SiO₂-Me reached 45.5%, while CoMn@Si and CoMn@Si-c were below 27%.
- **Observations and Trends:** The hydrophobic PDVB promoter substantially increases light olefin selectivity, with a high olefin-to-paraffin ratio and significant C₅⁺ olefin formation (C₅⁺ selectivity ~18.0%, with 92.0% as C₅–C₈ olefins).

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time. The deactivation rate is calculated as:
\[
\text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Total reaction time}} \quad (\text{h}^{-1})
\]
- **Experimental Data:** After an initial activation period (~15 h), CO conversion stabilized at ~64.7%. Over 120 hours, conversion remained at 62.8%, indicating negligible deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** The CoMnC/PDVB catalyst demonstrates excellent long-term stability, with consistent CO conversion and selectivity throughout extended operation.

#### 6.2.6 Additional Performance Indicators
- **Space-Time Productivity:** The catalyst exhibited high space-time productivity for light olefins, as measured in mmol·gCoMnC⁻¹·h⁻¹.
- **Olefin-to-Paraffin Ratio:** The olefin/paraffin ratio was notably high, reflecting the catalyst’s selectivity for olefins over paraffins.
- **Hydrophobicity Correlation:** Water-droplet contact angle measurements confirmed enhanced hydrophobicity, directly correlating with improved catalytic performance.

**Summary:** The CoMnC/PDVB catalyst, through physical mixing with hydrophobic PDVB, achieves high CO conversion, exceptional light olefin selectivity, suppressed methane formation, and outstanding operational stability, establishing a robust platform for efficient syngas-to-olefins conversion.
