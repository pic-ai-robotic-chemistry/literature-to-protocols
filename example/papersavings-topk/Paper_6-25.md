## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the synthesis procedures for the CoMnC catalyst and the hydrophobic PDVB polymer, emphasizing reproducibility and process clarity.

#### CoMnC Synthesis Procedure

1. • Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   - Prepare solution A by dissolving 29.4 g of Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O and 17.9 g of 50 wt% Mn(NO₃)₂ in deionized water (molar ratio Co:Mn = 2:1).  
   - Prepare solution B by dissolving Na₂CO₃ in deionized water.  
   - Under vigorous stirring at 30 °C, simultaneously add solutions A and B to a beaker containing deionized water while monitoring the pH, which is maintained at approximately 8.

2. • Precipitation–Deposition Step  
   - As the solutions mix, a visible precipitate forms, indicating the formation of CoMn oxide.  
   - Ensure uniform deposition during dropwise addition to promote reproducible nucleation of particles.  

3. • Drying  
   - After aging the suspension for 2 h at 30 °C, centrifuge and wash the precipitate thoroughly to remove residual ions.  
   - Dry the washed precipitate at 100 °C for 10 h.

4. • Calcination  
   - Calcine the dried precursor at 330 °C for 3 h in a controlled furnace to transform the oxide phase.

5. • Post‑treatment  
   - During subsequent syngas conversion, in situ reduction/carbonization is performed at 250 °C under a H₂/CO (2:1) atmosphere to yield the active CoMn carbide phase.

#### PDVB Synthesis Procedure

1. • Precursor Mixing  
   - Combine divinylbenzene with AIBN as the initiator.

2. • Polymerization in Autoclave  
   - For nonporous PDVB, conduct solvent‑free polymerization at 100 °C in a Teflon‑lined autoclave for 24 h.  
   - For nanoporous PDVB, use solvent-assisted conditions (with either DMF or EA) at 100 °C for 12 h.  

3. • Post‑processing  
   - Follow polymerization with methanol washing to remove unreacted monomers, then dry the polymer at 100 °C (overnight for nanoporous PDVB) to stabilize the final structure.

This structured approach, with precise reagent concentrations, controlled pH, and defined thermal treatments, ensures that another researcher can faithfully replicate the synthesis processes for the CoMnC/PDVB Fischer–Tropsch catalyst system.

#### 3.1.2 Liquid Transfer

#### 3.1.3 Mixing

This mixing operation ensures homogeneous distribution of the metal ions, which is crucial for the subsequent coprecipitation process. The vigorous stirring at a fixed temperature of 30 °C helps maintain a stable pH (~8) required for the controlled coprecipitation reaction. Collectively, these detailed steps—from weighing through liquid transfer to mixing—provide a clear and reproducible framework for the preparation of the CoMnC/PDVB catalyst reagents, aligning with the stringent requirements of reproducibility in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis experiments.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: CoMnC Catalyst

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**:  
  - 29.4 g Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O  
  - 17.9 g (50 wt%) Mn(NO₃)₂  
  - 24.0 g Na₂CO₃  
  - 75 mL deionized water for each solution  
- **Conditions**:  
  - Temperature: 30 °C  
  - Target pH: ~8  
  - Equipment: Beakers, pH meter, magnetic stirrer  
- **Process**:  
  - Dissolve Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O and Mn(NO₃)₂ in 75 mL deionized water to obtain Solution A. Separately, dissolve Na₂CO₃ in 75 mL deionized water to obtain Solution B.  
  - Add Solutions A and B dropwise simultaneously into 200 mL deionized water while stirring, carefully controlling the pH to remain near 8.  
  - Ensure complete dissolution of the metal nitrates and gradual precipitation of the corresponding hydroxides.

##### Step 2: Aging and Precipitation  
- **Conditions**:  
  - Stir the resulting suspension for 1 hour at 30 °C, then age for an additional 2 hours to promote complete precipitation.  
- **Key Steps**:  
  - Continuous pH monitoring during aging is essential for reproducibility.

##### Step 3: Purification  
- **Process**:  
  - Centrifuge the suspension (centrifugation conditions to be optimized) and wash the precipitate repeatedly with deionized water until washings are clear.

##### Step 4: Drying & Calcination  
- **Drying**:  
  - Dry the CoMn oxide precursor at 100 °C for 10 hours.  
- **Calcination**:  
  - Heat the dried precursor at 330 °C for 3 hours in a controlled atmosphere to form the CoMn oxide destined for in situ carbide conversion.

##### Step 5: Activation  
- **Process**:  
  - Under Fischer–Tropsch reaction conditions, perform in situ reduction and carbonization at 250 °C with a syngas mixture (H₂/CO molar ratio =2) to convert the CoMn oxide into the active CoMn carbide.

#### Catalyst Preparation [2]: PDVB Polymer

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation and Polymerization  
- **Reagents**: Divinylbenzene (DVB) and AIBN as the radical initiator.  
- **Conditions**:  
  - For nonporous PDVB: solvent-free at 100 °C for 24 hours.  
  - For nanoporous variants: use solvents (e.g., DMF or EA) at 100 °C for 12 hours in an autoclave.
  
##### Step 2: Post-Processing  
- **Process**:  
  - Wash the polymer with methanol to remove unreacted monomers. Dry at 100 °C overnight.

#### Catalyst Assembly  
- **Reagents**: Combine the prepared CoMnC and PDVB in defined weight ratios (e.g., 0.5, 1.0, or 1.5).  
- **Process**:  
  - Mix the powders thoroughly to ensure intimate physical contact, which promotes water desorption during CO hydrogenation.  
- **Key Points**:  
  - Strict adherence to sequential steps—precursor preparation, purification, drying, calcination, and activation—is critical for reproducibility and performance optimization.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The catalyst activation for the CoMnC/PDVB Fischer–Tropsch system is a two‐stage process that ensures both the complete reduction of the precursor oxide and the formation of a stable carbide phase under reaction conditions.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature
- **Definition:** The activation temperature is critical for the reduction and in situ carbonization of the CoMn oxide precursor, ensuring that the active carbide phase is formed without compromising the integrity of the PDVB promoter.
- **Experimental Conditions:** Initially, the CoMn oxide is pre‐reduced in a pure hydrogen atmosphere at 300 °C for 5 hours, which converts it into CoMn carbide. The catalyst is then cooled to 250 °C, the reaction temperature at which Fischer–Tropsch conversion is carried out.
- **Observations:** The moderate temperature used for pre‐reduction prevents sintering while ensuring effective transformation to the active phase. Activation at 250 °C supports efficient CO adsorption while minimizing competitive water adsorption.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure
- **Definition:** The pressure during activation influences the reduction kinetics and the interaction between the reducing gas and the catalyst.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The pre‐reaction treatment and subsequent reaction steps are conducted under a pressure of 0.1 MPa.
- **Observations:** Operating at 0.1 MPa maintains a controlled environment that supports complete reduction without inducing catalyst agglomeration.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas
- **Definition:** The choice of activation gas is essential, as it not only reduces the oxide precursor but also plays a role in the initial carbonization process.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The activation uses pure hydrogen (H₂) during the pre‐reduction step. Post reduction, the catalyst is exposed to a syngas mixture (H₂/CO with a molar ratio of 2) during in situ activation.
- **Observations:** The pure H₂ environment ensures an unimpeded reduction of CoMn oxide, while the subsequent syngas exposure promotes carbon deposition, aiding efficient carbide formation.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time
- **Definition:** The duration of activation affects how completely the catalyst is transformed into its active form.
- **Experimental Conditions:** The pre‐reduction is performed for 5 hours at 300 °C, ensuring complete transformation of CoMn oxide. In extended testing, an overall activation period of approximately 15 hours is observed before steady-state performance is reached.
- **Observations:** The chosen times strike a balance between full activation and preservation of catalyst morphology.

#### 5.1.5 Activation Space-Time Velocity

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal phase composition and stability of the catalyst were assessed using X-ray diffraction (XRD), which is critical for identifying active phases and monitoring structural changes during reaction and water exposure.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns confirm that cobalt carbide (Co₂C) is the predominant phase in the CoMnC catalyst, both before and after physical mixing with PDVB. The presence of PDVB does not alter the CoMnC structure, as evidenced by unchanged XRD patterns during reaction and after water treatment. In contrast, bare CoMnC exhibits diminished Co₂C peaks and the emergence of CoO peaks following water exposure at 250 °C, indicating oxidation.
- **Observations:** PDVB effectively suppresses the formation of CoO by hindering water access to the carbide phase, thereby stabilizing Co₂C and preserving catalytic activity.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Surface area and porosity were determined by N₂ sorption isotherms using the BET method, which are important for understanding catalyst accessibility and potential diffusion limitations.
- **Findings:** Nonporous PDVB exhibits a surface area of less than 5 m²/g, while nanoporous variants (PDVB-DMF and PDVB-EA) display significantly higher surface areas of 623.3 m²/g and 488.2 m²/g, with corresponding pore volumes of 1.98 cm³/g and 0.74 cm³/g, respectively. The CoMnC/PDVB physical mixture maintains the original surface area and pore structure of its components.
- **Observations:** The high surface area of nanoporous PDVB variants may facilitate more efficient water removal, but PDVB addition does not modify the intrinsic porosity of CoMnC.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Physical Mixing
- **Description:** Morphological features and the degree of physical mixing were examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), which provide insight into particle distribution and structural integrity.
- **Findings:** SEM images reveal that CoMnC and PDVB granules are tightly packed and randomly distributed, with intergranular distances ranging from nanometers to micrometers. TEM analysis shows that the morphology of CoMnC remains unchanged after reaction and mixing with PDVB, indicating structural stability.
- **Observations:** The intimate physical mixing of CoMnC and PDVB ensures close contact without inducing aggregation or sintering, supporting efficient water removal and catalyst durability.

#### 6.1.4 Surface Hydrophobicity
- **Description:** The hydrophobicity of PDVB was quantified by water-droplet contact angle measurements, which are relevant for understanding water management at the catalyst interface.
- **Findings:** PDVB exhibits a water contact angle of approximately 145°, confirming its highly hydrophobic nature.
- **Observations:** This pronounced hydrophobicity enables rapid desorption of water from the catalyst surface, preventing water-induced deactivation and maintaining active CO adsorption sites.

#### 6.1.5 Surface Chemistry and Reaction Intermediates
- **Description:** In situ Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and temperature-programmed surface reaction mass spectrometry (TPSR-MS) were employed to monitor surface intermediates and reaction steps.
- **Findings:** FTIR detects olefinic bands (2717–2955 cm⁻¹, 1320–1527 cm⁻¹, ~1039 cm⁻¹) during CO hydrogenation, with strong water signals observed on bare CoMnC but minimal on CoMnC/PDVB. TPSR-MS profiles show sequential evolution of water (166 °C), methane (203 °C), and propylene (217 °C), with stronger signals for propylene and water on CoMnC/PDVB.
- **Observations:** The data confirm that PDVB accelerates water desorption, maintains CO adsorption, and enhances the formation of desired olefinic products by preventing water accumulation on active sites.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction. It is calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard Fischer–Tropsch conditions (250 °C, 0.1 MPa, GHSV 1800 mL·h⁻¹·gCoMnC⁻¹, H₂/CO = 2), the bare CoMnC catalyst achieves a CO conversion of 32.2%. Physical mixing with PDVB significantly enhances conversion: at PDVB/CoMnC weight ratios of 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5, the conversions are 56.4%, 63.5%, and 70.5%, respectively. Nanoporous PDVB variants further increase conversion, reaching up to 92.4%.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing the PDVB content consistently raises CO conversion, attributed to improved water removal and sustained CO adsorption. Higher H₂/CO ratios (e.g., 4) also boost conversion (up to 88.7%).

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO that forms CO₂, reflecting the extent of the water–gas shift and oxidation reactions:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** For CoMnC/PDVB under standard conditions, CO₂ selectivity is typically 46.3%. Adjusting the H₂/CO ratio or introducing CO₂ into the feed can lower CO₂ selectivity to 23.7%.
- **Observations and Trends:** The presence of PDVB does not significantly increase CO₂ selectivity, and feed composition adjustments allow for further optimization.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity indicates the fraction of converted CO yielding methane, a less desirable product in olefin synthesis:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** CH₄ selectivity remains low across all catalysts: 3.3% for bare CoMnC and 4.8% for CoMnC/PDVB. Values consistently remain below 5% regardless of PDVB content.
- **Observations and Trends:** The hydrophobic PDVB component effectively suppresses methane formation, favoring olefin production.

#### 6.2.4 C₂–C₄ Olefin Selectivity and C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** Selectivity for light olefins (C₂–C₄) and heavier hydrocarbons (C₃⁺) is determined by the fraction of converted CO forming these products, typically reported on a CO₂-free carbon basis.
- **Experimental Data:** CoMnC/PDVB achieves C₂–C₄ olefin selectivity up to 71.4%. C₃⁺ selectivity is derived from summing the selectivities of C₃ and higher hydrocarbons, with detailed distributions provided in the article.
- **Observations and Trends:** PDVB addition markedly increases light olefin selectivity while maintaining high C₃⁺ yields. Nanoporous PDVB shifts selectivity toward heavier olefins (C₅–C₈).

#### 6.2.5 Olefin/Paraffin (o/p) Ratio
- **Description:** The o/p ratio reflects the preference for olefinic over paraffinic hydrocarbons, important for downstream processing.
- **Experimental Data:** For C₆ hydrocarbons, the o/p ratio reaches 14.7, indicating a strong bias toward olefin formation.
- **Observations and Trends:** High o/p ratios are sustained with PDVB, facilitating easier product separation.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion and selectivity over extended operation. The deactivation rate is defined as:
  \[
  \text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Time}} \quad (\text{h}^{-1})
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Over 120 h of continuous operation, CoMnC/PDVB maintains CO conversion from ~64.7% to 62.8%, with light olefin selectivity stable at ~70.0%. The deactivation rate is negligible.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalyst demonstrates excellent durability, attributed to PDVB’s hydrophobicity, which prevents water-induced deactivation.

#### 6.2.7 Additional Performance Indicators
- **Space-Time Productivity:** The catalyst achieves a productivity of 5.9 mmol gCoMnC⁻¹ h⁻¹ for light olefins.
- **Water Tolerance:** CoMnC/PDVB maintains high activity even under water-rich conditions, confirming the effectiveness of rapid water removal.

**Summary:** The physical mixing of CoMnC with hydrophobic PDVB significantly enhances CO hydrogenation performance by promoting water desorption, increasing CO conversion, maximizing light olefin selectivity, suppressing methane formation, and ensuring long-term catalyst stability.
