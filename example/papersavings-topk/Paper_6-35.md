## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The article reports two key synthesis routes: one for the cobalt–manganese carbide (CoMnC) catalyst and one for the hydrophobic polymer PDVB. The detailed procedures for each method are outlined below.

#### Co‑precipitation Method for CoMnC Catalyst

1. • Precursor Mixing:  
  - Dissolve cobalt(II) nitrate hexahydrate (Co(NO₃)₂⋅6H₂O) and manganese(II) nitrate (Mn(NO₃)₂) in deionized water to form solution A.  
  - Prepare solution B by dissolving sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃) in deionized water.  

2. • Precipitation–Deposition:  
  - Add solutions A and B dropwise under vigorous stirring, maintaining the pH at approximately 8.  
  - Observable formation of a precipitate indicates effective co‑precipitation of Co and Mn ions.

3. • Post‑Mixing Steps:  
  - Stir the resulting suspension for 1 hour, followed by an aging step for 2 hours at 30°C.  
  - Centrifuge and wash the formed precipitate.

4. • Drying & Calcination:  
  - Dry the washed precipitate at 100°C for 10 hours.  
  - Calcine the dried material at 330°C for 3 hours to form the CoMn oxide.

5. • Reduction and Carbonization:  
  - Subject the calcined oxide to in situ reduction under a hydrogen atmosphere (e.g., at around 250–300°C) to transform it into the active CoMn carbide phase.

This method ensures reproducibility through strict control of reagent concentrations (as provided, for example, in the use of Na₂CO₃ for pH maintenance) and precise temperature and timing for each step.

#### Free Radical Polymerization Method for PDVB

1. • Precursor Mixing:  
  - Combine 10 g of divinylbenzene (DVB) with 0.5 g of azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) and stir at room temperature for 1 hour.

2. • Autoclave Treatment for Product Variants:  
  - For nonporous PDVB, transfer the mixture into a Teflon‑lined autoclave and heat at 100°C for 24 hours.  
  - For nanoporous PDVB variants, replace the reaction medium with solvents (e.g., DMF or EA) and conduct the autoclave treatment at 100°C for 12 hours.

3. • Post‑Processing:  
  - Following thermal treatment, wash and dry the product to obtain the desired polymer, which acts as a hydrophobic promoter when physically mixed with CoMnC.

In both synthesis approaches, attention to numerical values (temperature, duration, pH) and equipment (beakers, autoclave, pH meter) is paramount, ensuring that these protocols can be accurately replicated for further catalytic evaluation and comparative studies.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: CoMnC Catalyst

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: 29.4 g of Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, 17.9 g of 50 wt% Mn(NO₃)₂ dissolved in 75 mL deionized water (Solution A); 24.0 g of Na₂CO₃ in 75 mL deionized water (Solution B).
- **Conditions**: Temperature maintained at 30 °C, pH adjusted to ~8 by dropwise simultaneous addition under vigorous stirring.
- **Process**:
  - Dissolve the metal nitrates separately to form Solution A.
  - Similarly, prepare Solution B by dissolving Na₂CO₃.
  - Add both solutions slowly into a reaction vessel containing deionized water while continuously monitoring pH, ensuring it remains near 8.
  - Stir the resulting suspension for 1 hour and allow aging for 2 hours at 30 °C.
- **Key Steps**: Maintain precise pH control to ensure uniform co-precipitation.

##### Step 2: Purification
- **Process**:
  - Centrifuge the aged suspension.
  - Wash the resulting precipitate thoroughly with deionized water to remove residual salts.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure complete washing to avoid contamination during later steps.

##### Step 3: Drying & Calcination
- **Drying**: Transfer the washed precipitate into an oven and dry at 100 °C for 10 hours.
- **Calcination**: Heat the dried precursor in air at 330 °C for 3 hours. Ramp rates can be optimized based on equipment calibration.
- **Key Steps**: Confirm phase formation of the oxide precursor for subsequent reduction.

##### Step 4: Activation
- **Process**:
  - Pre-reduce the calcined precursor in a pure H₂ environment at 300 °C for 5 hours, converting the oxide to the active CoMn carbide phase.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure controlled gas flow and temperature to prevent the formation of unwanted phases.

#### Catalyst Assembly [2]: Integration of PDVB
  
##### Step 1: PDVB Synthesis
- **Reagents**: 10 g divinylbenzene (DVB) with 0.5 g AIBN.
- **Conditions**:
  - For nonporous PDVB: Stir at room temperature for 1 hour, then autoclave at 100 °C for 24 hours.
  - For nanoporous PDVB: Follow a similar protocol using DMF or EA as solvent and shorten autoclave treatment to 12 hours.
- **Key Steps**: Adapt solvent conditions to tailor porosity.

##### Step 2: Final Catalyst Assembly
- **Process**:
  - Physically mix the activated CoMnC with PDVB at predetermined weight ratios (0.5, 1.0, 1.5) to enhance hydrophobicity.
  - Gently homogenize to ensure intimate contact.
- **Key Steps**: Consistent mixing promotes rapid water desorption during the catalytic reaction.

This detailed process sequence—encompassing precursor synthesis, washing, drying, calcination, activation, and final assembly—ensures reproducibility and optimal performance in CO hydrogenation experiments.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystal phase composition and stability of the catalysts were investigated using X-ray diffraction (XRD), with particular attention to phase transformations under reaction and water treatment conditions. XRD is critical for identifying active carbide phases and detecting unwanted oxide formation, which can impact Fischer-Tropsch performance.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns confirmed the formation of Co₂C (cobalt carbide) as the main active phase in the CoMnC catalyst. Upon water treatment at 250 °C, bare CoMnC exhibited diminished Co₂C peaks and the emergence of CoO (cobalt oxide) peaks, indicating oxidation and loss of the active carbide phase. In contrast, CoMnC physically mixed with PDVB retained the characteristic Co₂C peaks with no new oxide peaks after water exposure or prolonged reaction, demonstrating that the carbide phase remained stable and oxide formation was suppressed in the presence of PDVB.
- **Observations:** The preservation of the Co₂C phase in CoMnC/PDVB highlights the protective effect of PDVB against water-induced oxidation, ensuring sustained catalytic activity.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption (BET) analysis was employed to determine the surface area and pore volume of PDVB materials, which are relevant for reactant diffusion and product desorption in the catalyst system.
- **Findings:** Nonporous PDVB exhibited a BET surface area of less than 5 m²/g, while nanoporous PDVB samples showed significantly higher values: 488.2 m²/g for PDVB-EA and 623.3 m²/g for PDVB-DMF. Corresponding pore volumes were 0.74 cm³/g (PDVB-EA) and 1.98 cm³/g (PDVB-DMF).
- **Observations:** The high surface area and pore volume of nanoporous PDVB facilitate enhanced reactant and product diffusion, supporting improved catalytic performance and selectivity.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Physical Mixing
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were used to examine the morphology, particle size, and physical mixing of CoMnC and PDVB within catalyst granules.
- **Findings:** SEM images revealed that CoMnC and PDVB are tightly and randomly packed within the granules, with intergranular distances ranging from nanometers to micrometers. TEM analysis showed that the CoMnC structure remained unchanged after mixing with PDVB and after reaction, indicating that the physical mixture does not alter the catalyst’s morphology or induce aggregation.
- **Observations:** The intimate physical mixing ensures close proximity between CoMnC and PDVB, enabling effective water removal without blocking active sites or altering the catalyst’s intrinsic structure.

#### 6.1.4 Hydrophobicity and Water Adsorption
- **Description:** Contact angle measurements and water adsorption tests were conducted to assess the hydrophobicity of PDVB and its effect on water uptake by the catalyst.
- **Findings:** PDVB and its mixtures with CoMnC exhibited high water contact angles (e.g., 134°–143°), confirming strong hydrophobicity. Water adsorption tests demonstrated that CoMnC/PDVB adsorbed significantly less water than bare CoMnC at equivalent relative humidities.
- **Observations:** The hydrophobic nature of PDVB minimizes water adsorption on the catalyst surface, preventing water from occupying active sites and thereby maintaining high CO conversion rates.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric-mass spectrometry (TG-MS) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) were used to evaluate the thermal stability of PDVB under reaction conditions.
- **Findings:** PDVB showed no detectable weight loss or decomposition products at 250 °C. Significant decomposition and hydrocarbon formation were only observed above ~370 °C. An endothermal signal in DSC began at ~300 °C, suggesting possible melting above this temperature, but not under reaction conditions.
- **Observations:** The excellent thermal stability of PDVB ensures its integrity and function as a hydrophobic promoter during prolonged catalytic operation.

#### 6.1.6 Surface Species and Water Removal (FTIR)
- **Description:** In situ FTIR spectroscopy was utilized to monitor surface-adsorbed species during CO hydrogenation and CO adsorption experiments.
- **Findings:** On bare CoMnC, FTIR spectra showed increasing signals for adsorbed water over time, indicating water accumulation. In contrast, CoMnC/PDVB exhibited strong olefin signals and extremely weak water signals, suggesting rapid water desorption. CO adsorption experiments further revealed that water co-feeding suppressed CO adsorption on bare CoMnC but not on CoMnC/PDVB.
- **Observations:** These results confirm that PDVB accelerates water removal, maintains active sites for CO adsorption, and directly contributes to enhanced catalytic activity and selectivity.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction. It is calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Under standard conditions (250°C, 0.1 MPa, H₂/CO = 2, GHSV = 1800 mL·h⁻¹·g⁻¹), the CoMnC catalyst alone achieved a CO conversion of 32.2%. Physical mixing with nonporous PDVB increased conversion to 63.5%. Nanoporous PDVB variants further enhanced conversion: 88.7% (PDVB-EA, SBET = 488.2 m²/g) and 92.4% (PDVB-DMF, SBET = 623.3 m²/g). Increasing the PDVB/CoMnC weight ratio from 0.5 to 1.5 raised conversion from 56.4% to 70.5%.
- **Observations and Trends:** The addition of hydrophobic PDVB, especially in nanoporous form, significantly boosts CO conversion. Higher PDVB content and increased nanoporosity correlate with enhanced conversion, attributed to accelerated water desorption and improved CO activation.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity represents the proportion of converted CO that forms CO₂, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO}_2 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** For CoMnC/PDVB, CO₂ selectivity ranged from 46.3% to 49.3% under standard conditions.
- **Observations and Trends:** The presence of PDVB does not markedly increase CO₂ selectivity, indicating that the main effect of PDVB is on hydrocarbon formation rather than promoting total oxidation.

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity for CoMnC/PDVB is consistently low, at 4.7–4.8%.
- **Observations and Trends:** Low methane selectivity is maintained across all PDVB-containing catalysts, demonstrating effective suppression of undesired methanation.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity quantifies the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with three or more carbon atoms:
  \[
  \text{C}_3^+ \text{ Selectivity} = 100 - \frac{F_{\text{CO}_2,\text{out}} + F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_2,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_4,\text{out}} + 2F_{\text{C}_2\text{H}_6,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Light olefin (C₂–C₄) selectivity increased from 60.8% (CoMnC) to 71.4% (CoMnC/PDVB). With nanoporous PDVB, light olefin selectivity decreased (53.4% for PDVB-EA, 37.6% for PDVB-DMF), while heavier olefin (C₅–C₈) selectivity increased.
- **Observations and Trends:** PDVB promotes light olefin formation, but higher nanoporosity shifts selectivity toward heavier hydrocarbons.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor reflects the probability of chain growth versus termination, influencing hydrocarbon distribution. It is derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory (ASF) model, with values between 0 and 1.
- **Experimental Data:** While explicit α values are not provided, the high selectivity for light olefins and moderate C₅⁺ selectivity suggest α values in the lower to mid-range typical for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis under these conditions.
- **Observations and Trends:** Rapid water desorption by PDVB maintains lower α values, favoring short-chain olefins. Increased nanoporosity raises α, shifting product distribution toward heavier olefins.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time. The deactivation rate is calculated as:
  \[
  \text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}} \quad (\text{h}^{-1})
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** In a 120-hour test, CoMnC/PDVB maintained CO conversion from 64.7% to 62.8%, indicating negligible deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalyst exhibits excellent long-term stability, with minimal loss in activity and selectivity, confirming the durability of the PDVB-promoted system.
