## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the experimental procedures for synthesizing the active catalytic phases used in Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. It encompasses the formation of the sodium‑doped ferric carbide (Na‑FeC*x*) catalyst via carbonization of a sodium‑modified iron oxide precursor and the preparation of zeolite promoters using hydrothermal and organosilane‑assisted techniques.

#### Na‑FeC*x* Synthesis via Carbonization
1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   • Mix FeCl3·6H2O and FeCl2·4H2O in deionized water with aqueous HCl.  
   • Add NaOH until pH reaches 10, leading to the formation of Na‑Fe3O4, as indicated by precipitate formation.  

2. Precipitation–Deposition  
   • If applicable, introduce the support precursor dropwise while stirring continuously for 1h before proceeding to drying.

3. Drying  
   • Following centrifugation and washing, dry the Na‑Fe3O4 at 100°C for 6h.

4. Calcination & Carbonization  
   • Expose the dried precursor to syngas (CO/H2 with a 1:1 molar ratio) at 300°C for 24h, converting the ferric oxide phase to the Fe5C2‑dominant Na‑FeC*x* phase.  
   • Observable phase transformation is confirmed by the disappearance of oxide signals in XRD analyses.

5. Pre‑reduction  
   • Pre‑reduce the catalyst under pure H2 at 400°C for 6 hours at atmospheric pressure, ensuring enhanced phase formation.

#### Zeolite Promoter Synthesis (s‑ZSM‑5 and s‑S1‑OH)
1. Hydrothermal Method for s‑ZSM‑5  
   • Use a Teflon‑lined autoclave operating at 180°C for 2 days to achieve nanosheet morphology (thickness of 90–110 nm) and a Si/Al ratio of approximately 26.2.

2. Organosilane‑Assisted Synthesis for s‑S1‑OH  
   • Incorporate organosilane reagents under controlled conditions to introduce silanol groups into the zeolite framework.

3. Post‑processing for Both  
   • Dry the synthesized materials, then calcine at 550°C for 4h to remove residual organics and activate the framework.

These procedures are designed for accuracy and reproducibility. The sequence—from precursor mixing through drying, followed by carbonization and controlled pre‑reduction—ensures the precise formation of the active phases. Additionally, controlled mixing of catalyst granules (20–40 mesh) with zeolite promoters minimizes sodium migration, maintaining consistent catalytic performance.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: Na‑FeC*x*  
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**: Primary chemicals include ferric oxide and a sodium salt. For example, 10.0 g Fe₃O₄ and a stoichiometric amount of sodium source are dissolved in 200 mL deionized water.  
- **Conditions**: The reaction is performed at room temperature with continuous magnetic stirring. The pH is carefully adjusted to 10 by adding NaOH aqueous solution dropwise.  
- **Process**:  
  - Dissolve Fe₃O₄ in deionized water under vigorous stirring.  
  - Introduce the sodium salt gradually to ensure a homogeneous mixture.  
  - Adjust the pH to 10 by slow addition of NaOH; continue stirring for an additional 30 minutes to achieve complete precursor formation.  
- **Key Steps**: Emphasize controlled pH adjustment and uniform mixing to guarantee a reproducible precursor.

##### Step 2: Catalyst Assembly and Carbonization  
- **Reagents**: The sodium-modified ferric oxide precursor is combined with a carbon source (if applicable) and is subjected to a syngas atmosphere to promote carbide formation.  
- **Conditions**: The mixture is transferred into a fixed-bed reactor where a pre-reduction is initiated at 400 °C under pure H₂ (10 mL/min) for 6 hours.  
- **Process**:  
  - Load the prepared precursor into the reactor.  
  - Initiate pre-reduction under hydrogen to facilitate conversion into the Fe₅C₂-dominant phase.  
  - Monitor phase changes via periodic XRD sampling to confirm the successful formation of the target Na‑FeC*x* phase.  
- **Key Steps**: Controlled temperature ramping and gas flow are essential to minimize over-hydrogenation and ensure phase purity.

##### Step 3: Purification, Drying & Calcination  
- **Process**:  
  - Separate the solid product by centrifugation and wash it six times with deionized water to remove residual ions.  
  - Dry the washed solid at 100 °C overnight in a convection oven.  
  - Calcine the dried precursor at 550 °C for 4 hours in air, using a controlled ramp rate to achieve structural stabilization and the removal of organic residues.  
- **Key Steps**: Strict adherence to drying and calcination conditions minimizes nanoparticle agglomeration and preserves catalyst integrity.

#### Catalyst Preparation [2]: Composite Catalyst Assembly (Na‑FeC*x*/s‑ZSM‑5 and Na‑FeC*x*/s‑S1‑OH)  
- **Reagents**: Mix the preformed Na‑FeC*x* with zeolite promoters (s‑ZSM‑5 or s‑S1‑OH) at a weight ratio of approximately 3:1.  
- **Conditions & Process**:  
  - Blend granulated catalysts (20–40 mesh size) using magnetic stirring until a uniform composite is achieved.  
  - Ensure that catalyst granules are in close contact while maintaining controlled mixing parameters to minimize excessive sodium migration.  
- **Key Steps**: Uniform granule mixing is critical for reproducible catalytic performance and high olefin selectivity.

The catalyst activation procedure is an integral part of preparing the sodium‑doped ferric carbide (Na‑FeCₓ) catalyst for Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. The activation step is designed exclusively as a pre‐reduction treatment to convert the sodium‑modified ferric oxide precursor into the Fe₅C₂-dominant phase.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature  
• Definition: The activation temperature provides the thermal energy required to initiate the reduction of the Na‑Fe₃O₄ precursor, facilitating its transformation into catalytically active carbide.  
• Experimental Conditions: Pre‐reduction is performed at 400 °C.  
• Observations: This temperature is optimal for rapid reduction while minimizing risks of undesirable sintering.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure  
• Definition: The pressure during activation influences the kinetics of the reduction reaction.  
• Experimental Conditions: The pre‐reduction is carried out at atmospheric pressure.  
• Observations: Operating at atmospheric pressure ensures a controlled environment that supports uniform hydrogen penetration without excessive catalyst agglomeration.

• Experimental Conditions: A pure H₂ stream (considered as 100% H₂) is employed during pre‐reduction.

#### 5.1.5 Activation Time

• Experimental Conditions: The catalyst is pre‐reduced for 6 hours.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystalline phases of the catalysts and zeolite promoters were determined using powder X-ray diffraction (XRD), which is essential for confirming the formation of active phases and framework integrity.
- **Findings:** XRD patterns of the Na‑FeC*x* catalyst after the LT-FTO reaction display only ferric carbide (Fe₅C₂) signals, with the oxide phase nearly undetectable, confirming complete transformation from the sodium-doped ferric oxide precursor. The Fe₅C₂ phase matches the standard reference (PDF # 20-0509). For s‑ZSM‑5, XRD patterns align with the MFI structure (PDF # 42-0024), confirming successful synthesis and framework preservation. These results are consistent for both standalone and composite catalysts.
- **Observations:** The dominance of the Fe₅C₂ phase is directly linked to high Fischer–Tropsch activity, while the structural integrity of the MFI zeolite ensures effective promotion and product selectivity.

#### 6.1.2 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Morphological features and particle size distributions were examined using transmission electron microscopy (TEM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM).
- **Findings:** TEM images reveal well-dispersed Na‑FeC*x* nanoparticles with stable morphology before and after reaction. SEM images of s‑ZSM‑5 and s‑S1‑OH show nanosheet crystals with b-axis thicknesses of 90–110 nm. No significant aggregation or sintering is observed in either the catalysts or zeolites. HAADF-STEM and EDS mapping confirm uniform distribution of Fe and Na within the catalyst particles.
- **Observations:** The nanosheet morphology of s‑ZSM‑5 facilitates rapid olefin desorption, while the homogeneous dispersion of active components supports consistent catalytic performance.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Nitrogen sorption isotherms (BET analysis) were used to determine surface area and pore volume, which are critical for reactant accessibility and product diffusion.
- **Findings:** s‑ZSM‑5 exhibits a BET surface area of 337.3 m²/g and a pore volume of 0.21 cm³/g (Si/Al ratio ≈26.2). Other zeolites, such as n‑ZSM‑5 and SSZ‑13, show surface areas of 388.7 m²/g and 542.9 m²/g, respectively. The high surface area and tailored porosity of s‑ZSM‑5 are attributed to its nanosheet structure.
- **Observations:** Enhanced surface area and pore volume in s‑ZSM‑5 promote efficient olefin desorption and minimize secondary reactions, contributing to high selectivity.

#### 6.1.4 Acid Site Properties and Olefin Adsorption
- **Description:** NH₃-temperature-programmed desorption (NH₃-TPD) and diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform spectroscopy (DRIFTS) were employed to assess acid site characteristics and olefin adsorption/desorption behavior.
- **Findings:** NH₃-TPD of s‑ZSM‑5 shows desorption peaks at 200 °C (weak acid sites) and 440 °C (strong acid sites), while s‑S1‑OH displays negligible ammonia desorption, confirming the absence of acid sites. DRIFTS analysis demonstrates that over 91% of ethene and 96% of propene desorb from s‑ZSM‑5 within 4 minutes, whereas Na‑FeC*x* retains olefins much longer.
- **Observations:** The presence of acid sites in s‑ZSM‑5 accelerates olefin desorption, reducing hydrogenation to alkanes and enhancing olefin selectivity. Silanol-rich s‑S1‑OH promotes desorption without acid-catalyzed side reactions.

#### 6.1.5 Elemental Composition and Sodium Migration
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and inductively coupled plasma (ICP) analysis were used to determine elemental composition and monitor sodium migration.
- **Findings:** ICP confirms a Na loading of 5.35 wt% in Na‑FeC*x*. XPS Na1s peaks at 1071.8 eV (Na‑FeC*x*) and 1073.3 eV (zeolite-bound Na) indicate sodium migration, which is minimized by using larger granules (20–40 mesh) and lower reaction temperatures.
- **Observations:** Controlled sodium migration preserves catalyst activity and selectivity, while excessive migration (as seen with powder mixing or higher temperatures) leads to performance loss.




### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CO Conversion (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Under LT-FTO conditions (260 °C, 2.0 MPa, CO/H₂/Ar = 45/45/10, GHSV ≈ 2400 mL·h⁻¹·g⁻¹), the Na‑FeCₓ* catalyst alone exhibits low activity (1.9% CO conversion at 260 °C), increasing at 300 °C. In contrast, composite catalysts show markedly higher conversions: Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑ZSM‑5 achieves up to 82.5%, and Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑S1‑OH reaches 66.5% (H₂/CO = 1) and 82.6% (H₂/CO = 3).
- **Observations and Trends:** The addition of zeolite promoters dramatically enhances CO conversion, with optimal performance at a zeolite/Na‑FeCₓ* mass ratio of 3:1 and controlled granule mixing. Higher H₂/CO ratios further boost conversion for s‑S1‑OH.

#### 6.2.2 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** Methane selectivity reflects the proportion of converted CO forming CH₄, calculated as:
  \[
  \text{CH}_4 \text{ Selectivity (\%)} = \frac{F_{\text{CH}_4,\text{out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity is significantly suppressed in composite catalysts: Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑ZSM‑5 shows <3.8%, while Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑S1‑OH records 6.0% (H₂/CO = 3).
- **Observations and Trends:** The zeolite component accelerates olefin desorption, minimizing deep hydrogenation and methane formation compared to bare Na‑FeCₓ*, which produces abundant methane at higher temperatures.

#### 6.2.3 C₂–C₁₀ Olefin Selectivity and α-Olefin Content
- **Description:** Selectivity for C₂–C₁₀ olefins indicates the fraction of converted CO yielding light olefins, while α-olefin content denotes the proportion of linear terminal olefins among C₄⁺ products.
- **Experimental Data:** Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑ZSM‑5 achieves 67.8–71.0% selectivity for C₂–C₁₀ olefins, with 52.1% α-olefins among C₄⁺. Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑S1‑OH attains similar C₂–C₁₀ selectivity and 81.7% α-olefins among C₄⁺.
- **Observations and Trends:** The siliceous s‑S1‑OH zeolite, lacking Brønsted acid sites, further suppresses isomerization, resulting in higher α-olefin selectivity.

#### 6.2.4 Olefin/Alkane (o/a) Ratio and Chain Growth Factor (α)
- **Description:** The o/a ratio (olefin/alkane molar ratio) and chain growth factor (α) reflect the catalyst’s propensity for olefin formation and hydrocarbon chain extension.
- **Experimental Data:** Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑ZSM‑5 exhibits o/a ratios of 3.5–4.2 (α = 3.5 at 82.5% CO conversion; α = 4.2 at 66.8%). Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑S1‑OH shows an o/a ratio of 3.8 at 82.6% CO conversion.
- **Observations and Trends:** Both catalysts favor olefin production, with s‑S1‑OH achieving higher α-olefin selectivity due to its silanol-rich, acid-free framework.

#### 6.2.5 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time; deactivation rate is inferred from the change in conversion per hour.
- **Experimental Data:** Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑ZSM‑5 maintains CO conversion from 68.1% to 67.6% over 600 h, indicating negligible deactivation. In contrast, bare Na‑FeCₓ* drops to 6.0% after reuse.
- **Observations and Trends:** Composite catalysts demonstrate excellent durability, attributed to optimized granule mixing and minimized sodium migration.

#### 6.2.6 Additional Performance Metrics
- **Olefin Productivity:** Na‑FeCₓ*/s‑S1‑OH achieves 13.1 mmol g⁻¹ h⁻¹.
- **C₁₀⁺ Selectivity:** Both composite catalysts maintain low C₁₀⁺ selectivity (6.3% for s‑S1‑OH).
- **Observations:** The engineered zeolite morphology and controlled catalyst contact are critical for sustaining high activity, selectivity, and stability.
