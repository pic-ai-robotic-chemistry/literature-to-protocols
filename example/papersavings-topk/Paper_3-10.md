## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

This section details the synthesis routes applied for the Fischer–Tropsch catalysts, emphasizing reproducibility and precise experimental conditions. Two main synthesis routes are described: the co‑precipitation method for the iron-based catalyst precursor and the hydrothermal method for the ZSM‑5 nanosheet support.

#### • Co‑precipitation Method (Catalyst Precursor Formation)
1. Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation  
   - Reagents: Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O and Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O dissolved in deionized water, with a total molar amount of 0.18 mol and Fe:Zn ratios of 4:1, 2:1, or 1:1.
   - Conditions: The solution is stirred at 700 rpm for 30 minutes. Ammonia is added slowly until the pH reaches 9.
   - Observation: Formation of a stable precipitate, indicating the onset of ZnFe₂O₄ precursor formation.
2. Precipitation–Deposition  
   - Immediately following pH adjustment, the precipitation–deposition step occurs, where metal ions deposit to form a solid precursor.
   - The suspension is then stirred for an additional 1 hour and aged under static conditions for 2 hours.
3. Drying  
   - The wet precursor is dried at 100°C for 12 hours.
4. Calcination  
   - The dried powders are calcined at 400°C for 4 hours in air to convert the precursor into the active ZnFe₂O₄ spinel.
5. Post‑processing  
   - For select catalysts, after drying, a sodium source is impregnated (using Na₂CO₃ solution) and, where applicable, Na₂S is introduced during the incipient wetness impregnation step.
   - A reduction step is subsequently performed in flowing H₂ at 450°C for 5 hours, followed by passivation with 1% O₂/99% N₂ at room temperature for 2 hours.
   - Equipment such as pH meters, rotary evaporators (for solvent removal), and controlled furnaces ensures high reproducibility.

#### • Hydrothermal Method (ZSM‑5 Nanosheet Support)
- Preparation: TEOS serves as the silica source together with TPAOH/TPABr as structure-directing agents.
- Reactor: A Teflon‑lined stainless-steel autoclave is employed.
- Conditions: The hydrothermal synthesis is conducted at 70°C for 72 hours, followed by washing, drying, and a calcination at 550°C for 5 hours.

This systematic approach—starting with precise co‑precipitation steps followed by controlled drying, calcination, and reduction—ensures accurate phase transformations (ZnFe₂O₄ to Fe⁰ and ZnO) and reliable production of catalysts with tailored promoter effects, ultimately contributing to enhanced Fischer–Tropsch performance.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### Catalyst Preparation [1]: 1.5Na-Fe1Zn1 (and Variations 1.5Na0.1S-Fe1Zn1, 1.5Na0.2S-Fe1Zn1)

##### Step [1]: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**:  
  • Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O and Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O in deionized water (exact amounts adjusted to obtain 44.5 wt% Fe and 25.5 wt% Zn).  
  • Ammonia solution (to adjust pH to 9).  
- **Conditions**:  
  • Initial stirring at 700 rpm, temperature maintained at 35 °C.  
  • pH adjusted to 9 through dropwise addition of ammonia.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Dissolve Fe and Zn nitrates in deionized water to ensure a homogeneous solution.  
  2. Stir at 700 rpm for 30 minutes.  
  3. Slowly add ammonia to reach a pH of 9, then stir an additional 60 minutes.  
  4. Allow the mixture to age under static conditions for 2 hours to facilitate complete precipitation of the ZnFe₂O₄ spinel precursor.  
- **Key Steps**: Maintain constant stirring and controlled pH; ensure thorough aging for precursor formation.

##### Step [2]: Catalyst Assembly and Promoter Incorporation
- **Reagents**:  
  • Use the dried FeZn precursor.  
  • For Na modification: Incipient wetness impregnation with a Na₂CO₃ solution to achieve a total Na loading of approximately 1.5 wt%.  
  • For S-modified variants: Include Na₂S solution along with Na₂CO₃ to achieve S loadings of 0.1 or 0.2 wt% while keeping overall Na constant.
- **Conditions**:  
  • Impregnation performed at room temperature; stirring ensures uniform distribution.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Impregnate the dry precursor with the appropriate Na (and Na₂S) solution.  
  2. Stir for 30 minutes to secure complete adsorption of promoters.

##### Step [3]: Purification, Drying & Calcination
- **Purification**:  
  • Filter and wash the precipitate several times with deionized water.  
- **Drying**:  
  • Dry the washed material in a standard oven at 100 °C for 12 hours.  
- **Calcination**:  
  • Calcine the dried sample in air at 400 °C for 4 hours (ramp rate controlled though not specified).

##### Step [4]: Catalyst Activation
- **Conditions**:  
  • Reduce under a flowing H₂ atmosphere at 450 °C and 0.2 MPa for 5 hours (H₂ flow rate maintained at 40 mL/min).  
- **Process**:  
  1. Conduct reduction to transform ZnFe₂O₄ into active Fe⁰ and ZnO phases, ensuring catalyst dispersion.

**Key Control Points & Troubleshooting:**  
• Ensure uniform precipitation via consistent stirring and pH control.

## 5. Catalytic Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The catalyst activation process is a critical pre‐reaction treatment designed to convert the precursor ZnFe₂O₄ into the active phases—metallic Fe (Fe⁰) and ZnO—in a controlled hydrogen-rich environment. This reduction step is essential for developing the active sites necessary for efficient Fischer-Tropsch catalysis.

#### 5.1.1 Activation Temperature  
Activation is executed at 450 °C in a fixed-bed reactor. This temperature is specifically chosen to facilitate the complete reduction of iron oxides, ensuring the transformation of ZnFe₂O₄ into Fe⁰ and promoting the formation of ZnO. The chosen temperature supports high crystallinity of the active phases while maintaining catalyst stability during the reduction.

#### 5.1.2 Activation Pressure  
The reduction is conducted under a controlled pressure of 0.2 MPa in the reactor. Operating at this moderate pressure enhances hydrogen interaction with the catalyst surface, optimizing the reduction kinetics without causing excessive sintering or agglomeration of metallic particles.

#### 5.1.3 Activation Gas  
A pure hydrogen atmosphere is used for the catalyst activation, which is critical in driving the reduction reaction. The absence of other gases ensures maximal hydrogen availability, thereby achieving effective reduction of the ZnFe₂O₄ precursor into the active metallic and oxide phases. This pure H₂ environment contrasts with the mixed gas compositions employed during the later reaction phase.

#### 5.1.4 Activation Time  
A duration of 5 hours is applied to the reduction process. This length of time is pivotal to ensuring complete conversion of the precursor phases while minimizing the risk of over-reduction or sintering. The extended reduction period allows for uniform activation across the catalyst batch.

### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Phase and Structure
- **Description:** The crystalline phases and their evolution during catalyst preparation and reaction were determined by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a Bruker D2 PHASER with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å), scanning from 2θ = 5° to 90° at 0.02°/step.
- **Findings:** Fresh catalysts exhibited the ZnFe₂O₄ spinel phase (PDF#77–11), with a weak ZnO peak at 32° (PDF#79–208). Upon reduction, ZnFe₂O₄ was fully transformed into metallic Fe⁰ (PDF#87–721) and ZnO, as evidenced by the disappearance of ZnFe₂O₄ peaks and the emergence of Fe⁰ and ZnO peaks. The Fe⁰ phase displayed higher peak intensity, indicating improved crystallinity relative to ZnO.
- **Observations:** The transformation to Fe⁰ and ZnO confirms successful reduction, with enhanced Fe⁰ crystallinity supporting catalytic activity. The presence of ZnO inhibits Fe⁰ aggregation, promoting dispersion and stability.

#### 6.1.2 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Textural properties were assessed by N₂ physisorption using a Micromeritics ASAP 2020 analyzer at 77 K. Samples (~300 mg) were degassed at 200 °C for 4 h prior to analysis. Surface area was calculated by the BET method, pore volume at P/P₀ = 0.9997, and pore size by the BJH method.
- **Findings:** The BET surface area increased from 37 m²/g for 1.5Na-Fe to 47 m²/g for 1.5Na-Fe1Zn1 after Zn addition. The average pore diameter decreased from 5.90 nm to 3.10 nm, and the pore volume increased, indicating a more dispersed and porous structure.
- **Observations:** Zn promotion enhances surface area and reduces pore size, facilitating better reactant diffusion and improved catalyst dispersion.

#### 6.1.3 Elemental Composition
- **Description:** Elemental analysis was performed by ICP-OES (PerkinElmer Avio 200) after dissolving 0.05 g of catalyst in nitric acid.
- **Findings:** For 1.5Na-Fe1Zn1, the fresh catalyst contained Fe 44.5 wt%, Zn 25.5 wt%, and Na 1.7 wt%. After 20 h and 1000 h of reaction, Fe, Zn, and Na contents remained stable (Fe: 46.8/42.5 wt%, Zn: 27.3/29.0 wt%, Na: 2.1/1.8 wt%). S-modified catalysts contained 0.11 wt% (1.5Na0.1S-Fe1Zn1) and 0.22 wt% (1.5Na0.2S-Fe1Zn1) sulfur.
- **Observations:** The elemental composition remains consistent during long-term operation, indicating good catalyst stability and promoter retention.

#### 6.1.4 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Morphological features and particle sizes were examined by SEM (Hitachi Regulus 8100) and TEM (FEI TE20). HADDF-STEM and EDX mapping provided elemental distribution.
- **Findings:** SEM images of fresh catalysts revealed block-like ZnFe₂O₄ particles with dense ZnO protrusions. After reduction, TEM showed large spherical Fe⁰ nanoparticles (260–290 nm) decorated with smaller ZnO nanoparticles (60–70 nm), forming a Fe⁰@ZnO structure. HADDF-STEM/EDX confirmed Zn and Na enrichment on the ZnO surface.
- **Observations:** The Fe⁰@ZnO architecture, with well-dispersed ZnO on Fe⁰, inhibits Fe aggregation and enhances catalyst stability. Na preferentially locates on ZnO, further modifying surface properties.

#### 6.1.5 Reduction and Surface Properties
- **Description:** H₂-TPR and TPD (MicrotracBEL analyzer) characterized reduction behavior and gas adsorption/desorption. H₂-TPR was conducted up to 800 °C at 15 °C/min in 10% H₂/Ar; TPD tests used 100 mg samples with temperature ramps from 50 °C to 850 °C.
- **Findings:** H₂-TPR profiles showed three main reduction peaks: ZnFe₂O₄ to Fe₃O₄ (~335 °C), Fe₃O₄ to FeO (~425 °C), and FeO to Fe (~610 °C). Increasing Na₂S content shifted these peaks to lower temperatures, indicating facilitated reduction.
- **Observations:** Na₂S addition enhances reducibility by weakening the electron-donating effect of Na, but does not significantly alter textural properties.

#### 6.1.6 Iron Phase Distribution and Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Mossbauer spectroscopy and TG analysis (Mettler Toledo TGA/1100SF) were used to quantify iron phases and assess thermal stability.
- **Findings:** Mossbauer spectra of spent catalysts revealed the presence of Fe₃O₄, χ-Fe₅C₂, Fe⁰, and Fe₃C, with phase proportions correlating to catalytic activity. TG analysis showed that carbon deposition is a primary cause of deactivation.
- **Observations:** The distribution of iron phases and resistance to carbon deposition are critical for sustained catalyst performance.



### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO and CO₂ Conversion
- **Description:** CO and CO₂ conversion quantify the fraction of CO or CO₂ in the feed that is transformed by the catalyst. The conversion is calculated as:
\[
\text{CO}_2\ \text{Conversion} = \frac{(A_{\text{CO}_2}/A_{\text{N}_2})_{\text{in}} - (A_{\text{CO}_2}/A_{\text{N}_2})_{\text{out}}}{(A_{\text{CO}_2}/A_{\text{N}_2})_{\text{in}}} \times 100\%
\]
where \(A_{\text{CO}_2}\) and \(A_{\text{N}_2}\) are the GC peak areas of CO₂ and N₂, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Under CO₂-FTS conditions (320 °C, 1 MPa, GHSV 1200 mL·g_cat⁻¹·h⁻¹), the 1.5Na-Fe₁Zn₁ catalyst achieved a CO₂ conversion of 39.0% at H₂/CO₂ = 3, and 17.5% at H₂/CO₂ = 1. In CO-FTS, Na₂S-modified catalysts reached CO conversion of ~96%.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing the H₂/CO₂ ratio from 1 to 3 significantly enhances CO₂ conversion. The addition of Na₂S suppresses activity in CO₂-FTS but markedly improves stability and conversion in CO-FTS.

#### 6.2.2 Product Selectivity (CO, CH₄, C₂–C₅ Olefins, C₄⁺ Olefins)
- **Description:** Product selectivity is defined as the fraction of converted CO or CO₂ forming a specific product, calculated as:
\[
S_i = \frac{f_i \cdot A_i / (A_{\text{N}_2})_{\text{out}}}{f_{\text{CO}_2} \cdot [(A_{\text{CO}_2}/A_{\text{N}_2})_{\text{in}} - (A_{\text{CO}_2}/A_{\text{N}_2})_{\text{out}}]} \times 100\%
\]
where \(f_i\) is the calibration factor for product \(i\).
- **Experimental Data:** Coupling 1.5Na-Fe₁Zn₁ with ZSM-5 nanosheets increased C₂–C₅ light olefins selectivity up to 64.6%. In CO-FTS with Na₂S, C₄⁺ olefins selectivity reached 65.4%. Methane and light alkane selectivity were minimized under these optimized conditions.
- **Observations and Trends:** The use of ZSM-5 promotes light olefins by cracking heavier hydrocarbons, while Na₂S addition in CO-FTS shifts selectivity toward long-chain olefins and enhances catalyst stability.

#### 6.2.3 Chain Growth Factor (α Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor, derived from the Anderson-Schulz-Flory (ASF) distribution, represents the probability of chain growth during hydrocarbon formation:
\[
\alpha = \left(\frac{n_{n+1}}{n_n}\right)^{1/(n+1-n)}
\]
where \(n_n\) and \(n_{n+1}\) are the molar fractions of hydrocarbons with carbon numbers \(n\) and \(n+1\).
- **Experimental Data:** While explicit α values are not numerically reported, the product distribution follows ASF behavior. Lower α values (favoring light olefins) are achieved with ZSM-5, while higher α (favoring long-chain olefins) is observed with Na₂S in CO-FTS.
- **Observations and Trends:** Catalyst composition and promoter selection (Zn, Na, Na₂S) allow tuning of α, thus controlling the hydrocarbon chain length distribution.

#### 6.2.4 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO or CO₂ conversion over time. The deactivation rate is calculated as:
\[
\text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The 1.5Na-Fe₁Zn₁ catalyst maintained stable CO₂ conversion over 1000 h in CO₂-FTS, with minimal changes in elemental composition. In CO-FTS, Na₂S-modified catalysts sustained ~96% CO conversion with negligible deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Zn addition inhibits Fe₀ aggregation, enhancing long-term stability. Na₂S is critical for suppressing deactivation in CO-FTS, as evidenced by stable conversion and preserved catalyst morphology after extended operation.

#### 6.2.5 Additional Performance Indicators
- **Space-Time Yield (STY):** The STY of lower olefins is reported, with values maximized under optimal catalyst and reaction conditions.
- **RWGS Equilibrium Degree (η_RWGS):** The degree to which the reverse water-gas shift reaction approaches equilibrium is evaluated under varying GHSV, supporting mechanistic insights.
