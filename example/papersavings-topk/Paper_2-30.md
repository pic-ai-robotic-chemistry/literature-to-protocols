## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post‑processing Steps

The synthesis of Mn‑χ‑Fe5C2 is based on a controlled multi‐step procedure ensuring reproducibility and phase purity. The following detailed process is strictly extracted from the experimental report:

#### Precursor Mixing / Co‑precipitation
1. A Fe–Al alloy (50:50 by weight) is mixed with an 8 M KOH solution at 70°C under constant stirring.  
   • Reagents: Fe–Al alloy and 8 M KOH  
   • Observed phenomenon: Dissolution of aluminium, indicated by changes in solution clarity.
2. Upon aluminium removal, the precursor is washed sequentially with deionized water (approximately 10 rinses) and ethanol (7 rinses) to eliminate K⁺ and AlO₂⁻ ions.

#### Precipitation–Deposition (Mn Impregnation)
3. Immediately following the washing step—and prior to drying—the porous Raney iron precursor is subjected to wet impregnation using a Mn(NO₃)₂ solution.  
   • Reagent: Mn(NO₃)₂ with promoter levels ranging from 0.5 to 12.5 wt%  
   • Equipment: Standard laboratory glassware ensuring homogeneous mixing  
   • This step guarantees uniform manganese distribution, critical for enhanced catalytic performance.

#### Drying and Calcination (Passivation)
4. The impregnated precursor is dried in a vacuum oven at room temperature for 12 hours.  
   • Conditions: Vacuum drying at ambient temperature  
5. Subsequently, the dried sample is passivated (serving as a calcination step) by exposure to a flow of 1% O₂ in helium at room temperature for 20 hours.  
   • Equipment: Glove box under inert argon for initial drying, followed by controlled gas flow setup for passivation.

#### Carburization
6. Carburization is performed in a stainless‑steel tubular fixed‑bed reactor using a syngas mix (H₂/CO/He = 100/3.2/21.8).  
   • Temperature: Heated at a controlled ramp rate of 1°C/min up to 350°C  
   • Dwell Time: Maintained at 350°C for 6 hours  
   • Observed Results: Transformation of the passivated precursor into phase‑pure χ‑Fe5C2, as confirmed by in situ Mössbauer spectroscopy and ETEM analyses.

This structured synthesis sequence—emphasizing precise reagent concentrations, defined temperature profiles, and careful integration of manganese promotion—ensures the reproducibility and scalability of the Mn‑χ‑Fe5C2 catalyst for Fischer–Tropsch applications.

### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Phase Composition and Structural Purity
- **Description:** The phase composition and structural purity of the Mn‑χ‑Fe₅C₂ catalyst were determined using in situ Mössbauer spectroscopy, in situ X-ray diffraction (XRD) with Rietveld refinement, and environmental transmission electron microscopy (ETEM). These techniques are critical for confirming the exclusive formation and stability of the active χ‑Fe₅C₂ (Hägg carbide) phase, which is essential for optimal Fischer–Tropsch performance.
- **Findings:** In situ Mössbauer spectroscopy, conducted at −153°C under syngas flow, revealed only signals corresponding to χ‑Fe₅C₂, with no evidence of other iron phases, confirming phase purity and stability under reaction conditions. In situ XRD, performed during carburization (H₂/CO/He = 100/3.2/21.8, ramped to 350°C at 0.5°C min⁻¹, 6 h dwell), showed diffraction patterns matching published lattice parameters for χ‑Fe₅C₂. Rietveld refinement yielded a goodness of fit (χ² = 7.58%, Rp = 18.7%, Rwp = 15.3%, Rexp = 5.6%), further supporting the assignment of a single, phase-pure carbide.
- **Observations:** The exclusive presence of χ‑Fe₅C₂ was maintained after manganese promotion and throughout catalytic testing, demonstrating the robustness of the synthesis and activation protocol.

#### 6.1.2 Lattice Structure and Crystallographic Features
- **Description:** High-resolution ETEM, combined with inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) analysis, was employed to visualize the lattice structure and monitor the phase transformation at the nanoscale. This approach provides direct evidence of the catalyst’s crystallinity and orientation.
- **Findings:** ETEM images revealed the transformation from passivated Raney iron to phase-pure χ‑Fe₅C₂ grains. IFFT analysis identified a lattice spacing of approximately 2.7 Å along the (311) direction, characteristic of the χ‑Fe₅C₂ phase. The transformation was completed in less than 0.5 h, with nanoscale features clearly resolved (scale bars: 5 nm).
- **Observations:** The observed lattice spacing and orientation confirm the successful formation of the desired carbide phase at the atomic level.

#### 6.1.3 Morphology and Particle Evolution
- **Description:** The morphological evolution of the catalyst was tracked using ETEM and high-resolution TEM (HRTEM), providing insights into particle shape, size, and surface features before and after carburization.
- **Findings:** The passivated Raney iron precursor appeared as crystallized iron particles enveloped by an amorphous oxide layer. Upon carburization, these particles transformed into well-defined χ‑Fe₅C₂ grains, with no evidence of manganese migration or carbon deposition observed after extended catalytic operation.
- **Observations:** The preservation of particle morphology and absence of deactivation-related features (e.g., carbon buildup) are attributed to the manganese promoter, which aids in surface cleaning and oxygen removal.

#### 6.1.4 Elemental Composition and Oxidation State
- **Description:** The chemical composition and oxidation state of iron were confirmed by in situ Mössbauer spectroscopy, which is sensitive to the local environment and valence state of iron atoms.
- **Findings:** Mössbauer spectra collected after carburization and under reaction conditions showed only the signature of χ‑Fe₅C₂, with no detectable iron oxides or alternative carbides. This confirms the complete reduction and carburization of the precursor.

### 6.2 Catalyst Performance

#### 6.2.1 CO Conversion
- **Description:** CO conversion quantifies the fraction of CO in the feed that is transformed during the reaction, calculated as:
\[
\text{CO Conversion} = \frac{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The Mn‑χ‑Fe₅C₂ catalyst achieved CO conversion values up to 53% under industrially relevant conditions (320–325°C, 2.3 MPa, SV = 100,000 ml g_cat⁻¹ h⁻¹). Across the tested temperature range (250–320°C) and pressures (2.3–3.0 MPa), high CO conversion was consistently observed.
- **Observations and Trends:** The incorporation of manganese significantly enhanced CO conversion compared to unpromoted systems. Stable conversion was maintained over extended operation (225 h), indicating robust catalytic activity.

#### 6.2.2 CO₂ Selectivity
- **Description:** CO₂ selectivity measures the proportion of converted CO that forms CO₂, reflecting the extent of unwanted oxidation:
\[
\text{CO₂ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CO₂,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** CO₂ selectivity for Mn‑χ‑Fe₅C₂ was consistently low, typically in the range of 9–11% (e.g., 9.3% at 250°C, 2.3 MPa).
- **Observations and Trends:** Manganese promotion effectively suppressed CO₂ formation, favoring oxygen removal as water and enhancing overall carbon efficiency (~89% at 250°C).

#### 6.2.3 CH₄ Selectivity
- **Description:** CH₄ selectivity is the fraction of converted CO yielding methane:
\[
\text{CH₄ Selectivity} = \frac{F_{\text{CH₄,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Methane selectivity was minimized in the Mn‑χ‑Fe₅C₂ system, supporting high selectivity toward longer-chain hydrocarbons.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalyst’s design and manganese promotion contributed to low methane formation, which is advantageous for maximizing higher hydrocarbon yields.

#### 6.2.4 C₃⁺ Selectivity
- **Description:** C₃⁺ selectivity represents the fraction of converted CO forming hydrocarbons with three or more carbon atoms:
\[
\text{C₃⁺ Selectivity} = 100 - \frac{F_{\text{CO₂,out}} + F_{\text{CH₄,out}} + 2F_{\text{C₂H₂,out}} + 2F_{\text{C₂H₄,out}} + 2F_{\text{C₂H₆,out}}}{F_{\text{CO,in}} - F_{\text{CO,out}}} \times 100
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The catalyst exhibited high selectivity for C₃⁺ hydrocarbons, particularly C₂–C₁₀ linear α-olefins (LAOs).
- **Observations and Trends:** Enhanced C₃⁺ selectivity is directly linked to the high chain growth probability and the suppression of undesired by-products.

#### 6.2.5 α Factor (Chain Growth Factor)
- **Description:** The α factor, derived from the Anderson–Schulz–Flory (ASF) model, indicates the probability of chain propagation:
\[
\text{ASF:} \quad \ln\left(\frac{W_n}{n}\right) = n \cdot \ln\alpha + \ln\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{\alpha}\right)
\]
- **Experimental Data:** Mn‑χ‑Fe₅C₂ displayed α values in the range of 0.82–0.86, favoring the formation of longer-chain olefins.
- **Observations and Trends:** The high α factor correlates with the catalyst’s ability to produce C₂–C₁₀ LAOs efficiently, as confirmed by both experimental and simulated product distributions.

#### 6.2.6 Catalyst Stability and Deactivation Rate
- **Description:** Catalyst stability is assessed by monitoring CO conversion over time; the deactivation rate is defined as:
\[
\text{Deactivation Rate} = \frac{\text{CO Conversion}_{\text{initial}} - \text{CO Conversion}_{\text{final}}}{\text{Overall reaction time}} \quad (\text{h}^{-1})
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The Mn‑χ‑Fe₅C₂ catalyst maintained stable CO conversion over 225 h with negligible deactivation.
- **Observations and Trends:** Manganese promotion suppressed carbon deposition and maintained phase purity, resulting in exceptional long-term stability.

#### 6.2.7 Catalyst Time Yield (CTY)
- **Description:** CTY quantifies the amount of product generated per unit catalyst per unit time:
\[
\text{CTY}_{\text{CO}} = \frac{X_{\text{CO}} \times \text{CO}_{\text{inlet}}}{22,400 \times 3,600}
\]
- **Experimental Data:** The Mn‑χ‑Fe₅C₂ catalyst achieved CTY values up to 265 μmol g⁻¹ s⁻¹ under optimal conditions.
- **Observations and Trends:** High CTY reflects the combined benefits of high CO conversion, selectivity, and catalyst stability imparted by manganese promotion.
