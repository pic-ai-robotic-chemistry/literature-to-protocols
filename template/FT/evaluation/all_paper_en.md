### **Fischer–Tropsch Experimental Report Comprehensive Evaluation Questionnaire**  

#### **I. Assessment of Content Completeness**  

##### **1. Reagents and Equipment Section**  

1. All reagent categories (metal precursors, additives, gases, acid-base regulators, surfactants, redox agents, etc.) are accurately extracted without omissions or redundancies.  
2. Empty categories (categories without relevant reagents) are skipped as required by the template, and no redundant listing is present.  
3. Necessary details of reagents (name, chemical formula, purity, supplier, purpose, etc.) are fully covered.  
4. Multiple roles of multifunctional reagents (e.g., "promoter + surface modifier") are clearly stated and specifically listed in the reagent categories.  
5. All equipment categories (synthesis, characterization, activation, product analysis, etc.) are accurately extracted, covering the entire experimental stages (synthesis, characterization, reaction).  
6. Necessary details of equipment (model, specification parameters, manufacturer, etc.) are fully listed.  
7. Equipment function descriptions are concise and consistent with the actual experimental role.  

##### **2. Synthesis Methods and Procedures**  

8. Synthesis methods/procedures are strictly derived from the article, and the sequence of steps (precursor weighing → mixing → reaction → purification → drying → calcination, etc.) is completely consistent with the article.  
9. Operational details in each step (dosages of solid, liquid, and gas reagents, order of reagent use, equipment used) are fully recorded.  
10. For the synthesis of multiple samples, the synthesis process of each sample should be listed separately and completely, and the synthesis processes of different samples should not be described crosswise.  
11. Experimental phenomena (color changes, precipitation formation) and key parameters (concentration, temperature, pressure, pH, heating rate, stirring speed) are fully recorded.  

##### **3. Characterization Methods**  

12. All characterization techniques (XRD, TEM, BET, etc.) are from the original text, and no unmentioned methods are fabricated.  
13. The purposes of each characterization technique (component analysis, crystal phase identification, morphology, etc.) are fully listed.  
14. Details such as sample pretreatment, sample preparation processes, and instrument parameters (scanning range, gas flow rate) are fully extracted.  

##### **4. Catalytic Evaluation (Activation/Reaction Conditions)**  

15. Activation parameters (gas composition, space-time velocity, temperature, pressure, duration, heating rate) and reaction parameters (H₂/CO ratio, space-time velocity, temperature, pressure, duration) are accurate and without omissions, consistent with the original text.  
16. For activation and reaction parameters involving multiple samples, the specific parameters of each sample should be listed separately without crosswise description.  

##### **5. Results Section (Characterization/Performance)**  

17. Characterization data (crystalline structure, elemental valence state) and performance indicators (CO conversion, CO₂ selectivity, CH₄ selectivity, C₂+ hydrocarbon selectivity, α factor, etc.) are strictly extracted without omissions or inferred content.  

#### **II. Assessment of Information Accuracy**  

##### **1. Reagents/Equipment**  

1. Reagent chemical formulas and equipment models are consistent with the original text, and information such as purity and suppliers is error-free.  
2. Gas parameters (pressure, flow rate) and equipment size/quantity (reactor volume) match the experimental description.  
3. Descriptions of reagent functions and equipment purposes are accurate and complete.  

##### **2. Synthesis/Characterization/Evaluation**  

4. Reaction conditions (temperature, pressure) and process sequence, sample dosage and treatment methods, and instrument working parameters (voltage/current, heating rate) have numerical values and units completely identical to the original text.  
5. Descriptions of performance indicators (e.g., CO conversion) and trend analysis (e.g., influence of high temperature on selectivity) conform to the original conclusions.  

##### **3. Result Data**  

6. Characterization results (XRD peak positions, BET surface area) and catalytic performance data (CO conversion, CO₂ selectivity, CH₄ selectivity, C₂+ hydrocarbon selectivity, α factor, stability, etc.) are consistent with the original data without deviation.  

#### **III. Assessment of Format Normativeness**  

1. Title hierarchy (##, ###, ####) meets the template requirements, and numbering and ordering are correct (e.g., "#### 1.1.1 Metal Precursors").  
2. Each chapter is arranged in the order of the original text, and subsections (such as comparative analysis of synthesis methods and independent sections for characterization techniques) have clear logic.  
3. Reagent/equipment information is presented in a standardized list form (e.g., "- **Reagent Name**: Chemical formula, purity, supplier, purpose").  
4. Formula typesetting (LaTeX format) and unit notation (℃, m²/g, min, rpm) are uniform and standardized without conversion errors.  
5. Reagents/equipment under the same category are grouped by function (e.g., "promoter precursors", "pH regulators") for easy reference.  
6. Contents of different catalysts/methods are independent sections (e.g., "Catalyst A", "coprecipitation method") without confusion.  

#### **IV. Assessment of Practicality and Functionality**  

1. Reagent/equipment information is detailed enough for direct experimental reproduction (e.g., reagent purity, equipment model/quantity).  
2. Synthesis procedures/characterization methods are clearly and consistently described without omissions, and key parameters (stirring speed, activation time, test duration) are complete to support repeated experiments.  
3. Classification logic is clear (e.g., reagents divided by "metal precursors → additives → solvents → acid-base regulators", equipment grouped by "synthesis → reaction → characterization").  
4. Parameter definitions (space-time velocity, α factor) are friendly to non-professional readers, and trend analysis assists in understanding experimental principles.  
5. Empty categories and unmentioned steps (e.g., "activation not reported") are marked according to the template without information gaps or fabrication.  

#### **V. Open-Ended Questions**  

##### **1. Content Supplementation**  

- Which reagents/equipment/characterization methods in the report still need supplementary information? (Example: ______)  
- Are there missing key parameters in the synthesis procedures/catalytic evaluation (e.g., stirrer model, gas purity)?  
- Is the description of synthesis/characterization/evaluation content for multiple samples clear, complete, and non-crosswise?  

##### **2. Expression Optimization**  

- Are there difficult-to-understand parts in the format? (e.g., confusing title numbering, inconsistent list formats)  
- Are there ambiguous experimental conditions/data in the expression? (e.g., "high-temperature activation" without specifying the temperature range, "higher selectivity" without numerical values)  

##### **3. Reproducibility Suggestions**  

- From the perspective of experimental reproduction, what details are missing in the report? (e.g., equipment operation parameters, data processing steps, error range)  

### **Scoring Instructions**  

- **Quantitative Scoring**: The first four parts use a 5-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree), where higher scores indicate that the report quality more meets the template requirements.  
- **Qualitative Feedback**: Open-ended questions are used to locate specific improvement directions, such as "missing characterization equipment model" or "vague temperature range in synthesis procedures", optimizing the report's rigor combined with quantitative scores.
