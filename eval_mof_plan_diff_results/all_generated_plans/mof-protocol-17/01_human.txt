#### MOF Preparation [1]: SMM-MOF 1 (Dy-Based SMM-MOF)

  • Reflux set to 85 °C for 24 hours
  
- **Process**:  
  1. In a dry Schlenk flask, combine phenol and NaH in THF and stir for 30 minutes.  
  2. Add DyCl₃ and NaBPh₄ to the solution.  
  3. Heat the mixture under reflux at 85 °C for 24 hours, ensuring an inert environment throughout.

- **Key Steps**:  
  • Maintain strict oxygen-free conditions (using glovebox/Schlenk techniques).  
  • Ensure complete dissolution and proper reagent ratios before heating.

##### Step 2: MOF Assembly & Crystallization
- **Reagents**:  
  • Following the initial reflux, filter the reaction mixture to remove by-products.

- **Conditions**:

- **Process**:

## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

In this study, the MOF activation procedure was distinct from typical thermal or vacuum activation protocols. Rather than employing conventional parameters such as defined heating temperatures, controlled pressures, specific gas compositions, or flow rates, the activation step was exclusively achieved by a photochemical treatment aimed at modulating the magnetic properties of the MOF.

The activation process involved exposing the synthesized SMM-MOF to controlled 365 nm UV irradiation under an inert argon atmosphere. This photoinduced treatment was specifically designed to trigger electron transfer processes that generate BPy radical anions within the framework. As a result, the coordination environment around the single-molecule magnet centers is subtly modified, leading to the observed photomagnetic switching behavior. The generated radicals influence magnetic relaxation dynamics and facilitate a reversible modulation of key magnetic parameters such as coercivity, as verified by repeated diffraction and magnetic measurements.

It should be noted that the study did not report standard activation conditions—such as a defined activation temperature, an activation time period, vacuum levels, or activation gas flow rates—that are typically used to remove solvents or open the pore structure. Instead, the focus was on the photoactivation step, which was qualitatively monitored by maintaining the structural integrity of the MOF. This ensured that while the photoresponsive behavior was effectively induced, the overall crystallinity and framework order were preserved, as confirmed by PXRD and single-crystal X-ray analyses.

In summary, the selected activation strategy for the MOF relied solely on photochemical activation via 365 nm UV irradiation, thereby differentiating it from activation methods associated with characterization techniques like TGA or in-situ PXRD. This targeted approach ensured that the induced radical generation and subsequent modulation of magnetic properties did not compromise the material’s structural robustness.

### 5.2 Application/Performance Evaluation

The performance evaluation of the MOF materials focused on assessing their single-molecule magnetic behavior under controlled external stimuli. A test temperature of 150 K was employed during initial performance measurements, with low-temperature experiments conducted at 2 K to reveal intrinsic magnetic properties. At 2 K, the Dy-based MOF demonstrated a high coercive field of 4500 Oe under dc magnetic susceptibility measurements using a Quantum Design MPMS-squid VSM magnetometer. This low-temperature environment was critical for quantifying the energy barrier for magnetization reversal and confirming exceptional single-molecule magnet behavior.

Pressure conditions were not explicitly manipulated or reported during the evaluations. Instead, the experimental design emphasized gas composition control. While the MOF was exposed to ambient air to introduce oxidative stimuli for reverting the photogenerated state, UV irradiation was specifically performed under a nitrogen atmosphere to prevent premature oxidation. This careful management of the gas environment ensured reproducibility and clarity of the magnetic modulation observed.

UV irradiation played a pivotal role in the photomagnetic switching. The MOF samples underwent exposure to a 365 nm UV light source for 25 to 30 minutes, triggering a rapid, reversible color transition from yellow to deep blue-violet. This photochromic transition was directly linked to the generation of photogenerated radicals, which accelerated the magnetic relaxation dynamics and reduced the energy barriers. Reversible switching was confirmed by a subsequent oxygen exposure that restored the original yellow state, underscoring the material's responsiveness to external stimuli. Moreover, the performance tests were repeated over five irradiation cycles, with observations highlighting a gradual reduction in radical generation efficiency after the second cycle, pointing to fatigue effects in prolonged cycling.

These precisely documented parameters—test temperature, gas composition, irradiation conditions, and magnetic measurement techniques—provide a robust framework ensuring that the evaluation outcomes for the MOF’s magnetic and photoresponsive properties are both reproducible and directly traceable to the experimental conditions outlined in the article.

### 6.2 Performance Results

#### 6.2.1 Single-Molecule Magnet (SMM) Properties

- **Description:** SMM performance is characterized by the energy barrier for magnetic relaxation (\(U_{\text{eff}}\)), coercive field (\(H_c\)), blocking temperature (\(T_B\)), and the presence of magnetic hysteresis. These metrics quantify the ability of the MOF to retain magnetization and exhibit slow magnetic relaxation, essential for data storage and spintronic applications.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **Energy Barrier (\(U_{\text{eff}}\)):** For SMM-MOF 1 (Dy-based), \(U_{\text{eff}}\) values of 1048(17) K and 822(46) K were determined in the pristine state. After UV irradiation (1uv), these decreased to 1000(9) K and 641(34) K.
  - **Coercive Field (\(H_c\)):** At 2 K, \(H_c\) was 4500 Oe for the pristine sample and reduced to 1300 Oe after UV irradiation.
  - **Blocking Temperature (\(T_B\)):** The hysteresis loop remained open up to 20 K (pristine) and 14 K (UV-irradiated).
- **Observations and Trends:** The Dy-based MOF exhibits record-high energy barriers and coercive fields for 2D MOFs. UV irradiation induces a significant decrease in both \(U_{\text{eff}}\) and \(H_c\), demonstrating efficient photomodulation of magnetic properties. The structural integrity and SMM behavior are retained after multiple cycles of photoactivation and oxidation.

#### 6.2.2 Photochromic and Photomagnetic Switching

- **Description:** Photochromic response is assessed by reversible color change and corresponding modulation of magnetic properties upon UV irradiation and O₂ exposure. Performance is quantified by changes in optical absorption, magnetic relaxation, and coercivity.
- **Experimental Data:**
  - **Photochromic Response:** Rapid color change from yellow to deep blue-violet upon 365 nm UV irradiation (6 W), with new UV–vis absorption bands at 375 and 570 nm indicating BPy radical formation. Reversion to yellow and disappearance of these bands upon O₂ exposure.
  - **Magnetic Modulation:** UV irradiation accelerates magnetic relaxation (shorter relaxation times, τ) and narrows the hysteresis loop. The process is reversible, with magnetic properties restored after O₂ treatment.
- **Observations and Trends:** The MOF demonstrates robust, reversible photomagnetic switching over at least five cycles, though some fatigue is observed (declining radical yield and incomplete spectral recovery). The direct correlation between BPy radical formation and magnetic property modulation is supported by UV–vis, Raman, and EPR spectroscopy.

#### 6.2.3 Cycling Stability

- **Description:** Cycling stability evaluates the retention of photochromic and magnetic performance over repeated activation–deactivation cycles.
- **Experimental Data:** Five consecutive cycles of UV irradiation and O₂ exposure were performed. The material maintained photochromic and magnetic responsiveness, with gradual decline in radical generation efficiency and minor loss of spectral features.
- **Observations and Trends:** The MOF exhibits good cycling stability, with partial fatigue attributed to depletion of electron donors. Most structural and functional properties are retained after multiple cycles.

#### 6.2.4 Comparative Performance of Analogues

- **Description:** Performance comparison with Y(III) and Gd(III) analogues assesses the role of the metal center in SMM and photochromic behavior.
- **Experimental Data:** All analogues display reversible photochromic switching. Only the Dy-based MOF exhibits pronounced SMM behavior and photomodulation of magnetic properties; Y(III) and Gd(III) analogues serve as structural benchmarks with weaker or obscured radical signals.
- **Observations and Trends:** The unique combination of high SMM performance and efficient photomagnetic switching is exclusive to the Dy-based MOF, highlighting the critical role of Dy(III) centers and BPy radical formation in achieving advanced multifunctionality.
