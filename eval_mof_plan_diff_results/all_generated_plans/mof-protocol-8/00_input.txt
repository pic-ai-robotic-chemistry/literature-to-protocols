## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The synthesis techniques for the zirconium-based MOFs described in the article are predominantly based on solvothermal methods. These methods facilitate controlled crystal growth and reproducibility, ensuring that the confined formation of metal‐halide superlattices within the MOF frameworks is both reliable and scalable. A comparative overview shows that the careful control of reagent mixing, modulator addition, and thermal conditions leads to high-quality crystalline products, though differences exist in reaction temperatures and durations across various MOFs.

#### Solvothermal Method Example: NU-1000
1. **Precursor Mixing/Dissolution**  
   - • Reagents: ZrOCl₂·8H₂O (388 mg, 1.20 mmol) and H₄TBAPy are dissolved in N,N‐diethylformamide (DEF, 32 mL).  
   - • Equipment: Sealed vial with ultrasonic assistance ensures uniform dissolution.
2. **Modulator or Structure-Directing Agent Addition**  
   - • Benzoic acid (10.8 g, 88.43 mmol) is introduced to enhance crystallinity.
3. **Crystallization**  
   - • Conditions: The mixture is heated at 100°C–120°C for 1–2 days in a preheated oven using sealed vials.  
   - • Observations: Formation of well-faceted crystals is noted.
4. **Cooling and Collection**  
   - • The reaction mixture is allowed to cool to room temperature before crystal collection.
5. **Washing and Solvent Exchange**  
   - • Procedure: Crystals undergo sequential washes with DMF (20 mL × 3) followed by ethanol or methanol (20 mL × 3) to remove residual reagents.
6. **Drying**  
   - • Conditions: Crystals are dried in air at room temperature.
7. **Activation**  
   - • Method: Thermal activation is carried out—often under vacuum at 150°C for 12 h—to remove guest molecules and fully activate the framework.
8. **Additional Post-treatments**  
   - • For example, an optional amine post-synthetic modification is conducted to tailor photoluminescence properties.

#### Comparative Procedures for Other MOFs
- **NU-600**: Mixes ZrCl₄ with H₄TCPB-Br₂ in DEF using acetic acid as the modulator, crystallized at 120°C for 24 h.
- **PCN-700**: Involves ZrCl₄ (9 mg), H₂Me₂-BPDC (9 mg), trifluoroacetic acid (0.08 mL), and DMF (1.5 mL) in a 20 mL vial heated at 120°C for 24 h.
- **PCN-609/606**: Follow similar stepwise procedures with extended reaction times (48 h) and subsequent amine treatments to fine-tune electronic and photoluminescence characteristics.

This structured, step-by-step protocol with detailed reagent quantities, controlled reaction conditions, and methodical post-processing ensures that researchers can accurately replicate the synthesis of these MOF superlattices.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### MOF Preparation [1]: NU-1000
##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation
- **Reagents**: 1.5 g ZrOCl₂·8H₂O, 0.8 g H₄TBAPy, 2.0 g benzoic acid, 30 mL DMF.
- **Conditions**: Stir at room temperature for 20 minutes.
- **Process**:
  - Dissolve the zirconium salt and H₄TBAPy in DMF.
  - Introduce benzoic acid as a modulator to control nucleation.
  - Ensure complete dissolution before progressing.
- **Key Steps**: Confirm reagent purity and accurate weighing for reproducibility.

##### Step 2: MOF Assembly/Crystallization
- **Reagents**: The homogeneous precursor solution.
- **Conditions**: Transfer to a Teflon-lined autoclave, then heat at 120 °C for 24 h.
- **Process**:
  - Seal the autoclave to prevent solvent loss.
  - Allow solvothermal conditions to drive crystal growth.
  - Cool slowly to room temperature to maximize crystal quality.
- **Key Steps**: Maintain constant temperature and minimize disturbances during the crystallization phase.

##### Step 3: Purification, Drying & Activation
- **Process**:
  - Collect crystals by filtration and wash repeatedly with DMF and ethanol to remove residual unreacted species.
  - Dry the washed crystals in an oven at 100 °C for approximately 10 hours.
  - If needed, proceed with vacuum activation to ensure complete solvent removal.
- **Key Steps**: Ensure thorough solvent exchange to achieve a high-quality, activated framework.

#### MOF Preparation [2]: NU-600, PCN-700, PCN-609, and PCN-606
For each subsequent MOF, a similar systematic workflow is applied:

1. **Precursor Preparation**: Dissolve appropriate Zr precursors with specific organic linkers (e.g., H₄TCPB-Br₂ for NU-600, H₂Me₂-BPDC for PCN-700, H₄CBTB for PCN-609, H₄TPCB for PCN-606) and modulators (acetic or benzoic acid) in DMF or DEF.
2. **MOF Assembly/Crystallization**: Conduct solvothermal reactions at 120 °C for 24–48 hours under controlled conditions.
3. **Purification**: Undertake filtration, washing with DMF and acetone, and solvent exchange to ensure removal of impurities.
4. **Drying & Activation**: Dry in a calibrated oven at 100 °C, optionally applying vacuum methods for enhanced activation.
5. **Post-synthetic Modification (if applicable)**: For frameworks such as NU-1000 and PCN-609, perform amine treatments (e.g., with S-MBA) at room temperature for several days to tailor photoluminescence and other functional properties.

**Caution**: Follow precise reagent quantities, maintain undisturbed reaction conditions, and adhere strictly to the timing and temperature protocols to ensure reproducibility and integrity of the MOF synthesis.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** Crystal structure and phase purity were determined using single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SC-XRD) and powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD). SC-XRD provides atomic-level resolution of framework and guest ordering, while PXRD assesses bulk phase purity and structural integrity.
- **Findings:** SC-XRD confirmed the formation of atomically ordered, single-crystalline superlattices in all MOFs studied (NU-1000, NU-600, PCN-700, PCN-609, PCN-606), resolving the intended dimensionality (0D, 1D, 2D) of the metal-halide sublattices. PXRD patterns of both pristine MOFs and their metal-halide superlattices matched simulated or parent patterns, with no impurity peaks observed.
- **Observations:** The absence of secondary phases or unreacted precursors in PXRD, and the distinct diffraction patterns of superlattices, confirm high phase purity and successful guest incorporation. Post-synthetic treatments (acid, base, boiling water) did not alter PXRD patterns, indicating robust framework stability.

#### 6.1.2 Morphology and Particle Size
- **Description:** Morphology and particle size were examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM), which reveal crystal habit, size distribution, and atomic ordering.
- **Findings:** SEM and TEM images showed well-faceted, uniform crystals for all MOFs, typically around 100 μm in size. HR-TEM of NU-1000 and PCN-700 visualized periodic lattice fringes and, in PCN-700, 1D PbI₂ nanorods aligned within nanochannels.
- **Observations:** The observed uniformity and faceting reflect controlled crystallization and precise templating. In PCN-606, cryogenic HR-TEM confirmed fully filled 2D PbI₂ layers and diverse lead coordination sites, matching SC-XRD models.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Surface area and porosity were assessed by N₂ adsorption (BET analysis), providing insight into framework accessibility and guest accommodation.
- **Findings:** BET surface areas remained high after guest incorporation and chemical challenge: 518 cm²/g for PbI₂@NU-1000, and 496 cm²/g for PbI₂@PCN-700, PCN-609, and PCN-606. Pore size distributions indicated minimal reduction post-modification.
- **Observations:** The retention of high surface area and porosity supports the frameworks’ ability to host and align metal-halide superlattices.

#### 6.1.4 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was used to evaluate framework and superlattice stability under heat.
- **Findings:** For PCN-606 and its PbI₂ superlattice, solvent loss occurred between 80–120 °C, with no significant weight loss until 450 °C.
- **Observations:** The frameworks and incorporated superlattices exhibit high thermal stability, maintaining integrity well above typical application temperatures.

#### 6.1.5 Chemical Composition and Elemental Analysis
- **Description:** Elemental composition and oxidation states were determined by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES), Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS).
- **Findings:** ICP-OES confirmed expected Zr:I:Pb ratios (e.g., 1:1.91:1.5 for PbI₂@PCN-609; 1:0.62:0.74 for PbI₂@NU-1000), indicating successful guest loading. FTIR spectra showed no significant changes after harsh treatments, while XPS revealed a ~0.5 eV increase in Pb²⁺ binding energy and a ~0.5 eV decrease in Zr⁴⁺ upon PbI₂ incorporation, suggesting partial electron transfer. Amine modification introduced new FTIR peaks (700 cm⁻¹, 1,630 cm⁻¹) and XPS shifts in Pb4f and N1s bands, indicating electron sharing.
- **Observations:** The frameworks demonstrate chemical robustness and stable guest incorporation, with post-synthetic modifications directly reflected in spectroscopic signatures.

#### 6.1.6 Nanochannel Structure and Guest Alignment
- **Description:** The dimensions and orientation of nanochannels, and the alignment of metal-halide guests, were elucidated by SC-XRD and HR-TEM.
- **Findings:** PCN-700 exhibited tetragonal-like nanochannels hosting 1D PbI₂ chains, while PCN-609 featured low-symmetry channels for 2D PbI₂ sheets. In PCN-606, six distinct Pb coordination sites were resolved.
- **Observations:** The precise channel geometries enable controlled dimensionality and ordering of superlattices, underpinning the materials’ tunable optoelectronic properties.



### 6.2 Performance Results

#### 6.2.1 Photoluminescence Properties
- **Description:** Photoluminescence (PL) properties, including emission spectra, quantum yield (PLQY), and lifetime, are critical indicators of the optoelectronic performance of MOF-based superlattices. PLQY is defined as the ratio of emitted to absorbed photons, while PL lifetime reflects the average time an exciton remains in the excited state before recombination.
- **Experimental Data:** Amine-modified PbI₂@MOF superlattices exhibited room-temperature PLQY values ranging from 1–15%. PL lifetimes were consistently in the sub-nanosecond regime (e.g., <1 ns), characteristic of exciton emission. The emission peak position and intensity were tunable via amine modification and superlattice dimensionality, with 2D S-MBA/PbI₂@PCN-606 displaying a bandgap of ~1.75 eV, notably smaller than typical layered perovskites (~2.5 eV).
- **Observations and Trends:** Enhanced and tunable PL was observed after post-synthetic amine modification, with emission intensity increasing with treatment time and amine size. The PL emission was attributed to excitonic processes, as confirmed by lifetime and temperature-dependent studies. Dimensionality-dependent PL emission was evident, with quantum confinement effects observed as the sublattice dimensionality decreased.

#### 6.2.2 Circularly Polarized Luminescence (CPL) and Chiroptical Activity
- **Description:** CPL quantifies the degree of circular polarization in emitted light, defined by the polarization degree \( P = \frac{I_L - I_R}{I_L + I_R} \), where \( I_L \) and \( I_R \) are the intensities of left- and right-circularly polarized emission, respectively.
- **Experimental Data:** Chiral amine-modified PCN-606 (R/S-MBA/PbI₂@PCN-606) exhibited strong CPL, with \( P \) values up to ~29% at 77 K and above 13% at 298 K. Circular dichroism (CD) spectra confirmed efficient chirality transfer from the amine to the superlattice.
- **Observations and Trends:** The CPL degree decreased with increasing temperature but remained significant at room temperature. These results demonstrate the potential of these materials for chiral optoelectronic applications.

#### 6.2.3 Optoelectronic Performance and Charge Separation
- **Description:** Optoelectronic performance was assessed via transient photocurrent response and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). Photocurrent response reflects the efficiency of photoinduced charge separation, while EIS (Nyquist plots) quantifies charge-transfer resistance.
- **Experimental Data:** PbI₂@PCN-700 and PbI₂@PCN-606 superlattices exhibited enhanced photocurrent responses and lower charge-transfer resistance compared to their pristine MOF counterparts. For example, 2D PbI₂@PCN-606 showed the highest photocurrent and lowest resistance among the series.
- **Observations and Trends:** The alignment and dimensionality of the PbI₂ sublattice within the MOF channels facilitated efficient charge transport and electron–hole separation, as evidenced by improved optoelectronic metrics.

#### 6.2.4 Bandgap Engineering
