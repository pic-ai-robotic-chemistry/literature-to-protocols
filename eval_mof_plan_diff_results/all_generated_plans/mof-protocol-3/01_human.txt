## 5. Evaluation Methods

### 5.1 Activation

The activation of the MOF catalyst is a critical pre-use treatment aimed at converting the post‑synthetic Fe–Cl sites to active Fe–OH sites. This conversion enhances the catalyst’s performance for methane oxidation by establishing mono‑iron hydroxyl centers, which are essential for efficient C–H bond activation.

#### 5.1.1 Photo‑Activation Process  
• **Definition:** Photo‑activation involves irradiating the MOF catalyst to induce a ligand exchange reaction, effectively replacing labile Cl ligands with hydroxide ions. This transformation is essential for generating the catalytically active PMOF‑RuFe(OH) species.  
• **Experimental Conditions:** The process is carried out by dispersing the PMOF‑RuFe(Cl) catalyst in deoxygenated water and subjecting it to visible‑light irradiation using a Xe lamp fitted with a 400–780 nm filter. The photo‑activation step is performed at ambient conditions for 2 hours.  
• **Observations:** Following the 2‑hour irradiation, analytical techniques (including XPS and Raman) confirm the elimination of chloride signatures and the emergence of hydroxyl-related features. This successful ligand exchange correlates with enhanced methanol yield and selectivity during CH₄ oxidation, validating the formation of the active Fe–OH sites.

#### 5.1.2 Aqueous and Ambient Conditions  
• **Definition:** The aqueous environment during activation not only serves as the solvent but also supplies the necessary hydroxide ions for the exchange reaction.  
• **Experimental Conditions:** The entire activation is conducted at room temperature without additional heating, ensuring that the MOF framework remains intact and that residual solvents from the prior THF washing step are effectively managed.  
• **Observations:** The absence of external temperature stress contributes to the structural stability of the framework while facilitating the efficient exchange of Cl for OH. This mild activation process is distinctly separate from thermal or vacuum treatments used in characterization protocols.

The deliberate integration of the photo‑activation step with the post‑synthetic metalation ensures that the catalyst transitions smoothly from its Fe–Cl precursor to the catalytically active Fe–OH form, directly impacting its performance in the selective conversion of CH₄ to CH₃OH.

- **Findings:** The BET surface area of PMOF‑Ru was reduced by approximately 50% following post-synthetic metalation to form PMOF‑RuFe(Cl), indicating successful incorporation of Fe species and other functional groups within the MOF pores. #### 6.1.3 Morphology and Elemental Distribution
- **Description:** Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), combined with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX), were used to examine particle morphology and elemental homogeneity. These analyses are vital for confirming uniformity and compositional integrity.
- **Findings:** SEM images revealed distorted octahedral crystals for all MOF variants. EDX mapping and TEM/HAADF imaging demonstrated homogeneous distribution of Ru, Fe, W, and V throughout the MOF particles, both before and after catalysis.
- **Observations:** The uniform morphology and elemental dispersion confirm successful integration of the photosensitizer, polyoxometalate clusters, and iron sites, which is crucial for synergistic catalytic activity.

#### 6.1.4 Chemical Composition and Coordination Environment
- **Description:** FTIR, Raman, and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) were employed to probe the coordination environment of linkers and metal centers, as well as to confirm post-synthetic transformations.
- **Findings:** FTIR spectra showed characteristic carboxylate coordination to Zr₆ nodes. After photo-activation, a negative peak at 3617 cm⁻¹ (O–H stretch) and the absence of Fe–Cl features confirmed the formation of Fe–OH groups. XPS analysis indicated Fe³⁺ as the predominant oxidation state, with Fe 2p₃/₂ and 2p₁/₂ peaks at 710 and 723 eV, and additional features consistent with Fe–OH. The presence of V (V⁵⁺/V⁴⁺), W⁶⁺, and Ru²⁺ was also confirmed.
- **Observations:** The spectroscopic data collectively validate the successful ligand exchange (Cl⁻ to OH⁻) and the chemical environments of all key components.

#### 6.1.5 Thermal Stability
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was used to assess the thermal stability and compositional changes of the MOF materials. This analysis is important for determining operational limits and framework robustness.
- **Findings:** Initial weight loss between 25–300 °C corresponded to solvent removal, while further loss between 300–500 °C was attributed to ligand decomposition. The residual mass increased with post-synthetic metalation (30.3% for PMOF‑Ru, 35.7% for PMOF‑RuFe(Cl), 40.7% for PMOF‑RuFe(OH)), indicating successful incorporation of additional components.
- **Observations:** The MOF framework remains stable up to ~300 °C, with decomposition occurring above this temperature, supporting its suitability for catalytic applications under mild conditions.

#### 6.1.6 Host–Guest Interactions and Active Site Formation
- **Description:** Inelastic neutron scattering (INS) coupled with DFT calculations was utilized to investigate host–guest interactions and the formation of catalytically active sites.
- **Findings:** INS revealed that CH₄ binds to the mono-iron hydroxyl site via hydrogen bonding (Fe–OH···CH₄ distance = 2.39 Å), weakening the C–H bond as evidenced by a red shift in the C–H stretching vibration. The transformation from Fe–Cl to Fe–OH was confirmed by the disappearance of Fe–Cl features and the emergence of Fe–OH signals in spectroscopic analyses.
- **Observations:** These results provide direct evidence for the formation of the [Fe–OH···CH₄] binding motif, which is central to the observed catalytic activity.




### 6.2 Performance Results

#### 6.2.1 Catalytic Activity: Methane to Methanol Conversion
- **Description:** Catalytic activity is quantified by the time yield of methanol (CH₃OH) produced per gram of catalyst per hour (mmol·g_cat⁻¹·h⁻¹) and the iron turnover number (Fe-TON), defined as the number of CH₃OH molecules generated per iron active site.
- **Experimental Data:** Under continuous-flow photo-oxidation at ambient temperature and pressure, PMOF‑RuFe(OH) achieved a CH₃OH time yield of 8.81 ± 0.34 mmol·g_cat⁻¹·h⁻¹ (18,908 ± 730 mmol·mol_Fe⁻¹·h⁻¹). In batch mode, the yield was 3,145 ± 340 µmol·g_cat⁻¹·h⁻¹ (6,750 ± 730 mmol·mol_Fe⁻¹·h⁻¹), with a cumulative Fe-TON of 135 ± 16. The quantum efficiency was measured at 0.07.
- **Observations and Trends:** The catalyst outperformed state-of-the-art photocatalysts and natural methane monooxygenase in both activity and efficiency. The presence of mono-iron hydroxyl sites, photosensitizer, and polyoxometalate clusters was essential for optimal performance, as confirmed by control experiments.

#### 6.2.2 Selectivity
- **Description:** Selectivity refers to the proportion of CH₃OH among all products, calculated as:  
  \[
  \text{Selectivity} = \frac{\text{moles of CH}_3\text{OH produced}}{\text{total moles of products}} \times 100\%
  \]
- **Experimental Data:** In flow mode, CH₃OH selectivity reached 100%, with CH₃OH as the only liquid product detected. In batch mode, selectivity was 98% under ambient conditions. Trace CO₂ (<2%) was the only byproduct observed.
- **Observations and Trends:** Rapid desorption of CH₃OH from the MOF matrix, facilitated by water, minimized over-oxidation and ensured high selectivity. Isotopic labeling confirmed CH₄ as the sole carbon source.

#### 6.2.3 Cycling Stability and Durability
- **Description:** Cycling stability assesses the retention of catalytic performance over repeated reaction cycles, while durability evaluates structural and compositional integrity post-reaction.
- **Experimental Data:** PMOF‑RuFe(OH) maintained high activity and selectivity over 120 h of continuous-flow operation and 10 consecutive batch cycles (totaling 200 h), with negligible loss in performance. The accumulated Fe-TON reached 2,269 ± 88 over 120 h.
- **Observations and Trends:** Post-reaction analyses (SEM, TEM, PXRD, IR, UV–vis, N₂ sorption, XAS) showed no significant changes in morphology, crystallinity, or oxidation state, and minimal leaching of active sites, confirming robust catalyst stability.

#### 6.2.4 Synergistic Effects and Mechanistic Insights
- **Description:** The cooperative function of the MOF matrix, photosensitizer, and polyoxometalate clusters is critical for efficient photocatalysis.
- **Experimental Data:** Catalysts lacking any component or using physical mixtures exhibited only trace CH₃OH production.
- **Observations and Trends:** The integrated design enabled effective charge separation, O₂ reduction, and C–H activation, resulting in superior activity and selectivity compared to systems with isolated or missing components.
