### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Electronic Structure and Absorption Properties

- **Description:** The electronic structure and optical absorption properties of the MOFs were characterized using density functional theory (DFT) calculations, specifically employing the HSE06 hybrid functional with PBEsol-optimized geometries (HSE06//PBEsol). These computational methods are critical for predicting the highest occupied and lowest unoccupied crystal orbital (HOCO–LUCO) gap (Eabs), which directly correlates with the material’s light absorption and photocatalytic potential.

- **Findings:**
  - For UiO-66(Zr), the calculated HOCO–LUCO gap (Eabs) is 3.73 eV, closely matching the experimental UV absorption onset of 3.76 eV. Both HOCO and LUCO are predominantly localized on the organic linker, indicating that light absorption and excitation are linker-centered.
  - In UiO-66(Ce)-X (where X denotes various functional groups), DFT calculations reveal that the LUCO is primarily located on the Ce 4f orbital, resulting in a much lower and tunable Eabs. For example, functionalization with the SH group reduces Eabs to 2.54 eV, shifting absorption into the visible range. The absorption energy can be modulated by the nature and number of linker substituents, with values ranging from 2.54 eV (SH) to 4.09 eV (unsubstituted).

- **Observations:** The close agreement between calculated and experimental Eabs for UiO-66(Zr) validates the computational approach. In Ce-based MOFs, linker functionalization introduces new filled states within the original band gap, enabling precise tuning of optical properties for visible-light-driven photocatalysis.

#### 6.1.2 Ligand-to-Metal Charge Transfer (LMCT) Analysis

- **Description:** LMCT energy (ELMCT) was assessed via DFT by evaluating the energy difference between the lowest unoccupied linker orbital and the lowest unoccupied metal orbital. This parameter is essential for understanding charge separation efficiency, which underpins photocatalytic activity.

- **Findings:**
  - In UiO-66(Zr), the lowest unoccupied metal orbital (Zr 4d) is 2.0 eV above the LUCO, resulting in a positive ELMCT (+2.0 eV) and inefficient LMCT.
  - In UiO-66(Ce)-X, the LUCO is predominantly on the Ce 4f orbital, yielding negative ELMCT values (e.g., −1.33 to −1.65 eV), which favor efficient LMCT and charge separation. The LMCT energy is further tunable by linker functionalization; electron-donating groups (e.g., NH₂) make ELMCT more negative, while electron-withdrawing groups (e.g., NO₂) make it less negative but still favorable.

- **Observations:** Only Ce-based MOFs exhibit energetically favorable LMCT, attributed to the low-lying empty 4f orbitals of Ce⁴⁺. This property is further enhanced by appropriate linker functionalization, directly correlating with improved photocatalytic performance.

#### 6.1.3 Density-of-States (DOS) and Functionalization Effects

- **Description:** DOS analysis was performed to elucidate the distribution and nature of electronic states, particularly the impact of linker functionalization on the electronic structure.

- **Findings:**
  - In Ce-based MOFs, functionalization introduces new filled states within the original HOCO–LUCO gap. Electron-donating substituents (e.g., NH₂) create states near the middle of the gap, while electron-withdrawing groups (e.g., NO₂) introduce states just above the original HOCO.
  - Increasing the number of substituents (e.g., disubstitution at 2,5-positions) amplifies these effects, further reducing the band gap and enhancing visible-light absorption.

- **Observations:** The ability to fine-tune the electronic structure through both metal node selection and linker functionalization enables targeted optimization of MOF photocatalysts for specific applications.





### 6.2 Performance Results

#### 6.2.1 Optical Absorption Energy (E_abs)
- **Description:** The optical absorption energy (E_abs) represents the energy required to excite an electron from the highest occupied crystal orbital (HOCO) to the lowest unoccupied crystal orbital (LUCO) within the MOF. It is a key indicator of the material’s ability to absorb light, particularly relevant for photocatalytic applications. E_abs is typically reported in electronvolts (eV) and can be determined both experimentally (via UV–vis spectroscopy) and computationally (via DFT calculations).
- **Experimental Data:** For UiO-66(Zr), the calculated E_abs is 3.73 eV, closely matching the experimental value of 3.76 eV (UV–vis onset). For Ce-based MOFs (UiO-66(Ce)-X), E_abs values are tunable through linker functionalization, ranging from 2.54 eV (SH substituent) to 4.09 eV (H), with several functionalized variants (e.g., NH₂, I, Br) exhibiting E_abs values within the visible light region (1.7–3.2 eV).
- **Observations and Trends:** The close agreement between calculated and experimental E_abs for UiO-66(Zr) validates the computational approach. In Ce-based MOFs, electron-donating substituents on the linker significantly lower E_abs, enhancing visible-light absorption and thus photocatalytic potential.

#### 6.2.2 Ligand-to-Metal Charge Transfer Energy (E_LMCT)
- **Description:** The ligand-to-metal charge transfer energy (E_LMCT) quantifies the energy change associated with transferring a photogenerated electron from the excited linker orbital to the lowest unoccupied metal orbital. It is defined as \( E_{LMCT} = E_{LUCO,metal} - E_{LUCO,linker} \) and reported in eV. Negative E_LMCT values indicate favorable charge separation, which is critical for efficient photocatalysis.
- **Experimental Data:** UiO-66(Zr) exhibits an E_LMCT of +2.00 eV, indicating inefficient charge transfer. In contrast, UiO-66(Ce)-X frameworks display negative E_LMCT values, ranging from –1.33 to –1.65 eV, depending on the linker substituent.
- **Observations and Trends:** The positive E_LMCT in UiO-66(Zr) correlates with poor charge separation and limited photocatalytic activity. Ce-based MOFs, with their negative E_LMCT, demonstrate enhanced charge separation, supporting superior photocatalytic performance.

#### 6.2.3 Band Edge Alignment and Photocatalytic Suitability
- **Description:** The absolute positions of the HOCO and LUCO relative to redox potentials for water splitting and CO₂ reduction determine the MOF’s suitability for these photocatalytic reactions. Proper alignment ensures that photogenerated electrons and holes can drive the desired redox processes.
- **Experimental Data:** In UiO-66(Ce)-X, functionalization enables tuning of band edge positions to straddle the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER) potentials, as well as the CO₂ reduction potential.
- **Observations and Trends:** The ability to modulate band edge positions through linker functionalization allows for the rational design of MOFs optimized for specific photoredox applications.

#### 6.2.4 Electronic Structure Modulation via Linker Functionalization
- **Description:** Functionalizing the BDC linker with electron-donating or withdrawing groups introduces new filled states within the original band gap, directly impacting E_abs and E_LMCT.
- **Experimental Data:** Density of states (DOS) analyses reveal that electron-donating groups (e.g., NH₂, SH) introduce filled states near the middle of the gap, while electron-withdrawing groups (e.g., NO₂, CF₃) shift states closer to the HOCO. This results in a reduction of E_abs and maintenance of negative E_LMCT across all functionalized Ce-MOFs.
- **Observations and Trends:** Increasing the number or strength of functional groups further enhances visible-light absorption and charge separation, providing a versatile platform for tuning photocatalytic properties.
