## 2. Synthesis Methods

### Extraction of Detailed Preparation and Post-processing Steps

The article outlines a solvothermal methodology for MOF synthesis, implemented under a controlled nitrogen atmosphere in a 100 mL Schlenk flask to avoid moisture and oxygen interference. The process begins with the dissolution of 10 mg of NiTPz-(OH)₈ (or H₂TPz-(OH)₈ for DC-102) in 25 mL of anhydrous DMSO. Sonication for 15 minutes ensures complete and homogeneous precursor dissolution, as verified by the observed clarity of the solution.

• Step 1: Precursor Mixing / Dissolution  
 - Reagent: 10 mg NiTPz-(OH)₈ or H₂TPz-(OH)₈  
 - Solvent: 25 mL anhydrous DMSO  
 - Equipment: 100 mL Schlenk flask; sonicator (15 min)

• Step 2: Modulator or Structure-Directing Agent Addition  
 - Agent: Ethylenediamine (EDA) added in 1600 equivalents for DC-100/DC-101 or up to 3200 equivalents for DC-102  
 - Purpose: Acts as a modulator to slow the nucleation process for enhanced crystallinity

• Step 3: Addition of Metal Precursor  
 - For DC-100 and DC-102: Cu(NO₃)₂; for DC-101: Zn(NO₃)₂  
 - Amounts are carefully controlled to achieve the desired metal-to-linker ratio

• Step 4: Crystallization  
 - Conditions: Heating at 85 °C for 2 days in a controlled oven, allowing proper MOF crystal formation (evidenced by a black solid formation and characteristic PXRD peaks)

• Step 5: Cooling and Collection  
 - The reaction mixture is cooled to room temperature, and the crystalline solid is separated via centrifugation

• Step 6: Washing and Solvent Exchange  
 - Washing protocol: Methanol (20 mL × 2), deionized water (20 mL × 2), and acetone (20 mL × 3) to remove residual synthesis components

• Step 7: Drying (Activation)  
 - Procedure: Dry in a vacuum oven at 65 °C for 24 hours to ensure complete removal of solvents

This stepwise procedure, including controlled reagent addition and precise temperature and duration settings, offers a reproducible synthesis route. Comparative notes in the article highlight that while the solvothermal route necessitates longer reaction times and specific equipment, the resulting MOFs exhibit high crystallinity and uniform morphology, critical for their subsequent NO sensing applications.

### 3.2 Detailed synthesis process

#### MOF Preparation [1]: DC-100

##### Step 1: Precursor Preparation  
- **Reagents**: 0.44 mM NiTPz-(OH)₈, Cu(NO₃)₂ (2.1 equivalents), and 1600 equivalents of ethylenediamine (EDA) dissolved in anhydrous DMSO.  
- **Conditions**: In a 100 mL Schlenk flask, use sonication for 15 minutes to ensure complete dissolution of NiTPz-(OH)₈.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Add the specified NiTPz-(OH)₈ into anhydrous DMSO in the Schlenk flask and sonicate for 15 minutes until fully dissolved.  
  2. Introduce 1600 equivalents of EDA to modulate the nucleation rate, ensuring controlled crystallization.  
  3. Add the required amount of Cu(NO₃)₂.  
- **Key Steps**: Maintain a nitrogen atmosphere to avoid oxygen interference and ensure precise reagent ratios throughout.

##### Step 2: MOF Assembly/Crystallization  
- **Conditions**: Heat the reaction mixture at 85 °C for 2 days under nitrogen.  
- **Process**:  
  1. Seal the flask and transfer it into a temperature-controlled oven.  
  2. Allow the mixture to react at 85 °C for 2 days, facilitating gradual nucleation and growth of the MOF crystals.  
  3. Cool to room temperature.

##### Step 3: Purification, Drying & Activation  
- **Process**:  
  1. Collect the black crystalline solid by centrifugation.  
  2. Wash with methanol (20 mL × 2), followed by deionized water (20 mL × 2), and acetone (20 mL × 3) using vortex mixing to remove impurities.  
  3. Dry the final product in a vacuum oven at 65 °C for 24 hours.

## 6 Results


### 6.1 Characterization Results

#### 6.1.1 Crystal Structure and Phase Purity
- **Description:** Crystal structure and phase purity were determined by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD), a key technique for confirming framework formation and assessing crystallinity in MOFs.
- **Findings:** PXRD patterns for DC-100, DC-101, and DC-102 exhibited distinct peaks at 2θ = 3.9°, 5.6°, 7.8°, and 27.5°, corresponding to the (100), (110), (200), and (001) facets, respectively. These peaks matched simulated patterns based on the P4/mmm space group, confirming the formation of well-ordered 2D frameworks with AA (eclipsed) stacking.
- **Observations:** The absence of impurity peaks across all samples indicates high phase purity. DC-100, in particular, displayed sharper and more intense peaks when synthesized under optimized conditions (low concentration of NiTPz-(OH)₈ and high EDA), reflecting enhanced crystallinity.

#### 6.1.2 Morphology and Pore Structure
- **Description:** Morphology and pore structure were examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), which provide insights into particle size, shape, and internal porosity.
- **Findings:** SEM images revealed nanoscale cubic crystallites for all MOFs. TEM and high-resolution TEM (HR-TEM) visualized well-defined square pores with dimensions of 2.2–2.3 nm. Fast Fourier transform (FFT) analysis confirmed the periodicity of the pore structure.
- **Observations:** The uniform cubic morphology and regular pore arrangement indicate controlled crystallization, attributed to the use of excess EDA as a structure-directing agent during synthesis.

#### 6.1.3 Surface Area and Porosity
- **Description:** Surface area and porosity were quantified by nitrogen adsorption measurements at 77 K using the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) method, which is critical for evaluating gas accessibility in MOFs.
- **Findings:** BET surface areas were reported as 396 m²/g for DC-100, 305 m²/g for DC-101, and 408 m²/g for DC-102. All materials exhibited significant porosity, consistent with their open 2D frameworks.
- **Observations:** The high surface areas and mesoporous channels (2.2–2.3 nm) are optimal for rapid gas diffusion, supporting the materials’ suitability for gas sensing applications.

#### 6.1.4 Thermal Stability and Guest Content
- **Description:** Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was used to assess thermal stability and quantify volatile guest molecules within the frameworks.
- **Findings:** For DC-100, TGA showed an initial mass loss of 11–12% at 100 °C, corresponding to the release of ethylenediamine (EDA) and water guests. The framework remained stable up to high temperatures, with decomposition occurring only at elevated temperatures (residue measured at 900 °C).
- **Observations:** The observed mass loss aligns with the proposed composition, confirming guest inclusion and robust thermal stability.

#### 6.1.5 Chemical Composition and Electronic Structure
- **Description:** X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) were employed to determine elemental composition, oxidation states, and the presence of paramagnetic centers.
- **Findings:** XPS confirmed the presence of C, O, N, Ni, and Cu in DC-100, with a Ni 2p to Cu 2p peak area ratio of 1:2. High-resolution Cu 2p spectra revealed Cu(I) (932.5 eV) and Cu(II) (934.0 eV) in a 6:4 ratio. O 1s and N 1s spectra supported the presence of EDA and a semiquinone structure. EPR spectra indicated stable Cu(II) centers, with only a slight (4%) increase in signal after NO exposure.
- **Observations:** Minimal changes in XPS and EPR signals after NO exposure demonstrate high redox stability and the persistence of active metal sites, essential for reversible sensing.




### 6.2 Performance Results

#### 6.2.1 NO Sensing Sensitivity and Detection Limit
- **Description:** Sensitivity is quantified by the normalized chemiresistive response (percentage change in current) upon NO exposure. The detection limit (LOD) is the lowest NO concentration that produces a measurable response, typically expressed in parts-per-trillion (ppt).
- **Experimental Data:** DC-100 exhibited an ultralow LOD of 0.47 ppt, with a normalized response magnitude of −184,000% at 1 ppm NO. In comparison, DC-102 and DC-101 showed lower responses (−107,000% and −57,000%, respectively).
- **Observations and Trends:** DC-100’s sensitivity surpasses previously reported MOF sensors, attributed to the synergistic effect of dual active sites (Ni and Cu/EDA). The LOD range (0.47–6.20 ppt) is the lowest among benchmarked chemiresistive sensors.

#### 6.2.2 Response Time and Kinetics
- **Description:** Response time is defined as the duration required for the sensor to reach a steady-state response upon NO exposure. The initial response rate quantifies the speed of signal change, reported as % per minute.
- **Experimental Data:** DC-100 achieved saturation within 5 minutes at 1 ppm NO, with response observable within seconds. The initial response rate ranged from 370 to 102,000% per minute for 20–1000 ppb NO, and the pseudo-first-order rate constant was calculated as 118 min⁻¹.
- **Observations and Trends:** The rapid response of DC-100 (5 seconds at 1 ppm) is 370–2600 times faster than previous MPc-based cMOFs, enabling real-time NO detection.

#### 6.2.3 Selectivity
- **Description:** Selectivity is the ratio of the sensor’s response to NO versus other gases, indicating its ability to distinguish NO in complex environments.
- **Experimental Data:** DC-100 demonstrated a 250-fold higher response to NO compared to NO₂ at 1 ppm over 5 minutes. Negligible responses were observed for H₂S, SO₂, CO, and NH₃.
- **Observations and Trends:** The pronounced selectivity is attributed to the specific interaction of NO with Cu(II) edge sites, as confirmed by control experiments with DC-101 (Zn-based, no NO response).

#### 6.2.4 Cycling Stability and Reversibility
- **Description:** Cycling stability assesses the retention of sensing performance over repeated exposure–recovery cycles. Reversibility is evaluated by the recovery percentage after each cycle.
- **Experimental Data:** DC-100 maintained full sensitivity for at least 5 cycles (5 min exposure, 30 min recovery) and high recovery (83–96%) after 30 minutes in N₂. Consistent performance was observed over 15 cycles with only gradual decline.
- **Observations and Trends:** The sensor exhibited excellent reversibility and reproducibility under both dry and humid conditions, with stable performance across multiple batches.

#### 6.2.5 Comparative Performance of MOF Variants
- **Description:** Comparative analysis highlights the impact of metal node and ligand selection on NO sensing.
- **Experimental Data:** DC-101 (Zn-based) showed negligible NO response, while DC-102 (Cu, metal-free ligand) had intermediate sensitivity. The combined response of DC-101 and DC-102 was still less than that of DC-100.
- **Observations and Trends:** The dual active site architecture in DC-100 is critical for achieving ultrahigh sensitivity and selectivity, underscoring the importance of molecular engineering in MOF sensor design.
